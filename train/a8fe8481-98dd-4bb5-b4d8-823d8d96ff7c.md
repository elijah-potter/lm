The auditorium fell into an eerie, unbroken silence after the final chord dissolved. Every breath held, every heartbeat a faint, rhythmic hum in the dark. The empty rows of chairs stretched like polished obsidian, their once-vibrant reds now faded to a sickly, ashen hue. A single flickering bulb dangled from the ceiling, its glow barely piercing the thick, humid air, casting long shadows that seemed to writhe.

Outside, the city’s pulse—cars, sirens, distant chatter—had vanished. The streets, once a cacophony of movement, were now a still expanse of glass and concrete. A single, rusted trash can sat abandoned, its lid half-buried in the pavement. The air smelled of damp earth and something metallic, like old blood left to dry.

A child’s laughter, once a constant in the neighborhood, had stopped. Now, only the occasional creak of a door or the distant, rhythmic *tap-tap-tap* of someone’s bare feet on pavement reached them. No one moved. No one spoke. The world had paused, and in that silence, the weight of the unseen pressed down—something vast, something waiting.

Then, the first sound: a single, deliberate *clap*. The sound was soft, almost imperceptible, but it broke the spell. The silence didn’t dissolve. It *answered*.
