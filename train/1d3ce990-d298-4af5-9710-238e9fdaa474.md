A winding dirt road, half-buried under loose gravel, curves and loops like a lost thought. Overgrown shrubs sprout at random angles, their branches clawing at the sky, while the earth itself seems to sag in places, forming shallow, irregular depressions that catch and hold puddles of rainwater. The air hums with an eerie silence, broken only by the distant, rhythmic drip of something unseen—a leak, perhaps, or a trickle of something darker beneath the surface. The path disappears into a tunnel of thorned vines, their thorns glinting dully in the faint light filtering through, as if the earth itself is whispering to you not to follow.
