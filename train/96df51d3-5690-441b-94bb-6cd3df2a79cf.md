The first mistake came in the form of a misplaced key. Elias had forgotten to lock his apartment door before leaving for work, and by the time he remembered, he was already halfway to the subway. His apartment door swung open as he turned the corner, revealing a stranger standing in the hallway—his neighbor, Mr. Chen, clutching a half-eaten bowl of noodles and a suspiciously warm package wrapped in newspaper.

Mr. Chen’s eyes widened. “You’re late.” His voice was sharp, but his hands trembled as he tossed the package onto Elias’s coffee table. Inside lay a single, bloodstained knife—its blade still wet with something dark and viscous. Elias’s blood turned to ice. “What the—?” he stammered.

Mr. Chen’s face twisted. “You *shouldn’t* be here. That’s not my knife.” His voice cracked. “It’s yours. But you’re not supposed to know.” He pressed something into Elias’s palm—a crumpled receipt from a diner in Brooklyn, dated the same day. The words on it read: *“Service: $0.00. Item: ‘The One That Got Away.’”*

Elias’s fingers brushed the knife’s hilt. The moment he did, the apartment’s lights flickered. The walls seemed to pulse. Then, from the fridge, a voice—his own—whispered, *“You’re supposed to kill her.”*

Outside, the subway doors hissed shut. Elias stumbled into the station, clutching the knife, the receipt, and the stranger’s terrified gaze. The next thing he knew, he was standing in a dimly lit alley, surrounded by figures in black suits. One of them, a woman with silver-streaked hair, handed him a file. Its cover bore a single word: **ERROR.**
