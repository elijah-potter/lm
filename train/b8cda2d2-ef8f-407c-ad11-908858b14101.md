A cavernous, half-submerged cathedral of rusted iron and glass, its vaults sagging under the weight of centuries. The air hums with the faint, ghostly echo of a drowned choir, their voices lost in the damp, thick silence. The water—deep, still, and teal—mirrors the sky, which is visible only through the jagged, cracked surface above, as if the world above is suspended in suspended motion. The walls are lined with driftwood and driftstone, each piece bearing the weight of time, their edges worn smooth by centuries of slow erosion. The only movement is the occasional ripple in the water, like a breath held too long before releasing. The air smells of damp earth and iron, and the weight of an unseen, unseen presence lingers, pressing down—not from above, but from the very fabric of existence itself, waiting.
