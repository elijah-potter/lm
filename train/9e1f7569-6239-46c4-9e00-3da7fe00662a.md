**The hidden rules of "The Unspoken"** operate implicitly through social cues and unspoken conventions. Here are key examples:

1. **Public Spaces (Cafés, Parks)**
   - **No loud conversations** – Silence is a signal of respect, even if no one is explicitly asking.
   - **Queue etiquette** – Interrupting lines is frowned upon; a brief glance to check for movement suffices.
   - **Shared surfaces** – Knives forks must be returned to the table; chairs fold neatly without asking.

2. **Digital Interactions (Texts, Slack)**
   - **Reply-all etiquette** – Assuming replies go to *all* when only a few are copied.
   - **Punctuation as tone** – Capitalization and emojis replace explicit apologies (e.g., "Sorry!" → `sorry`).
   - **Bypassing DMs** – Asking someone else to "pass a message" implies trust in their discretion.

3. **Workplace Dynamics (Offices)**
   - **Time management** – Lunch breaks start when the clock hits 12:00, not when hunger strikes.
   - **Initiative** – "Let me know if you need X" is often a request for *no further action*.
   - **Greetings** – A nod to a new colleague is usually enough; no need for introductions.

4. **Social Hierarchies (Family Gatherings)**
   - **Age precedence** – The oldest person speaks first in debates; younger members defer without being told.
   - **Gift-giving norms** – "Just a small token" implies the recipient should accept it graciously, even if it’s inadequate.
   - **Silence as consent** – Not answering a question in a group means "yes" or "no," unless you’re *sure* you want to speak up.

5. **Cultural Norms (General)**
   - **Eye contact** – Holding it too long can signal interest; too short may imply disinterest.
   - **Personal space** – Touching someone’s arm to "get their attention" is common; lingering touches require consent.
   - **Food etiquette** – Dipping bread in soup is rude; sharing it implies you’ve already eaten.

**Core Principle:** Rules here exist in the gaps between actions. What’s *not* said carries as much weight as what *is*. Omitting them is often the only way to comply.
