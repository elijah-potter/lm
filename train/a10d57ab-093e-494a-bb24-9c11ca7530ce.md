A recent study in *Nature* identified a previously unknown enzyme in archaea that catalyzes the reverse of photosynthesis, converting atmospheric oxygen into water under extreme conditions (pH 10, 100°C). This challenges the dominant assumption that oxygenic evolution was a one-way process. The discovery raises new questions about:
1. The evolutionary pressures driving this adaptation.
2. Potential metabolic pathways in extremophiles for oxygen recycling.
3. The implications for early Earth’s oxygenation and the role of extremophiles in biogeochemical cycles.
Further research may reveal whether this enzyme is widespread or a rare niche adaptation, potentially reshaping hypotheses on the origins of oxygenic respiration.
