After the hum of distant traffic faded, the kitchen’s fluorescent light dimmed into warm amber. The sink’s water, once boiling, now sat still, its surface rippling like a pond reflecting the soft glow of the night sky through the open window. The fridge’s quiet whir replaced the clatter of dishes, and the hum of the fridge’s cooling motor filled the space where the fridge had once been. A single candle flickered in the center of the table, casting a flickering light over the empty plates and the half-eaten sandwich left on the counter. The silence wasn’t absolute—just the absence of voices, the absence of the rush, the absence of the world moving past. The air smelled like old bread and lavender, the scent of something that had been there for hours but was now the only thing that mattered. A breath escaped her lips, slow and steady, and she leaned back against the chair, the weight of the moment settling over her like a blanket. The world had stopped.
