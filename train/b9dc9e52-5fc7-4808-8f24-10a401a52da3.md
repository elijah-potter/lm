The old clock tower at the edge of Blackwood Hollow always chimed midnight exactly, though no one in town believed the stories about the clock’s ghostly hands. Every summer, Jake and his best friend, Mira, would sneak out after their parents’ arguments about the "no after-dark activities" rule.

Tonight was different. The sky was too bright—like the moon had been scraped clean and left with only a silver ribbon. Jake’s fingers brushed the rusted latch of the tower’s door, and the metal groaned. Inside, the air smelled like wet stone and something older, something that whispered if you listened closely.

Mira’s breath hitched. "You sure this isn’t just the wind?"

Jake ignored her. The clock’s face was cracked in the top left corner, and the hands had stopped at 11:58. But the second hand—usually a blur—moved slowly, like it was counting down to something. A cold draft slithered up their backs. Then, the chime. Not the deep, resonant *ding-dong*, but a single, sharp *clang*, as if someone had struck a bell with something sharp.

Mira grabbed his arm. "We should—"

A voice, smooth as old oil, drifted from the shadows. *"You’re late."*

Jake’s stomach dropped. He hadn’t realized he’d been holding his breath until it rushed out. The voice belonged to an old man who’d died years ago—the one who’d warned them about the Hollow’s secrets. But he wasn’t standing anywhere in particular. He was *there*, just behind them, his coat too long, his face half-lit by the faint glow of the broken clock.

"Who—?" Mira’s voice cracked.

The man smiled, revealing teeth too white, too sharp. "The Hollow remembers everything. And it’s hungry."

Jake’s eyes widened. "What do you mean?"

Before the man could answer, the clock’s hands spun backward—fast, frantic. The seconds ticked backward, too. Then, the tower shuddered. The ground beneath them groaned, and the world tilted. The last thing Jake saw before the darkness swallowed them was the man’s laugh, echoing like a chainsaw.

And then—nothing.
