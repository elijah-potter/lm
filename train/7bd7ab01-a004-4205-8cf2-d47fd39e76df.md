I remember the way the rain fell on the old factory roof,
a steady, rhythmic patter that drowned out the world outside.
The scent of damp earth and rusted metal clung to my clothes,
a ghost of something forgotten, buried deep in the soil.

I remember the handwriting on the walls—words scratched in chalk,
each one a half-remembered promise, each one a half-forgotten fear.
A name, a date, a number, each one a puzzle piece,
somehow fitting together, yet still missing.

I remember the sound of my grandmother’s voice, low and slow,
telling stories of a time before the war, before the world grew gray.
She’d spin tales of love and loss, of courage and despair,
her words like seeds planted in my mind, growing slowly, unseen.

I remember the way the fire burned in the hearth,
casting long shadows on the walls where my father’s face once hung,
a silhouette frozen in time, a ghost that still watched over us.
The embers glowed red, the scent of smoke thick in the air.

I remember the first time I tasted freedom—
a stolen cigarette, the taste of rebellion on my tongue.
A spark, a flame, a fire that refused to be extinguished,
burning bright in the dark, refusing to dim.

I remember the way the city hummed at night,
a million voices, a million dreams, all tangled in the same thread.
I remember the fear, the hope, the quiet desperation,
the quiet strength that held me up when the world felt like it was crashing down.

I remember the letters I wrote, the words I couldn’t say,
each one a confession, each one a half-truth, each one a lie.
I remember the way the ink blurred on the paper,
the way my hands shook, the way my heart raced.

I remember the weight of silence, the quiet in the room,
the way the air felt thick, heavy with unsaid words.
I remember the way the past pressed against me,
a ghost that refused to stay buried.

I remember the way the light changed in the afternoon,
the way the shadows stretched long, the way the world seemed to hold its breath.
I remember the way I stood at the edge of something,
something I couldn’t quite name, something I couldn’t quite see.

I remember the way I wanted to run, to disappear,
to lose myself in the crowd, to forget the weight of everything.
But I remember too, the way I stood my ground,
the way I chose to stay, to fight, to keep going.

I remember the way the world turned, the way the seasons changed,
the way I grew, the way I changed, the way I stayed the same.
I remember the way I loved, the way I lost, the way I found,
the way I was, the way I am, the way I think.
