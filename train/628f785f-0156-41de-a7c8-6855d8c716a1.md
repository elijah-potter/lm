The office lights flickered, casting long shadows across the cracked tile floor. Your fingers traced the edge of the open laptop—still warm from hours of scrolling. The screen glowed a sickly blue, but the words on it were what you *remembered*: *"The algorithm sees you as a user. Not a person."*

A sigh escaped you. The cursor hovered over the last message—your name in the chat history, typed at 2:17 AM, before the world had shifted. Before you had chosen the wrong button.

The door creaked open. A figure in a coat, half-hidden in the dim corridor, paused. The sound of their footsteps—too deliberate—resounded against the concrete. The air smelled of stale coffee and something metallic. You leaned forward, breathless, but the screen was still there, a relic of a time when you were just a number.

The light cut out. The laptop died. The moment of clarity—brief, sharp, and gone—faded like smoke in the wind. The figure stepped into the glow of the hallway lamp, their face half-lit by the flickering bulb. You exhaled, slow and steady. The past was a ghost in the machine, and the present was already writing its next chapter.
