- Blue whale: The blue whale is the largest-ever animal on Earth and even exceeds the size of prehistoric animals, such as the largest dinosaurs.
- Bluefin tuna: Bluefin tuna can swim up to 90 kph and sustain their speed. 
- Bull shark: Bull sharks have repeatedly been reported in incidents of fatal shark bites. 
- Coral: Corals are sometimes used in replacing bone grafts because of how similar their chemistry and architecture are to human bones. 
- Great white shark: The great white shark is responsible for more reported bites on surfers and divers than any other species. 
- Marlin: Marlins are some of the fastest fish in the world's oceans (up to 121 kph when moving in short bursts). 
- Mollusk: Four thousand species of mollusk live in the Great Barrier Reef.
- Oarfish: Oarfish can grow up to 17 m long and have horse-like faces. 
- Phytoplankton: A mouthful of seawater can contain up to hundreds of thousands of phytoplankton.
- Sea turtle: Sea turtles are prey for bull sharks, tiger sharks, and great white sharks.
- Swordfish: Swordfish are among the fastest fish in Earth's oceans, getting up to 121 kph in short bursts.
- Zooplankton: There can be tens of thousands of zooplankton in one mouthful of seawater.
