A cramped attic room in a decaying Victorian house, dampness clinging to the walls. The fire crackles low, casting flickering shadows of a half-eaten meal—half-eaten because the promise of a meal had been kept only until the first mouthful. The old man at the table, fingers steepled, watches his daughter’s hands tremble as she pours tea. The girl’s eyes are hollow, her breath shallow. The last of the salted beef is gone; the last of the bread, too. Outside, the wind howls through the broken panes. The father’s voice is quiet: *"You promised you’d never leave."* The girl’s mouth curls. *"I did."* The silence after that is heavier than the rain.
