A bureaucratic loophole in the *Employee Code of Conduct* allows managers to reclassify "unproductive" tasks as "mandatory training" for a one-time credit toward performance reviews. The fine print stipulates that "training" must be "relevant to career development," yet vague definitions like "self-directed learning" or "mentorship opportunities" are often misinterpreted as such. Over time, this ambiguity has enabled supervisors to shift work assignments into this category without explicit approval, effectively erasing accountability for tasks previously deemed essential. The system’s ambiguity fosters a passive compliance culture where employees—especially junior staff—assume oversight is sufficient, while leadership quietly redefines labor standards.
