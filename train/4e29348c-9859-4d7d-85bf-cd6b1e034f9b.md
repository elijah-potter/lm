**Earth’s pulse beneath my fingertips**—soft moss clings like damp silk, the first touch of morning dew. The air hums with the warmth of unseen breath, thick as honeyed silence. The scent of damp soil and wildflowers lingers, a rich, earthy perfume that lingers on my tongue like a forgotten memory. The wind carries whispers of rustling leaves, a low, rhythmic song that vibrates through my bones, a melody older than my name. The weight of a fallen acorn rests in my palm, heavy with the weight of centuries, its shell whispering secrets of storms and storms of time. The ground beneath me is alive—each step a dance with the pulse of the earth, a symphony of roots and decay. The chill of morning mist clings to my cheeks, cool and tender, a caress from a world that never sleeps.
