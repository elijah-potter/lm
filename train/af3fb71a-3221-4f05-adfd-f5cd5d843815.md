In the dim glow of a cracked fluorescent bulb, Daniel sat hunched over his half-eaten sandwich, the plastic wrapper tangled around his fingers. His mother’s voice echoed in the quiet kitchen, sharp with unsaid accusations: *"You should’ve been more responsible."* His stomach twisted—not from hunger, but from the memory of the late-night calls, the excuses he’d spun, the lies that had turned his childhood into a battleground of his own making.

Outside, the city pulsed with life, a neon blur of laughter and noise, oblivious to the storm brewing within. Daniel’s reflection in the fogged glass of the fridge door was a stranger—eyes hollow, a smile stiff as the plastic wrap around his sandwich. He reached for the phone on the counter, fingers trembling. The call was always the same: *"Why did you do this?"* But the answer, whispered into the dark, was always the same: *"I didn’t have a choice."*

The sandwich lay untouched, half-eaten, a silent confession of what he’d already decided: the walls of the apartment would swallow him whole, and no one would ever know. The key to the safe deposit box, tucked into his pocket, was the only thing that kept him from burning everything—his past, his regrets, the weight of a life he’d carved out, one choice at a time.
