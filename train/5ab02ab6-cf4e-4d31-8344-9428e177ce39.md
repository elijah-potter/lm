In a café where the hum of conversation and distant chatter echoes through the morning haze, a stranger sits across from you—same hat, same coffee stain on the sleeve of their coat, the same way their fingers tap the table rhythmically. The memory of this moment flickers like a half-remembered dream, the details dissolving into the static of a recording that’s been played too many times.

Your phone buzzes with an unread notification. The screen glows with a message from someone else’s name: *"Remember me?"* The text isn’t there, but the voice is—just a whisper in your head, the same one that once said, *"We were here together."* The café disappears. The stranger fades. The time is suspended.

The borrowed moment isn’t in the space between you and them, but in the way your body recognizes the shape of it—how your breath catches, how your pulse quickens when the air smells like stale cigarettes and something else, like rain on hot pavement. It’s not stolen; it’s just *known*. The lullaby of it plays in the background, the same one that made you cry as a child, the same one you hum in the shower now, unaware of its origin. The moment feels like a ghost in your own life, a fragment of someone else’s story that has found its way into yours.

Outside, the city wakes. The borrowed moment lingers like a half-remembered promise—something you almost remember, but not quite. The stranger’s hand, the way they leaned forward at the table, the scent of their cologne—all of it exists in the space between you and the world, untethered, waiting to be claimed or forgotten.
