A winding dirt road, shoulders eroded by time and rain, leading toward an overgrown fence. The fence’s rusted pickets sway slightly in the breeze, marking the end of a once-traveled path. Beyond the fence, the terrain levels out into a vast, featureless plain—no trees, no signs, just endless stretches of flat, grassy earth. A single, cracked wooden signpost stands abandoned, its once-bold lettering now faded to nothing. A faint, half-buried wheel rims of an old wagon lie half-collapsed in the dirt, its contents long scattered. The air smells of damp earth and something metallic, like rust and forgotten iron. No buildings loom, no lights flicker; the horizon is a blur of indistinct shapes, too distant to make out. The path feels like it could stretch on forever, vanishing into the horizon in all directions.
