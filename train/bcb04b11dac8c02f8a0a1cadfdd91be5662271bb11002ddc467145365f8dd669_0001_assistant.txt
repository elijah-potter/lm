Robert Moog is most well-known for inventing the Moog Synthesizer. The Moog synthesizer helped to revolutionize the music industry and the face of music, itself, by giving artists and composers the ability to create a new palette of sounds. The device was the first complete voltage-controlled modular synthesizer and was capable of producing a wide variety of electronic sounds. He first introduced the Moog Synthesizer in 1964. Composer Wendy Carlos was one of the first to bring attention to the synthesizer with her 1968 album "Switched-On Bach." As a young person, Moog studied the piano while growing up in New York City. He earned undergraduate degrees in both physics and electrical engineering. He completed his Ph.D. in engineering physics at Cornell University in 1965. Robert Moog died in 2005. The Bob Moog Foundation and Moog Music, Inc. carry on his legacy.
