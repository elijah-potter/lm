The weight of choices pressed against my chest like a storm. The door to the café stood ajar, half-lit by the amber glow of a streetlamp outside, casting flickering shadows on the wall. One hand hovered over the keyboard, fingers twitching—*submit*, *undo*—the cursor blinking a question mark.

Behind me, the city pulsed, a heartbeat of neon and distant laughter, but none of it mattered. Not really. The clock on the wall read 11:47 PM, but the time was irrelevant. What mattered was the hollow ache in my stomach, the way my mind replayed every possible outcome like a broken record.

*"What if I choose the wrong one?"* The words were a whisper, but they echoed louder than the hum of the AC. The screen blurred—static, then the draft of a draft. My fingers paused. The cursor waited.

Outside, a car horn blared, sharp and sudden. The sound cut through the silence, a reminder that time didn’t stop for indecision. But it didn’t matter. Not anymore.
