The morning rain left the garden floor damp, but it didn’t stop the old man from kneeling beside his favorite tree. He traced the bark with his gloved fingers, the same way he had for twenty years. Today, though, he pressed a small, crumpled newspaper clipping into the fissure where the roots curled upward, a ticket stub from the last concert he’d ever attended. It was a small thing—a discarded moment—but it was all he had left of the past.

As the water pooled around his feet, he whispered to the tree. *"This is for you."* The tree didn’t respond. No roots grew out of the clipping. But for the first time in decades, he felt something new: a quiet weight lifting. The rain kept falling, the world kept turning, but the act of planting the memory in the earth had changed the way he saw it all.
