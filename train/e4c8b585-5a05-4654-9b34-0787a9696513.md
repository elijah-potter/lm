A dimly lit alley, cobblestones slick with dew, stretches under a cracked neon sign reading *"Last Call"* in faded letters. The air hums with the distant murmur of a city waking—siren wails, distant car horns, and the occasional laugh from a passing group. Shadows stretch long, pooling around the rusted dumpster where a half-eaten sandwich sits forgotten. A single, flickering streetlamp casts a jagged glow over a discarded newspaper with a headline from yesterday: *"Stock Market Drops Again."*

A lone figure moves through the gloom, their silhouette sharp against the dim light—clothes patched, boots worn smooth by years of tread. They pass the alley’s mouth, where a graffiti-covered fence whispers names in faded spray paint: *"Old Friends"* and *"Never Came Back."* The figure pauses at the edge of a crumbling dumpster, where a child’s toy—an old stuffed animal with a missing button—still clutches a half-remembered toy truck. The toy’s eyes are hollow now, but the truck’s wheels still turn in the dirt. The figure doesn’t look away.

A shadow moves behind them—too quick, too deliberate. The neon sign flickers. The alley breathes. No one sees.
