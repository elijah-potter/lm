The rain hammered against the old wooden windows of the abandoned library, each drop a silent whisper of forgotten stories. Elias sat cross-legged on the floor, his fingers tracing the worn spine of an ancient book—*The Chronicles of Eldros*—the one that had once belonged to his great-grandfather.

Outside, the wind howled through the broken eaves, carrying the scent of damp earth and something older, something like the breath of the land itself. Elias opened the book to a yellowed page where the ink had faded to a ghostly blue. The text read:

*"When the last light fades from the world, the voices of the dead will call from the hollows between the stars. Only those who walk away will hear."*

His breath caught. The words felt like a promise. He stood, the book clutched in his hands, and turned toward the door. The library’s grand staircase loomed ahead, its polished marble worn smooth by centuries of footsteps. At the top, the air was thinner, colder, as if the walls themselves were listening.

He didn’t look back. The door creaked open, and the world beyond was just noise—cars honking, laughter from the street, the distant wail of a siren. But none of it mattered. The library had given him a name now. His own.

He walked out into the storm, the book tucked under his arm, and let the rain wash away the last of what he had known.
