The crowd moved in silence, each absorbed in their own rhythm—phones glued to hands, whispers lost in static. She watched the woman near the café, her fingers tracing the rim of an empty cup, as though it held more than liquid. The child on the bench, staring at the pavement, traced patterns in the cracks with a lost rhythm. The man in the suit, his eyes fixed on the screen, missed the way the autumn leaves curled at the edges of the pavement.

She saw the quiet in the hum of the city—the unspoken conversations, the unread texts, the way the world kept moving without them. The way the wind carried stories no one wanted to hear, but she listened anyway. The way the sun dipped behind the trees, painting the sky in gold, and yet, no one noticed how the light bent around them, shaping everything they saw.

She had learned to see what was never meant to be seen. The cracks in the sidewalk, the way the light lingered on the edges of shadows. The way the world was always just a little too bright, too loud, and yet, she found herself drawn to the quiet spaces between. She watched. She listened. And she saw what others missed—because she had been taught to look.
