The cobblestones underfoot feel rougher than before, embedded with years of weathered stones and the faint scent of damp earth lingering beneath the old oak door. Sunlight filters through the stained glass in the arched windows, casting shifting patterns of gold and crimson across the worn wooden floors—some cracked, others warped—while the scent of fresh-baked bread from the bakery downstairs mixes with the faint aroma of coffee from the café on the corner. The walls, once smooth, now bear a dusty sheen, but the faded murals of the city’s history still peek through, though more faded than when I last saw them. The grand staircase, once polished to a gleam, now creaks underfoot, and the scent of old books and aged leather from the library above lingers in the air. The familiar hum of distant chatter from the market below blends with the occasional laugh echoing from the tavern around the corner. The street lamps, though still standing, feel heavier, their brasswork tarnished, as if they’ve been waiting for me. The air here—warmer than it was last time, but still carries the same quiet resilience of a place that endures.
