**City of Eldermere** reports a dramatic breakthrough in energy efficiency as the newly installed solar microgrid at **Central Park Public Library** achieved a 32% reduction in electricity consumption during peak hours yesterday. The system, developed in collaboration with local firm **Nexus Solar**, utilized AI-driven adaptive panels that dynamically adjust orientation based on real-time weather data, reducing reliance on the grid by an estimated 15% over the past week. Library staff noted improved indoor lighting efficiency by up to 40%, with energy costs slashed by nearly $800 monthly for the facility.

Residents celebrated the milestone during a community event, where Mayor Elias Voss praised the project as a "game-changer" for Eldermere’s sustainability goals. The city’s **Green Initiative Board** is now evaluating partnerships to expand the microgrid to residential zones, with plans to roll out modular units to low-income households by spring. Meanwhile, a protest outside the library yesterday demanded faster expansion, citing concerns over equity in distribution. Authorities remain cautious, urging residents to submit feedback through the city’s online portal before final approvals are made.
