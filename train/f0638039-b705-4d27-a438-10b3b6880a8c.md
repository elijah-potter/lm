The bell rang, signaling the end of another day at Oakwood High. Mia sat in the last row of her English class, her fingers tracing the rim of her coffee cup. The teacher had just finished reading the opening lines of *The Giver*, and Mia had to admit, she was hooked.

"Today, we’re discussing themes of memory and choice," Professor Carter said, tapping her pen against the lectern. "The novel explores how society’s decision to eliminate pain and suffering might also eliminate the complexities of human experience."

Mia leaned forward. She’d always loved books where characters made tough choices—like in *The Hunger Games*, where Katniss took a risk for her sister. But *The Giver* felt different. It was about a world where people didn’t remember joy, and that scared her.

After class, she found her best friend, Leo, in the library stacks. "So, what do you think?" he asked, nudging her shoulder.

"Like, do you think Jonas would make a good leader?" Mia said, gesturing to the book’s protagonist. Leo rolled his eyes. "He’s too idealistic. The Council would’ve picked someone smarter."

Mia frowned. She’d always thought Jonas was brave. He’d given up his memories to save his community. But what if he was wrong? What if the world *needed* pain, not just to survive, but to *understand*?

That night, Mia dreamed of a world without color. The sky was gray, and her mother’s voice was hollow. She woke up trembling, her notebook clutched in her hands. The last page was scribbled with her own notes from class: *"Memory isn’t just pain—it’s everything."*

The next day, she brought the book to school and placed it on the teacher’s desk. "I think you should read it," she said quietly. Professor Carter blinked, then picked it up. "I’ll think about it," she replied, but her eyes were already on the page.

By the end of the week, Mia was convinced: Jonas wasn’t just saving his community—he was saving them from themselves. The world needed choices. Even the hardest ones.

Leo found her in the courtyard, staring at the sky. "You’re serious about this, aren’t you?"

Mia nodded. "I think we’re all just waiting for someone to remind us that the world isn’t perfect. But it’s ours to change."

The wind carried the scent of rain, and for the first time in a long time, Mia felt like she belonged. Not to a world without memory, but to one where she could choose.
