I remember the first time the wind carried the scent of rain on the streets of New Orleans, how it felt like the city itself was breathing, and I stood there, arms crossed, staring at the flickering neon signs that whispered secrets to passersby. I remember the way my grandmother’s hands trembled when she poured tea, the way her voice softened when she told stories of the bayou before the levees. I remember the sound of children’s laughter echoing off the concrete walls of our apartment, the way the world seemed to shift when they were around, brighter, less heavy.

I remember the weight of a letter addressed to my future self, sealed in a brown envelope, waiting to be opened on a birthday I hadn’t yet celebrated. I remember the way the pages of that letter curled slightly, as if the ink had been pressed by time itself. I remember the way my heart raced when I realized how little I knew about the person who would one day write back.

I remember the quiet moments after the chaos—when the noise of the city faded into silence, and all that remained was the hum of the air conditioner, the distant hum of a radio tuned to a station I didn’t recognize. I remember the way the world looked through the cracked lens of an old photograph, how it blurred and sharpened in equal measure, as if the past and present were two sides of the same coin.

I remember the first time I touched the surface of a lake at midnight, the cold water pooling around my feet, the stars above me so vast they made my breath feel small. I remember the way my fingers traced the edge of a map, tracing a path that led nowhere but back to the same place I started. I remember the taste of salt on my tongue, the way the night sky felt like a promise.

I think of all the things I’ll forget before I’m gone, the stories I’ll leave behind like unopened letters in a drawer. I think of the people who will remember me, not for what I did, but for the way they felt when I was near. I think of the silence that follows a life well-lived, the quiet that comes after the noise, the way the world moves on without me, but not without me. I think of the future, and how it will be shaped by what I remember, by what I leave behind, and by the choices I made while I was here. I think of the last time I’ll stand still, and the last time I’ll see. I think.
