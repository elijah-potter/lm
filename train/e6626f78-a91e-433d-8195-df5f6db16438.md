**BREAKING:** New Haven’s **Project Horizon** officially launches as city debuts first-ever **AI-powered flood prediction drone fleet**—aiming to slash damage from storm surges by 30% within five years. Mayor Elena Vasquez unveiled the system at a press conference at **Central Park Plaza**, where drones equipped with hyperspectral sensors and thermal cameras patrol coastal zones 24/7. "This isn’t just technology—it’s a lifeline for our most vulnerable neighborhoods," Vasquez said, pointing to data showing 42% of flooding incidents in the past decade hit low-income districts.

The fleet, deployed on **Floating City 300**, a repurposed cargo ship moored off the harbor, uses real-time data to trigger automated barriers in critical zones. Critics, however, warn of privacy concerns, citing potential misuse of facial recognition in emergency response. Meanwhile, **Tech Haven Solutions**, the private contractor, announced a $1.8M incentive for homeowners in flood-prone areas who install **smart flood sensors**—upgraded from last year’s $800 model. "It’s not perfect," admitted CEO Dr. Raj Patel, "but it’s the best we’ve got." The city has already received 1,247 pre-orders.

**Local business owner Marcus Chen** (who operates **New Haven’s Spice Market**) gave a mixed review. "The drones make sense in theory, but if they’re scanning my shop’s customers, I’d rather pay for a private security guard." The mayor responded by offering Chen a **$500 rebate** for participating in a **pilot AI anonymization program**. As the storm season begins, residents are urged to report unusual drone activity via the new **#HorizonAlert** Twitter account—though the city’s IT team has already received 17 spam complaints from "concerned citizens."
