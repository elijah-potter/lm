Silence hums like a closed door left ajar, the air thick with the weight of stillness. The last echo of laughter fades into the dim glow of a streetlamp, its flickering light casting long, jagged shadows on pavement. A single, warm breath escapes a closed mouth, exhaling like a sigh of relief. The world breathes too—birds pause mid-song, their chorus suddenly stilled, as if holding their notes in anticipation. The hum of distant traffic softens into a distant murmur, a whisper that dissolves into the quiet like mist on glass.

Tactile, the world feels different: the cool of a stone wall pressed against bare skin, the faint texture of grass beneath bare feet. The sky, once a canvas of chaotic storms, is now a vast, unbroken expanse of soft blue, unmarred by clouds. The silence isn’t empty; it’s alive with the slow pulse of earth, the distant rustle of leaves, the occasional, deliberate rustle of fabric against fabric—a curtain opening or a hand slipping into a pocket.

This is the quiet that doesn’t erase, but *refines*—where noise, once overwhelming, now lingers as a memory, a ghost in the present. Here, time isn’t measured in ticks, but in the way light bends through water, or the way your own heartbeat feels like a metronome counting down to something unseen.
