The morning light spilled through the curtains like a slow, reluctant confession. I had planned to write this story differently—with more fire, more certainty, a bold declaration of what came next. But the words had already begun to whisper, and now they demanded to be heard.

I had decided to end the novel with a choice, a moment of clarity where the protagonist stood at the edge of a cliff, fingers trembling over the trigger of their own fate. But the words refused to settle. They kept circling back to the moment before, to the hesitation, the way the air had thickened like smoke, and the voice inside me had not been mine at all.

So I rewrote the ending. Not because I was sure, but because the story had found its own way. The protagonist didn’t leap—she knelt, pressed her forehead to the stone, and let the words spill out of her like blood. The last line wasn’t a choice, but a surrender.

The paper trembled. The ink blurred. And somewhere, a door slammed shut.
