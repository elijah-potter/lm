The first time rain fell as fine, slow droplets on my unshaven cheek, catching the light of a setting sun through the branches of the oak. The warmth of the day lingered in the air, but the moment was fleeting—just as the moment when the world felt still enough to hear the quiet hum of cicadas, when the scent of damp earth and crushed petals hung in the breeze like an unspoken promise. That was the moment when time bent, when the weight of what could be and what was held my breath, and the world, for a breathless instant, was both perfect and perfecting. No replay. No repeat. Only the echo of it, now lost.
