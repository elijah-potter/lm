## Bookstore Serendipity (Powell's City of Books, Portland)

The rain was relentless that afternoon, a soft drumming against the skylights of Powell's, and I was lost, as I often am, amongst the poetry section. You were browsing the shelves nearby, a cascade of auburn hair falling across your shoulders, and I remember noticing the quiet intensity with which you considered each volume. I was reaching for a collection by Mary Oliver when I heard you softly hum a line from “The Cure” – a melody that instantly transported me back to a particularly vibrant summer. I briefly met your eyes, a fleeting moment of shared recognition, before you moved on to the travel writing section. The scent of aged paper and brewing coffee hung in the air, a comforting, familiar embrace. I hesitated, wanting to say something, anything, but the moment passed. I'm hoping you felt a similar resonance. If you recall a woman with dark glasses and a fondness for lyrical verse, please respond with the name of Oliver’s poem that begins, "Why I Wake Early Thinking of You.”
