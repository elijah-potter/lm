**The Last Light**

The door creaked open, a whisper of dust and forgotten time. Inside, the air smelled of old books and something metallic—rust, perhaps, or the faint tang of blood long dried. The walls were lined with shelves, though most were empty except for a single, cracked photograph: a child’s face staring blankly from behind a glass.

She stepped inside, her boots silent on the worn floor. The light from the single bulb above flickered, casting long shadows that danced like specters. Her breath caught—not from fear, but from the weight of what lay ahead. The shelves were no longer empty. Glasses shattered. Pages curled into shapes that writhed in the dim glow.

A voice, soft as a sigh, echoed from the darkness:
*"You came."*

She followed the sound, her pulse hammering in her throat. The room twisted, the walls pulsing like living things. At the center stood a figure—tall, gaunt, wrapped in tattered robes. The child in the photograph was there too, but this one was older, its eyes hollow, its mouth stretched in a silent scream.

*"I knew you would,"* the figure whispered. *"The world is a cage, and you are the key."*

Outside, the wind howled through the broken window. The last light in the room dimmed, extinguished by the weight of what had been revealed. And then—nothing.

But in the silence, the child’s laughter echoed, just for a moment, before fading into the abyss.
