The rain fell in silence, a slow and steady whisper against the windowpane, as I sat cross-legged on the cold floor of the empty apartment, my fingers tracing the worn edges of the old notebook. The pen hovered above the blank page, but my mind was a storm of questions—*Did I really want to do this?*

Outside, the city pulsed with life: laughter, the hum of machinery, the distant clatter of traffic. They moved forward, unafraid, while I stood paralyzed, my heart heavy with doubt. The letter lay unopened on the desk, its words still fresh, its tone sharp enough to cut through the haze of indecision. *What if I fail? What if I’m not enough?*

A knock at the door shattered the quiet. My breath hitched. The door swung open, and there stood her—tall, poised, her dark eyes reflecting the dim light of the hallway. She held a coffee cup, the steam curling lazily. "You’re late," she said, but there was no accusation in her voice. Just quiet understanding. "The meeting starts in twenty."

I exhaled, the weight of the moment shifting slightly. But the doubt lingered, a shadow that refused to fade.
