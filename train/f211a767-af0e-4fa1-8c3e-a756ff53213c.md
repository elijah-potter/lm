**The Last Light**

The house stood silent, its windows like hollow eyes watching the dusk bleed through the cracks. Outside, the air hummed with the weight of forgotten promises—rustling leaves whispered secrets to the wind, which carried them away like smoke.

Inside, the fire had burned low, its embers dancing like trapped stars in the hearth. The room was warm, the scent of woodsmoke thick enough to taste. On the coffee table, an old journal lay open, its pages filled with scribbled notes and half-finished poems. The last entry read: *"They say the sky remembers everything. Does it?"*

She picked up a pencil, the one with the frayed tip, and began to write again—not about the world, not about the weight of time, but about the sky. The one above them now, vast and indifferent, stretching endlessly in all directions. It was not a canvas of color, not yet, but a void where dreams might fade or take flight.

She closed her eyes. The sky was a thousand stories tall, a library of forgotten constellations, a stage for forgotten actors. It was the first thing they had seen when they were born, the last thing they would see before they died. It was the ocean of the air, the endless horizon of the mind.

The wind shifted, carrying the scent of rain. Outside, the clouds had begun to thicken, their edges curling like the lips of something ancient. The sky was changing—softening, deepening, as if it were breathing.

She exhaled slowly, watching the smoke curl from her lips into the air. The sky watched back.
