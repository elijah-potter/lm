The garden shed stood unremarkable against the afternoon sky, its weathered paint chipped like the remnants of a forgotten era. Inside, the air hung thick with the scent of damp wood and forgotten tools, each one silent, their purpose long since lost to the passage of time. The single lamp, its bulb flickering weakly, cast long shadows that danced like ghosts of things not yet understood.

The floorboards groaned underfoot, whispering secrets of the past—some whispered of the children who played here, others of the hobbies that once consumed this space. The clock on the wall, though still ticking, felt like a relic from another century, its hands moving sluggishly, as if the time itself had slowed to match the inertia of the place.

Outside, the world moved on—rushing, loud, indifferent. But here, in the stillness of the shed, the world was just another name on the list of things that had been, and perhaps one day would be again. The quiet was not emptiness, but a pause, a moment suspended between then and now, between what was and what might have been.
