The memory of that evening remains fragmented, like a film reel with missing frames. Only snatches of dialogue and fleeting impressions linger—

*"You really think you’re clever, don’t you?"*
A voice, sharp with amusement, cut through the murmur of the crowded bar. The memory of his smirk, the way his fingers traced the rim of his glass.

*"I’m just asking questions,"* she replied, her voice low, deliberate. A laugh bubbled in her throat, but it was gone before she could control it.

*"Questions that lead to answers you don’t want,"* he countered, leaning in just enough to make her heart race. The bar’s noise faded to static around them.

*"Maybe so,"* she whispered, her fingers brushing his wrist. A pause. A beat of silence where they both remembered the last time they’d been this close.

The conversation dissolved into a blur—laughter, stolen glances, the ghost of a kiss that never quite happened. Later, she’d piece it together from scattered notes, half-remembered fragments of a night that felt like a story she’d never finish.
