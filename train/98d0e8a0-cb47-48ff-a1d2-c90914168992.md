The elevator doors dinged open, releasing a gust of chilly autumn air into the dimly lit lobby. Behind her, the security guard nodded in acknowledgment as she sprinted toward the exit, her heels clicking sharply against the marble floor. The scent of stale coffee and cigarette smoke clung to the air, but she barely registered it.

Her phone buzzed in her bag—another reminder of the missed call from her client. The "You're late" text was already saved in her contacts. She shoved it back into her purse, fingers trembling slightly as she fumbled with her keys.

The train station was packed. She cut through the crowd, weaving between a group of tourists snapping photos and a man arguing with his phone. The platform was nearly full, and she spotted her seat—empty, just as she’d hoped.

Her breath hitched. *Five minutes late.* Five minutes to make up for an hour of lost time. She grabbed her bag, slid into the seat, and exhaled sharply. Relief flooded her—until she saw the man across the aisle, already reading the news on his tablet. She forced a smile, grateful for the reprieve.
