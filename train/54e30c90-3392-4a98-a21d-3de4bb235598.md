The garden was a quiet place, though not as still as one might expect of a space where the earth has been tilled. The soil, once rich with the promise of spring, now held memories of summer’s heat—dry, crumbling, and waiting. The roses, once proud with their thorny grace, had surrendered to the weight of time, their blooms wilting into something less than their former self.

The path, worn smooth by countless footsteps, led nowhere in particular. It curved like an old woman’s fingers, bending toward the horizon rather than a destination. The house stood at its end, its windows like hollow eyes, watching the world with a detachment that suggested it had long since stopped listening.

The rain came as it always does—not with thunder or fanfare, but in the soft, steady rhythm of a secret shared between the sky and the earth. It didn’t announce itself; it simply *was*, and the world absorbed it like a slow drink of something cold and necessary.

Even the birds had stopped singing. Or perhaps they had just learned to sing differently—quieter, more deliberate, as if acknowledging that the old ways had outlived their purpose. The wind carried the scent of damp earth and something older, something buried beneath the surface, something that had not yet been given a name.
