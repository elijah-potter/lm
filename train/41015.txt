WASTE vegetable oil from schools and restaurants is to be turned into an environmentally-friendly fuel at the UK's first worker-owned biodiesel plant.
The six workers at Sundance Renewables all earn an equal wage and own an equal share of the co-operative non-profit making company in Carmarthenshire.
Social Justice Minister Edwina Hart visited the Ammanford company yesterday to open the plant where Sundance hopes to produce 7,000 litres of fuel a week using around 8,000 litres of oil.
The company will collect the used oil from local kitchens, filter it and turn it into a fuel that can be used to run almost any diesel vehicle.
Biodiesel costs as much as normal diesel and cars running on it perform in the same way as those on regular fuel, but it does not contribute to carbon dioxide levels and does not damage the environment. If spilt, it can be burned off cleanly without harmful fumes.
