**BROOKLYN, NY – June 15, 2024** – The city’s newly installed smart streetlights, part of Mayor Rivera’s "Bright Future Initiative," have begun piloting adaptive lighting to reduce energy use by up to 25% in downtown areas. Data from the first week shows a 19% reduction in power consumption during peak hours, while improving nighttime visibility by 12%. Residents report a quieter downtown core, though some small businesses near the test zones have noted a 10% drop in foot traffic during low-light hours. Officials emphasize that the system remains fully operational and stress that energy savings will be reinvested into public transit upgrades.

Separately, the Brooklyn Historical Society announced the discovery of a previously unrecorded 19th-century ledger in the archives, detailing early 20th-century labor disputes between dockworkers and shipping companies. Scholars say the documents, recovered from a storage unit behind a closed warehouse, could rewrite local labor history, with particular focus on how unions formed in the Williamsburg waterfront. The society plans to publish a draft of the findings in partnership with the New York Historical Society by year-end.

Meanwhile, Mayor Rivera’s office responded to ongoing concerns about the city’s "Digital Divide Task Force" by unveiling a free Wi-Fi pilot program in 15 under-served neighborhoods. The service, powered by a partnership with Comcast, will launch next month, with plans to expand to 50 locations by 2025. Critics warn the rollout could overwhelm existing infrastructure, though officials say a phased approach will minimize disruptions.
