“No, no, no, not today,” I snarled, firmly clamping my strong jaw around the metal leg, knocking the guardian off balance. I pulled and shook hard. Something ground, then popped, exposing wires. I smoothly took a couple of steps back, composite leg housing in my mouth. No, they weren’t going anywhere. I lept smoothly to the next threat, lunging for the legs again. 

Jin watched the large animal fluidly jump to the next grasping robot. He and Lena were lucky to have found something as strong, smart and loyal as Heathen. Heathen. Jin laughed to himself. The name was ironic but had stuck. Before the widespread augmentation of everything, combining technology with living organisms had been decried as “abominable” and those doing so as “heathens.” In this grim and barren landscape though, Heathen’s cybernetic improvements had saved him and Lena many times. He’d take the labels if it meant their survival. 

Waiting for Heathen to distract the guardians, Jin eyed the hangar. The tools Lena needed were inside. He crouched behind the overturned barrel, poised to dart into the building. 

Lena peered from inside the dusty cockpit. Using her sleeve to wipe the window, she tried to get a better view of what was causing the delay outside. “Ugh, guardians again?" she said in an annoyed tone. "That's twice in one day."

Former security at the ports, the haywire sentry bots weren’t armed but would apprehend any human they found. With the larger security forces being out of commission, that meant being held in custody indefinitely by an unfeeling bot.

Lena looked around for any item to help her with the aging avionics or the motherboard. The crispy and faded advertising and shriveled flight vests wouldn’t help at all. She’d just have to wait for Jin.
