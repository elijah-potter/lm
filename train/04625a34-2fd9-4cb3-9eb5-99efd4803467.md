The dim glow of a flickering bedside lamp cast long shadows across the room. The air was thick with tension, the silence broken only by the distant hum of the city outside. Across the room, **Kael** sat in an armchair, legs crossed, fingers tracing idle circles on the armrest. His knuckles were white, gripping the edge of the coffee table where a phone lay open, screen glowing with a single notification: **Incoming Call**.

He had been waiting for this call for three days.

The phone rang.

Kael didn’t move. The sound was sharp, like a blade cutting through air. He let it go to voicemail, but the message came immediately—pre-recorded, urgent.

**"Kael, it’s Dain. We need you at the hospital. Now."**

He exhaled sharply, fingers still twitching. The call ended. He stood abruptly, the chair scraping back, and strode toward the door. Outside, the city pulsed with life, indifferent. But inside, his pulse was a drumbeat, every second counting.

The elevator doors hissed open, and he stepped inside, fingers digging into the strap of his jacket. The number **12th floor** burned into his mind.

He didn’t look back.

---
**Waiting for the elevator, his phone buzzed again—another call. This time, it wasn’t Dain.**

A name he didn’t recognize: **Lira**.

*"Kael, it’s Lira. We need to talk."*
