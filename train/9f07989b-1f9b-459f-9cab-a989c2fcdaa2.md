But what struck me was how she folded it just so: a precise, deliberate crease that matched the angle of her wrist as she held the cup. By the third visit, I counted her movements: the way she adjusted her coat sleeve, the exact moment she set her cup down. She moved like a clockwork mannequin, yet there was something alive in the stillness. The pattern wasn’t just in her actions—it was in the pauses between them. The way her breath hitched when she thought I wasn’t looking. The way she never lingered past 4:22 PM, even when the café was empty.

I followed her home one night. The house was small, just two stories, the windows dark. The pattern was in the shadows: the way the curtains hung unevenly when she left, the single step she always took before the door shut. I stayed until dawn, watching from the alley. The pattern was in the dust motes swirling in the morning light, in the way the streetlight flickered at 4:22 AM. It wasn’t magic. It was repetition. And repetition, I realized, was the only thing that ever truly stayed the same.
