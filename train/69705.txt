Most dogs absolutely love being close to their human companion. They seek out physical contact, such as snuggling, sleeping by your feet or body, or simply being in the same room with you. This close contact is an essential part of the bond between a dog and their human and it also gives them a sense of security and comfort.
Research has shown that spending time with your dog releases both hormones in both you and your pup that are associated with soothing and relaxation. When you’re close to your pup, your oxytocin (the ‘love hormone’) levels rise and the same happens with your pup – levels of the hormone vasopressin, which is related to bonding, are elevated.
So yes, dogs definitely like being close to you, and they love it when you spend quality time with them, be it cuddling, walking, playing, or just hanging out together.

How do dogs choose their alpha?
The exact process of how a dog chooses its “alpha” or leader is not always clear, as there is no set, universal method for determining dominance in a pack of dogs. Generally, though, dogs select their alpha by evaluating their behaviors, body language and hierarchy within the pack.
Most of the time, the alpha dog is naturally chosen by the other members of a pack based on the individual characteristics they display, such as dominant posturing and vocalizations.
Naturally, during this process, there is likely to be some conflict as two or more dogs try to determine who should assume the top position. Dogs tend to communicate and trust their instincts during these types of interactions and will naturally choose the alpha based on the signals they are given.
The alphas of a pack are not necessarily the strongest or most aggressive dog. Instead, they are usually the most confident and assertive dog who can effectively establish their position as the leader of the pack.
Alphas also display many of the traits expected of a leader, including good problem-solving skills, excellent communication and the ability to set achievable goals for the pack. As an alpha, the dog also tends to be the first in line for meals, has access to the best resting areas and is typically the one calling the shots.

What are the seven signs that your dog loves you?
There are many signs that your dog loves you, but these are seven of the most common:
1. Tail Wagging: A wagging tail is one of the surest signs that your pup loves you. Dogs use tail wagging to express a variety of different emotions, and when it comes to humans, it’s usually an expression of love and happiness.
2. Sit Beside You: When your pup sits beside you rather than at your feet, it’s usually a sign of affection. This is especially true if they curl up beside you while you’re watching TV or catching some sleep on the couch.
3. Licking: Though it may be an inconvenience to clean up, dogs lick their owners to show their affection. This can mean licking your face, hands, hands, or anywhere else thatmakes them feel close to you.
4. Yawning: Yawning is another sign of affection that your dog may display around you. Yawning is a sign of comfort and trust, so when your pup yawns when you’re around, it usually means they feel relaxed and REALLY love being around you.
5. Playful Gestures: When your pup jumps up and down, paws at you, or brings you their favorite toy, they’re trying to say something: they love spending time with you! Though it may seem like they’re asking for a raucous game of tug o’ war or fetch, they’re really just expressing their love in the best way they know how.
6. Follow You Around: Another unmistakable sign that your pup loves you is when they follow you everywhere. Dogs are social animals and like to stick close to their owners, especially if they’re showing puppy eyes, expectant looks, and sitting patiently nearby when you’re not giving them attention.
7. Vocalizations: Much like humans, dogs use vocalizations to convey their emotions. Whether it’s a soft whine, a hearty bark, or a cute growl, dogs use vocalizations to coo, whistle, and “talk” to their owners.
If your pup has specific noises that they only make with you, it’s a good sign they love you very much!

Why do dogs put their paw on you?
Dogs often lay their paw on us as part of a display of trust and affection. Dogs communicate in part through body language, and they may be indicating protection or loyalty when they place their paw on us.
This is a common behavior among dogs, likely passed down from pack dynamics in their wild canine ancestors. Laying a paw on a more dominant pack leader was a sign of submission and respect. In a pet-owner dynamic, placing their paw on us reinforces the bond between pet and human, which is an essential factor in their overall well-being.
Placing their paw on us is also a sign of comfort for the dog. It can be used to ground them when they feel scared or overwhelmed, just like we physically comfort people we care about. Further, touching releases oxytocin in both the puppy and the person, which is the same hormone responsible for the strong bond between mother and baby.
Oxytocin promotes trust, which explains why a pet is more likely to put their paw on the owner they trust the most.
In addition, when a dog places its paw on us, it may be a form of communication asking for a response from us. They are inviting us to make contact or show affection. Dogs are intelligent creatures and can even pick up on subtle cues such as our tone of voice and body language.
By placing their paw on us, they may be seeking our attention and a reaction such as hugs, petting or playing.
All in all, when dogs place their paw on us it can mean a variety of things. Whether they are seeking comfort, communication or affection, there is no doubt the experience is a sign of trust and of the strong bond between human and pup.
