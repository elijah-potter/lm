A corporate retreat where the same PowerPoint slides are projected over a coffee-stained table, each slide labeled with the same bullet points—"Teamwork," "Innovation," "Strategic Alignment"—repeated ad nauseam. The presenter’s voice rises and falls in a familiar cadence, the same script read from a notepad, while attendees nod in unison, their heads tilted slightly to the side in a synchronized, unnatural way. The whiteboard is filled with scribbles that match the last retreat’s, the same brainstorming circles, the same half-erased arrows pointing to vague, undefined goals. The air smells of stale air conditioning and the faint, lingering scent of the last session’s stale pretzels. Every handshake is measured, every compliment delivered with the same robotic efficiency. The only deviation is a single, half-hearted meme shared in Slack—a photo of a cat doing a handstand, captioned *"When your idea is so bad it’s genius."* The rest of the group pretends not to notice.
