Waiting in the dimly lit lobby of an old, weathered building, the air thick with the scent of aged wood and stale coffee. A large, ornate clock ticks every hour, its hands moving slowly against the backlit glass. The individual sits on a worn leather couch, knees drawn up, fingers tracing the faded design of a cracked coffee mug. A half-eaten sandwich rests on the table beside them, untouched. The door creaks open, and the faint sound of boots on marble steps reaches them—then silence, save for the occasional rustle of fabric as someone moves nearby. A sign on the wall reads: **"Patience is a virtue."** Outside, the city hums, distant and indifferent.
