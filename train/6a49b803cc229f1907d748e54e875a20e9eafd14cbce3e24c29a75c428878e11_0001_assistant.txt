Dear Martin,

My dear friend, how long it has been since we last spoke! Much has happened since then, and I would be overjoyed to have told this tale over a cup of mead. Alas, tidings of the printed form shall do for now. My kingdom has successfully managed to repair the bridge that was destroyed by those meddlesome goblin mercenaries just two fortnights prior. This has done well for our trade routes, as merchants and bards alike return in droves. Why, just the day prior, I was approached by a kind old man peddling herbs and spices. I daresay I bought his entire stock! It was just what I'd needed for my potion-making escapades. Yes, I can surely imagine your face right now; "has Velia not given up her alchemy?" you ask in confusion. The answer, my friend, is a resounding no! Though I am not as talented at the art of magic as you are, I find potion-making to be my gift. I have improved much since I last had you drink my mana potion, though again, I must apologize for its grotesque taste. Unfortunately, I could not magic that away.

Ah, forgive me, for I digress. I am writing to let you know the frequency of these letters may falter for several moons; I am to depart on a journey to parley with the goblin mercenaries. I suspect the exchanging of words will soon turn to the exchanging of blows, but the opportunity to speak with them is a sign of our kingdom's goodwill. Perhaps we could live in peace if things go favorably. My father has deemed me strong enough, both in swordplay and magic alike, to take leadership of the entourage. As I have mentioned several times, I may be a princess, but I refuse to hide in my chamber while the good people of our kingdom suffer. I am prepared to fight, dear Martin; I do not intend to die if they refuse to reason. It is late, so I must sleep. I hope this letter finds you well and that you are in good tidings. I promise to write again as soon as I can!

Your friend,

Velia Scionne
