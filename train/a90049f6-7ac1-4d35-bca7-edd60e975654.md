In the dim glow of an overcast afternoon, the abandoned bookstore’s high ceilings seemed to press down heavier than usual. **Lena** shuffled her way down the aisle, her fingers brushing against the cracked spines of forgotten tomes. She reached for a worn leather-bound journal, its cover stiff with age, when a sharp *crack* echoed from behind her.

Turning, she saw the familiar silhouette of **Marcus**—her childhood friend—stumbling over a loose floorboard near the back. His breath came in ragged gasps as he clutched a small, tarnished key in his trembling hand. His face paled. "I—I lost it ages ago," he muttered, fingers still curled around it. "The one that fits my father’s old safe."

Lena’s pulse quickened. "You’re sure it’s the same one?"

He nodded, voice barely audible. "I’ve been looking for this since last winter. Dad’s safe is in his study—some old paperwork inside. We could have it back."

As Lena reached for the key, the journal slipped from her grip. It landed open on the floor, its pages fluttering like dead leaves. Inside, the last entry read: *"If you find this, know the past isn’t dead—it’s just waiting for the right key."*

Marcus hesitated, then held out the key. "Take it. And… maybe don’t ask too many questions."

Lena pocketed the key, the weight of it unfamiliar. She picked up the journal, flipping to the final line: *"The key opens more than a safe."*
