By 8:47 AM, I arrived at the office, where the fluorescent lights buzzed like a swarm of angry wasps. My first task was reviewing yesterday’s reports, but my brain was still stuck in a loop of the meeting I’d missed. The team was already in a heated debate over the quarterly budget cut—again.

By 12:30 PM, my stomach growled, and I grabbed a sandwich from the vending machine that smelled like regret. The meeting ended at 2 PM with a decision that felt like a slap to the face, but I nodded along anyway. The afternoon dragged with endless emails and spreadsheets, my mind drifting to the 1:47 PM break when I accidentally spilled coffee on my laptop.

By 6 PM, I was exhausted but still had two more hours of work. The office was emptying, but my laptop screen flickered with a half-finished draft. I printed it out, stuffed it in my bag, and made it home just as the rain started—again.

The bed felt good, but sleep wouldn’t come. I stared at the ceiling, replaying the day’s mistakes: the missed call, the half-finished project, the way my boss’s tone had changed. Instead of closing my eyes, I opened my laptop again, scrolling through old emails, waiting for the next day to arrive.

By 2:30 AM, the light was dim, the rain had stopped, and I finally dozed off—just enough to dream of coffee.
