## Project Chimera: A Cooperative Research Game

**A Rulebook for Two to Four Players**

**Designer’s Note:** *Project Chimera aims to simulate the complex decision-making involved in responding to a rapidly evolving global crisis. The game emphasizes collaboration, resource management, and the difficult choices that arise when facing limited information and escalating consequences. The intended educational outcomes are an enhanced understanding of systemic risk, the importance of interdisciplinary collaboration, and the ethical considerations inherent in scientific advancement under pressure.*

### I. Components

The game includes the following components:

*   **Crisis Board:** A large, illustrated board depicting a global map with designated research locations and crisis zones.
*   **Researcher Cards (12):** Cards representing specialized researchers, each with unique skills and starting resources.
*   **Event Cards (60):** Cards detailing escalating global events and unforeseen complications.
*   **Research Cards (48):** Cards representing potential breakthroughs and mitigation strategies.
*   **Resource Tokens:** Representing Funding (blue), Personnel (yellow), and Data (green).
*   **Containment Markers (20):** Used to represent the severity of outbreaks in crisis zones.
*   **Anomaly Tokens (10):** Representing unexpected and dangerous developments.
*   **Research Track Marker:** Indicates the team’s progress towards a solution.
*   **Crisis Level Marker:** Tracks the overall severity of the global crisis.
*   **Six-Sided Dice (3):** Used for skill checks and event resolution.

### II. Setup

1.  **Place the Crisis Board** in the center of the playing area.
2.  **Shuffle the Event Card deck** and place it face down near the board.
3.  **Shuffle the Research Card deck** and place it face down near the board. Draw five Research Cards and place them face up next to the deck, forming the Research Pool.
4.  **Place the Crisis Level Marker** on the “Stable” space of the Crisis Level Track.
5.  **Place the Research Track Marker** on the “Initial” space of the Research Track.
6.  **Each player chooses a Researcher Card.** Read the card carefully to understand your starting resources and special abilities.
7.  **Distribute starting resources** as indicated on each Researcher Card.
8.  **Place one Containment Marker** in each of the three designated Crisis Zones on the Crisis Board.

### III. Turn Structure

Each turn consists of the following phases, completed in order:

1.  **Event Phase:** Draw the top Event Card and resolve its effects. Event Cards may introduce new Containment Markers, shift the Crisis Level, or trigger other complications. Some Event Cards require dice rolls to determine their outcome.
2.  **Researcher Action Phase:** Each player may perform two actions from the following list. Actions can be performed in any order and may be the same action twice.
    *   **Move:** Move your Researcher to an adjacent location on the Crisis Board.
    *   **Gather Resources:** Collect one Resource Token of your choice from your current location. Some locations provide specific resource types.
    *   **Research:** Spend one Data Token to draw two Research Cards, choose one to keep, and discard the other.
    *   **Analyze:** Spend one Personnel Token to remove one Containment Marker from your current location.
    *   **Mitigate:** Spend one Funding Token to reduce the Crisis Level by one step (if possible).
    *   **Collaborate:** Exchange one Resource Token of your choice with another player at the same location.
3.  **Crisis Escalation Phase:** If any Crisis Zone has three or more Containment Markers, increase the Crisis Level by one step.
4.  **Discard Phase:** If you have more than seven Resource Tokens at the end of your turn, discard down to seven.

### IV. Win and Loss Conditions

**Winning the Game:** You win the game if the Research Track Marker reaches the “Solution” space before the Crisis Level Marker reaches the “Catastrophic” space.

**Losing the Game:** You lose the game if the Crisis Level Marker reaches the “Catastrophic” space at any point during the game.

### V. Mechanics Driving Team Trade-offs

1.  **The Data Dilemma:** Research Cards often present choices between two potential breakthroughs: one offering immediate containment but with a potential long-term risk, and another offering a more permanent solution but requiring significantly more Data to acquire. Players must debate the acceptable level of risk and the feasibility of acquiring the necessary resources.
2.  **Resource Allocation Roulette:** At the beginning of each turn, before the Event Phase, a single die is rolled. The result determines which resource type (Funding, Personnel, or Data) is temporarily scarce that turn. Players must adjust their planned actions, potentially sacrificing short-term gains to prioritize the scarce resource. This introduces uncertainty and forces players to re-evaluate their strategies.
3.  **Crisis Zone Prioritization:** When multiple Crisis Zones are escalating simultaneously, players must decide which zone to focus on. Removing Containment Markers from one zone may alleviate immediate pressure, but neglecting another could lead to a more severe crisis later. This necessitates a strategic assessment of risk and potential consequences.

### VI. Difficulty Scaling

*   **Two Players:** Start with the Crisis Level Marker on the “Stable” space. Reduce the number of initial Containment Markers in each Crisis Zone to one.
*   **Three Players:** Play with the standard setup as described in the Setup section.
*   **Four Players:** Start with the Crisis Level Marker on the “Elevated” space. Increase the number of initial Containment Markers in each Crisis Zone to two.

### VII. Advanced Variants

*   **Specialist Focus:** Each player selects a "Specialty" at the beginning of the game, granting them a bonus to specific actions but imposing a penalty to others. This encourages specialization and interdependence.
*   **Hidden Agendas:** Each player receives a secret objective card at the beginning of the game. Completing the objective awards bonus points, but pursuing it may conflict with the team's overall goals. This introduces an element of mistrust and strategic deception.
*   **Unstable Research:** Introduce a new deck of "Unstable Research" cards that are shuffled into the Research Card deck. These cards represent potentially dangerous breakthroughs that offer powerful benefits but carry a high risk of unforeseen consequences.
