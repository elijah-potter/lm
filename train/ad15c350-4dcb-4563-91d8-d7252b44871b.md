The clock ticked silently as rain tapped against the window, each drop a whisper of fate. Your hands hovered over the keyboard, fingers tracing the air like lost ghosts. The email sat unopened, its subject line a tangle of half-formed words: *"Should I say yes?"*

The door creaked open, the hallway’s dim glow bleeding through the crack. A shadow moved—was it someone else’s reflection, or the first true presence in this quiet room? Your breath hitched. *What if I’m wrong?* The voice inside your head was a chorus of "what-ifs," each one louder than the last, drowning out the one voice that mattered: the one that said, *"You don’t have to decide now."*

The phone buzzed—another text from someone who needed an answer. It was an emergency, a crisis, a moment that demanded your attention. Your stomach twisted. *But what about later?* The words *"I’ll think about it"* curled around your throat, thick and sticky. The screen flickered, and for a heartbeat, you were still. Then—*click*—you hit send. The message vanished like smoke.

The screen went black. The silence stretched, thick with the weight of choices. Outside, the rain had stopped. The world, as it always had, carried on. But inside, something had shifted. The indecision wasn’t gone—it was just… waiting. For what? For the next decision. For the next mistake. For the next breath. For the next *yes*.
