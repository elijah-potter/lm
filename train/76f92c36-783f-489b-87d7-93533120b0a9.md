## Project Chimera: Rulebook

**A Cooperative Game of Viral Containment**

Welcome, Researcher. The world faces a dire threat: Chimera, a rapidly evolving viral pathogen exhibiting unprecedented adaptability and lethality. You and your colleagues are the last line of defense, tasked with understanding, containing, and ultimately neutralizing this existential crisis. Project Chimera is a cooperative game for two to four players, demanding careful planning, difficult choices, and unwavering teamwork.

### Components

*   **Game Board:** A world map depicting key research locations and population centers.
*   **Researcher Pawns (4):** Representing your team’s scientific specialists. Each pawn possesses a unique starting ability.
*   **Chimera Strain Cards (40):** Detail the evolving viral characteristics, introducing new challenges each round.
*   **Research Tokens (60):** Represent gathered data used to develop countermeasures. Divided into three types: Genetic (red), Cellular (blue), and Epidemiological (green).
*   **Containment Markers (30):** Used to slow the spread of Chimera.
*   **Outbreak Markers (40):** Indicate the severity of viral spread in affected regions.
*   **Event Cards (30):** Introduce unexpected complications and opportunities.
*   **Crisis Track:** A gauge indicating the overall severity of the global situation.
*   **Research Dice (6):** Custom dice used for research actions. Faces display symbols for Genetic, Cellular, Epidemiological research, and a “Failed Analysis” symbol.
*   **Player Aid Cards:** Summarizing key actions and phases.

### Setup

1.  Place the Game Board in the center of the playing area.
2.  Place the Crisis Track marker at its starting position.
3.  Shuffle the Chimera Strain Cards and place them face down as a draw pile. Draw the top three cards and place them face up next to the deck. These represent the current viral strains in play.
4.  Shuffle the Event Cards and place them face down as a draw pile.
5.  Each player chooses a Researcher Pawn and takes the corresponding Player Aid Card.
6.  Place each Researcher Pawn at the Global Research Facility (marked on the Game Board).
7.  Place three Outbreak Markers on the starting locations indicated on the Game Board.
8.  Distribute a starting amount of Research Tokens (as indicated on the Player Aid Cards) to each player.

### Turn Structure

Each round consists of the following phases, executed in order:

1.  **Strain Evolution:** Draw a Chimera Strain Card and add it to the face-up display. The effects of the new strain are immediately applied. If three strains are already in play, discard the oldest strain.
2.  **Researcher Actions:** Starting with the first player and proceeding clockwise, each player takes two actions. Available actions include:
    *   **Move:** Move your Researcher Pawn to an adjacent location on the Game Board.
    *   **Research:** Attempt to gather Research Tokens. Roll the Research Dice. The number of dice rolled is determined by your Researcher’s ability and any location bonuses. Collect Research Tokens matching the symbols rolled. Any “Failed Analysis” symbols result in the loss of one Research Token of your choice.
    *   **Contain:** Place a Containment Marker on a location with Outbreak Markers. This reduces the number of new Outbreak Markers placed during the Spread Phase.
    *   **Analyze Strain:** Spend Research Tokens of matching types to reduce the severity of a face-up Chimera Strain Card. Specific requirements are detailed on the card.
    *   **Share Research:** Transfer Research Tokens to another Researcher in the same location.
3.  **Spread Phase:** For each location with Outbreak Markers, place new Outbreak Markers. The number placed is determined by the severity of the Chimera Strain Cards in play, modified by any Containment Markers present.
4.  **Event Phase:** Draw an Event Card and resolve its effects.
5.  **Crisis Phase:** Advance the Crisis Track marker by one space. Certain Event Cards or Strain Card effects may cause further advancement.

### Win and Loss Conditions

**Winning the Game:** You win the game if you successfully Analyze all three face-up Chimera Strain Cards and reduce the Crisis Track marker to its starting position.

**Losing the Game:** You lose the game if:

*   The Crisis Track marker reaches its maximum position.
*   There are no more Outbreak Markers available to place.
*   A location runs out of Researcher Pawns due to overwhelming infection (a specific condition detailed on certain Event Cards).

### Advanced Variants & Trade-offs

**1. Limited Resources:** At the start of the game, reduce the starting amount of Research Tokens distributed to each player. This forces players to prioritize research targets and share resources more strategically.

**2. Rapid Mutation:** Increase the number of Chimera Strain Cards drawn each round. This accelerates the viral evolution and demands faster adaptation. The trade-off here is increased pressure and a faster pace.

**3. Political Interference:** Introduce a deck of “Political Pressure” cards. These cards are drawn periodically and impose restrictions on research or containment efforts, representing bureaucratic obstacles and conflicting priorities. Players must decide whether to spend resources to overcome these pressures or risk further deterioration. This introduces a resource allocation trade-off between scientific progress and navigating external forces.

**4. Specialized Containment:** Certain locations on the board have unique properties. Players can choose to focus containment efforts in these areas, potentially gaining significant benefits but also neglecting other regions, creating a geographic prioritization trade-off.

### Difficulty Scaling

*   **Two Players:** Begin with the Crisis Track marker one space below its starting position.
*   **Three Players:** Play the game as described in the standard rules.
*   **Four Players:** Begin with the Crisis Track marker one space above its starting position.



### Designer's Note

Project Chimera was designed to be more than a game; it is intended as a thought exercise in collaborative problem-solving. The escalating crisis and the need for difficult choices mirror the complexities of real-world scientific challenges. The mechanics emphasize the importance of communication, resource management, and the understanding that progress often requires compromise. The educational goals are to foster an appreciation for scientific rigor, an understanding of systemic risk, and the value of collective action in the face of adversity.
