**The storm raged outside the broken window, its howling winds shattering glass as rain lashed the crumbling brick wall. Inside, the flickering lantern cast long, trembling shadows across the floor. A single candle guttered in the wind, its flame a dying ember—both flickering and fragile. The air smelled of damp earth and rust, the scent of something long buried but still clinging.**

**Dara knelt beside the old piano, fingers hovering over the keys. The keys were cold, their polished surfaces slick with moisture. A melody played itself—distorted, half-remembered—something once vibrant now reduced to a whisper. The storm’s fury mirrored the weight in her chest: unspoken sorrow, the weight of lost time, and the hollow ache of a life that had outgrown its purpose.**

**The piano’s strings groaned, their resonance vibrating through the walls like the groan of the earth itself. Outside, the lightning split the sky, illuminating the ruins of the house—collapsed walls, a chair toppled over, a child’s toy left behind, forgotten in the chaos. The storm wasn’t just rain. It was the world’s way of saying what words couldn’t.**

**Dara exhaled, her breath misting in the cold air. The keys beneath her fingers were no longer just wood and ivory—they were the cracks in her own heart, the places where the music of her life had drowned in silence.**
