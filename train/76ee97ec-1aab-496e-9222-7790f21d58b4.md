The old clock tower creaked at midnight, its brass face reflecting the last embers of a dying candle. The air smelled of damp earth and something metallic, like the rusted hinges of forgotten dreams. I sat in the abandoned library’s dim corner, the pages of a book I’d never read spread across my knees, the ink smudged with the weight of years. The first light of dawn crept through the cracks in the floorboards, painting the room in gold.

Outside, the garden had transformed. Roses, once vibrant, now drooped like skeletal fingers, their petals withered. The pond’s surface was still, mirroring the gray sky above—no stars, just a vast, bruised canvas of muted hues. The wind whispered through the broken glass of the windows, carrying the scent of rain and something older, something buried in the soil. The earth pulsed beneath my feet, alive with the slow, quiet pulse of time.
