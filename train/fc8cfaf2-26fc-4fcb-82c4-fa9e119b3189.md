A dimly lit café hummed with the soft murmur of whispered conversations. The air smelled of coffee and old paper. Lila sat at a corner table, her fingers tracing the rim of her empty cup. Outside, rain tapped against the window in a slow, rhythmic pattern—like a heartbeat.

She had come here for the quiet. The kind of silence that swallowed sound whole, that let the world breathe in its absence. No noise, no chatter, no endless stream of people trying to fill the void with words. Just the weight of her own thoughts, unburdened by noise.

The bell above the door chimed, signaling another customer. Lila didn’t look up. She had already heard it. The silence was her own. She leaned back in her chair, closed her eyes, and let the rain’s steady rhythm lull her into a trance of stillness.

The café emptied. The last customer left, taking the noise with them. Lila exhaled, slow and deep, and let the silence settle over her like a blanket. Somewhere, a clock struck midnight. The world outside was still, but inside her, the silence was perfect.
