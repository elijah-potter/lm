**The Last Transmission**

*Countdown: 04:22:15:00*

The control room hummed with tension as the last data packets of the *Europa Clipper* transmitted its final transmission. Engineers at NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory watched the countdown timer bleed into zero across the screens.

**"Liftoff confirmed. Primary mission objectives achieved. End of mission protocols activated."**

Outside, a storm raged over Jupiter’s moons, lightning flickering across Europa’s fractured ice. Inside the lab, Dr. Chen adjusted the last sensor readings—anomalous readings from the subsurface ocean beneath Europa’s frozen crust.

**"10... 9... 8..."**

The probe’s core reactor sputtered as it reached the 8-minute mark of its descent. The team held their breath. **"7..."**

A holographic display flickered—Europa’s subsurface ocean, now visible beneath the ice. **"6..."**

The probe’s camera feed captured a vast, liquid world beneath the frozen shell, its depths illuminated by the faint glow of hydrothermal vents.

**"5..."**

Dr. Chen’s voice cracked. **"We’ve found life."**

**"4..."**

The probe’s systems began a final shutdown sequence. **"3..."**

The last image—a single, glowing filament of microbial life—streamed before the feed cut to static.

**"2..."**

The probe’s transmitter beamed one final message to Earth: **"The ocean is alive."**

**"1..."**

The transmission ended. The countdown was over. Outside, the storm’s roar drowned out the silence of the void.
