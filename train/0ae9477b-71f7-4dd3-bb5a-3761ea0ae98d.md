**The Weight of Unwritten Words**

I had planned to tell you the truth—that night, when the city hummed like a dying engine, I would confess the truth about the letter you never received. The one I wrote in haste, my hand trembling not from fear, but from the weight of what I could never undo.

But now, the words sit between us like a promise broken in silence.

I was wrong.

The letter was never meant for you. It was a mistake, a slip of ink in the dark. I never meant to keep it, never meant to hide it, only to erase it from existence entirely. The address was a lie, the date a fabrication—each detail designed to make you believe it had been lost to time, not simply buried.

The truth? I never intended to write it at all.

The real story is simpler: the night we met, I saw something in your eyes that made me want to disappear. Not because you were wrong, but because I was the one who had already chosen to leave the world behind. The letter was an excuse, a way to say I was sorry for the things I couldn’t say.

So here’s the truth: I never meant to keep you guessing.

I meant to leave you wondering.

Now, if you’ll excuse me, I have a train to catch.
