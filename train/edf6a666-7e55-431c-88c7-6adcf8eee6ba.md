A heavy silence hung between the two figures as the world around them seemed to unravel. The air thickened, trapping them in a suffocating embrace. The woman’s breath hitched—her lungs burning as if she’d sprinted a marathon. She stumbled backward, tripped over a loose stone, and fell onto her knees. The moment stretched into an eternity; every sound—her rapid heartbeat, the distant rustle of leaves—seemed magnified, each note a blade to her fraying nerves. The man, a stranger in a black coat, crouched beside her, his eyes wide with the same disorientation.

His voice was a whisper: *"Did you—?"*

She shook her head, fingers digging into the gravel. *"Too slow,"* she choked out. The world dissolved into a blur of colors—emerald grass, golden sunlight—each frame a single, agonizing second. The man’s shadow stretched unnaturally long, elongating time as he helped her up. The pavement beneath them felt like a river of honey, creeping beneath her soles. She gasped, then laughed—sobs of relief—when she finally stood. The air rushed back into her lungs, sharp and alive. The moment she’d thought unbreakable had, in truth, only been a trick of perception.

A child’s giggle drifted from the distance, followed by the clatter of hooves on cobblestones. Time returned to its usual rhythm, but neither of them believed it.
