The late afternoon sunlight slanted through the window of room 312, painting stripes across the floral wallpaper. I adjusted the height of Mr. Alistair Finch’s bed, ensuring he was comfortable. He was a man of routine, Mr. Finch, and I had come to appreciate the quiet predictability of his needs. He was recovering from a fractured hip, sustained after a rather unfortunate tumble from his garden shed. He’s seventy-eight, a retired botanist, and possessed a dry wit that often surprised those unfamiliar with him. 

"Feeling a bit peckish, Nurse Davies," he observed, his voice a low rumble. He was propped up on pillows, a worn copy of *The Secret Garden* resting on his lap.

"I'm glad to hear it, Mr. Finch. I’m just about to check on the meal trays. Would you prefer the chicken or the salmon tonight?" I inquired, making a note on his chart.

"The salmon, please. A bit lighter, you see. One must consider one's digestive system at this age." He smiled, a network of fine lines crinkling around his eyes.

I left the room to collect his tray, returning shortly with a small, neatly arranged plate. The aroma of dill and lemon filled the air. He accepted it with a nod of appreciation. 

"The roses are looking particularly lovely in the courtyard today," he commented, between bites. "I do miss tending to them."

I paused, recognizing a flicker of sadness behind his usual composure. "Perhaps we can arrange for you to view them tomorrow, Mr. Finch. Physical therapy will allow you a little more mobility, and a brief walk outside might do you good."

He seemed genuinely pleased by the suggestion. "That would be most agreeable, Nurse Davies. Most agreeable indeed."

As I prepared to leave, he spoke again, his voice softer this time. "You know, Nurse Davies, I’m not particularly fond of hospitals. They lack… vibrancy. But your kindness makes it a little less so."

I smiled, feeling a warmth spread through me. It wasn't a grand gesture, simply offering a small comfort, a glimpse of the world he missed. "It’s my pleasure, Mr. Finch. Please let me know if you need anything at all." 



I made a note to discuss arranging a brief garden view with the physical therapist. Small things, I thought, often make the biggest difference. The quiet rhythm of caring, of attending to the needs of another, was, in its own way, deeply satisfying.
