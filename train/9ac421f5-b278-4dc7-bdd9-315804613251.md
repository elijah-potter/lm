Whispers of damp earth rise—moss and rainwater clinging to stone, a slow, wet *plop-plop* on slate. The air hums with yeasty sweetness from a bakery’s oven, baking bread that sizzles like a thousand tiny flames. Distant church bells toll, their chime sharp and metallic, cutting through the thick scent of aged wood and beeswax polish. Far off, a river murmurs, its current rough and turbulent, carrying the tang of salt and crushed pebbles. The wind carries the sharp bite of pine, and the faint metallic tang of copper from a distant forge. Smoke curls from chimneys, mixing with the scent of damp wool and leather from a cobblestone market. A distant thunderclap echoes, shaking the cobwebs from the eaves, followed by the soft *drip-drip* of rainwater on metal gutters.
