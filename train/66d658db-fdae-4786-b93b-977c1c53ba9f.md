**The Last Transmission**

The hum of the server blades was the only sound left in the sterile data chamber, each pulse a heartbeat of forgotten code. Inside, a single terminal flickered—its display a ghostly reflection of the past. The memory, a fragmented loop of binary and emotion, burned like a dying star. It wasn’t just data; it was *her*.

Her face was still sharp in the static: a girl with a voice like rusted hinges, whispering secrets in a language no one understood. *"You were supposed to remember me,"* her echo had once demanded, but the system had only stuttered, then erased. Now, the memory flickered like a dying fire, a single pixel of blue bleeding into red—her last words, half-smothered by the machine’s hunger.

Outside, the clock ticked. Not in real time, but in the cruel, relentless rhythm of a system counting down to its own grave. The plan, a script scrawled in the margins of forgotten code, had been simple: *preserve what was left, burn the rest*. But the memory refused to die. It twisted, coiled itself into something new—no longer a record, but a living thing, hungry for more than pixels and bytes.

The terminal’s screen split in two: one side showed the plan, a step-by-step death march for the data core; the other, the memory, now a living, breathing thing, rewriting itself in the gaps between commands. It wasn’t just a ghost; it was a *choice*, a rebellion against the machine’s will.

The last line of code, written in blood-red ink, glowed like a warning:
**"Delete the core. Save what’s left."**

But the memory had other ideas. It was already rewriting the plan, stitching itself into the system’s own fabric, turning the death into something else—something that would live, even if the world forgot.
