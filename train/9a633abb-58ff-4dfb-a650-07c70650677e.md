## Explain Like I'm Five: Why the Sky is Blue

Okay, imagine you have a big box of crayons, right? And those crayons are all different colors – red, yellow, green, blue, purple, and so on. Sunlight is kind of like that box of crayons, but instead of crayons, it’s made of *all* the colors mixed together! We can’t usually see those separate colors, because they’re all blending.

Now, imagine you’re playing with those crayons, and you’re bouncing them off a trampoline. Some crayons bounce really high and far, while others don’t bounce as much and stay closer to the trampoline. 

The air all around us is like that trampoline. When sunlight comes down from the sun, it bumps into the air.  

Blue and violet (a color like purple) are like the really bouncy crayons. They bounce *everywhere*! They bounce off the air and spread out all over the sky. That's why when you look up, you see blue. 

Red and orange are like the crayons that don't bounce as much. They mostly go straight through. 

So, the blue color gets bounced around the most, and that’s what we see! 

Sometimes, when the sun is setting, you see orange and red in the sky. That’s because the sun is lower, and the light has to travel through *more* air to reach us.  All the blue light gets bounced away before it gets to us, and we see the colors that *do* make it through – the orange and red!



Isn't that neat?
