An abandoned subway tunnel, its concrete walls slick with condensation and the faint, metallic tang of rust. The air hums with the ghost of a distant train, its rumble muffled by decades of debris—packed cardboard, rusted metal, and the occasional half-buried newspaper clipping. The ceiling groans under the weight of sagging metal, where once it might have been reinforced. The platform is littered with discarded tools, their purpose forgotten, their edges dulled by neglect. A single flickering fluorescent bulb hangs from a rusted fixture, casting long, wavering shadows. The floor is uneven, cracked in places where the weight of time has crumbled the earth beneath it. The scent of damp earth and old oil lingers, thick and heavy. At the far end, a single, half-destroyed sign reads *"Exit"* in faded letters, its reflection warped by the grime on the glass. The silence is suffocating, broken only by the occasional creak of metal or the whisper of wind through gaps in the walls. It feels like a place that was never meant to be finished.
