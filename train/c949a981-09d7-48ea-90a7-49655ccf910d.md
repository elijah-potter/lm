**The Last Transmission**

It wasn’t supposed to happen like this.

The stars had been aligned for decades—predictable, cold, indifferent. But now, here we were, circling the edge of something that didn’t exist in the old books. They called it the "Fracture Line," a whisper of the void where the universe’s rules bent and broke. The scientists called it "unverified," the generals called it "improbable," and the soldiers who walked into the black—well, they just *were*.

She had been a patrol observer, tracking anomalies near the old mining outpost at the edge of Sector 9. The reports were sketchy—just glitches, static, things that shouldn’t have been there. But she’d seen it too: the way the air hummed when the cameras glitched, the way the drones’ trajectories *wobbled* like they’d been caught in a current she couldn’t name. The data was wrong. The math was wrong. The universe had decided to play a game no one was supposed to win.

They found him first—they always did.

He was a recruit, fresh from the academy, his uniform still damp with sweat. He’d been assigned to the perimeter check, but he’d gone too far. Too far to ask questions, too far to *fear*. The screens flickered. The alarms didn’t sound. Just silence. Then—light. Not the sun. Not anything natural. Something *wrong*, pressing against the glass like a hand. His eyes locked onto the camera. His mouth opened. The feed cut to static. The last thing she saw was him smiling.

They called it a "misdirection." The investigation team called it "experimental." The ones who came back called it "the call."

They called it *home*.

Now, the Fracture Line was growing. Not a crack, not a tear—something deeper, something that had been waiting. And when it opened, it wouldn’t be the end. It would be the beginning of something else. Something that didn’t have a name. Didn’t have a map. Didn’t have a warning.

She stood at the console, fingers hovering over the last transmission. The voice on the other end wasn’t hers. Not anymore. It was something else. Something that had been listening for decades, waiting for the right moment to answer.

The screen cut to black. The static hissed.

*They were here.*
