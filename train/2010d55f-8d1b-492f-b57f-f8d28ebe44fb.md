One crisp autumn evening, the wind carried whispers of memory through the hollowed trees as you stood beneath the same overhang where you first felt the weight of fate in your hands. The firelight flickered on the cracked pavement, casting long shadows that danced like specters of time. The scent of damp earth and woodsmoke mingled with the distant chime of a church bell—its tolling sharp and final. Your fingers traced the scar from the last time you held this exact moment in your grip, but now, they trembled. The clock on the wall read 9:17 PM, just like the night you swore you’d never forget. The café’s empty tables seemed to hum with the ghost of laughter you shared, now lost to the echoes. A stranger’s laughter drifted from the alley—unrelated, but the sound of a life you’ll never live again. The last text you sent to them, unread, lay open on the table: *"I’m sorry."* The coffee cup’s warmth was gone. The moment was over.
