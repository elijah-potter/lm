Teaching your child how to be helpful at a young age is beneficial to their development. It will also help them learn how to be more self-sufficient as they get older. Generally, it's a good idea to let them shadow you while you're doing chores and include them where you can. Here are some more specific ways to include your young child in age-appropriate ways and teach them how to help around the house:

- If you're dusting, hand them a dust cloth and show them how to wipe a surface (remove any breakables for them first).
- If you're vacuuming, let your child turn on the vacuum and/or let them help you steer it.
- When drying dishes, give your child a dry kitchen towel and show them how to dry things off (they should only be handling plastic cups, containers, etc.) and then show them where to put dry items away.
- When it's time to pick up toys or put dirty clothes in the hamper, let your little one help put things where they need to go.

There are lots of little ways to include your child in daily chores. For their safety, always monitor them while they're helping and provide simple guidance where necessary. Do not let them help if it puts them in danger or exposes them to harsh chemicals in cleaning products, such as bleach. As your child gets older and learns how chores are done, you can advance their skills by showing them the next step in a task or introducing a new chore they can help with. Remember to only let them help at an age-appropriate level.
