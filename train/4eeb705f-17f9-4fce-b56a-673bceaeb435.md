The rain hammered against the rusted metal roof of an abandoned warehouse as **Lena** stumbled through the muddy alley, her breath coming in ragged gasps. The neon sign of the last shop flickered, casting eerie blue light on the crumpled map in her pocket. She traced the jagged line from the train station—*5 miles north*—back to where she’d left the bus stop. The bus stop didn’t exist.

A distant wail of sirens echoed from the highway overpass, but the only sound now was the trickle of water seeping through her soaked boots. The city skyline—a blur of towering spires—had vanished behind a wall of storm clouds. Her phone’s battery was dead, and the GPS on her phone was either broken or had no signal. The last thing she remembered was the name of the café she’d been meeting friends at—*Blackthorn*—now just a forgotten ghost in the map’s faded ink.

She clutched the map tighter, her fingers numb. The wind howled through the skeletal trees of the park she’d passed hours ago, their leaves turning black in the fading light. Somewhere beyond the crumbling bridge, the highway stretched endlessly, a silver ribbon cutting through the chaos. She didn’t know which way to go. The map was useless. The city was just… gone.
