The air is thick with the scent of damp earth and rusted metal, clinging to the skin like a second layer. The horizon is a hazy, inverted silhouette against a sky that’s not quite blue—too pale, too still, as if the sun has paused mid-sunset and forgotten to resume its descent. Buildings loom like skeletal fingers, their windows dark, their doors slightly ajar as if waiting for someone who never came. The wind, if there is one, moves in slow, deliberate pulses—sometimes absent entirely, leaving the silence so heavy it’s almost audible.

Streetlamps flicker to life like dying stars, casting long, uneven shadows that stretch and warp across the pavement, as if the light itself is afraid of burning out. The pavement is cracked in a pattern that mimics breathing—deep, shallow, and then a long pause where the ground seems to hold its breath, too. The only sound is the occasional creak of an old wooden door, the distant murmur of a radio playing a song that’s playing backward. Even the clouds above are still, suspended in a state of suspended animation, their edges blurred as if they’ve been painted on by someone holding a brush too far from the canvas.

The world is a place where time has slowed to a crawl, where every movement feels deliberate, every breath is measured. It’s not just quiet—it’s holding its breath.
