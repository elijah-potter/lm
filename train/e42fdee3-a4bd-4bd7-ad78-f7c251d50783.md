A moment when a stranger’s hesitant question about the missing clock in their apartment led me to realize how easily we overlook small details—how the world is often made up of unnoticed connections. The clock, a symbol of time’s relentless march, stood frozen on a shelf, its hands stuck between 11:58 and 12:00, as if time itself had paused. I didn’t recognize it until the stranger’s voice cut through the quiet, and then it clicked: the clock had been replaced by one with a broken face, its hands misaligned, a silent metaphor for the fractures in our shared humanity. The moment was less about the clock than the realization that we all carry our own broken timepieces—some visible, others hidden—and it was in that split second that I understood how deeply we’re all connected, even if we never meet again.
