The cavern’s walls hum with static, a low, resonant frequency that vibrates through the bones of the empty chamber. No air moves—only a faint, ghostly whisper of ozone lingers, thick as syrup, clinging to the dust motes suspended in the stagnant air. The floor is a cracked, obsidian slab, its surface etched with symbols that shift under infrared light, pulsing like veins of molten glass. Shelves along the walls groan under the weight of abandoned tools: rusted wrenches, half-melted circuit boards, and what appears to be a single, intact human skull, its hollow eye sockets reflecting the dim, flickering light from a single, dead bulb hanging from the ceiling. The air smells of damp earth and something metallic—old blood? The silence is deafening, but the absence of sound is louder than any noise could ever be. The space is a tomb of unused potential, every corner holding a memory of what was never meant to be.
