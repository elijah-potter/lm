The icy rain started in the evening.  As the last of the daylight faded, Alice could see the trees were beginning to sag under the weight of the ice.  When a car drove past, the branches sparkled and shimmered.  As she was going to sleep, she heard a large crash.  A branch had fallen from one of the trees.  Hopefully, it hadn't hit anything on the way down.  There were a lot of big trees around the house, and she was nervous about one falling on her while she slept. Because of this, sleep took a long time to come.  She seemed to sleep for years, and when she woke up, she was shivering with cold.  It was terribly bright, and everything was white.  Just white.  Even the sky.  The sky!  She was outdoors, but somehow not cold. Where was she?  There was absolutely nothing in any direction but white snow and ice.  She could hardly tell which way was up.  She began to walk, and she walked for a long time.  She was surprised to see she was wearing snow boots and a snowsuit, both as white as the snow.  Eventually, she came down a hill and saw a little glimmer of darkness.  Was it water?  As it got closer, she realized it wasn't.  Instead, it was hundreds of penguins, all huddled up together.  She approached the group, and they squawked at her in alarm.  She couldn't stay here alone forever, though.  One penguin left the group and came over to her.  Instead of squawking, it said in a polite British accent, "Miss?  Can you help me with something?  I'm trying to find the answer to Fermat's last theorem.  Do you have any ideas what the next step might be?" The penguin handed her a notebook full of math symbols.  Pages and pages of them.  Alice looked at them, and then back at the penguin.  She fainted.  This was all too much.
