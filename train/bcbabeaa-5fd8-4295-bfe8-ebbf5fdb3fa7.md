The incantation reads *"Om shanti"*—but the syllables dissolve into static. The ritual’s steps are memorized but feel hollow: the salt sprinkled over the left shoulder, the whispered name of an ancient god, the offering of a half-eaten cake left untouched. The priestess, her fingers trembling, traces the same symbols in the dust, but the ink bleeds into the air, never forming complete shapes. The drumbeats echo in empty spaces, and the crowd murmurs, not in devotion, but in exhaustion. The meaning is a ghost in the machine—once a promise, now just noise. The temple’s doors remain open, allowing wind to shift the incense sticks, their smoke curling into shapes that shift before dissolving. The final act is the most absurd: the priestess kisses a statue’s cold eye, then steps back, shaking her head. *"It was never real,"* she thinks, but says nothing. The ritual’s end is its undoing.
