A crumpled paper note, half-eaten by a child’s sticky fingers, was passed between three figures in a dimly lit alleyway. The first, a woman in a faded coat, caught it from a hunched figure in a hooded jacket. The second, a teenager with a scar running down their cheek, plucked it from a third, who was hunched over a laptop, eyes glued to the screen.

The note—damp with sweat—fell into the hands of the second, who unfolded it with a sharp exhale. The paper was brittle, the ink smudged but legible: *"Meet me at the clock tower at midnight."* The third person, now standing, slid the note into their pocket without a glance. The alley hummed with the distant chatter of a crowd, but the exchange was silent.
