A fluorescent-lit office at 3:17 AM, the hum of a server rack blending with distant rain pattering against the skylight. A lone employee, hunched over a keyboard, types furiously, their fingers skimming over keys like a pianist improvising—each stroke a single note in a composition no one understands. The monitor displays a grid of static, but the pixels subtly shift when not looking, forming a faint, shifting outline of a city at night, its lights flickering like distant fireflies.

Across the room, a clock on the wall ticks backward. Every 12 seconds, a single red dot appears at the center, then vanishes—like a ghost watching itself vanish. The employee glances up, sees nothing, but the dot lingers in their peripheral vision for a split second longer than it should. Outside, the skylight’s glass reflects a silhouette: a figure standing motionless, watching the office from the rooftop of a building that doesn’t exist on any map. The figure’s hands are empty, yet hold two objects—an old pocket watch and a half-eaten sandwich from last Tuesday. The watch ticks backward.
