The Great Ticking Hour
---
In a city where the streets were paved with cracked glass and shadows moved in unnatural rhythms, a clock tower stood sentinel over the decaying spires of forgotten dreams. Its face was not polished brass but a rusted, skeletal frame, each hour marked by the slow, deliberate hands of a man who had long since vanished.

At midnight, the city’s heartbeat stopped. The hands paused, suspended in the moment between one second and the next, as if frozen mid-tick. A woman—her name was **Elara**—found herself standing where the clock’s shadow had once stretched across the cobblestones. Her reflection in the tower’s cracked mirror showed a stranger: her eyes were hollow, her mouth a silent scream.

She knew the truth: the clock was not just time; it was a prison. Every tick was a warning. Every hour, a soul was dragged from its body, its essence wound into the gears beneath the clock’s face. The hands did not move—they *ate*.

Elara reached for the second hand, her fingers trembling. The moment she touched it, the tower groaned, the gears whined like dying things. The clock *laughed*—a sound like breaking glass—and the seconds began their descent, one by one.

She ran.

But the city had already begun to unravel. The streets folded in on themselves, the buildings melting like wax, and the air hummed with the sound of a thousand whispers. The clock’s chime echoed through the hollows of her skull, a final, desperate countdown.

Outside, the hands were already moving again. And the city was still.
