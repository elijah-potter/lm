**The old leather-bound journal** lay half-buried beneath a pile of crates in the back of the abandoned warehouse. Dust motes swirled in the dim light of the flickering streetlamp outside. Elias knelt, brushing off the faded cover with a rag, his fingers tracing the cracked pages where once his father had jotted notes on the old clockwork mechanisms he’d salvaged from shipwrecks.

*"What’s this?"* he muttered, turning the first page. Inside, a sketch of a strange, half-dismantled device—a brass-and-iron contraption with gears that looked like they’d been designed by someone who didn’t fully understand them. Below it, a single line scrawled in ink:

*"The first time it stopped, it was already running."*

A cold prickle ran down Elias’s spine. He’d heard the stories—the last crew who’d found this place, how they’d found the device and then… vanished. No bodies. Just the warehouse, and the clockwork mechanism humming away, as if waiting.

The journal’s pages grew heavier. On the next one, a list of components, names of parts Elias recognized: *the "magnetic stabilizer," the "vane dampeners,"* even a sketch of a key. His breath hitched. This wasn’t just a relic. It was a key.

The streetlight flickered. Somewhere in the distance, a distant train whistle echoed, but the warehouse was too far from the tracks for that to be real. Elias shoved the journal into his pack, his mind racing. The device was the key to something. *Something* that hadn’t been supposed to exist.

He pulled out his pocket watch—his father’s last gift to him—and checked the time. Too late. The train whistle was coming closer.
