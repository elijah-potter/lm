**"The Last Transmission"**

It wasn’t supposed to happen like this. The sky should have been a vast, indifferent void when she first noticed the flicker—just a static hiss at the edge of her vision, the kind of noise that makes you double-check your settings before dismissing it as interference. But this wasn’t interference. This was the sound of a circuit breaking, of a connection severed, of a universe holding its breath.

She was supposed to be on a mission. Not this one. Not *this*. The briefing had been clear: no surprises, no variables. Just data points, no emotion. But emotions, they were always the first to crack under pressure. And pressure? That was the thing she’d been warned against. Pressure was the glue that held the world together, the force that kept everything from unraveling into chaos. But chaos? That was what she’d been trained to avoid.

The console beeped again, a sharp, metallic pulse that cut through the hum of the ship’s systems. A warning. Not an error—an *opportunity*. She exhaled slowly, the breath shallow, the fingers on the controls steady. This wasn’t supposed to happen. But it did.

The screen flickered to life, displaying a single, looping message: **"Warning: Unauthorized access detected."** Below it, a list of files—*personal files*. Not the ones she’d been searching for. Not the ones she’d been *supposed* to find. These were hers. Or at least, they should have been.

A voice crackled over the comms, distorted, urgent: *"Dr. Carter? Are you still with us?"* Her name. The mission commander. The one who’d given her the orders, the one who’d *not* given her permission to dig this deep. She swallowed. *"I’m here. Everything’s fine."* But it wasn’t. It never was.

The ship’s AI, *Echelon*, began parsing the data streams, compiling a report. The files weren’t just personal. They were *compromised*. Encrypted, yes—but not by a hacker. By someone who knew the keys. Someone who’d *wanted* her to find them.

The mission changed course. Not by her choice. By the hands of someone else. The console lit up in red, then green, then back to red again. The final message was a single word: **"End Transmission."**

She was supposed to be going home. Instead, she was standing in a server room, her hands trembling, her mind racing through possibilities. The files. The access. The warning.

The door hissed open. A figure stepped inside, clad in a sleek, black jumpsuit, their face obscured by a visor. *"Dr. Carter."* The voice was synthetic, precise. *"You were supposed to find this. Or so you thought."* They held up a device—a small, metallic cube, pulsing faintly. *"But you were never supposed to ask."*

The screen at her console glitched. The last line of the log disappeared, replaced by a single question mark. Then, silence.

Outside, the ship’s engines roared to life. The door sealed behind her.

She was supposed to be working. She was supposed to be *safe*.

But she wasn’t. And the question was, what was she supposed to do now?
