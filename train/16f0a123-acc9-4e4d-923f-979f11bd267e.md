In the quiet alley behind the diner, a lone figure in a frayed coat hunched over a rusted bike lock, fingers trembling as they fumbled for the keys. The second hand on the diner’s clock—now a jagged needle—ticked toward 11:59. A child’s laughter echoed from the next block, but the figure pressed harder, the lock clicking open with a metallic groan. The bike’s chain snapped as they rolled it into the alleyway, the single wheel wobbling dangerously before the figure grabbed the handlebars, steering it into the street with a screech of tires.

The clock struck midnight. The figure turned, their face etched with relief, and saw the neon sign of the diner flickering above them. They exhaled, then—without thinking—spun the bike handlebars and pedaled away, the single wheel now spinning freely toward the neon glow, the alley no longer an escape but a door.
