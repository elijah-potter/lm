The rain had been coming down for hours, a steady, relentless drumming against the windows. I’d told myself I wouldn’t leave, wouldn’t risk getting soaked again, but my feet were already moving before I’d even thought to argue. The car’s engine roared to life, and I let the wind rush in, the cold air biting through my jacket like a reminder of how foolish I’d been.

I had been so sure—so *certain*—that leaving was the right move. That my apartment was too small, my job too stressful, my life too quiet. But now, as I pulled into the parking lot, the pavement slick under my tires, I realized I hadn’t even *noticed* how much I missed it. The way the light filtered through the blinds at night, the scent of coffee from the café downstairs, the way my bed felt under my hands when I crawled in after a long day.

The door opened before I could decide again. My hands trembled as I pushed it shut, the familiar hum of the city outside muffled by the glass. I exhaled, slow and shaky, and for the first time in weeks, I didn’t have to pretend.
