The library’s fluorescent hum buzzed like a half-remembered dream. Eli sat hunched over the stack, fingers tracing the spine of a book he’d read three times—each time losing a page. The last one was missing, the one with the inkblot that had once been his mother’s handwriting.

Across the aisle, Clara leaned back in her chair, arms crossed, watching him. Her eyes flickered to the empty space where the book had been, then back to the page she’d been reading—*The Weight of Silence*—before closing it. She didn’t speak, but the pause between them was thick enough to cut.

Eli exhaled, slow and deliberate, like he was choosing his next word carefully. The air between them shifted, heavy with unspoken things. Clara’s gaze lingered on his shoulder, where the sleeve of his coat was damp—not from rain, but from something older, something he hadn’t told anyone in years.

The bell above the door chimed, but neither moved. The moment stretched, then dissolved into the rustle of pages turning—just Clara flipping to the next chapter. Eli stood, his boots silent on the worn tiles, and walked toward the door without looking back. Clara didn’t follow.
