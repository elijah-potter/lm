**Ex parte Doe v. Doe**
**Court of General Jurisdiction, County of [X]**
**Case No.: [Y]-[Z]-[A]**

The plaintiff, **John Doe**, brings this action against the defendant, **Jane Doe**, for breach of a non-binding agreement regarding a trivial matter: the return of a **used paperclip** and a **$0.01 refund** from a 2019 Amazon order.

### **Facts:**
1. On [date], the defendant, **Jane Doe**, purchased an item from Amazon, later returning it and receiving a $0.01 refund.
2. The plaintiff, **John Doe**, alleges that the defendant retained the paperclip and refused to return it, despite a prior informal request for its return.
3. The dispute stems from a **non-legally binding "understanding"** between the parties, which the plaintiff claims was violated.

### **Jurisdiction:**
The Court has jurisdiction over this matter because:
- The dispute is **minimal in value** (total claim: $0.01 + paperclip).
- The parties reside within the county.
- The matter concerns **personal property** (a paperclip) and a **non-contractual refund**.

### **Findings:**
1. **No enforceable contract exists** between the parties for this dispute.
2. The **$0.01 refund** was a one-time transaction and cannot be reclaimed.
3. The **paperclip** is a **trivial, non-commercial item** and falls outside the scope of property law enforcement.
4. The plaintiff’s claim is **frivolous and lacks legal merit**, as it does not meet the standard for small claims court jurisdiction.

### **Order:**
- The plaintiff’s claim is **dismissed with costs**.
- The defendant is **not required to return the paperclip** or refund the $0.01, as no enforceable obligation exists.
- Both parties are **directed to cease further correspondence** on this matter.
