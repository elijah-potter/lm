The first time I noticed the pattern, it was the way the streetlamps flickered just before dawn—every third one would dim for exactly two seconds, then return to full brightness. At first, I dismissed it as a quirk of the city’s aging infrastructure, but the consistency caught my attention.

By the second week, I began marking the times in my phone. The dimming always occurred at 5:42 AM, 5:45 AM, and 5:48 AM, each time on the same block. The city maintenance crew claimed it was a "controlled experiment," though no one explained why.

Then came the next pattern: the fire hydrant on the corner never spilled until after the third dimming cycle. The street signs, too, seemed to align with the rhythm—one that shifted slightly each month, as if adjusting to an unseen clock.

The most unsettling observation was the way shadows stretched longer than they should at those times, as if the sun moved in unison with the flickers. I started watching the birds. They paused mid-flight, as if waiting for something.

One evening, I counted the steps between the third dimming of each night and the following morning’s first light. It was always the same number. And then—*the pattern moved into the city itself*. The clock hands on the old clock in the square all pointed to 3:06 on the third day of each month.

I didn’t tell anyone. Some patterns are better left unspoken.
