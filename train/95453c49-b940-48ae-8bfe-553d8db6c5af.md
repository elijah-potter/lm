## Explain Like I'm Five: Why the Sky is Blue

Okay, imagine you have a box of crayons, right? And in that box, you have *every* color imaginable: red, yellow, blue, green, purple, orange – all of them! Now, imagine the sun is like a giant crayon box way, way up in space, and it sends out light. That light isn't just one color; it's actually *all* those colors mixed together!

When that light travels all the way to Earth, it bumps into tiny bits in the air, like little invisible bouncy castles. Now, blue and violet light are like little, energetic bouncy balls. They bounce around *a lot* when they hit those bouncy castles in the air. They bounce off in all directions!

Red and orange light are more like big, slow bouncy balls. They mostly go straight through the air.

Because the blue and violet light are bouncing everywhere, that’s the color we see when we look up! It's like the air is showing off its blue and violet colors.

Sometimes, at sunset or sunrise, the sun’s light has to travel through *even more* air to reach us. All that extra air bounces the blue light away, and then we see more of the red and orange light that was there all along. That's why sunsets are so beautiful and colorful!

So, the sky is blue because the little bouncy balls of blue light are the bounciest and bounce all around, letting us see them!



Does that make sense?
