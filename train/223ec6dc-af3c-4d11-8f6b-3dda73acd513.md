The **Cape of Storms**—a jagged, mist-shrouded promontory where the Atlantic’s endless gray breaches the horizon like a wound. Salt-crusted winds howl through gnarled pines, their branches bent into skeletal silhouettes. Below, the black water churns, reflecting neither sky nor stars. The air smells of damp wool and ozone, thick with the scent of kelp and old blood. Here, the earth stops abruptly, as if someone carved it with a knife. The cliffs, striated with erosion, rise sheer from the abyss, their faces etched with gulls and the occasional abandoned fishing boat, half-submerged in the tide. The wind carries whispers—voices from ships that vanished here, or the distant, muffled laughter of those who dared to look over the edge. Some say if you stand perfectly still at the crest of the tide, you can almost see the world bend around this place, the land curving back into the horizon like the end of a line.
