**Mayor’s Office Statement: "Neon Haven" City Council Approves Night Sky Light Pollution Ordinance**
Residents of Neon Haven, the city known for its neon-lit streets and futuristic skyline, are invited to celebrate a historic moment as the City Council today passed an ordinance to regulate night sky light pollution. The new rule, effective immediately, mandates that all new commercial establishments must use "sky-friendly" LED lighting, limiting brightness to 2500K warm white and limiting glare to less than 30 candelas. Violations will incur fines starting at $500.

Mayor Elias Voss emphasized, *"Neon Haven’s future depends on balancing our iconic aesthetic with responsible stewardship. We’re the first city to implement this policy, and we hope others follow suit."*

Affected businesses have 30 days to upgrade, while existing establishments with older lighting will have 90 days. The ordinance also includes a pilot program for "sky-saving" retrofits in historic districts, with incentives for participation.

Local astronomer Dr. Naomi Chen praised the move: *"This is a game-changer for stargazing. Already, reports of increased nocturnal wildlife activity are flooding in."*

The council also announced a public hearing next week to discuss additional measures, including a proposal to designate the first "Dark Sky Alley" in the downtown plaza for residents to enjoy unobstructed night skies.

For updates, visit the city’s official website or call the Public Works Department.
