The desk’s cluttered surface hummed with half-finished scribbles—drafts, torn-out pages, sticky notes in jagged stacks. A half-eaten apple core sat beside an empty coffee cup, steam still curling from its rim. The pen clicked sharply against the paper as they traced over a line, rewriting with deliberate precision: *"She didn’t say that."* Below, the original had read *"He didn’t listen."* The eraser scraped faintly against the ink as they smoothed the revision, then folded the page neatly, tucking it into the spiral notebook with a satisfied sigh.

The computer screen beside them flickered with a new version’s first paragraph, the text glowing faintly blue. The cursor blinked. They paused, fingers hovering over the keyboard, then typed:

*"The first time I met her, the air smelled like rain and something old—like a library that hadn’t been cleaned in decades."*

A pause. Then, the final click—save.

Outside, rain tapped the window like a slow, rhythmic heartbeat. The house felt quieter now, the story’s bones rearranging themselves.
