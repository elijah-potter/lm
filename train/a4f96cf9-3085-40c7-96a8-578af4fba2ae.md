The neon glow of the alley flickered against the rain-soaked pavement, casting long shadows that seemed to stretch into the night like forgotten dreams. Elias adjusted his worn coat, fingers brushing the cold metal of his old revolver. The case file on his desk—thick, yellowed with age—had been his only companion for weeks, and now it was slipping away with every step.

He had come here to finish this. Not for justice, not for revenge—just to make sure no one else would ever have to face what they had. The woman he had loved, the one who had called him "Elias" for the first time, was gone now. The city had swallowed her whole, leaving only the hollow echo of her laughter in his mind.

The door of the abandoned warehouse creaked open behind him, the hinges groaning like a dying animal. Inside, the air was thick with dust and the faint scent of old gasoline. His breath hitched as he stepped inside, the weight of the past pressing down like a physical force. He had to do this.

A figure moved in the darkness—a silhouette against the flickering bulb overhead. She stood near the back, her back to him, arms crossed. Elias exhaled slowly, his hands trembling just enough to make him wonder if he was already too late.

"You knew," he said, his voice raw. The words felt like they were coming from someone else.

She turned slowly, her face half-lit by the dim glow. The scars along her collarbone looked like old wounds. "I always knew."

Elias stepped forward, the revolver raised, but his fingers froze. She had seen it all. The way he had loved her. The way he had failed her. The way he had let her go.

He lowered the gun, the weight of the moment pressing harder. "Why?"

She shrugged, but her eyes burned with something he couldn’t name. "Because I was the one who had to leave first."

The door slammed behind him. The alley was silent except for the distant hum of traffic. Elias walked away, his coat flapping in the wind, his mind already drifting toward the next place he had to disappear to. The city would never forget him, but he would forget it.
