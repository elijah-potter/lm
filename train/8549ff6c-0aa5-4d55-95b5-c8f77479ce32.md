The shadows stretch long and jagged along the cracked pavement, casting jagged, geometric patterns across the asphalt. A low sun, perhaps 3 PM, glints off a rusted car parked diagonally near the edge of a cracked sidewalk. The shadows of the city’s skeletal buildings loom over them, their facades carved by deep crevices where windows once were. The alley is narrow, lined with peeling graffiti in faded hues—reds, blues, and a lone splatter of green that doesn’t quite match the rest. A single, tangled vine clings to the concrete, its tendrils reaching like skeletal fingers toward the shadows. The ground is littered with discarded fast-food wrappers and a half-burned cigarette butt. At the far end, a flickering neon sign—now half-dead—glows weakly, casting a sickly blue-green cast over the scene. The air smells of exhaust and damp concrete, with a faint metallic tang where the shadows meet the pavement.
