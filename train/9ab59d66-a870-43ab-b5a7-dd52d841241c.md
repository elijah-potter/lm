## Proposal: The Willow Creek Pocket Park – A Climate-Resilient Community Oasis

**Executive Summary for Municipal Funders**

The Willow Creek neighborhood, a historically underserved area within our municipality, faces increasing challenges from urban heat island effect and localized flooding. This proposal outlines a transformative project to revitalize a currently neglected, underutilized space – the vacant lot at the corner of Elm and Oak Streets – into the Willow Creek Pocket Park, a climate-resilient community asset. This project will directly address these challenges by incorporating permeable paving, native drought-tolerant landscaping, a rainwater harvesting system for irrigation, and shaded gathering spaces. The Willow Creek Pocket Park will provide critical respite from extreme heat, mitigate stormwater runoff, enhance biodiversity, and foster a stronger sense of community ownership and pride. We request \$75,000 in municipal funding, matched by \$25,000 in community contributions and in-kind services, to realize this vital project, representing a modest investment with significant, measurable returns for the Willow Creek neighborhood and the municipality as a whole.

**Project Plan: Phases and Deliverables**

The Willow Creek Pocket Park project will be implemented over a nine-month timeframe, divided into three distinct phases: Planning & Design (Months 1-3), Construction & Implementation (Months 4-7), and Community Launch & Stewardship (Months 8-9).

**Phase 1: Planning & Design (Months 1-3)**

*   **Responsible Partner:** Willow Creek Neighborhood Association (WCNA), Landscape Architecture Firm “Green Spaces Collaborative” (GSC)
*   **Deliverables:**
    *   **Month 1:** Community Needs Assessment Report (WCNA) – detailing resident priorities and design preferences.
    *   **Month 2:** Preliminary Design Concepts (GSC) – three distinct design options incorporating community feedback.
    *   **Month 2:** Site Assessment Report (GSC) – including soil analysis, drainage patterns, and existing infrastructure mapping.
    *   **Month 3:** Final Design Plan (GSC) – incorporating feedback from the community and relevant municipal departments (Parks & Recreation, Public Works).  This plan will include detailed planting schedules, material specifications, and construction drawings.
*   **Estimated Time Commitment:** 120 hours (WCNA), 180 hours (GSC)

**Phase 2: Construction & Implementation (Months 4-7)**

*   **Responsible Partners:** WCNA, Green Spaces Collaborative, Municipal Public Works Department (PWD), Volunteer Labor Force
*   **Deliverables:**
    *   **Month 4:** Site Preparation & Demolition (PWD & Volunteer Labor) – removal of debris, grading, and initial site clearing.
    *   **Month 5:** Permeable Paving Installation (PWD & GSC) – installation of permeable pavers to reduce stormwater runoff.
    *   **Month 6:** Rainwater Harvesting System Installation (GSC & Volunteer Labor) – construction of a rain barrel system for irrigation.
    *   **Month 6:** Native Landscaping Installation (GSC & Volunteer Labor) – planting of drought-tolerant native trees, shrubs, and groundcover.
    *   **Month 7:** Installation of Benches, Picnic Table, and Signage (GSC & Volunteer Labor) – creation of comfortable gathering spaces and clear park identification.
*   **Estimated Time Commitment:** 200 hours (WCNA), 160 hours (GSC), 80 hours (PWD), 300 hours (Volunteer Labor)

**Phase 3: Community Launch & Stewardship (Months 8-9)**

*   **Responsible Partners:** WCNA, Municipal Parks & Recreation Department (PRD)
*   **Deliverables:**
    *   **Month 8:** Grand Opening Ceremony – official park launch event with community celebration.
    *   **Month 8-9:** Community Stewardship Workshops – training residents on park maintenance, rainwater harvesting system operation, and native plant care.
    *   **Month 9:** Park Stewardship Plan – outlining ongoing maintenance responsibilities and volunteer coordination.
*   **Estimated Time Commitment:** 80 hours (WCNA), 40 hours (PRD)

**Budget Transparency**

The total project cost is estimated at \$100,000. We are requesting \$75,000 in municipal funding, which will be matched by \$25,000 in community contributions and in-kind services. The budget is broken down as follows:

*   **Professional Services (Landscape Architecture):** \$30,000 – Covers design fees, site assessment, and construction oversight.
*   **Materials (Permeable Pavers, Rain Barrels, Native Plants, Soil Amendments):** \$40,000 – Represents the largest portion of the budget, prioritizing sustainable and durable materials.
*   **Equipment Rental (Excavation, Grading, Planting):** \$10,000 – Covers the cost of necessary equipment for site preparation and landscaping.
*   **Signage and Amenities (Benches, Picnic Table, Park Sign):** \$8,000 – Provides comfortable and welcoming park features.
*   **Community Engagement & Launch Event:** \$5,000 – Supports community outreach, workshops, and the grand opening celebration.
*   **Contingency Fund:** \$7,000 – A 7% contingency is included to address unforeseen costs or material price fluctuations.

The community contributions of \$25,000 are comprised of \$15,000 in pledged volunteer labor (valued at \$20/hour), \$5,000 in donated materials from local nurseries, and \$5,000 in in-kind support from the WCNA for event coordination and community outreach.

**Community Engagement, Equity Impacts, and Risk Mitigation**

Community engagement has been central to this project’s development. The initial Needs Assessment involved door-to-door surveys, public forums, and online questionnaires, ensuring that the park design reflects the priorities of Willow Creek residents. The project directly addresses inequities by providing a much-needed green space in a historically underserved neighborhood, mitigating the urban heat island effect, and providing recreational opportunities for all ages.

Potential risks include contractor delays, material shortages, and adverse weather conditions. To mitigate these risks, we have secured commitments from multiple suppliers, established clear communication protocols with contractors, and built a contingency fund to address unforeseen expenses.  A detailed risk assessment matrix is available upon request.

**Post-Grant Sustainability**

Long-term sustainability is ensured through a multi-faceted approach. The native plant selection minimizes water requirements and reduces the need for chemical fertilizers. The Rainwater Harvesting System significantly decreases reliance on municipal water sources.  A Park Stewardship Plan, developed in partnership with the PRD and WCNA, outlines ongoing maintenance responsibilities and fosters a sense of community ownership.  Regular volunteer workdays and community events will be organized to maintain the park’s beauty and functionality.
