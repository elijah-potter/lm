Morning sunlight filters through half-open curtains, casting golden streaks across the coffee-stained floor. The alarm—once set for 6:00—doubles as a siren, its beep repeating in a loop, the volume muted by the crack of an early morning breeze through the window. The coffee machine hisses as it brews, its steam curling into the stale air of the kitchen. The first sip is bitter, the second too weak, the third finally satisfying, though the taste lingers like regret.

The phone buzzes—a text from an unreadable number: *"Late. Sorry."* The reply is immediate: *"No worries."* The laptop sits open, half-eaten breakfast forgotten, the cursor blinking at a half-written email. A meeting scheduled for 9:30 arrives at 10:15, the calendar reminder a distant whisper in the background.

Lunch is rushed: a sandwich from a drive-thru, the wrapper crumpled in a bag on the floor. The commute is a blur of traffic lights and distracted drivers, the radio static between stations. By 4:00, the last call is answered on hold, the phone pressed to the ear like a lifeline. Home at 5:15, the day’s energy spent on half-finished tasks and unresolved decisions.

The bed is a battleground of unmade piles—books, papers, forgotten socks—each item a silent reminder of the day’s failures. The screen glows with an open spreadsheet, numbers scattered like scattered thoughts. The first step is to delete the most recent draft of the report, the second to open the gym bag, the third to realize the workout will be a lie.

The day ends with a half-finished sketch, a half-remembered conversation, and the unspoken question: *Why did this happen?* The answer is already there, half-formed in the dark, waiting to be acknowledged tomorrow.
