Whispering Post Office, hidden behind a moss-covered stone archway in the quiet village of Eldermere’s old town. A small, dimly lit room with a single round table draped in a faded tapestry. A dozen empty chairs surround it, their backs carved with intricate, unidentified symbols. The air hums with static, the scent of aged paper and damp earth clinging to the walls. Inside, a low, crackling fire glows in a grate lined with cracked glass, casting long shadows. On the table, a single, sealed letter waits, its wax seal cracked, the message inside barely legible—only the faintest whisper of a name remains: *"Lena."*

At night, when the village’s lanterns flicker to life, a few locals—women in aprons, children with half-closed eyes—gather here. They sit for hours, leaning forward, mouths just inches from the table’s edge. The whispers slither between them like smoke, drifting through the room’s narrow vents, carried on the wind that whistles through the village’s ancient stone bridges. The postmaster, a gaunt man with a beard woven with silver thread, listens from behind a half-opened door. He never speaks aloud, but sometimes, when the wind picks up just right, the letter’s contents seem to answer back—*not in words, but in the silence between them.*
