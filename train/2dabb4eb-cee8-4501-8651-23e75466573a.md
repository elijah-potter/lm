A weathered envelope lay tucked inside an old wooden crate in the attic of an abandoned farmhouse. Inside was a folded sheet of paper, its edges singed from years of damp. The message, written in faded ink, read:

*"Meet at the old mill at midnight. Bring the black key. The river is rising. —L."*

A single candle flickered beside the crate, casting long shadows. The name on the paper—*L.*—was the only clue. The farmer’s grandson, Elias, traced his finger over the words, gripping the crate tighter. The mill was twenty miles away, but the warning was clear: something was coming.

Elias packed a lantern, a knife, and the black key hidden in his coat. The night air was thick with the scent of damp earth and pine. As he stepped onto the dirt road, the first raindrops began to fall. The river below the bridge had swollen, its surface a churning mass of dark water.

At midnight, the mill doors stood open, rusted and creaking. Inside, the walls pulsed faintly, like a heartbeat. Elias found the black key beneath a rotting stool and pressed it to the door. It turned. Behind it, the mill’s machinery groaned, the gears turning slowly, as if waking from a long sleep.

A figure stood at the far end of the hall—a woman in a tattered dress, her face half-hidden in shadow. She held a lantern, its light casting long, dancing shadows. "You have the key," she said, her voice like dry leaves. "But the river is not the only thing rising."

The water outside the mill was still rising. Something was drowning the world. Elias realized then that the river wasn’t just water—it was a memory, a flood of forgotten things, and the key was the only way to stop it.

The woman smiled. "The choice is yours."
