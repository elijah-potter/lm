## Explain Like I'm Five: Why the Sky is Blue

Okay, imagine you have a big box of crayons, right? And those crayons have *every* color you can think of – red, yellow, green, blue, purple, even sparkly ones! Now, imagine those crayons are like sunlight. Sunlight looks white, but it’s actually made up of *all* those colors mixed together.

When sunlight comes down from the sun and travels to Earth, it bumps into tiny bits in the air – like little dust particles and even smaller things you can’t see. Now, blue and violet colors in the sunlight are like bouncy balls. They bounce around *a lot* when they hit those tiny bits in the air. Red and yellow colors are more like bowling balls – they mostly go straight through.

Because the blue and violet colors bounce around so much, they spread out *everywhere* in the sky. That’s why when you look up, you see blue! It’s because the bouncy blue light is bouncing all around.

Now, you might be wondering, "Why not violet then? Violet is also bouncy!" That's a really good question! Violet light *does* bounce a lot, but our eyes aren't as good at seeing violet as they are at seeing blue. Plus, the sun doesn't send out quite as much violet light as it does blue light.

So, the sky looks blue because the bouncy blue light from the sun is bouncing all around, making the whole sky look blue. It’s like a giant game of bouncy ball in the air!



Isn't that neat?
