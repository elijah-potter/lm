The firelight flickered against the worn walls, casting long shadows that danced like restless spirits. Outside, the wind howled through the skeletal branches of the old oak, whispering secrets no one dared to listen to. Elias sat hunched over his journal, fingers tracing the inked lines without seeing them. The words were there—*the truth*, the unspoken things that had been gnawing at him for years.

A knock at the door. He didn’t look up.

“It’s open,” came his mother’s voice, soft but firm.

The door creaked open, revealing her silhouette, shoulders squared against the cold. She didn’t speak. Just stood there, hands resting on the doorframe, her gaze lingering on the half-finished page where his name was scrawled in blood-red ink. The words were meant for someone else—*for the one who would understand*.

She stepped inside, and the air between them crackled. The fire popped. The silence stretched, thick as the unspoken weight hanging between them. Then, in a voice barely above a whisper, she said:

*“You shouldn’t have written this.”*

He exhaled, a laugh that was more sorrow than amusement. “It’s not yours to take back.”
