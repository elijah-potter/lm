## Lost in the Bookstore (Seattle, WA)

I was browsing the poetry section at Elliott Bay Book Company on Saturday afternoon, searching for something to soothe a lingering melancholy. You were examining a collection of essays on naturalism, your brow furrowed in concentration. I noticed the way the afternoon light caught the auburn highlights in your hair, creating a halo effect against the muted gray of the shelves. We didn’t speak, but I felt a quiet resonance, a shared appreciation for the quiet sanctuary of the store. 

The rhythmic ticking of a grandfather clock in the corner punctuated the silence, a steady, almost hypnotic beat. The scent of aged paper and leather-bound volumes hung in the air, a comforting and familiar fragrance. I lingered a moment longer, hoping for a shared glance, a subtle acknowledgment, but you moved on. I’d genuinely like to know if you felt anything similar. If you were the person admiring the essays, please reply with the title of the book you were considering.
