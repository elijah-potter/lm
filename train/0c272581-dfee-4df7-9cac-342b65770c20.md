The rain fell in silent sheets that night as Elias wiped the last of the crumbs from his fingers, the stale scent of the bakery clinging to his coat. His apology had hung between them like a half-eaten cake—unfinished, messy. *I’m sorry*, he had said, his voice rough as gravel.

He sat on the edge of the kitchen table, the wood groaning under his weight. Outside, the city lights blurred through the window, indifferent to his guilt. The letter in his pocket—handwritten, the ink smudged—was his only defense. He had meant every word.

*"I don’t know what came over me. You deserved better than my excuses, better than my silence."* The words were still fresh, the paper brittle with unshed tears. He had spent the afternoon drafting them, then forgotten them until now.

A knock at the door made him jump. The door creaked open to reveal her—Lena, his neighbor, arms crossed, her dark eyes unreadable. She stepped inside, her boots wet, the hem of her jacket brushing against the threshold.

"You didn’t have to say it," she said, voice low. "But I’m glad you did." Her gaze flicked to the letter in his hands. "I’ve been waiting for this."
