## Explain Like I'm Five: Why the Moon Looks Different Sometimes

Okay, imagine you have a ball, like a bouncy ball. Let's pretend that ball is the Moon! And you’re standing outside, and the Sun is shining on it.

Now, sometimes you can see the whole ball, right? The Sun is shining on the whole thing, so you see the entire round shape. That’s like a **Full Moon**. It’s when the Moon is facing us just right, and the Sun is lighting it up perfectly.

But sometimes, you only see a little bit of the ball. Maybe just a sliver, like a tiny fingernail clipping! That’s a **Crescent Moon**. It’s because the Moon is turned a little bit, so only a small part is facing the Sun and showing it to you.

And sometimes, you can’t see the Moon at all! It’s like you’re looking at the ball, but it’s turned away from you completely. The Sun is shining on the back of it, but you can’t see it. That’s called a **New Moon**.

The Moon isn’t actually changing shape. It’s always round, just like your bouncy ball. It just *looks* different because it’s moving around the Earth, and as it moves, we see different amounts of the part that's lit up by the Sun. 

Think of it like this: you’re walking around your ball. Sometimes you see the whole front, sometimes just a little bit of the side, and sometimes you can't see it at all because you’re looking away from it! The Moon does the same thing as it goes around the Earth.

These different shapes we see – the Full Moon, the Crescent Moon, the New Moon – they're all part of what we call the **Moon Phases**. It’s just a fancy way of saying how the Moon *looks* from where we are on Earth.
