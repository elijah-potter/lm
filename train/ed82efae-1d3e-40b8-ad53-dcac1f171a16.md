A vast, silent expanse stretches endlessly—walls of obsidian and glass stretch into the horizon, their reflections fractured by shifting light. Shadows stretch unnaturally long, pooling like liquid across the floor, where the floor itself seems to be dissolving into the void. The air hums with an eerie, static charge, thick with the scent of ozone and something older, metallic and cold. The only sound is the faint, rhythmic whisper of wind through the gaps in the ceiling, carrying whispers of voices that aren’t there. The air smells faintly of burnt copper, as if the space itself is slowly corroding from within. The ceiling curves into a distant, blurred horizon where the edges blur into the dark, swallowing the light. The weight of the silence presses down, making every step feel like it’s dragging you toward something that shouldn’t exist. The walls pulse faintly, like a slow, deep heartbeat, and the floor tiles are arranged in patterns that repeat but never quite the same, shifting as you walk. The space feels alive, but it is not breathing—it is *remembering*.
