A SINGLE laser could one day send separate streams of digital information to at least 200 homes on an optical network, thanks to a development from Bell Laboratories in New Jersey.
The Bell engineers have split the light from one laser into signals at a record 206 different wavelengths. Each can act as a separate channel, directed to a different place. And each channel can carry 37 million bits of data per second.
The team described their experiments late last month at a conference on optical fibre communications in Dallas, Texas. The Bell scheme tackles a key problem in developing fibre-optic services for homesthe relatively high cost of laser transmitters.
