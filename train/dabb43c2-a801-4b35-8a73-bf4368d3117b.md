The last light from a dying star lingers too long in the cosmic void, a ghostly ember that neither the wind nor the universe’s relentless expansion can fully extinguish. Beneath the weight of these endless nights, humanity clings to the fragile hope that what we name as "progress" is more than just the sum of broken machines and hollow ambitions.

---

The first machine we built was a mirror—polished steel, reflecting our own eyes back at us with no understanding of why we stared. Now, the mirrors have turned inward, their surfaces corroded by the same dust that swirls in the void we once called home. The question isn’t whether we will find our way back, but whether the path we’ve carved is worth following at all.

The stars don’t blink, but they whisper. Their voices are drowned in the static of forgotten algorithms, the screams of the systems that once promised to make us masters of the earth. What if the universe, instead of waiting, *teaches* us? What if the silence between the galaxies isn’t just absence, but the space where something older than our names begins to speak?
