The recent findings from the **Black Hole Atmosphere Composition (BHAC) study** suggest that high-energy jets emanating from supermassive black holes (SMBHs) may not simply dissipate via traditional plasma interactions. Instead, newly detected **ultrarelativistic electron-positron pairs** (UERPs) in the accretion torus reveal that magnetic reconnection events in the corona produce coherent plasma waves capable of accelerating particles beyond **200 GeV/c²** without requiring direct interaction with the event horizon.

These UERPs exhibit **anomalous polarization signatures**, hinting at non-thermal processes in the magnetized accretion flow, potentially linked to the **primordial plasma instability** (PPI) theory. While this challenges classical magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) models, the data supports the hypothesis that **quantum chromodynamics (QCD) effects** in dense plasma could explain the observed suppression of pair annihilation signatures, implying an alternative energy transfer mechanism.

Key unanswered questions:
1. **PPI vs. QCD dominance**: Do PPI-driven fluctuations or QCD-mediated interactions dominate the UERP acceleration?
2. **Coronal structure**: Are these waves sustained by a **dynamic, fractal-like corona** or a quasi-stationary field?
3. **Black hole spin alignment**: Does the UERP flux correlate with SMBH spin orientation or accretion rate fluctuations?
4. **Extragalactic implications**: Could these processes explain the **missing satellite problem** in galaxy clusters via altered cosmic ray propagation?

Further simulations with **3D relativistic MHD + quantum corrections** and **Chandra/X-ray polarimetry** may resolve these tensions.
