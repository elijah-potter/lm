**The Old Watch**

Elias clutched the brass key in his palm, its surface worn smooth by years of neglect. The weight of its history pressed against his fingers—not just the metal, but the memory of a forgotten man who once cared for it. The door to the attic creaked open as he slipped it inside, the dust motes swirling in the dim light from a single bulb. Inside, the clutter of decades had settled into silence. Dust motes danced in the half-light, and among the moth-eaten fabric of old clothes and half-empty bottles, a single object caught his eye: a brass watch, its face cracked, its hands frozen mid-tick.

He traced the familiar curve of its band, the way the engraving read *"For the man who left me behind."* The key turned, and the door swung open to reveal a locked chest. Elias had no memory of this place, but the weight of the key made him pause. Inside, the watch was encased in a wooden box, its presence as old as the attic itself. He lifted it carefully, and as he did, the movement of its rusted gears sent a current through him—something he didn’t recognize at first, but then the faint hum of a forgotten mechanism pulsed beneath his fingertips.

He turned it over. The back revealed a tiny, cramped room, its walls lined with faded maps and sketches of a city long erased from memory. The name *"Dr. Elias Vey"* was inscribed in ink that had faded but not vanished entirely. His breath hitched. This wasn’t just a relic. It was a key—and not just to this chest, but to something far older.

The watch’s hands suddenly spun backward, the second hand circling the dial in reverse. A sound echoed from the chest—a whisper, barely audible, but unmistakable: *"You were never supposed to find it."* Elias’s fingers tightened around the key, and for the first time in years, he wondered what he was about to wake up.
