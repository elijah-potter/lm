A fading dawn at the altar of rusted iron,
where candles gutter and shadows stretch too long.
The incantations are hollow—words repeated
like a prayer on a wind that does not answer.
The offerings are stale: bread cracked by time,
the wine left to ferment into dust.
The initiate stands in a circle of names
now forgotten, whispered in a tongue
that has long since slipped into silence.
The priest’s voice is a whisper, cracked by years,
and the ritual moves like a ghost’s step,
unbound by purpose, only by habit.
The stone altar bears no carvings—just a single
burnt offering, a coin, its edges
scored by rust, its value long ago
lost to the weight of what was never meant.
