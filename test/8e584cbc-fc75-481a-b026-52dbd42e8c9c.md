Lena had always loved the old library at the edge of town, where the books smelled like old paper and forgotten dreams. One rainy afternoon, she found a worn leather book in the basement—its cover cracked with age. The inscription inside read: *"For those who dare to listen."* That night, she read aloud from the pages, and suddenly, the words came to life.

A shadowy figure, a girl named Mira, emerged from the darkness. She explained that the book contained a secret: a hidden door in the library’s cellar that led to a place where lost dreams could be stored. Lena, curious, followed Mira down the creaky stairs. The air smelled of pine and something sweet. Inside, shelves lined with glowing lanterns held dream fragments—some jagged, others soft as silk.

Mira whispered, *"You can take one, but choose wisely."* Lena hesitated, then plucked a dream—a memory of a golden sunrise over a quiet lake. As she held it, the world around her faded. When she returned, the library was empty, but the book was gone. Mira vanished too.

Days passed, and Lena kept the dream in her pocket. Sometimes, when she closed her eyes, she saw the golden light again. But the library’s whispers grew louder, and the stairs in her house felt unnaturally warm. One evening, she followed the sound to the basement. The book was back, and Mira stood beside it, her smile sad. *"You took something that wasn’t yours,"* she said. *"But dreams are meant to be shared."*

Lena realized the truth: the book had been a bridge. Mira wanted to teach her that dreams were alive, that they could change and grow. Lena reached out, and the two girls’ hands touched. The book shimmered, then vanished. The library doors closed behind them, and the rain outside stopped.

Lena woke up in her bed, the dream still warm in her hand. She carried it with her, knowing now that some stories weren’t meant to stay hidden.
