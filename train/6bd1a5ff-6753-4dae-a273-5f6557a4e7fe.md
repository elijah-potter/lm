Waiting—standing in the quiet between the end of one thing and the start of another—is the most radical act of resistance. Hands folded, eyes closed, the body still, the mind refuses to rush. The air hums with the weight of decisions left undecided, yet the pause itself is a choice. It is the art of letting the storm pass before stepping back into the light.

A friend once told me waiting is like holding a flame—it burns away what isn’t needed, leaving only what matters. The waiting is not passive; it is active. It demands patience, but patience is not weakness—it is the alchemy of transformation. Somewhere in that space, the heart learns to trust, to slow, to listen. The world moves faster, but stillness is the compass that guides the soul through the noise.

Waiting is the silence before the song begins. It is the pause after a breath. It is the act of choosing not to act, and in that choosing, the act becomes something else entirely. The world does not stop. It simply stops rushing past the person who knows how to be still.
