The autumn wind whispered through the skeletal branches of the old oak, carrying with it the scent of damp earth and decay. I sat beneath its boughs, the weight of yesterday’s decisions pressing upon my shoulders like a stone. The fire crackled low in the hearth, its embers flickering like forgotten promises. Outside, the village slept, unaware of the storm brewing in my chest.

I had come here for solitude, but the silence had swallowed me whole. My father’s voice still echoed in my ears—*"You’ll never be good enough."* I clenched my fists, the nails biting into my palms. The mirror on the wall reflected nothing but the hollow places where courage used to dwell.

A knock at the door. The doorbell, a sound too familiar, too *wrong*. I stood, my legs unsteady, and let myself in. The hallway was empty, but the scent of spices lingered—someone had been here before. My breath hitched. The door swung open to reveal a stranger in a coat lined with stolen secrets. They held a letter, sealed with black wax, addressed to someone I didn’t recognize.

My fingers trembled as I reached for it. The ink was fresh, the paper thick. The name at the top was unfamiliar. I should have known better. The stranger watched, silent, as I unfolded the parchment.

*"They’re coming for you. Not today. Not tomorrow. But soon."*

The stranger’s face paled. *"You shouldn’t have read it."*

I exhaled, slow and deliberate. *"Why not?"*

The letter was nothing more than a warning. A distraction. The real danger had been waiting in the shadows all along. I had been too busy chasing ghosts to notice the one standing in the doorway.
