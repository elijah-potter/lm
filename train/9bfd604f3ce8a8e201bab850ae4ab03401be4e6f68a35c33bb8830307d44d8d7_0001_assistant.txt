Made with a delicate, flaky pastry, almond croissants give off the strong buttery smell of fresh baked goods. These rich croissants are heavily ladened with butter, giving them a unique, flaky texture that crumbles away each time you take a bite. As they are baked and the layers of pastry begin to rise, the outside of the croissant begins to turn a rich, golden-brown color that resembles the hue of caramel. The slices of almond that are often added to the top of these croissants also become lightly roasted during the baking process, exaggerating their flavor and providing an added element of crunch.

Even though they look and smell delicious, the true magic of almond croissants is what they conceal on the inside. Unbeknownst to many, these croissants that seem bland upon one's first impression actually contain a rich, sweet, and flavorful almond-based cream. Known as frangipane to those more acquainted with baking jargon, this delicious filling is sugary enough to satisfy the sweet tooth of consumers who typically prefer pain au chocolat. Although almond croissants can be overlooked and misunderstood, these delectable pastries make a great choice for pairing with your morning coffee or an afternoon cup of tea.
