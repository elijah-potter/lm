A dust collector draws in contaminated air, filters out particulate matter, and returns the cleaned air to circulation. Dust collectors are common in settings such as factories, plants, workshops, and warehouses, although they can be found in any facility that produces particle pollution. Specific industries that rely heavily on dust collectors include manufacturing, woodworking, metal fabrication, chemical processing, food processing, waste management, and pharmaceuticals.
While dust collectors vary depending on the application, they all rely on the same central components: a blower to collect and recirculate the air, a filter to remove the contaminants, and a receptacle to collect matter for disposal.
