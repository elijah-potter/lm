A survey by the lobby group Consensus Action on Salt and Health (Cash) has revealed that the levels of salt in many fast food meals are posing a serious health risk.
Salt contributes to high blood pressure in both adults and children, increasing the risk of a heart attack or stroke.
The new study, which looked at 346 individual food and drink items and 264 advertised meal combinations from McDonald's, Burger King, KFC and Pizza Hut, found that many of the meals provide well over the maximum daily recommended limit of salt.
Researchers noted that all of the advertised standard size individual 'meal deals' from McDonald's had lower levels of salt than those at Pizza Hut, even if the desserts at Pizza Hut were omitted.
Cash researcher Carrie Bolt also welcomed the fact that McDonald's and Burger King provide information on the amount of salt in their products and urged all fast food outlets to follow their example.
Meanwhile, the group's nutritionist, Jo Butten, pointed out that heart disease and stroke kill and disable more people in the UK than any other disease or condition.
'Thousands of lives can be saved each year if we just choose lower salt foods,' the expert claimed.
