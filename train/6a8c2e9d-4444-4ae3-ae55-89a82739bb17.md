A dimly lit interrogation room hums with the low hum of fluorescent lights. A file folder labeled **"Case #472-B"** rests on a metal desk, its edges slightly bent from years of handling. The fluorescent light casts long shadows across the walls, where a faded map of the region hangs—one marked with a red circle, another with a blue line. The air smells of stale coffee and something metallic, like old blood.

Inside the room, **Dr. Elias Voss**, a gaunt man in a rumpled suit, sits across from **Agent Mara Kovalik**, her uniform crisp despite the tension. The desk between them is littered with papers: DNA samples, a partially melted fingerprint, and a single, bloodstained photograph—two women standing side by side, their faces obscured by shadow. The older woman, **Lena Carter**, had been found dead in the woods; the younger, **Sophie**, was a witness who’d vanished weeks ago.

Voss clears his throat. "You’ve got a problem, Agent," he says, tapping the photograph. "The autopsy showed Lena died from a gunshot to the head—but the ballistics match the .45 from your description, *not* the one found at the crime scene. The evidence suggests someone else fired it."

Kovalik’s fingers tighten around her pen. "Someone who knows Sophie wasn’t with Lena that night. Someone who knows the truth about her past—how she was recruited by the **Project Horizon** years ago, how she was the only one who ever saw what happened to the others."

Voss leans forward, his voice dropping to a whisper. "The blue line on the map—it’s the route Sophie took before she disappeared. The red circle? That’s where the next meeting was supposed to be. But the last known location of the second woman? **Midway between them.**"

A beat. The clock on the wall ticks. Kovalik exhales, her mind racing. "You think someone’s covering up a bigger truth?"

Voss smiles, cold and knowing. "I think they’re playing a game. Two truths colliding: the one in Lena’s body and the one in Sophie’s mind. And someone’s waiting for the right moment to extract the second one."

The screen behind them flickers—static, then a distorted voice, muffled by distance: *"They’re getting close. The others will know soon."* The audio cuts out. The room is silent except for the creak of the floorboards. Kovalik stares at the desk, at the file, at the two women’s faces in the photograph—one dead, one lost. The weight of it presses down.
