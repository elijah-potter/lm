The river hummed a slow, steady pulse beneath the blackened boughs of the oak, its waters whispering secrets of the earth that had long since forgotten how to cry. The wind carried the scent of damp earth and something older—pine resin, perhaps, or the faintest trace of the forest’s forgotten medicines. She sat on the moss-stained stone, her fingers tracing the worn grooves of a journal, each line a thread connecting her to the weight of things unsaid.

*"Why does the world feel like a question mark?"* she had written, her ink bleeding through the paper. The pages were full of that—the ache of questions, the quiet storm of unanswered ones. But here, in this place where time moved slower, she had learned that some questions were not meant to be answered all at once. They were meant to be held, like the breath in her lungs, until the body remembered how to breathe again.

A shadow fell over her—a raven, its feathers ruffled with the weight of the world, too. It perched on the branch above her, watching. She smiled. The raven tilted its head, as if acknowledging the same quiet understanding. The storm outside had passed, leaving only the distant thunder of cicadas, the rustle of leaves, the unspoken promise of a return to calm. The water lapped at her ankles, steady as the heartbeat of something that had always been here, always waiting.

She exhaled, letting the breath out slowly, like the tide receding. The journal closed itself. The world, for now, was quiet.
