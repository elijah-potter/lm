**Character:** Elias Veyne

The dim glow of the old lantern flickered against the dust motes suspended in the air, casting long shadows across the cracked leather of his desk. His fingers traced the rim of the empty coffee cup, the heat of the liquid long since evaporated. Outside, the wind howled through the skeletal branches of the garden, but Elias did not feel the cold.

His reflection in the cracked mirror above the desk stared back, but it was not him. The Elias in the glass had a smile, a warmth that had long since faded. This one wore a mask of indifference, a shield against the weight of everything he had been through.

The letter was on the desk, unopened. A note from his father, delivered by the same courier who had brought news of his mother’s illness, then the death of his younger sister. The words were formal, polite—*a necessary reminder of the world that had moved on without him.* Elias had read it a hundred times.

His hands clenched around the edges of the desk. The wood creaked under the pressure. He should have been angry. He *should* be furious that his father had not bothered to write in years, that the world had forgotten him like everyone else. But anger was a luxury he could no longer afford.

The door creaked open. His sister, Mira, stepped inside, her breath shallow, her face pale. She had always been his strength, his anchor. But she was gone now. Just like the rest.

"Elias," she whispered, her voice raw. She reached for him, her fingers brushing his shoulder, and he flinched. He didn’t want to be touched.

"You know I can’t," he said, his voice breaking. "I can’t—"

"Shh," she cut in, her voice trembling. "You don’t have to say it."

The truth was a knife twisting in his chest. He had been waiting for this moment for years. The moment when he realized he had been the one who had let himself go. The moment when he had stopped being the man his father had hoped for, the man his sister had loved.

Mira’s eyes filled with tears. "You’re not the same person you were," she said. "You’ve become… hollow."

He laughed, a hollow sound that echoed in the room. "I’m not hollow. I’m just—" He couldn’t finish. The words stuck in his throat.

She pulled him into her arms, and this time, he didn’t resist. For a moment, he let himself be held. Then, with a suddenness that startled even him, he pushed her away.

"I can’t do this anymore," he said, his voice raw. "I can’t pretend I’m not broken. I can’t keep pretending I’m still whole."

Mira’s face crumpled. "You *are* whole," she insisted, but Elias only shook his head.

"I don’t know who I am anymore," he admitted. "And I don’t think I ever did."

The lantern’s flame guttered out, plunging the room into darkness. The sound of his own breathing filled the silence. Somewhere, a clock ticked. Time had stopped. And for the first time in his life, Elias Veyne realized that he didn’t mind.
