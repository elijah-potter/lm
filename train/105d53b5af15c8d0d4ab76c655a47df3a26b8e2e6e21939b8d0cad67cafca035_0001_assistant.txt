Sitting across the young couple on the train, Mark thought the young man looked so much like him. It wasn’t that they looked similar physically – the young man had dark features while Mark was always fair. No, it was the tension with which the young man carried himself – it was the sternness in his voice with which he addressed his wife. It was the worry that lined his face, and the stress that seemed to weigh his shoulders down. Throughout the train journey, Mark overheard the young man complaining and worrying about one thing or another. It started with worrying about their tickets, and then about whether the babysitter would take proper care of their child, and finally he grew anxious about whether their friends would remember to pick them up from the station.
When the journey finally came to an end, Mark approached the young man before they disembarked. “Young man,” he started, “My eyesight is poor. Can you tell me what sceneries we passed by on our journey, so I might tell my grandchild?”
The young man stopped, and then replied gingerly, “I wasn’t paying attention.”
“I can tell you,” said the young man’s wife. “We passed by the city, with all its many shops and buildings. And then when we left the city, we crossed wide open plains lush with grass and spring flowers, with a lake in the distance. It was all really quite beautiful.”
The young man looked dismayed for having missed such a sight. Mark understood and patted his shoulder. “If you’re always so wrapped up in your problems, you will never be able to see the beauty around you. Don’t miss the prettier sights of life before you grow old like me, and it’s too late.”
The young man answered Mark with an embarrassed smile, but it looked like the weight had been taken off his shoulders for a short moment.
