**BREAKING:** In the wake of recent technological advancements, the city of **Neo-Vancouver** has experienced a surge in cybersecurity threats targeting its smart infrastructure. Authorities report that over **300,000 residential smart home devices**—ranging from IoT thermostats to security cameras—have been compromised by a new strain of malware dubbed **"GhostNet-9"** over the past 48 hours. The breach appears to have exploited vulnerabilities in unpatched firmware, allowing unauthorized access to user accounts and control over devices.

Elderly residents in the **Downtown Core district** reported their smart locks and voice assistants responding erratically, while schools in the **North Shore** confirmed compromised IoT sensors affecting classroom lighting and security systems. The city’s emergency response teams are investigating reports of **autonomous drones**—typically used for maintenance—suddenly altering flight paths, potentially as a distraction.

Local tech firms have scrambled to roll out emergency patches, but officials warn that recovery may take weeks. Meanwhile, a **shadowy hacker collective** linked to the attack has demanded a **5% increase in city property taxes** as a "ransom," though no ransomware encryption has been confirmed. Police are urging residents to **unplug non-essential devices** and avoid using smart assistants until further notice.

Neo-Vancouver Mayor **Dr. Elena Chen** issued a statement: *"This is a coordinated, high-skill attack targeting our digital future. We’re prioritizing emergency repairs, but the full extent of this breach remains unclear."* A city spokesperson added that **no critical services**—like water, power, or public transit—have been disrupted, though **non-essential smart infrastructure** (e.g., vending machines, ATMs) may still be vulnerable.
