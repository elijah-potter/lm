It wasn’t supposed to happen like this.

The first time I saw the stars through the broken window, I thought it was just the wind. Then the rain came, slow at first, but then it came in waves, seeping through the cracks in the floorboards. The old house creaked in protest, its bones whispering secrets no one wanted to hear.

I found the letter in the sink—half-dissolved, the ink still running like blood. My name was written in it, but not my own. The address was a name I didn’t recognize. The words on the page were too familiar, too *alive*.

The mirror reflected something that wasn’t me—something with eyes too wide, a mouth that didn’t belong to me. I touched it, and the skin split open, warm and dark, like the inside of a fruit that had gone bad.

The door behind me wasn’t there anymore. The walls pulsed, veins of black light threading through the plaster. I could hear them breathing.

And then the clock stopped.

The hands froze, still, as if holding its breath.

Outside, the sky split open. Not rain this time. Not wind. Something older, something that had been waiting.

It was supposed to be a warning.

It was supposed to be a dream.

It wasn’t supposed to happen like this.
