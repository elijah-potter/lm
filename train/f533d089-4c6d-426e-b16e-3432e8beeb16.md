The detective reviewed the witness’s testimony and the surveillance footage. The suspect’s alibi—claiming they were at a café all evening—had been carefully fabricated, yet the café’s security footage showed the suspect lingering near the exit, then slipping into a shadowed alley before dawn.

The key detail: the witness had mistaken the suspect’s car for another vehicle. The license plate, once thought to be from a parked taxi, was later confirmed to belong to the suspect’s own vehicle—a rental with a distinctive logo. The truth emerged when the detective cross-referenced the rental records with the time of the alleged crime.

The suspect’s alibi, a carefully constructed lie, crumbled under the weight of misinterpretation and overlooked evidence. The misunderstanding—whether intentional or accidental—had been the only flaw in the prosecution’s case.
