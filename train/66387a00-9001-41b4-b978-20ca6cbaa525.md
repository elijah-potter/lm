Feeling like an outsider in a familiar space, the hum of conversation around me was a foreign language. My fingers traced the worn leather of the chair, the only place I could find comfort. The others around the table—laughing, debating, oblivious—seemed to have an easy familiarity with each other. They moved like magnets, drawn to shared rhythms, while I hovered, half-listening, half-watching, searching for the right words to fit in.

The room’s warmth felt suffocating. I’d been here before, but not like this. The weight of silence pressed on me, a quiet accusation that I didn’t belong. My pulse quickened, the kind of flutter that makes you want to disappear. The walls seemed to close in, not just the physical space, but the invisible ones that divided us.

I clenched my hands, the knuckles white. What if I said the wrong thing? What if they saw through the act, the desperate attempt to blend? The air smelled like old coffee and something metallic—maybe fear. I wanted to scream, but the words stuck in my throat. Instead, I stared at my reflection in the corner mirror, a stranger staring back. The moment stretched, endless, until the others finally turned away, engrossed in their own world.

Then, just like that, the pressure lifted. The silence was replaced by the sound of footsteps—my own. I stood, the chair scraping against the floor, and left. The door clicked shut behind me, and for the first time in a long while, I felt something lighter. But the weight of being unseen didn’t vanish. It just waited, like a shadow that refused to fade.
