In the quiet corners of a forgotten library, where the air hummed with the faint scent of aged parchment, an old book lay open—its pages yellowed with time, the ink faded like forgotten dreams. The spine cracked with the weight of centuries, whispering secrets only those with the patience of stone could hear.

A hand, trembling not from age but from curiosity, picked up the book. The title, once bold and clear, was now smudged with time: *"The Weight of Silence."* Inside, the margins were filled with fragmented thoughts, half-finished sentences, and equations that defied the laws of nature. One passage stood out, scribbled in hurried, desperate ink:

*"What if the universe is not a stage, but a question marked for no answer?"*

The author’s signature—if it was meant to be one—was smudged, as if erased by the passage of time itself. The last page was torn, the words beneath it lost to the elements. But the question remained, echoing like a ghost in the wind:

*Why do we ask questions when the answer is already hidden in the silence?*
