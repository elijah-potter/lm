The rain hammered against the sidewalk as I watched the crowd disperse toward the alley. A woman in a frayed coat stumbled forward, clutching a crumpled receipt. Her hands shook—not from cold, but fear. The door to the diner creaked open, and the scent of coffee mingled with the sharp metallic tang of blood. Inside, the air was thick with the sound of raised voices, then silence. The bartender wiped his glasses, his jaw set.

She was gone. Just like that. No screams, no struggle. A single, dark stain on the floor where she’d collapsed. The police had already arrived, their radios crackling. I stood frozen, my boots sinking into the puddles. Somewhere, a siren wailed in the distance.
