A crumbling stone archway in a forgotten village square, its surface etched with faded inscriptions and cracked like ancient parchment. Beneath, the remains of a well with brass fittings, once used to draw water from a spring that dried centuries ago. The roots of a gnarled oak tree twist through the moss-stained floor, their branches bearing wildflowers and vines that cling to the ruins like living memory. Nearby, a half-buried lantern stands, its glass cracked but still holding the last embers of a forgotten lamp. The air smells of damp earth and iron, carrying whispers of stories half-heard—names of those who lived here before the world changed.
