The rain blurred the streetlamps into streaks of silver, but I saw the cracks in the sidewalk—each one a map of the city’s forgotten paths. My neighbor’s coffee cup, still warm, held condensation that shaped into tiny rainbows, while they stared at the steam, mistaking it for a ghost. The dog’s tail thumped against the pavement with purpose; hers just thrashed in boredom. The bus driver’s fingers, tapping rhythmically against the wheel, told a story of routine and control—while his eyes drifted, searching for something no one else saw. The traffic light flickered from red to green, but I saw the faint pulse of the streetlight’s bulb, a heartbeat in the dark. The children laughing on the playground—no, not the sound, but the way the wind caught their skirts, swirling them into a dance no one else noticed. The shadows stretched long across the alley, but I saw the way the lamppost’s reflection rippled like water, reflecting the faces of the people who passed by but never looked back.
