A flickering neon sign flickers outside a cramped, half-built café, its blue glow casting jagged shadows across the warped floorboards of a repurposed warehouse. The air hums with the sound of soldering irons and the occasional scream from a construction worker in a hard hat, their laughter muffled by dust masks. Shelves groan under the weight of half-assembled furniture, tools left scattered like abandoned corpses. A child’s toy car lies abandoned near a half-built table, its paint chipped, its wheels missing. The scent of damp wood and burnt wiring clings to the air, mixed with the faint metallic tang of desperation. The door swings open, revealing a hallway where the walls are lined with hastily hung posters—some advertising jobs, others just scribbled notes from people who’ve already moved on. A single, unpaid bill rests on a cracked coffee table, its edges curled from age and neglect. Outside, the city’s distant hum is drowned out by the whirring of a generator powering a makeshift AC unit. This is a place that exists between things—between the old and the new, the abandoned and the forgotten, the "we’ll fix this tomorrow" and the "this is it, now."
