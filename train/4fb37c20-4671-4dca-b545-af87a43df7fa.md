The rain pattered against the old barn’s rusted roof, each drop a whispered secret against the wooden planks. Inside, the scent of damp earth and aged wood clung to the air like a ghost’s memory. A lantern flickered weakly, casting long shadows that danced across the cobwebs clinging to the rafters. The walls were bare, save for a single, half-burned sketch of a man’s face pressed into the dirt—a relic of a forgotten artist who had once sought solace here.

Outside, the wind howled through the broken windows, carrying the distant call of a crow. It was late, past the time when the village’s lanterns had long been extinguished, when the streets were empty save for the occasional figure stumbling home, their breath visible in the chill.

Then, a single step echoed against the uneven floorboards. The lantern’s flame trembled, and for a heartbeat, the room was shrouded in darkness. Then—light. A figure stood at the threshold, their silhouette sharp against the glow of the lantern, their presence as sudden as it was inevitable.

The door creaked open, and the first thing the newcomer saw was the half-smoldering candle on the table, its wick unlit, the wax pooling like blood. They exhaled sharply, the breath curling into the cold air, and for a long moment, the silence stretched between them, thick with unspoken words. The lantern’s flame danced again, and this time, the room felt less like a place and more like a stage, its inhabitants still.

Outside, the wind sighed through the ruins, as if the world itself had paused to listen.
