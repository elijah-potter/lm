The term sedan has been used to describe certain cars since 1912, but it does have a deeper past. Historically, “sedans” were transportation methods used by wealthy people throughout the world at least as far back as the 1600s. A sedan was a specific type of litter with a single enclosed chair with windows. It was carried by two porters – one in front and one behind the enclosed litter.

The first recorded use of the term to describe a motor vehicle was in 1912 by the Studebaker company. The specific definition has evolved over time. Today’s sedans are defined as fixed-roof passenger cars that contain three separate compartments: one for the engine, one for passengers, and one for cargo. Though many vehicles blur the lines between sedans and other styles, in common parlance, this definition still holds.

Incidentally, some countries use different terms for this style of passenger car. In Great Britain, for example, it is instead known as a “saloon.”
