A career as an ecologist often begins with a bachelor’s degree in ecology, where the study of conserving ecological systems is combined with the restoration of terrestrial and marine systems damaged by overuse or neglect. Those wishing to improve their job prospects — not to mention their income — may go on to earn a master’s degree or even a doctorate in ecology. This can open doors to positions in colleges, universities and research institutes, as well as government agencies and private consulting organizations.
Ecologists — as well as zoologists and wildlife biologists — earned an average annual salary of nearly $62,000 in 2011. Higher salaries, however, can sometimes skew this figure. For this reason, median wage is often used to predict income. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, or BLS, sets this wage at closer to $57,420 a year. Both numbers group all specialties together. An ecologist earns a much different wage than a zoologist, for example.
A survey conducted by the Conservation Job Spot, an employment resource for the conservation and wildlife industry, found that ecologists averaged a salary of $54,458 a year as of 2011. But wages can vary greatly by region. For example, ecologists in Missoula, Montana, earned an average of $43,000 a year, while those in Anchorage, Alaska, earned an average of $61,000 annually. In Seattle, Washington, the average ecologist earned $63,000 a year. Salaries in Denver, Colorado, were almost on a par with this, coming in at $60,000 a year.
Like any job, experience affects salaries, and an ecologist is no exception. Entry-level salaries in ecology average out to about $45,833 a year as of 2011, according to the Conservation Job Spot. As with average salaries, starting salaries vary by region. For example, entry-level ecologists in Bismarck, North Dakota, earn an average of $38,000 a year, while those in Boston, Massachusetts, average $52,000 annually. Entry-level ecologists in San Francisco also fared better than most, averaging $60,000 a year.
As experience increases, so do earnings, and a senior ecologist will earn more than his entry-level counterparts. In 2011, a senior ecologist earned an average of $67,917 a year. Of course, location has a direct impact on earnings. In Portland, Maine, for example, senior salaries average out to $60,000 a year — on par with an entry-level salary in San Francisco. The same can also be said for senior-level ecologists in New Orleans. But New York City and San Francisco tend to pay the most for their ecologists, whose salaries were $83,000 and $89,000 a year, respectively.
