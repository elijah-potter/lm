In the quiet town of Blackwood, a clock tower stood sentinel over the river, its hands frozen at 11:11 PM. Each year, at this precise moment, the town’s secrets resurfaced. The last time, a thief stole the town’s gold, but the clock’s hands stopped, trapping him in a loop of time.

This year, the clock chimed differently. The thief was a woman named Elara, who had been searching for her missing brother, Elias, for ten years. The clock’s hands moved backward, showing the past. She saw Elias—standing in the church choir robes, laughing at a joke she’d never heard. He vanished when the clock struck midnight, but his voice echoed: *"You’re the one who always runs."*

By dawn, Elias was found—alive, but hollow-eyed. The clock tower’s hands moved forward again, and Elara realized the truth: time was a cage, and she had been the key. The clock’s chime was a warning: **11:11 PM tomorrow, choose.**

Elias grabbed her hand. "We’ll fix it," he whispered. The clock’s gears groaned, and the hands began to spin wildly. The town’s ghosts shrieked as the past unraveled, revealing Elias’s crime—the clock had been built to seal it. He had sabotaged it, hoping to erase his guilt. But time, like the clock, could not be undone without consequences.

At midnight, the tower exploded in a burst of stars. The clock’s hands shattered, but a single second remained: **11:11 PM, frozen forever.**

The town fell silent. Some said Elias and Elara were still inside, trapped in the seconds between past and present. Others whispered the clock’s final ticking was just the wind. The river ran black, reflecting no one’s face.

By morning, Blackwood was quiet. The river was still. And somewhere, a clock kept time—slowly, relentlessly, **forever.**
