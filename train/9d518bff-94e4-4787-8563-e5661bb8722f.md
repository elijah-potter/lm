A flickering neon sign flickered weakly over the rusted metal door of the abandoned gas station, casting eerie shadows on the cracked windshield. Inside, the hum of a dying generator filled the air, its sound muffled by the thick silence that had settled over the place. The last of the fluorescent lights dimmed, plunging the station into near darkness.

Inside, a group of strangers huddled around a battered table near the counter. Their faces were half-lit by the half-dead glow of a single lantern, its wax dripping onto the old wood. One man, clad in a stained overcoat, sat hunched over a laptop, fingers dancing over the keys as he scrambled to save documents on an external drive. His breath came in shallow gasps, his knuckles white from the strain.

Across from him, a woman in a torn trench coat sat with her back against the counter, her phone buried in her hands. She stared at the screen, her expression a mix of frustration and determination. Nearby, a teenage boy, arms crossed, watched the flickering light with a mix of boredom and curiosity, occasionally glancing at the generator’s failing roar.

Suddenly, the generator sputtered, then died. The room plunged into darkness. The silence was absolute. The woman’s phone screen flickered to life, casting a cold blue light on her face. She grabbed it, her fingers trembling as she tapped the screen. A message lit up the screen: **"Call for help"**—a number flashing in the bottom corner. She hesitated, then dialed.

The line crackled to life, static hissing. A voice, muffled and distant, answered: *"This is 911. What’s your emergency?"* She spoke quickly, her voice barely audible over the generator’s faint whir. *"We’re at the old gas station. There’s a power outage, and we need help."* The line went dead.

The room fell silent again. The lantern’s flame guttered out, leaving only the faint glow of the phone screen. The man on the laptop’s fingers stilled, the laptop screen darkening. The boy stared at the phone, then at the door, his expression unreadable.

Outside, the wind picked up, rattling the broken windows. A distant siren wailed in the distance, growing louder, then fading. The generator’s final breath was a shuddering sigh, and it died. The outage was over.
