A weathered wooden pier extending into a misty bay, its planks groaning under the weight of salty wind. The stone foundations beneath are carved with faded names, each one a ghostly testament to time. The waves lap against the dock, but the roots of the old oak trees, knotted deep in the earth, resist erosion. Sunsets paint the horizon in hues that shift between sunset and dawn, yet the shadows cast by the pier’s lanterns remain constant. The tide marks on the pilings show no progress—only the relentless return. A child’s laughter echoes through the hollows of the docks, but the sea hums a different tune, a rhythm older than memory. The place isn’t temporary—it’s a paradox: held together by what seems fleeting yet endures.
