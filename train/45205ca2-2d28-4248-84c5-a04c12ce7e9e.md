The pause was deliberate, not rushed. The clock stopped not for time, but for thought. Inside the grand hall of the old library, the air was thick with the scent of aged paper and quiet. He paused before the weight of the ledger he carried, its edges worn from countless readings. The moment lingered—neither hesitation nor hesitation, but the weight of a question he hadn’t asked aloud yet: *What if this was a mistake?* The ink on the page glowed faintly under the dim light, as if the answer were written in the pause itself.

The door behind him creaked open, and she stepped in, her boots whispering on the marble. The pause dissolved into motion, but not before the corners of her mouth twitched—just for a second—like a memory of something unspoken. The ledger fell open to the same page. The pause had been the first step.
