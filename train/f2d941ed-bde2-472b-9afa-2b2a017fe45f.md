Being overheard evokes a mix of **insecurity and vulnerability**. The sensation of being observed—even unconsciously—creates an acute sense of exposure, as if one’s thoughts, emotions, or private thoughts are being scrutinized. This can trigger **anxiety** or **self-consciousness**, especially if the listener is unknown or perceived as judgmental. The brain often experiences a heightened state of alertness, as if the person is being watched, which may amplify feelings of **exposure** or **embarrassment** in response to unintended confessions. The experience can also feel **intrusive**, like an unwanted presence in one’s own mind, disrupting focus or composure. In some cases, it may evoke **guilt** or **self-doubt**, as if one is being judged by an unseen audience. The paradoxical tension of being *unaware* of being overheard adds to the disorienting effect.
