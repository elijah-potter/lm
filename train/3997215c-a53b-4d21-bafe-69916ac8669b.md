**The Last Map of Memory Lane**

She traced the edges of the worn-out notebook with trembling fingers, each line a ghost of what had been—names scribbled in ink that had long since faded. *Lena’s laugh*, *Mira’s whispered secrets*, *the way Daniel’s coffee always burned the roof of his mouth*. The plan was etched in the margins, half-drawn, half-forgotten: *"If the door creaks at midnight, the key is in the hollow of the old oak chair."* But the chair was gone. The key was lost in a pile of dust beneath the bed, forgotten when she’d moved the night she left.

Outside, the city hummed like a thousand trapped bees, indifferent to the quiet ache in her chest. She clenched the notebook tighter. Some memories were maps—directions to places that no longer existed. Others were maps of the future she couldn’t see. The plan wasn’t just about the door anymore. It was about the weight of things left unsaid, the hands that had held her too tight, the promises she’d made in haste. The ink smudged. The paper curled.

She exhaled. The plan was still there, but it had started to bleed into the edges of the notebook, like ink in water. That was how memory worked, wasn’t it? It didn’t just preserve the past. It dissolved it, remade it into something else—something that could be held, or lost, or buried with the weight of what had been. She opened her palm. The key was still there, though she hadn’t been looking for it anymore. It was just a key. A thing with teeth.
