The **Cove of Veiled Lights**—a narrow, mist-shrouded inlet where the water’s surface splits like a scar. The rocks here are carved with ancient, half-eroded symbols, their edges glowing faintly blue under the phosphorescent plankton that swarm at dusk. The tide moves in a slow, deliberate rhythm, pulling the shore back just enough to reveal something buried in the sand: a half-sunk stone circle, its center cracked open like a wound. The air hums with static, the kind that feels alive, as though the land itself is holding its breath. At its heart, a single lantern burns eternally, its flame flickering in sync with the tide’s ebb. Some say the water here isn’t just water—it’s a threshold, a place where the past and future bleed together. The deeper you go, the more the edges of perception blur.
