The engineer’s last test run had been a disaster. The server’s cooling fans were misaligned, causing a sudden spike in temperature. At 3:17 AM, the core failed. The backup power cut out. When the alarms finally stopped, the team found the fire department already on-site.

---

The CEO’s office was locked behind a red velvet rope, but the security guard let him in anyway. He stood in the smoky hallway, breathing through his handkerchief, as the first responders extinguished the flames. The damage report was worse than expected: not just the server farm, but the entire data center’s power grid had collapsed, plunging the city into chaos. By morning, the city’s digital heartbeat had stopped.

---

By noon, the mayor had declared a state of emergency. The city’s emergency response systems were offline, but the public’s panic was offline too—no one knew where to turn. The engineer, still clutching his ruined lab coat, watched as the first panic-stricken citizens started looting the hardware stores.

---

That night, the first blackout in decades stretched across the city. The engineer found himself in a diner at 2:30 AM, nursing a coffee that was already lukewarm. The waitress, a woman who had once worked in IT, handed him a half-eaten sandwich and said, *"You ever think maybe we should’ve just turned off the whole grid?"*

Outside, the sirens wailed. The city’s new leader, a former hacker with a reputation for chaos, had just announced his first order: *"Everyone in the city will get a new ID. No more passwords. Just your name and a fingerprint."* The engineer stared at his coffee. The mistake had already been made. But this time, it wouldn’t be fixed by engineers alone.
