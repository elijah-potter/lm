Rough stone carvings of ancient columns, their surfaces worn smooth yet still bristling with tiny fractures. The air hums with the scent of damp earth and aged cedar, thick as a summer mist. Moss drapes in velvety green clumps over mossy stone steps, some cracked to reveal the pale blue of underground veins. Warm, honeyed leather wraps around worn wooden benches, their grain deeply etched with age. Sunlight filters through cracked glass panes, casting flickering shadows that dance over a polished brass doorknob, its patina dark and ancient. A rusted iron gate, its hinges squeaking faintly, stands half-open, letting in the cold whisper of history. Soft, plush wool blankets are folded neatly atop a velvet-upholstered chair, their texture rich and tactile, as if woven from memories. Rainwater pools in shallow puddles, reflecting the eerie glow of neon streetlights through the peeling paint of the old storefront windows. The cobblestones beneath your feet are uneven, some cracked and splintered, others smooth as glass from centuries of being polished by countless feet.
