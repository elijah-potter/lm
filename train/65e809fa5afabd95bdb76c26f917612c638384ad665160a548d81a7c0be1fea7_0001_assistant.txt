Stanley Kubrick, the director of 2001: A Space Odyssey, was intrigued by the science fiction genre but believed it rarely produced films that were interesting to watch. He was inspired to make a sci-fi film himself and used the story The Sentinel for source material while also getting help from its author, Arthur C. Clarke, to be his co-writer. The plot involves two scientists and an extremely intelligent super computer that are sent to investigate an event near Jupiter. While this plot sounds like the conventional narrative of a sci-fi movie, several scenes are much more surreal and open-ended, such as early hominids learning to use tools before discovering a mysterious monolith that suddenly appears before them. A giant centrifuge was built to serve as the interior of the spaceship as well as many expensive visual effects. An iconic psychedelic sequence that takes place near the conclusion of the film was so complex it was referred to by staff as the "Manhattan Project." 15 minutes was removed from the final cut by Kubrick, but the total runtime of the film still ran well over the 2 hour mark.
