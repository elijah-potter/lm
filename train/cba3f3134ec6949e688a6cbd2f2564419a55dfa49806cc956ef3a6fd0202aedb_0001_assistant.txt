This is not a problem. They say that if you love what you do, you do not work a day in your life. Of course, this is a little cliché, but it is undoubtedly better to work in a field you have passion for than one you do not. There are plenty of jobs in this field. However, they will require a minimum of a bachelor's degree. Many jobs require a higher education than that; some even require a Ph.D. Here are some options to consider when finalizing your major.

•	Astronomy Teacher: You can teach high school students astronomy, sometimes with only a bachelor's degree. However, requirements for high school teachers vary by state. The median pay is just over 30K a year, with the high end being nearly 60K yearly.
•	Climatologist: A climatologist studies climate and weather and uses the information they gather to predict long-term weather patterns. They also study climate change and how it is affecting the planet. You will need a least a bachelor's degree, but depending on location and employer, you may need a Masters's degree. The median pay is 70K, but it can go up to about 100K annually.
•	Astronomy Curators: If you want to do something different and fun in this field, you can get a job as an astronomy curator at a planetarium or museum. You will ensure all the exhibits are correct and up to date. You may also work with the public by giving tours or working behind the scenes to create website content or brochures. You will need at least a bachelor's degree. This is a good-paying job with a median pay of just over 90K and a high range above 160K a year.

There are plenty of other jobs in the field, including technical writers, college professors, astronomers, aeronautical engineers, astrophysicists, research scientists, and meteorologists.
