**The Weight of Silence**

We didn’t mean for this to happen.

The first time the phone rang, it was just a mistake—another forgotten call, another voice left unanswered. But by the time we reached for it again, the message was already playing: *"We’re sorry."* Not the kind of apology that comes in a text with a single emoji, but the kind that wraps around you like a shroud, slow and suffocating.

She was the first to notice the way the lights flickered in the kitchen at midnight, the way the coffee machine hissed like a dying animal. He was the one who found the note pinned to the fridge—*Our hearts are heavy. We won’t be able to keep pretending*—written in a hand that trembled more than hers ever had. The police said it was an accident. They said *we* were the accident.

But the accident was already done by the time they arrived. The way the door had been kicked in wasn’t from fear, but from something worse—something that had been gnawing at them for years, something they’d buried under layers of small, meaningless lies. The walls here still hum with the sound of it, the way the floorboards groan underfoot like old bones.

We didn’t mean for this to happen.

But it did.

And now, the only thing left to say is the one thing that doesn’t need to be said at all.
