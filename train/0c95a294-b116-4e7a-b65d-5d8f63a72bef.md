**The Last Light**

The city exhaled smoke like a dying beast, its neon veins cracked under the weight of forgotten dreams. On the sidewalk, a woman crouched beside a rusted bicycle frame, fingers tracing the faded paint of a once-vibrant mural—now faded to dust between the cracks of the pavement. She had come here every night for weeks, searching for the image that had once been her salvation.

The mural was supposed to be a constellation: a star with seven arms, each bearing a different emotion—love, joy, fear, despair, wonder, sorrow, and peace. But the city had stolen the colors, leaving only shadows where the light once danced.

Then, one evening, she found it.

A single, perfect star glowed in the dark, its edges sharp as a blade. It wasn’t part of the mural. It was *new*. The woman knelt, her breath steady, and pressed her palm to the glass of a half-empty café window. The star pulsed like a heartbeat, its light bleeding through the cracks in reality.

She reached inside.

---

The image flickered—

A **black-and-white photograph of a child**, their eyes wide with unshed tears, clutching a small, crumpled star in their palm. The star was identical to the one she had found, though smaller, more fragile. The child’s mouth formed a silent scream.

The woman gasped. The image dissolved.

And the city—once cold and indifferent—began to *shimmer*. The neon signs flickered. The pavement glowed. The mural, now complete, pulsed with the same light as the star.

She stood, her hands empty, and the city sighed.

The star had taken her. The city had given her back her child.
