**The Last Light**

The clock struck midnight in the old lighthouse, its brass hands frozen in the slow, relentless march of time. The waves crashed against the stone pilings, their rhythm a lullaby of forgotten promises. Inside, Elias sat by the flickering lantern, his breath shallow, his fingers tracing the edge of the glass like he was carving names into eternity.

*Who was the man in the raincoat who stood on the dock last night?*

The wind howled through the broken sails, carrying whispers of the sea—words that slithered past his ears, unheard, unanswered. The lantern’s flame flickered, casting long shadows that danced like specters of the past. He had been here before, each night, each year, yet nothing changed. The questions piled up like driftwood, each one heavier than the last.

*"Why do they come back?"* the voice inside his head whispered. *"Why do they keep looking for what was never meant to be found?"*

Elias exhaled, his chest tight. The lantern burned low, its light dimming into embers. Outside, the storm raged, but inside, silence settled—not the quiet of the deep, but the weight of something unsaid.

The ocean whispered. The lighthouse stood alone.

What was it that made the drowned men return, again and again?
