People often use the words poisonous and venomous interchangeably.  Both involve a toxic substance, and while the meaning is generally understood regardless of which word is used, there is a key difference between the two:

Poison is a toxin that is harmful if ingested, absorbed through the skin, or inhaled.  For example, poisonous mushrooms are harmful when ingested, methane gas is harmful when inhaled, and poison ivy is harmful when absorbed through the skin.

Venom is a toxin that needs a more direct method of reaching the bloodstream, and is delivered by injection.  Snakes, for example, use teeth to inject venom, bees use stingers, and urchins use spines.
