A slow knock echoes at the door, faint yet deliberate. The house is quiet, the only sound the distant hum of the refrigerator. Inside, the living room’s dim light casts long shadows across the worn leather couch, where a half-empty mug of coffee sits untouched beside a half-eaten sandwich. The curtains are drawn, but the glow of the streetlamp outside spills through the cracks, painting the hallway in a muted gold.

A pause—long enough for the knock to feel like an invitation. Then, another. Steadier. The doorknob rattles, the wood groaning under pressure.

The door swings open halfway. A pair of sharp eyes, rimmed in dark circles, narrow as they take in the room. The person inside doesn’t move. The air between them feels charged, thick with unspoken tension.

*"You’re late,"* the stranger says, voice rough with exhaustion. Their fingers tap against the doorknob, fingers that are too long, too precise. *"I’ve been waiting for this for longer than I should have."*
