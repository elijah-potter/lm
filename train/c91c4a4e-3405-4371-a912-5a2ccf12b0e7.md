The guilt was sharp as the first sip of whiskey, but I swallowed it down like a lie. The way my fingers traced the edge of the empty glass—too slow, too deliberate—betrayed nothing. Outside, the city lights blurred into streaks of motion, a distraction I needed. The text message had been sent at dawn, just before the call with her. *"I’m sorry, I can’t make it."* The words had been real, but the regret had been a slow burn, waiting to be extinguished in the hum of traffic or the clink of glasses with strangers.

I wiped my mouth, the condensation glistening under the neon sign. The moment of truth had passed—if only for a second—before the weight of it settled back into my chest like a stone. No one saw the way my pulse quickened when I thought I heard someone’s voice behind me. No one saw the way I almost turned around, just to make sure. But then, the door swung open, and the familiar scent of her perfume—so close, so *wrong*—waved through the air like a ghost. I exhaled, slow and steady, because the truth was simpler than the lies I’d tell myself. I had run. And that was enough.
