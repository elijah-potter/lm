A hasty decision often stems from urgency or emotional pressure. Here’s an example of its consequences:

---

**Context:** A manager at a tech startup, under deadline pressure, approves a rushed product release without thorough testing. The team had 48 hours to ship a critical feature after a client emergency.

**Outcome:**
- Bugs surfaced hours later, causing a major outage for 12,000 users.
- The client threatened legal action for "unforeseen damages" (lost revenue from downtime).
- Internal morale plummeted; engineers blamed the rushed process.
- The company lost a key partnership due to the incident’s PR fallout.
- A new policy was introduced: *"No decision without a 12-hour review cycle"* (later criticized as too rigid).

**Key Lesson:** Speed ≠ quality. The trade-off between urgency and thoroughness created cascading risks. Postmortem revealed the decision failed due to:
1. **Lack of data** (no stress-testing scenarios).
2. **Overconfidence** (assuming the team could fix issues in 12 hours).
3. **Misaligned priorities** (short-term release vs. long-term reputation).

---
*(Note: This is a fictional scenario for illustrative purposes. Actual decisions have varying impacts.)*
