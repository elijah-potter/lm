The quiet village of Eldermere, nestled in the mist-shrouded valley of Whisperwood. The old stone church bell rings every evening, its chime drifting through the pines like a sigh. The town square is paved with moss-covered cobblestones, where the few residents—an elder, a blacksmith, and the librarian—sit beneath the grand oak, their voices barely audible over the distant river’s murmur. The streets are lined with ivy, and the scent of lavender from the elder’s garden lingers in the air. Children whisper secrets to the wind, and the occasional dog barks softly in the distance. The only sounds are the crunch of leaves underfoot and the distant, rhythmic clap of the blacksmith’s hammer. Even the birds pause, listening.
