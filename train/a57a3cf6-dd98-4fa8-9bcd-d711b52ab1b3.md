The rain had begun to fall in sheets when we reached the old warehouse on the outskirts of town. The sign above the door was missing, the paint peeling in jagged strips, and the rusted gates groaned as they swung open, creaking under the weight of the storm. Inside, the air smelled of damp wood and something older—something like mildew and forgotten secrets.

I stepped forward, my boots sinking slightly into the muddy floor. The space was vast, the high ceiling supported by skeletal beams that had seen better days. A single flickering bulb dangled from the center, casting long, wavering shadows across the walls.

*"They’re here,"* whispered Mara, her voice barely audible over the distant drip of water.

I nodded, my gaze lingering on the far corner where the dust motes danced in the dim light. The floorboards creaked under my weight as I moved, careful not to disturb anything. The silence was thick, broken only by the occasional rustle of fabric or the distant echo of something moving beyond the open doors.

Then I saw it—the ledger, half-buried under a pile of crates. My fingers brushed against the worn cover, and for a moment, I thought I saw something glinting beneath it. But as I reached out, the ledger slid away, the cover flapping shut with a sharp *click*.

Mara’s breath hitched. *"What was that?"*

I didn’t answer. The room was too quiet. Too still. The weight of what we were about to find pressed down on me like a physical force. I turned, my pulse quickening, and saw her already moving toward the corner where the ledger had been—where nothing was left but the faintest imprint of something that had been there before.

I followed her gaze. The corner was empty. No ledger. No crates. Just the same dust motes, the same flickering light.

Outside, the storm raged on, but inside, the truth was quieter. Deeper. And it had already begun to unravel.
