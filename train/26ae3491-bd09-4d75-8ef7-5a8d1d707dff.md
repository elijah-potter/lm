The first routine I recall began on a Tuesday afternoon, March 14th, 1998, in my dorm room at the University of Michigan. The sequence started with the alarm clock buzzing at 7:30 AM. I grabbed my backpack, already half-asleep, and shuffled into the bathroom to brush my teeth. The routine followed a rigid order: toothpaste, mouthwash, quick shower, get dressed in jeans and a hoodie, grab coffee from the café across campus, and head to the library for a 9 AM lecture. The cycle repeated every weekday, punctuated by the same rituals—checking emails, studying for 2 hours, then repeating. Over time, the routine became second nature, and the hours blurred together. The first time I truly noticed was when I skipped coffee on a rainy Monday, and the entire day felt off.
