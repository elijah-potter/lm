The neon glow of the city at midnight flickers through the rain-slicked windows, each light a fragile promise of warmth in an endless, cold hum.

A stranger leans against the alley wall, fingers tracing the edge of a half-eaten sandwich wrapped in foil. Their breath steams in the air like smoke, but the cigarette they hold—its tip still glowing—is the only thing they smoke. The sandwich is gone, but the wrapper still clutches the corner of their coat, where the grease has stained the fabric black.

They don’t speak. They don’t need to. The city hums a language of its own—car horns, distant laughter, the occasional scream from a broken window. They listen. And for a moment, they forget they’re listening.
