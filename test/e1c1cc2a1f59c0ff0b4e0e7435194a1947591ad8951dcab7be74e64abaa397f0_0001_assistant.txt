The Taj Mahal, located in Agra, India, is one of the most famous and architecturally significant structures in the world. It was commissioned by Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan in 1632 to serve as a mausoleum for his favorite wife, Mumtaz Mahal, who died during childbirth. The Taj Mahal is an architectural marvel of white marble and semi-precious stones, reflecting influences from Persian, Islamic, and Indian architectural styles. In 1983, it was designated a UNESCO World Heritage site, and it continues to be a symbol of India's rich history.
