The clock strikes 7:00 AM. The same coffee mug sits on the kitchen counter, but the coffee inside is different—this time, it’s cold and bitter. The same black-and-white photo of the old house hangs on the wall, but the shadows cast by the lamp are longer and the reflections in the window are blurred. You dress in the same clothes, but the fabric feels slightly different, as if the fabric has absorbed a faint memory of yesterday’s wear.

The same song plays on the radio, but the lyrics are slightly altered, a word or two shifted. You walk the same route to work, but the traffic lights are red for a different duration, and the same car is parked in the same spot, but the license plate reads a different number.

Your desk is identical—same notebook, same pen, same open email. You type the same words, but the cursor moves differently, as if the keyboard has a mind of its own. The same meeting starts at 10:00 AM, but the agenda items are reordered, and the same person’s name at the top of the email is now typed with an extra keystroke.

By 4:00 PM, the same restaurant is packed, but the menu is rearranged, and the dish you order is served in a different way—extra sauce, missing ingredient. The same conversation unfolds with the same words, but the tone shifts subtly. You leave the same building, but the street sign you pass is now upside-down.

As you sleep, the same dreams repeat, but the details are different—your face in the mirror is slightly younger, older, or more worn. The same alarm clock rings at 5:00 AM, but the sound is slightly off, and the same coffee is ready, but the same bitter taste lingers. The cycle begins again.
