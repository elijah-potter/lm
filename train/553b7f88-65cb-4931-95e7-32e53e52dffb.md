The warehouse hummed with the distant clatter of machinery, the air thick with the scent of aged wood and rust. Elias adjusted his cap, fingers tracing the scar above his eyebrow—a relic of a battle he’d rather forget. His pocket was empty, the last of his ration bars gone cold.

He knelt by the cracked floor, brushing dust from the half-buried crate. Inside, a rusted metal box lay half-sunken, its lid twisted free by years of neglect. The label—peeling, barely legible—read *"EMERGENCY STORAGE #4."*

Elias pried it open. Inside, a single device glinted: a cracked but functional walkie-talkie, its antenna frayed but still humming a faint, distorted signal. A map, folded and crumpled, lay beneath it—a trail leading to the old radio tower at the edge of the valley.

His stomach twisted. *Why now?*

He pocketed the device, the map, and the box, his mind racing. The tower wasn’t just a signal relay anymore. It was a gateway—one that hadn’t been opened in decades. And whatever waited on the other side might not be what he’d hoped to find.

The warehouse door creaked open behind him.

A figure stood there—tall, cloaked in tattered black, face obscured by a hood. The walkie-talkie’s signal flared to life.

*"You shouldn’t have found it."*
