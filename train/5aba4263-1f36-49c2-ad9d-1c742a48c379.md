A tiny brass key, worn smooth from years of turning locks,
now hangs on a rusted chain in the attic. Its once-polished surface
glints faintly under the dim light of a flickering bulb.

Every night, it whispers to the old woman who still visits,
a secret no one else knows—each turn of the chain a prayer
unspoken but heard by the wind that carries her name
across the threshold of forgotten things.

Outside, the city hums with its own keys—keys that unlock
doors of glass and steel, but this one is the only one
that opens the heart of a house no one lives in anymore.
