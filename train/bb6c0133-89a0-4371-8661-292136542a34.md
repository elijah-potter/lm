The world outside had fallen silent, a stillness so profound it felt like an absence of sound itself. Shadows stretched long and thin across the floor, pooling like ink in the corners of the room. A single candle flickered, its flame dancing erratically in the dim light, casting jagged patterns on the walls. The air hummed faintly with the scent of damp earth and something floral, lingering like a memory.

A cup of cold tea sat untouched on the table, its surface rippling with condensation. Outside, the city had exhaled—no cars, no honking horns, no distant chatter. The only sound was the occasional, slow sigh of the breeze through the open window, carrying whispers of forgotten conversations and the distant murmur of a river. The body relaxed, muscles yielding to the ease of stillness, as if gravity had become a gentle, unspoken rhythm. Even time seemed to stretch, stretching beyond the usual ticking, into something softer, more patient.

The mind drifted, no longer racing. Thoughts coalesced into a quiet hum, like distant stars, each one a tiny, unhurried light. The world had learned to listen.
