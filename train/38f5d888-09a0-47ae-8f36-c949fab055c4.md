The winding forest path stretched ahead, its moss-covered stones whispering secrets of time. The shorter route, a straight-cut trail through the dense undergrowth, led toward the village square. But the traveler hesitated, tracing the longer path—a detour through the ancient oak’s roots, past crumbling stone ruins, and beneath the towering canopy where sunlight filtered like fractured glass.

The air hummed with unseen life: the rustle of squirrels, the distant cry of a hawk. A half-buried lantern glowed faintly ahead, its amber light casting shadows that seemed to shift with intention. The choice was made. The longer path called to them, its mysteries promising something deeper, something unspoken. The stones beneath their feet pulsed with the weight of forgotten choices.

They turned, following the faint scent of damp earth and something older—earth itself breathing. The forest did not judge. It only waited.
