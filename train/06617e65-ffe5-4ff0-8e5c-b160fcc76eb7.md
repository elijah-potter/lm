The hourglass fell silent at midnight’s edge,
its grains now still as snow on a cathedral’s spire.
The clock tower’s pendulum swayed,
counting down the years like a lover’s sigh.

Seven o’clock struck, but the hands did not pause—
they danced in a circle, forever bound,
each second a heartbeat, each minute a promise,
each hour a ghost in the hollowed-out shell.

By noon, the clock was a wound,
its gears whispering secrets of time’s cruel art.
A man walked past, unaware—
the face beneath the brass was hollow, a mirror of regret.

The clock’s chime at 11:59 was the last breath of the world,
but 12:00 dawned—
nothing changed. The hands kept turning.
The hourglass refilled.
The tower stood still again.

Time is a clock.
