A hollow vow slipped between fingers—warm with the weight of what was promised, now brittle with doubt. The clock ticks relentlessly, each second stretching into a silent accusation. *"I’ll be back"* echoed once, but the door stays closed. The promise was never a spark; it was a lighthouse, its beam too far, too stubbornly persistent for the tide to wash it away.

The night hums with the ghosts of half-finished words. The letters on the page blur like smoke. The pen hesitates, then writes:
*"I don’t know how to keep this."* The ink fades into the margin. The page is a wound, the words stitches too long to undo.
