The manager rushed into the boardroom at 4:57 PM, clutching a hastily drafted email with the heading **"Urgent: Immediate Rebranding Initiative – 100% Approval Needed"**. The text read:

---
*"Given the recent competitor’s market expansion in Q4, we must pivot our brand identity to emphasize innovation and agility. The new logo will feature a dynamic, fractal-like design (see attached mockups) and a slogan: ‘Reinvent. Repeat.’*
*This aligns with our Q2 strategy but requires full buy-in by Friday. Please approve budget ($12K for design + $8K for print), and I’ll draft a memo for the team by end of day tomorrow."*

---
The email was sent to a list of 12 stakeholders, including the CFO, who replied at 5:00 PM with a single line:
*"Budget is approved, but I’ll need the revised financial projections by tomorrow morning to align with the 10% margin target."*

The rest of the team reacted with varying degrees of skepticism:
- **Marketing Director (CC’d):** *"The mockups look like a 2005 ad campaign. The fractal design is overkill for a consumer product."*
- **Operations Lead:** *"Printing 500 units of the new logo? That’s 20% of our Q4 budget. Where’s the plan for the transition?"*
- **Security Analyst (BCC’d):** *"The email itself has no encryption. Who’s handling data during the rebrand?"*

By the time the meeting started at 6:00 PM, the decision had already been made—without consensus, without contingency plans, and with only a 12-hour window to execute. The CFO’s follow-up note arrived at 7:30 PM:
*"I need the projections ASAP. The 10% margin is non-negotiable."*
