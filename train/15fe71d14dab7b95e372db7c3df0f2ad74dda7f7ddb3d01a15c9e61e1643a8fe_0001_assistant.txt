Magnesium is an essential mineral required by the human body for a multitude of biochemical reactions. It plays a crucial role in energy production, protein synthesis, muscle and nerve function, blood glucose control, and blood pressure regulation. Magnesium is also important in bone development and helps with DNA and RNA synthesis. Despite its importance, magnesium deficiency, or hypomagnesemia, is not uncommon and can result from various factors such as poor dietary intake, chronic alcoholism, certain medications, or problems with intestinal absorption. Symptoms of magnesium deficiency can range from mild to severe. Mild symptoms may include loss of appetite, nausea, vomiting, fatigue, and weakness. As the deficiency worsens, more serious symptoms can develop such as numbness, tingling, muscle cramps, seizures, personality changes, abnormal heart rhythms, and coronary spasms. Long term magnesium deficiency can also lead to more chronic conditions. For example, it can exacerbate osteoporosis by affecting the balance of calcium in the body and reducing the effectiveness of Vitamin D. Several studies have associated low magnesium levels with chronic diseases such as Alzheimer's disease, insulin resistance and type-2 diabetes, hypertension, cardiovascular disease, and migraine headaches.
