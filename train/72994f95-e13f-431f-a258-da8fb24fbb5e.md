**The Last Light**

The city hummed like a dying bee, its neon veins flickering under the weight of dusk. I stood on the rooftop, the wind carrying the scent of ozone and rain, and the sound of distant sirens—somewhere, someone was still awake, still bleeding into the night.

Below, the river glistened, reflecting the last embers of a streetlamp that had burned out long ago. The water was still, a mirror for the shadows stretching long across the pavement. A pigeon took flight, its wings a blur against the twilight, and I watched it disappear into the hollows between buildings.

Then—something moved in the alley behind me.

A figure, half-hidden in the dim glow of a flickering sign, turned slowly. Their face was pale, the skin stretched tight over hollow cheeks. They held a cigarette, the tip glowing like a dying star. They exhaled, and the smoke curled into the air, a ghostly spiral before dissolving into the night.

I didn’t move.

The city had always been watching.
