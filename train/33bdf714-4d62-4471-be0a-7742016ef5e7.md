A storm front rolled in just as the team’s last-minute preparations for the expedition were complete. The wind howled through the abandoned workshop, rattling the rusted doors of the old tool shed. Engineer Vasquez wiped sweat from his brow as he checked the pressure gauges on the modified drone: the readings were erratic, a sign of instability in the fuel mix. The commander’s voice crackled over the comms—static, then static.

"Vasquez, we’re scrubbing."

The words cut through the chaos. The plan for the deep-sea core sample had been perfected over months, but now, the *Gale* was bearing down on the research vessel like a vengeful beast. The backup generator’s oil line had failed during a routine test, and the backup engines were still half-assembled. The team’s adrenaline had been holding them up, but the storm’s ferocity was relentless. The captain’s radio crackled again.

"Shelter in place. No more risks."

The storm’s fury had already torn through the docks, knocking out power and sending debris crashing into the research facility. The last of the supplies were already being loaded into the evacuation helicopters. The mission’s final sequence—drilling into the abyss beneath the waves—was now a ghost of what it could have been. The weight of failure settled over them like the storm’s own invisible hand.

The team exchanged glances, some in frustration, others in resignation. The plan had been set aside not by design, but by the unforgiving march of nature. The core sample would have to wait.
