A city clock struck twelve at dawn, but the first light was always a golden mist swirling through the alleyways of a district that never aged. The morning coffee tasted the same—bitter, sweetened with a syrup no one remembered naming—yet the steam curled into shapes that shifted like living things as it dissipated. A stranger in a coat from another era lingered at the café, whispering to the empty seats, their voice echoing through the same worn wood and plaster walls.

By noon, the market stalls sold the same fruits and breads, but the produce was always slightly different: the tomatoes had a different shade of red, the herbs a hint of warmth from yesterday’s sun. A child, the same age every day, pressed a coin into a merchant’s hand, but the change was always a fraction too large or small, the way the world had learned to bend. A train arrived at midnight, carrying passengers whose faces were identical yet not: the same eyes, but the same subtle shift in the corners of the mouth that made them feel different.

The cycle resumed.
