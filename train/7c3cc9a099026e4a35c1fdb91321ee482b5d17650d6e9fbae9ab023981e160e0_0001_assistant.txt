A few miles down Perkinsville Road was an infamous off-roading trail full of rock features and steep descents, located precisely where Bo and Billy were headed. Billy had recently purchased a brand new Jeep and outfitted it with every Overlanding upgrade he could get his hands on. However, since Bo could not afford such niceties, he was trailing behind in his old hand-me-down Tacoma. This was nothing anyone ever heard Bo complain about as he seemed wholly content with his rig and, in any case, he could go anywhere someone like Billy could go in a souped-up new model.

The friends arrived at the 4x4 trailhead, Billy ahead of Bo. They exited their vehicles and eyeballed the route they were going to travel down. Ultimately, the two decided it was nothing their vehicles could not handle. The trail was titled Smiley Rock at some point, likely because those who drove down it in the past were grinning the same as Billy and Bo were now. After conquering a few of the easier early sections, their two-car caravan came to an abrupt stop.

Over half of the road had disappeared. Washed away by erosion over time, the trail was deemed impassable—at least, that was Bo’s conclusion. Billy wanted to try it, and Bo knew he could do little to persuade him otherwise. Therefore, the only option Bo had was to act as a spotter while Billy attempted the crossing.

As soon as Billy’s rugged tires touched where the road had begun to disappear, the dirt and rocks slid out from under his Jeep and left him dangling over a cliff with two tires hanging in the air. Bo wasted no time jumping into action and attached the winch from his truck to Billy’s steel bumper and turned the motor on at the final moment. Billy’s truck stopped sliding and was pulled back onto safer ground. Instead of continuing, the two cut their losses and headed back to Perkinsville Road, just happy to be alive with both their rigs.
