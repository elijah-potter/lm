A crumbling stone archway halfway up an abandoned hill, its weathered surface etched with faint, forgotten carvings. The air hums with the slow, rhythmic drip of water from a missing roof above, pooling in a shallow basin that mirrors the pale, twilight sky. Beneath it, a moss-grown bench faces the horizon where the last train of the season rolls to a stop at the rusted tracks, its engine sputtering one final time before vanishing into the mist. The silence between the last bell and the next dawn is thick, the kind that presses against your ribs like a quiet breath. Inside a nearby, half-destroyed tavern, a single candle flickers in a cracked glass, casting long shadows that seem to stretch forever. The walls whisper with the echoes of laughter and sorrow, each word a ghost that lingers just beyond hearing. The place feels like a moment suspended in time—neither here nor gone, but waiting.
