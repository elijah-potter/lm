**Local officials in the historic coastal town of Port Vaelis announce a major breakthrough in sustainable energy after a successful trial run of the newly installed tidal wave generator on Main Harbor Bridge.**

The device, developed in collaboration with marine biologists and engineers from the university in neighboring Solara, converts kinetic energy from incoming tides into clean electricity. The first operational test revealed an average output of 25% of the city’s peak energy demand, with plans to expand capacity over the next decade.

Mayor Elara Dain stated, *"This isn’t just energy independence—it’s a step toward zero-emission living for Port Vaelis. The harbor’s tidal patterns are ideal, and we’re already negotiating with neighboring towns for regional grid integration."* Critics warn of ecological concerns, particularly for local fish migration patterns, but the city’s Environmental Review Board is expected to finalize permits by the end of next month.

Residents praised the announcement, with some pointing out that the project could double employment in the local shipyard. Meanwhile, Solara’s university has secured a $4.2M grant from the Green Energy Fund to refine the generator’s efficiency, with a prototype expected by 2026. The Port Vaelis Tidal Initiative remains open to public feedback through the city’s new online portal.
