The bell rang, signaling the end of another day at Maplewood High. Liam sat on the bleachers, staring at the rain-soaked pavement. His phone buzzed—another text from his best friend, Marcus: *"Dude, you gonna come or what?"*

Liam didn’t reply. He had a test tomorrow, and the last thing he needed was drama. But then he saw her—**Ava**. She was cutting class, her dark hair tangled, her shoulders slumped as she slouched against the fence, staring at her phone. The screen flickered with some cryptic message.

Liam’s fingers tightened around his own phone. He’d been avoiding her since that night at the party when she’d nearly drowned in the pool. The police called it an accident. But Liam knew better. She’d been acting weird ever since.

That night, he’d found her in the back of the school storage room, clutching a half-empty bottle of whiskey. Her eyes were bloodshot, her voice hoarse. *"I don’t want to die,"* she’d whispered. *"But I don’t know how to stop."*

Liam had wanted to help, but what could he do? The school didn’t care. No one did.

He shoved his phone back in his pocket and stood up. "Come on," he muttered, walking toward her. "Let’s talk."

Ava didn’t look up. "I just want to disappear," she said quietly. "Make it stop."

Liam clenched his fists. He didn’t know what to say. But he knew he couldn’t let her disappear.

---

The next morning, Liam showed up at the principal’s office. He had no idea what he was doing, but he was determined. The principal, Mr. Calloway, raised an eyebrow. "You’re sure this is important?"

Liam nodded. "Ava’s been hurting herself. She’s not herself anymore."

Mr. Calloway sighed. "I’ve tried to reach her. She’s avoiding everyone. I don’t know what to do."

Liam’s heart pounded. He could see the panic in the principal’s eyes. He wasn’t sure if he was doing the right thing, but he had to try.

---

That afternoon, Liam cornered Ava in the library. She was curled up on a chair, her face buried in her hands. "You’re not alone," he said softly.

Ava didn’t look up. "Why?"

Liam hesitated. "Because I care about you. And I think we can fix this."

She finally raised her eyes, her expression dark. "What if I don’t want to fix anything?"

Liam swallowed hard. "Then we’ll figure it out together."

Ava sighed, then reached out, gripping his hand. "Okay."

---

Over the next few weeks, Liam did everything he could. He talked to Marcus, who had some old friends who worked in social services. He researched self-harm, how to help someone who was hurting. And he talked to Ava every day, no matter how hard it was.

Ava started keeping a journal. She wrote about the night she’d fallen into the pool, how she’d felt alone, how she’d wanted to drown herself so she wouldn’t have to feel anything anymore. But Liam kept it from Marcus, at first. He didn’t want to scare her off.

Then one day, Ava’s mom called. She was worried. She’d found her daughter’s journal. The principal had already called the school counselor.

Liam’s stomach twisted. He had to tell Marcus. He had to tell someone.

---

Marcus was furious. "You’re crazy! You didn’t think about what this could do?"

Liam didn’t answer. He knew Marcus was right. But he also knew he couldn’t just let it go. "I don’t know what to do," he said quietly.

Marcus grabbed his arm. "Look, I get it. You care about her. But this is way out of your league. Ava’s not just some girl. She’s got a whole life, and if we mess this up—"

Liam cut him off. "I know. I’m sorry."

Marcus exhaled sharply. "Fine. But you’re doing this. You’re the one who started it. You handle it."

Liam nodded.

---

That night, Liam called the counselor. He told them everything. Ava’s history, her struggle, the journal. They promised to look into it, to get help.

Ava’s mom was in the waiting room when the counselor called her. She was tearful. "I don’t know what to do," she whispered. "I just want her to be happy."

The counselor told her to take it easy, to give them time. To work with them.

---

Weeks passed. Liam visited Ava every day, made sure she was safe. He brought her snacks, kept her company. He learned to read between the lines, to see the fear in her eyes, the way she avoided looking at him when she was lying.

One day, Ava finally admitted it. "I’m scared," she said. "Scared of dying. Scared of not being able to stop."

Liam understood. He’d been there once himself. He’d thought about jumping off the roof of the old theater one night, just to escape the pain.

But Ava wasn’t ready to talk about it. She was still hurting, still struggling. But she was listening.

---

The school counselor called one day. They had an idea. Ava was going to therapy, but they wanted Liam to be part of it. To support her, to help her through it.

Liam didn’t know what to say. He was scared, but also excited. He wanted to help Ava. He wanted to make sure she was okay.

---

The first session was rough. Ava was anxious, her hands shaking. The counselor asked her about her feelings, her fears. Liam sat quietly, letting her talk.

Ava started crying. "I don’t know if I can do this," she whispered.

Liam reached out, squeezing her hand. "You can. You’re stronger than you think."

---

Months turned into years. Ava didn’t just stop self-harming—she started living again. She made friends, joined clubs, even started talking about college. She was still scared sometimes, still had bad days. But she was fighting.

And Liam? He was there for her. Every day.

One day, as they sat on the bleachers, Ava looked up at him. "Thank you," she said.

Liam smiled. "For what?"

"For believing in me," she said. "For not giving up."

Liam didn’t say anything. He just looked at her, really looked at her. Because he knew—this was only the beginning.
