The dim glow of a flickering lantern cast long shadows across the cracked wooden floor. A wooden chair, its seat worn smooth by time, stood empty in a dimly lit room. The air was thick with the scent of damp earth and old books, the only sounds the distant hum of a clock ticking away in the hallway and the occasional rustle of leaves outside the single, narrow window.

A figure sat hunched in the chair, back straight but head bowed. Clad in a faded leather jacket and sturdy boots, they held a half-empty flask, the glass trembling slightly in their hands. Their fingers traced patterns in the condensation on the windowsill, but their eyes darted restlessly to the door, half-opened as if listening to something unseen.

The waiting was endless. They knew what they were waiting for—someone who had left without saying goodbye, or perhaps a letter that might never arrive. The room felt like a tomb, the silence a heavy weight pressing down. Every creak of the floorboards, every whisper of wind outside, seemed to echo the unspoken question: *When will it be over?*
