The receptionist paused mid-keypress, her fingers hovering over the keyboard as she read the unsigned note: *"I messed up. I’m sorry."* The paper crackled under her scrutiny, the edges frayed like old nerves.

Across the hall, the intern stood frozen, phone gripped tightly, the screen reflecting the flickering hallway light—no message, no reply. The tension in the break room was palpable, the usual chatter replaced by the hum of unspoken tension.

The manager, usually sharp-eyed, now had a half-smile tugging at his lips. He’d seen this before—words that didn’t quite land, like a punch that missed the mark. The clock ticked. The door to the cafeteria stood open, the silence between them a thick, unbroken glass.

*"You know what?"* the intern whispered, voice too loud. *"I think she’s just… waiting for something to change."* The manager exhaled slowly, fingers steepled. The apology, already half-erased, sat between them like a question mark.
