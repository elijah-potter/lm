The last light of twilight bleeds across the cracked pavement, painting fractured hues of crimson and violet over the skeletal remains of the old warehouse. Its rusted iron gates groan, half-buried in ivy, their paint peeling away like skin from bone. Inside, the air is thick with dust and the ghostly scent of old coffee, still clinging to the cracked wooden floors.

Through the broken window, the last of the city’s neon signs flickers—**EXIT**, **SOS**, **LIFE**—their colors dulled by time, their messages now a faded whisper. The walls whisper too: fragments of conversations, laughter, screams, all muffled by layers of dust and the slow creep of mold.

A single streetlight hums weakly, casting long shadows that stretch like fingers toward the concrete. The pavement beneath your feet is uneven, a maze of cracks and hidden fissures. To the east, the skeletal remains of a bridge loom—its steel girders bent like the necks of long-dead birds, their once-graceful arches now twisted and broken. The river below flows sluggishly, its surface a mirror of the city’s decay, reflecting the last embers of a dying fire.

The wind carries the scent of damp earth and something older—something that wasn’t here yesterday. It’s not just the city ending; it’s the world ending in this place. The last thing you hear before the silence takes hold is the distant, hollow laugh of a ghost in the wind.
