A clock ticks relentlessly, its hands a relentless reminder of time’s relentless march. The morning sun peeks through the curtains, casting a golden haze over the room. Coffee brews, steaming and bitter, a temporary escape from the weight of the day. The alarm blares, a shrill wake-up call that echoes through the house before dawn’s first light has fully risen.

The commute is a blur of traffic lights, sirens, and the hum of the city, a symphony of noise that masks the quiet dread of what comes next.

At lunchtime, the kitchen is a battleground. A half-eaten sandwich sits forgotten on the counter, a relic of a meal that lasted only minutes. The afternoon slides into evening with the same relentless pace—errands, deadlines, the relentless grind of obligations. The evening fades into night, and still, there’s more to do.

The bed is a place of escape, but it’s not rest. The mind races, thoughts tangled in a web of unfinished business. The clock on the wall ticks louder, each second stretching into an eternity. The day has passed, and yet it feels like only a fraction of time has gone by.

The night is quiet, the house empty. The silence is a reprieve, but it’s not enough. The day lingers, a ghost of exhaustion and unfulfilled potential. Tomorrow will be the same, another cycle of time’s relentless march. The day feels too short.
