The rain fell in quiet sheets outside the window, tapping rhythmically against the panes like a drummer playing an endless, monotonous beat. Inside, Elias sat at his desk, fingers tracing over the worn edges of his notebook, the same pattern of his childhood—a loop of lines, circles, and dashes he’d traced over and over until they formed a shape.

“It’s like the world is just repeating itself,” he muttered, and the words hung in the air between him and the door where his sister, Mira, stood with her arms crossed, her dark eyes reflecting the dim glow of the lamp.

Mira didn’t answer. Instead, she turned and walked to the window, her heels clicking against the floor with the same steady, relentless cadence as the rain outside. Elias watched her profile, his breath shallow. “You know,” he said, “I think it’s not the rain that’s the problem. It’s the *listening*.”

She didn’t look back. The silence stretched, thick with unspoken words and the weight of things left unsaid. Then, just as the rain’s rhythm started again, Mira whispered, “I’m listening.”

And Elias knew, with a certainty that cut deeper than the storm outside, that the cycle would continue.
