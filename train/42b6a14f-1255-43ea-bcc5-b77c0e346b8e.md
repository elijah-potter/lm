A flight from Chicago to Detroit was delayed by weather, forcing an overnight stay in a small hotel in Milwaukee. The next morning, while browsing the local newspaper, an advertisement caught his eye: *"Urgent: Medical Records Needed for Emergency Surgery."* The name on the ad matched the name of the patient—his best friend from college, who had been hospitalized the same night.

He rushed to the hospital, arriving just as the surgery began. The anesthesiologist recognized him immediately. *"You’re the one who called,"* the doctor said, glancing at the chart. *"I was wondering how you knew."*

His plan—securing a job in Detroit—had unraveled in the space of one chaotic day, but the coincidence had redirected him toward helping his friend instead. The surgery was a success, and in the aftermath, he found himself in a room with a new purpose: organizing his friend’s medical records and starting a small nonprofit to support underfunded clinics. The city that once felt like a dead end now held the first brick of something meaningful.
