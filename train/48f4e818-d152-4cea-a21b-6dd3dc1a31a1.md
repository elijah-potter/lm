**The Last Light**

The city hummed beneath a weight of forgotten dreams, its neon veins pulsing with the ghostly glow of forgotten promises. The rain had turned to silver, coating the pavement in a mirror of fractured reflections—each drop a tiny, trembling star. Somewhere, a clock struck midnight, though no one paid it any mind.

She stood on the edge of the docks, where the old wharves groaned like sleepwalkers. The wind carried the scent of salt and rust, the faintest tang of something older, something buried beneath layers of concrete. Her fingers traced the damp edge of a broken sign, its letters worn smooth by time: *"DO NOT ENTER."*

A child laughed from the alleyway, their voice bright as a stolen sugar cube. The sound dissolved into static when she turned away.

Then the sky split open.

Not with fire, not with thunder, but with something quieter—the slow unraveling of the world’s silence. The clouds, once a uniform gray, had dissolved into a thousand fractured pieces, drifting like confetti into the twilight. The stars blinked awake, not as they should have, but as if remembering a language they’d forgotten. The horizon stretched into a vast, trembling void, where the edge of the world seemed to whisper.

The light above her was neither day nor night, but something in between—a shifting, breathing thing, pulsing like a heartbeat. It wasn’t blue or gold, but something older, something that had always been there, waiting. The air itself seemed to thicken, to catch on her skin like the first breath of dawn.

She closed her eyes. The sky wasn’t just sky anymore. It was a map. A warning. A promise. And for the first time in years, she didn’t feel alone.
