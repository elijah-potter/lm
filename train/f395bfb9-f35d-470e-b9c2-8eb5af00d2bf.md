Once upon a time, in an office where efficiency was the name of the game, a rule was introduced: **"No more than two emails per day to the same person without a response."** It was meant to curb spam and improve workflow.

The rule was enforced once—on the morning of the first Tuesday in March—when a junior employee flagged the email queue for excessive duplicate messages. The IT manager, recognizing inefficiency, implemented it with a memo: *"Avoid redundant communication; reply or archive."*

Then, as if by default, the rule was forgotten. Over time, the office’s email habits returned to their chaotic pre-memo state. The "two-email limit" became a distant memory, replaced by the chaos of unchecked replies and the occasional "Sorry, forgot to reply" note.

It was a rule that faded like a forgotten password—briefly remembered, then lost in the noise of daily work.
