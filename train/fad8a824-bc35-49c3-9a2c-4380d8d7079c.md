A sterile, fluorescent-lit office atrium with repetitive, monotonous cubicles. Overhead, a maze of tangled cables and blinking sensors creates an oppressive, artificial glow. The walls are lined with glass partitions that reflect distorted images of strangers, their faces blurred by distance. The air hums with the sound of constant background chatter and keyboard clicks, drowned out by the rhythmic drip of a leaky faucet in the far corner. The floor is a patchwork of worn tiles and sticky rubber mats, some cracked, others stained with spilled beverages from the coffee machine that never turns off. The only human presence is a single, perpetually slumped figure in a chair, their back to the space, their movements sluggish. The scent of disinfectant and stale coffee lingers, thick and suffocating.
