The city hummed under a sky of fractured light, its neon veins pulsing with the ghostly glow of a forgotten future. I stood on the edge of the docks, the cold salt wind biting through my coat, the weight of the past pressing down like a tombstone. My fingers traced the worn wood of the railing, where time had etched itself into every grain, every scar.

I had spent years chasing this moment—years of half-truths, half-steps, half-lives. The letters I had burned, the names I had erased, the faces I had buried under layers of lies. But here, in this moment, the truth clawed its way out, raw and unfiltered. The city was a graveyard of dreams, and I was just another corpse in the dirt.

I had always known the truth would come. It always does. But I had never expected it to taste like this—like ash in the wind, like the weight of all the things I had never said. The admission was not a victory. It was surrender. And for the first time in my life, I accepted it.
