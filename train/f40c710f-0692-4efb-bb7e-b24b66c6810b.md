Eyes of a forest at dusk: jagged canyons of light split by thickets of elongated, fractured shadows—some sharp as knives, others stretched into slow, trailing veins along stone. The longest cast by ancient oaks, their roots knotted in deep earth, whispering time. The shortest belong to forgotten paths, where the sun bends into a ragged smile against the sky’s rim, flickering like a dying ember. Between them, the lake’s ripples blur shadows into watery reflections of birch branches, their edges dissolving into mist. The wind, a thief, steals the edges, leaving only the core—a silent, hollowed-out space where light refuses to linger. Here, shadows do not tell stories; they are the bones of the world’s silence.
