The shadows cast at dawn are delicate, long, and sway like river currents, bending toward the east where the morning light barely touches. They stretch in jagged lines across cracked, sun-bleached stones that hum with warmth. A dry creek bed, its once-full bed now a dusty whisper, reflects them in shallow pools, each ripple like a whispered secret. Towering acacias cast deep, narrow shadows that cling to ancient bark, their canopies framing the edge of the world like the rim of a vast, unfurled tapestry. The shadows dance in a slow, hypnotic ballet with the wind, shifting unpredictably as the sun climbs—sometimes merging into a single, elongated serpent, sometimes splitting into a swarm of tiny, restless wings. At dusk, they become denser, thick as smoke, pooling around the edges of forgotten ruins where the earth breathes in silence. The shadows are the breath of this place—lifeblood in a world where light is fleeting, and time is measured in the length of their descent.
