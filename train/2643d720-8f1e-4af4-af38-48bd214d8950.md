The alarm buzzed, a harsh metallic pulse through the dim room. Kael sat rigidly in the chair, fingers curled around the edge, eyes tracking the ceiling fan’s slow spin. His breath hitched—*one minute left*. The door creaked open, and his mother’s silhouette framed the doorway, hands shoved into pockets like she’d just run a mile. “You’re late,” she said, voice flat, but not unkind. “The shift starts in five.”

Outside, the neon glow of the city pulsed, a blur of red and blue. Kael’s phone buzzed—another text from his coworker: *“Meet at the usual spot. Don’t be late.”* He exhaled, the sound a whisper of defeat. The fan’s blades sliced through the air as the seconds ticked down. His mother moved closer, pressing a key into his palm—*the one he’d lost half a year ago*. “You’re not ready,” she murmured, but her eyes were soft. The elevator doors hissed open, and the first customer stepped inside, already holding a stack of files. Kael’s stomach twisted. He nodded once, turned, and followed, his shadow stretching long against the fluorescent light. The minute ended. The world kept spinning.
