The rain slashed against the cracked windshield of the abandoned van as Daniel gripped the steering wheel. The GPS had died hours ago, replaced by a flickering LED on the dashboard that read **"No Signal"** in Morse code. The headlights cast long, skeletal shadows on the crumbling road signs: **"EXIT 172 – HOME"** now just a faded smear. His phone battery was dead, and the dead-end exit sign—**"TOURIST TRAIL"**—now read **"NO EXIT"** in thick rainwater.

A branch scraped against the roof. His breath came in shallow bursts as he turned the key. The engine sputtered, coughing once before stalling. Outside, a distant wail of a siren echoed through the storm, but the road ahead was a blur of pavement and mud. Ahead, the flickering neon sign of a diner—**"DANGEROUS LANE – HOT FOOD"**—now glowed weakly, its "CLOSED" sign flapping in the wind like a dying bird.

Daniel’s reflection in the rain-streaked window showed him: a face half-lit by the dying dashboard light, his fingers trembling as he adjusted the radio—only static now. The words **"RETURN"** echoed in his head, but the road stretched forward, endless, and wrong.
