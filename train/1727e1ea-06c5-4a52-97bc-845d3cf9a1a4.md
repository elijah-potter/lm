The jagged cliffs of **Vermilion Reef** at dusk, where the Atlantic’s last light bleeds through the mist. Coral bones twist like skeletal fingers against the dark, and the tide carves through the shallows in a slow, hungry pulse. A lone boat’s wake spirals into the horizon, vanishing before it can determine if it’s heading toward the next storm or the next dawn. The air smells of salt and ozone, thick with the weight of unseen depths pressing just beneath the surface. Below, the reef’s underbelly hums—a low, rhythmic vibration that hums with the pulse of unseen creatures. The sea here is not just water; it’s a membrane, stretching, thinning, ready to tear. At your feet, a single, crushed starfish lies still, its body decomposing into the very fabric of the reef. The wind carries the whispers of the drowned, voices that echo but never quite repeat themselves. This is where the world ends—not with a crash, but a whisper.
