The warehouse lights flickered to low dimmer settings as the forklift driver adjusted the pallet, barely lifting it enough to slip a single, oversized box into the back. The stack was visibly uneven—one corner sagged under the extra weight—but the foreman’s clipboard, still held tight, showed no note about "excessive loads." The foreman, mid-conversation with the shift supervisor, glanced up only long enough to nod, then turned back to his phone, as if the box were already accounted for. The supervisor, a few steps behind, murmured something about "flexibility in load management," but the tone carried no warning. The next shift would find the stack just as the day started: slightly off-center, but still structurally sound enough to pass inspection.
