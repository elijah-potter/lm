A chaotic skirmish erupts in the crowded marketplace as a sudden gust of wind disrupts the stalls. Vendors scramble as glass bottles shatter, spilling colorful syrups across the cobblestones. A group of armored mercenaries, clad in mismatched gear, storm past, their leader shouting orders in a language that shifts between guttural and melodic. A merchant’s child clutches a half-eaten pastry as the crowd parts to let a masked figure slip through the fray—a dealer of unknown currency, their hand gliding over the air like smoke.

The rules of engagement have been rewritten mid-battle: now, only those who can read the flickering sigils on the ground—blinking like trapped fireflies—are permitted to claim the spoils. The mercenaries snarl, their weapons humming with energy they didn’t know they carried. The child’s father lunges, but the air itself seems to resist, bending their blade into a question mark before he falls. The dealer’s smile never wavers; they’re already trading a memory for a coin that wasn’t there before.
