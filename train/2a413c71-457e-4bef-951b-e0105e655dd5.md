A pause that altered the course of history occurred in 1945 during the final days of World War II. The atomic bomb dropped on Hiroshima on August 6 marked a pivotal moment in military strategy, but the decision to use a second bomb on Nagasaki on August 9—after a brief strategic pause—had lasting geopolitical consequences.

That pause allowed the Soviet Union to declare its entry into the war on August 8, effectively dividing the Pacific Theater. It also demonstrated the devastating power of nuclear weapons, accelerating the Cold War’s arms race. The pause became a turning point not just in warfare but in shaping modern global politics and ethics.
