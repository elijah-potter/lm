**Local officials in the historic coastal city of Port Veyra announced today that the city council has approved a new sustainable waste management initiative to combat the ongoing plastic pollution crisis. Starting January 1, residents will be required to separate their waste into three categories: biodegradable organic matter, recyclables, and hazardous waste. Non-compliance will result in fines starting at $15 per violation. Mayor Elena Carter stated, "Port Veyra is leading by example—our harbor is a national landmark, and we can’t allow plastic to mar our beauty. This change is a step toward a cleaner, healthier future."**

Environmental advocates praised the move, calling it a "bold first step" in the region. However, critics warn that enforcement will be critical to ensure fair implementation, especially for low-income households. Meanwhile, a local artist group has launched a "Garbage to Gold" initiative, converting recyclables into public art installations to raise awareness and funds for the program. The city’s marine biologists have also expressed concern that improper waste disposal could harm local coral reefs, already threatened by rising sea temperatures. Port Veyra remains committed to balancing economic growth with ecological stewardship.
