Christopher Marlowe was born in 1564 and died young in 1593. He was regarded as a maverick and was a supposed spy for HM Government. He was killed, in short, by Ingram Frizer, allegedly in self-defense, although his death is surrounded by much mystery and intrigue for over five hundred years.

Christopher Marlowe wrote many plays, including A Jew from Malta, Tamburlaine the Great plays and The Massacre at Paris. However, it was Dr. Faustus, his most famous work, that many remember him for. He wrote Faustus was written probably close to his death, in around 1592 or 1593.  

Two different versions of the play exist: The play is in blank verse and prose in thirteen scenes (1604) or twenty scenes (1616).

Dr. Fautus quickly rose in prominence from a poor start and earned a doctorate in theology. His hunger for knowledge and pride leads him to communicate with the dead, Mephistophilis, and signs a contract with the devil in his blood. His thirst for knowledge over 24 years leads him to bargain with Lucifer and sell his soul to him.
