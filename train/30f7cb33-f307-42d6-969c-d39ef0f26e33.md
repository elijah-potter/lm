I remember the first time the wind carried the scent of rain
over the fields of autumn, how my fingers traced the cracks
in the pavement where the city’s past whispered secrets.
I remember the way my mother’s hands still trembled
when she spoke of the war, how she pressed my forehead
against her chest like she could absorb the weight of it.
I remember the library’s fluorescent hum,
the way books fell open to old letters, the way
some words stayed stuck in the air like dust.
I remember the way the river bent like a question mark
across the horizon, and how I used to think
that if I followed it long enough, I’d find the answer.
I remember the way the light changed in winter,
how it turned everything to ice, how even time seemed
to slow down, like the world was holding its breath.
I remember the way my brother’s laugh sounded like laughter
after a long silence, how he always knew
the things I couldn’t say.
I remember the way the night sky stretched
so vast it made me feel small, and how I used to think
that if I reached up high enough, I could touch the stars.
I remember the way my voice cracked when I spoke of ghosts,
how the shadows seemed to lean closer when I talked.
I remember the way the wind carried the sound of voices
from the past, and how I used to think
that some things were meant to be left behind.
I remember the way my hands shook when I held a pen,
how the paper was always blank, how the words would not come.
I remember the way the rain fell like tears,
how it seemed to cleanse the world of everything it had been.
I remember the way my heart beat in my chest like a drum,
how it seemed to echo the rhythm of the world.
I think of the stories I never told, the truths I buried,
the names I couldn’t say, the words I left unsaid.
I think of the way the world keeps turning, indifferent,
as if nothing I remember or think could ever change it.
I think of the way I was once just a child, watching,
listening, absorbing, and now I am a voice in the wind,
a memory left to fade into the nothingness.
I think of the way the past is a door I cannot open,
and the future is a door I do not know how to close.
I think of the way I am still here, still listening, still wondering.
I think.
