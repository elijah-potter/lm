At exactly fifteen minutes past eight in the morning, on August 6, 1945, an unanticipated event struck Hiroshima. Miss Toshiko Sasaki, a diligent worker at the East Asia Tin Works, had just begun her day when the eerie silence was shattered by the appearance of an alien spaceship above the city. Simultaneously, Dr. Masakazu Fujii, engrossed in his morning read, was jolted by the alien arrival; Mrs. Hatsuyo Nakamura, a tailor’s widow, paused in her kitchen as the surreal scene unfolded; Father Wilhelm Kleinsorge was enjoying a peaceful morning when the strange ship appeared; Dr. Terufumi Sasaki, a young doctor at the Red Cross Hospital, found his routine disrupted by the sudden turn of events; and Reverend Mr. Kiyoshi Tanimoto, while preparing to unload a handcart, was met with the shocking spectacle.

An alien presence had manifested in Hiroshima, and these six individuals were among the first to experience it. They still ponder the reasons why they were chosen to witness such a monumental event, wondering about the strange turns of fate that led them to that moment. And now, each of them understands that in the act of witnessing, they have been thrust into a new era of interstellar relations, a reality more profound than they ever imagined.

Earlier that day, Mr. Tanimoto, restless and uneasy, had risen at five in the morning. The cities of Kyoto and Hiroshima had been spared by the mysterious “B-san”, the unknown phenomenon, causing tension among their inhabitants. Hiroshima had been subject to numerous alerts of the imminent “B-san”, leading to rumors that the city was being kept for some extraordinary event.

Mr. Tanimoto, a man of short stature but abundant energy, had a unique mix of youthfulness and wisdom, a peculiar balance of impulsiveness and restraint. In anticipation of an unknown disaster, he had been evacuating items from his church to a safer location. Mr. Tanimoto was driven by an uncanny sense of responsibility, even agreeing to help a friend evacuate his daughter's belongings. Little did he know that he was not preparing for a disaster, but for the first contact with an extraterrestrial civilization.
