A dimly lit warehouse with exposed concrete pillars glows faintly under a low-hanging streetlight. Raindrops pound the rusted corrugated metal roof, creating a steady, rhythmic cadence. The flickering fluorescent lights cast long, wavering shadows across the cluttered workbench, where half-assembled circuit boards and discarded parts spill onto a stained tarp. Dust motes swirl lazily in the air, trapped between the gaps of a cracked window.

A lone figure crouches behind a metal filing cabinet, fingers tracing the grain of the wood beneath a flickering phone flashlight. The cabinet’s hinges groan under the weight of old files, some marked with faded labels like *"Project X"* or *"Security Risk."* The walls are lined with discarded schematics, half-printed blueprints, and a single, bloodstained glove—its fingers too long, too jagged. The scent of ozone and burnt wires clings to the air, mingling with the metallic tang of damp concrete and something older: the stale, coppery smell of old fear.

Outside, the city hums—car horns, distant sirens, the occasional burst of laughter from a passing crowd. But inside, the warehouse is a tomb of forgotten decisions, each crack in the ceiling a whisper of what could have been. The floorboards groan underfoot, each step a betrayal of time, and the air is thick with the weight of choices made and ones left unanswered. The background isn’t just walls—it’s the story of what was *hidden* here.
