A dimly lit warehouse hums with the low hum of machinery in the background. A lone figure—clad in an oversized lab coat—works at a bench under flickering fluorescent lights. Their hands move with practiced precision as they adjust a complex apparatus. The screen in front of them flickers with data, but the reading is slightly off: a critical threshold has been crossed by a margin of error.

The figure sighs, but doesn’t stop. "Not quite there yet," they murmur, adjusting the calibration once more. The machine beeps softly, a warning that’s just past the red line—barely. They take a slow breath, then nod to themselves before turning off the machine with deliberate slowness.

The door creaks open behind them, and a uniformed guard steps inside. The figure doesn’t look up. "You’re late," the guard says, but there’s no real urgency in their voice. Instead of rushing, they linger, studying the setup with mild curiosity.

The guard glances at the screen again—just to confirm. "Well, well. Looks like you’re still working on that one, huh?" They step back, leaving the door open. The figure doesn’t react. The warehouse continues to breathe, the bend in the rule a quiet, unspoken thing.
