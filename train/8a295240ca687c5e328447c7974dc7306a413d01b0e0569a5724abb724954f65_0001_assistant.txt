Titus awoke to the sound of trumpets blaring throughout the camp. Titus was a soldier in the army of the Roman Republic; he was part of a force assembled to oppose Hannibal of Carthage. Hannibal was an incredibly skilled and intelligent general leading an army comprised of native Carthaginians and foreign soldiers from Spain and Gaul. He had not yet lost a battle in his war with Rome. But Titus had felt encouraged while marching from Rome to the town of Cannae. The force that Rome had assembled was massive. Surely such an overwhelming numerical advantage would bode well for Titus and his countrymen. 

The trumpets were sounded to rouse the Romans and get them ready for a pitched battle. Titus donned his armor, left his tent with his tentmates, and headed to the road leading out of the Roman camp's gates. Soon, the entire army was assembled. The heavy infantry was positioned in the center of the army with the cavalry on the wings. Across the field, the Carthaginian battalions filed into position. For some time, the two armies gazed at one another across the plains. The sun was rising in the sky, bringing a sweltering heat with it. Sweat dripped down Titus's brow. Finally, the order came for the Romans to advance. Their forces dwarfed the opposition, and there was a great sense of determination amongst the Romans to avenge Hannibal's prior victories against their fellow citizens. Titus was near the front line and was looking forward to proving himself. The skirmishers from each army volleyed stones and javelins at one another, then retreated behind their respective frontlines. The two groups of infantry were very close now; the battle was about to begin.
