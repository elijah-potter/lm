In the quiet hum of the morning, as the first light painted the rooftops a delicate gold, I noticed it again: the way the streetlamps flickered in a slow, rhythmic dance. Not random—each one, in its own time, would dim for a single, measured second before returning to its steady glow. The pattern was subtle, almost imperceptible at first glance, but with patience, it became undeniable.

I sat beneath an oak, watching as the light shifted in unison with the breath of the city. The pattern was the same: a pause, a flicker, a reset. As if the city itself was learning to count, or perhaps remembering a language lost to time. The pattern wasn’t in the lamps alone—it was in the way the shadows stretched and compressed, the way the air carried the scent of rain before it fell. Even the pigeons, those restless sentinels, moved in sync with the rhythm, their steps deliberate, their murmurs soft and deliberate.

I traced the pattern in my fingers, feeling its pulse in the wood beneath my palm. The city wasn’t just alive—it was *organized*. And for the first time, I realized, I wasn’t the only one who saw it. The pattern was everywhere.
