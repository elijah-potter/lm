The dust swirled around the flickering overhead light, settling unevenly on the cracked linoleum. The air smelled faintly of damp wood and old coffee, thick with the scent of burned cinnamon from the abandoned crockpot. A shattered glass jar lay half-buried under a pile of papers on the cluttered desk—ink-stained pages and a crumpled receipt from the last bar visit, the faded logo of a defunct band still visible.

The sofa groaned under a single, damp blue pillow, its cushioning still warm where it had been abandoned. The windows, draped in faded curtains, let in the muted glow of a storm outside, the wind rattling the loose frame. On the floor, a half-empty bottle of whiskey and a half-eaten sandwich wrapper glinted in the dim light. A single, half-pressed button on the coffee machine suggested a forgotten brew cycle. The walls, painted a peeling green, bore a single, half-peeled poster of a face that no longer existed.
