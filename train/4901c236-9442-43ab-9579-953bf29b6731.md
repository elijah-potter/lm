A crumbling stone wall, its mortar eroded by time and weather, stands as a jagged skeleton of an archway, half-buried beneath overgrown ivy. The floor beneath is a patchwork of uneven slabs, some cracked, others warped from moisture, revealing the ghostly imprint of missing tiles. A rusted metal ladder spirals downwards, its planks bent and loose, leading to a basement door that barely holds its frame. Dust swirls in the dim, flickering light from a single, flickering bulb hanging from a chain.

Above, windows are boarded with salvaged wood, some splintered, others warped from dampness. The air smells of damp earth, mildew, and something faintly metallic—like old blood or rust. A half-finished mural on the wall depicts a scene of a city that never was, its figures frozen mid-motion, their faces blurred by the passage of time.

The silence here is not absolute; it’s thick, like the air, heavy with the weight of things left undone. A draft creeps through the cracks, carrying the scent of decay. At the center of the room, a half-assembled desk stands, its surface littered with half-drawn blueprints, pencils, and tools that have been abandoned. A single, half-finished sketchbook lies open, its pages filled with sketches of buildings that never rose, roads that never connected, and faces that never smiled. The place hums with the restless energy of a mind that couldn’t finish what it started.
