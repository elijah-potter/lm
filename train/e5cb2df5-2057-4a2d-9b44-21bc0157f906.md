A crumbling stone bridge, its archways half-buried in moss, stretches across a riverbed where the water flows sluggishly through ancient roots. The railing, once polished iron, now rusts into jagged spikes, clinging to the damp stone like a last stubborn whisper. The ground beneath is uneven—some patches of earth have been trampled flat, while others remain untouched, as if waiting for something to grow.

The walls of the old church at the edge of town are lined with peeling frescoes, their colors faded to muted grays, the faces of saints now mere blurs. A single candle flickers in a broken window, casting long shadows that dance like restless spirits. The clock tower stands silent, its hands frozen at 11:59, as if time itself has paused here.

At the center of the square, a fountain once carved with intricate designs now sits empty, its stone basins cracked and stained with years of neglect. The air smells of damp earth and something metallic—old iron, rust, the faintest trace of decay. Children sometimes play here, their laughter too bright against the silence, their presence a sharp contrast to the hollow space around them. They don’t seem to notice the cracks in the pavement, the way the wind howls through the empty spaces between the buildings.

The town’s last resident, an elderly woman with eyes like hollow moons, sits on a bench beneath a sagging streetlamp, her hands wrapped around a worn leather journal. She doesn’t look up when you pass, but you know she’s watching. The place hums with a quiet, unnatural energy—like something is waiting, just beneath the surface, something that shouldn’t have been left behind.
