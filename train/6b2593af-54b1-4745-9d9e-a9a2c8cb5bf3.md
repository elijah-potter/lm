The room was dimly lit by flickering overhead lights, casting long shadows across the worn leather couch. A half-empty cup of cold coffee sat untouched on the coffee table, its condensation dripping onto the fabric. The air smelled of stale cigarette smoke and the faint metallic tang of the city outside.

Across the room, the figure sat hunched over, knuckles white around the armrests. A watch, its face dull and scratched, ticked slowly against the wood of the nightstand. No phone buzzed, no doorbell rang—just the distant hum of traffic and the occasional car horn, muffled by the window.

Time stretched like a stretched rubber band, each second longer than the last. The silence was thick, the weight of anticipation pressing down like a physical force. Outside, a streetlight flickered, casting eerie glows across the street, reflecting in puddles that had already frozen over in the cold night air.

A key turned in the lock—someone was coming. The door swung open, and the sudden burst of light and noise made the waiting person jump. The door slammed shut behind them.
