Salvia plants grow best in well-draining soil that is moderately fertile and slightly acidic. However, avoid overwatering and over-fertilization, as it increases the risk of fungal disease. If you amend your soil with compost, additional fertilizer isn't usually necessary for perennial salvias. In poor soils or for annual salvia varieties, use slow-release fertilizer when planting. 
There are over 900 species of salvia, so be sure to check the zone for your plants to know whether or not they're cold-hardy. With proper care, salvia plants will produce an abundance of colorful, fragrant flowers that attract pollinators and add beauty to any garden.
