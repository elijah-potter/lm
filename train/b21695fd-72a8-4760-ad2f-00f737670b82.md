The candle flickered low, its wax dripping onto the ancient parchment. Elias adjusted the draft on the hearth, his fingers tracing the edges of the worn leather binding before he began.

*"You know how in old tales, there’s always that one moment—something whispered in the dark—that changes everything?"* Elias’s voice was low, the words carrying the weight of years. *"Well, last winter, I was walking through the woods near Blackthorn Hollow, and I heard it. Not the wind, not the crunch of leaves—something else. A child’s voice, high and trembling, calling out, ‘Help me…’"*

He paused, the firelight dancing on the hollows of his face. *"The next morning, I found the girl. She was lying in the snow, her dress torn, her hands clutching a half-buried chest. Inside was nothing but rusted keys and a single, bloodstained letter—addressed to someone named ‘Daughter of the Hollow.’"* Elias exhaled sharply. *"I followed the clues. The keys led to an old lighthouse, the letter to a hidden path behind the ruins of the village. And there, in the ruins’ attic, I found the truth: the Hollow had been a sanctuary for things that shouldn’t exist. But when the girl’s mother—who had been a witch—died, the balance broke. The thing that had been watching her for years emerged. It took her, and in doing so, it whispered to me its name: *Veythari.*"*

Elias’s breath hitched. *"The next night, I found the lighthouse. The keys fit the door, but when I turned them, the wood screamed. The walls pulsed. And then—she was there. Not in the flesh, but in the air. A shape shifting between smoke and shadow. She asked me one question: ‘Why did you come?’ I lied. I said I was here for the girl. She let me leave—but not before pressing the keys into my palm. Said if I ever heard her call again, I’d know what to do."* He swallowed. *"I don’t know if I did."*

The fire popped. *"But tonight, I’m telling you. Because I think she’s coming again."*
