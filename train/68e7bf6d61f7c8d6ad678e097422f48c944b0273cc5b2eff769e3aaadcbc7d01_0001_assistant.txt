A panster writes "by the seat of their pants". They start with a general idea, or maybe even just a character, and just let the story go where their imagination goes. Stephen King, Diana Gabaldon, and George R.R. Martin are great examples of pansters.  A plotter is a writer who outlines the story before they write a word of it. The most famous plotters are J.K. Rowling and James Patterson. Then, there are authors who combine a bit of both, like Agatha Christie. 

As far as what is best, it really depends on both the writer and the story. A panster may write an entire first draft without outlining. A plotter usually outlines everything first - settings, characters, scenes - and then starts writing. The drawback to pantsing is the writer might box themselves into a corner and need to re-write. The drawback to plotting is that as the story continues, the characters begin driving the story and tweaks need to be made. Genre is also a consideration, as it is much easier to pants science fiction than a mystery.
