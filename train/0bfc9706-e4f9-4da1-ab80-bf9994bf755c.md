The briefcase sat unopened on the desk, its contents—printers, staplers, and a single crumpled map—now scattered across the floor. The fluorescent lights hummed as the rain tapped against the window, its rhythm too familiar to ignore. Across the room, the coffee machine hissed, its last drip of bitter brew pooling on the counter.

"Was there a reason you didn’t call?" she asked, her voice dry, fingers tapping the armrest of the chair. His jaw clenched. He exhaled through his nose, the sound sharp. "The meeting’s canceled."

The words hung between them, heavy as the silence that followed. The plan—the one they’d built for weeks, the one that had kept them afloat—was a ghost now. The map had been their compass, but the tide had turned. The data had been compromised. A mistake in the code, a misplaced email, and now everything felt like it had been left to drift.

Outside, the city lights blurred into streaks of color, each one a reminder of how far they’d fallen. The briefcase’s contents lay abandoned, its weight forgotten. The next step was unclear, but the past was already buried.
