Touro Synagogue in Newport, R.I., the only remaining synagogue from the Colonial era, is the first religious building in the select collection of the National Trust for Historic Preservation. Dedicated in 1763, the Georgian building designed by Peter Harrison is the oldest synagogue in America. Designated a National Historic Site in 1946, it now qualifies for promotional, financial and technical support as one of the 21 National Trust sites.
The synagogue, named for two benefactors, Abraham and Judah Touro, is still home to an active Sephardic Orthodox congregation, Jeshuat Israel, with more than 100 families. The building is open to public tours when services are not being held.
Built of brick from England, the two-story building with rounded Palladian windows and a four-sided roof stands at a sharp angle to Touro Street, so that the holy ark containing the Torah faces east, toward Jerusalem. Above the ark is a painting from around 1800 of the tablets bearing the Ten Commandments in Hebrew by the Newport artist Benjamin Howland.
