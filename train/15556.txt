Gravelly or sandy soil typically drains too quickly leaving your flowers and vegetables struggling to find water for healthy growth. The soil may also be difficult to cultivate. Although some plants thrive in gravelly soil, most prefer humus-rich friable soil that drains well, while retaining the necessary moisture for the roots to transport nutrients from the soil. Before you can garden in gravelly soil, you need to add soil amendments to improve the soil structure.
Spread a 2-inch layer of organic matter such as compost or well-rotted manure over the top of the soil. Organic matter helps to bind the fine particles in gravelly soil together so that it can retain water and nutrients necessary for plant growth.
Work the organic matter into the top 8 inches of the soil with a garden tiller or tractor. Small areas can be worked with hand tools. This creates soil with 25 percent organic matter and improves the soil's ability to retain water and nutrients.
Amend the soil regularly with organic matter, typically in the spring and fall, to maintain its structure. It takes time to change the structure of the soil, but each addition of organic matter improves the soil's structure.
Things You Will Need
- Organic matter
- Garden tiller
- The amount of organic matter required for your gravelly soil may differ, as the size and shape of the gravel particles affects the overall structure of the soil. Soil with fine gravel particles that resemble coarse sand is typically easier to amend than heavy gravel. Heavy gravel may take several years of regular amending to reach the desired structure.
- As an alternate to amending gravelly soil, many gardeners choose to create raised beds above the soil or to grow flowers and vegetables in containers.
- Gravelly soil that drains too quickly leaches away nutrients. Moderate watering on a frequent basis works better than traditional deep watering when it comes to gravelly soil.
