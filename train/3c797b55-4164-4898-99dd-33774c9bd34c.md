The clock tower struck midnight as Elias stood at the edge of the old bridge, his breath fogging in the cold winter air. The river below churned with dark water, reflecting the faint glow of distant streetlights. His hands clenched the cold metal railing as he stared at the name carved into the stone beneath him: *"Here Lies the Truth."*

A shadow moved in the alley behind him. He turned just as the figure emerged—a woman with silver-streaked hair, her eyes sharp and calculating. *"You’ve been looking for this,"* she said, holding up a small, rusted key. *"The bridge leads to the vault beneath the church. But you’ll need more than just the key."*

Elias hesitated. The key was all he’d found in the abandoned attic of his late uncle’s house, a place he’d only entered out of curiosity. His fingers trembled as he took the key from her. *"What if I’m not supposed to be here?"*

She smirked. *"Then you’ll find out soon enough."* The wind howled, carrying the distant sound of a church bell tolling once. The moment hung between them, thick with tension. The key was his only chance—and his only choice.

As he stepped onto the bridge, the woman’s voice cut through the silence: *"Don’t forget, Elias. The truth is never as simple as the key."*

The water below rushed past, unyielding. The hinge moment was the choice to turn back—or not. The key was in his pocket. The bridge stretched ahead. The night was endless.
