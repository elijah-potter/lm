In a world where silence spoke louder than words, the old clock tower stood sentinel over the city, its hands frozen midway between midnight and dawn. Every evening, as the sun dipped below the horizon, it would chime the hour—not with the mechanical *ding* of a bell, but with the slow, deliberate *clink* of a single silver coin dropped into its bowl.

The mayor, a man who had ruled this town for forty years, would always listen. He knew the coin’s sound was more than a ritual; it was a whisper from the unseen. *"Another year passes,"* he’d murmur, though no one could hear him. *"Another coin falls."*

One evening, the coin did not fall.

The mayor’s hands trembled. He reached out, fingers brushing the bowl’s edge, and instead of the metallic *clink*, there was only the hollow *thud* of his palm against empty air. The clock struck twelve, and the town held its breath.

The next morning, the mayor found the bowl empty—except for a single, glistening silver coin, now rusted and old, resting at the bottom. It had never been there before.

*"Did you hear it?"* the mayor asked the wind, though the wind only laughed, a sound like distant thunder. *"What happens when the last coin falls?"*
