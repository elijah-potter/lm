Copper is one of our planet’s precious natural resources that is quickly running out. In fact, the Mining, Minerals and Sustainable Development Project reports that copper recycling needs to increase to around 100 percent before the end of the twenty-first century if we are to have the copper necessary for industry and commerce. 

The worldwide demand for copper is growing because of the many new products coming out every year, like PDAs, smartphones, pad computers, laptops, and many other electronics. The circuit boards on many of these devices require copper, so recycling copper wire isn’t just a good idea; it’s imperative.

For these reasons, copper recycling has become a big business. Copper doesn’t break down or disintegrate over time like aluminum and other metals. It retains its good properties for many years and can easily be melted down for use in all sorts of industries. Copper wire is one major area where recycling has really grown in the last few years. 

This is good news for those who have copper wiring they don’t need anymore. You can help the planet out and make some money on the side. Copper wire retains its value, but all copper recyclers are not the same. Some are a bit on the shady side and will use faulty scales that weigh your copper light, so they don’t have to pay the full amount. It’s best to deal with a reputable copper recycler in your area that has a good name. It also helps to know the current copper prices. The more you know, the better your chances of not being taken advantage of.

Figuring the Weight & Value of Copper Wire
First, strip the copper wire so that you remove all surrounding plastic. Regardless of what the copper was previously used for, it may have rubber or plastic around it that was acting as a ground. Once the copper is clean, weigh it. Commercial scales are the most accurate, but a good set of home scales can work.
