The café’s hummed like a shared breath—someone else’s coffee scent lingering on the air, a faint echo of their morning routine. The tablecloth, creased from another group’s lunch, bore the ghost of yesterday’s takeout wrappers. I traced the edge of the empty mug with my thumb, but the warmth was already gone, stolen by the machine’s next cycle.

Outside, the streetlights flickered like static—each one a borrowed pulse, a moment where the city held its breath before exhaling again. I watched the reflection of the neon sign in the puddle at my feet: not mine, not theirs, just another flicker, another pause between what was and what will be. The moment was a ghost in the machine’s hands.
