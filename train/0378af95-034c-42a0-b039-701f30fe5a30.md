A cracked windshield reflected the neon glow of the city at midnight. Rain hammered against the roof, but the raincoat held tight. The hand gripping the steering wheel trembled as the car lurched to a stop beside the abandoned train tracks. The flickering headlights illuminated a crumbling wooden fence, half-swallowed by ivy.

*"You said you’d come,"* she whispered, voice raw from the wind. The other figure stood at the edge of the tracks, arms crossed, silhouette sharp against the dark. The air smelled of damp earth and something metallic—gunpowder? The woman’s fingers curled around the keys, the promise written in her eyes: *"I don’t run."*

The train whistle, distant but unmistakable, cut through the silence. The first car rolled into view, its red brake lights pulsing like a heartbeat. The promise was already fraying.
