The rain had been falling for three days when Elias finally arrived at the old mill. His hands were calloused, his boots damp from the muddy path, but his shoulders were lighter than they had been in weeks.

The mill had been his family’s home for generations, but the last owner had left behind a mess of broken promises and unpaid debts. The place stank of rust and regret. The door creaked open under his weight, revealing a space that had been shuttered for months. Dust swirled in the dim light from the single broken window.

Inside, the floorboards groaned under his feet. The workshop was a disaster—tools scattered like lost children, half-finished projects abandoned where they had fallen. His father’s old workbench was covered in a thick layer of grime, the lathe still there, but silent.

Elias knelt beside it. "Father," he whispered, wiping his brow with the back of his sleeve. The machine was cold, as if it had never been touched again. He traced the worn leather of his father’s favorite tool, a chisel that had been passed down through generations. The weight of it comforted him, though the past few years had been a blur of debt collectors and empty promises.

He stood, adjusting his coat. "I’m sorry," he said, his voice rough. "I should’ve done more."

Outside, the wind howled through the empty mill. Somewhere in the distance, a train whistle echoed, carrying the sound of the world moving on without him. Elias turned back inside, his resolve hardening. He would fix this. One chisel, one broken machine at a time. The mill would be his again.
