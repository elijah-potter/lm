Deep shadows stretched long across cracked pavement, where the last embers of a dying sunset painted the air in hues of sickly orange. A lone figure crouched behind a rusted metal fence, fingers tracing the edge of a half-eaten sandwich wrapped in waxed paper. The crumbs had long since scattered, but the scent of stale bread lingered in the humid, stagnant air.

A distant wail—someone’s child, perhaps—cut through the silence, swallowed by the weight of the night. The figure stood abruptly, coat billowing as they moved toward the sound, boots crunching on glass shards. The streetlights flickered, casting eerie reflections on the pavement. The air smelled of damp earth and something metallic, like old blood or rusted metal.

They reached the corner where the wail came from and found a woman huddled against a neon sign that flickered weakly, its letters half-erased by time. Her hands trembled as she clutched a child’s small hand, eyes wide with fear. The child, bundled in a tattered coat, clung to her skirts, voice a small, broken cry.

The figure didn’t speak. Just stood there, watching the woman’s face twist in a mix of terror and something sharper—guilt, perhaps, or something far older. The neon sign’s glow dimmed, then died entirely, plunging them into darkness. The sound of distant sirens filled the silence.
