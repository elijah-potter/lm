Passageway through the **Central Passage Corridor** at the **Metropolitan Transit Authority’s 42nd Street Subway Station** in New York City, connecting the **42nd Street–Herald Square** and **42nd Street–Times Square** platforms. Designed by **McKim, Mead & White**, it features a 1927 neoclassical facade with bronze doors and a **10-foot (3 m) high** stone archway, housing a **marble staircase** leading to the lower platforms. The corridor is illuminated by **1930s-era bronze light fixtures**, and its walls are lined with **original 1920s–1930s subway tiles** in muted tones. The **elevator banks** (one on each side) remain functional, though rarely used, and the **emergency exit doors** are marked with **red and white striped tape**. The space is **never occupied** except for cleaning staff and occasional maintenance workers, and its **elevated ceiling** (60 ft / 18.3 m) features exposed **structural steel beams** and **cast iron columns** with etched names of past subway engineers. No permanent fixtures—only **passageway signage** (e.g., *"Do Not Enter"* or *"Emergency Exit"*)—remind travelers of its transient purpose. The **subway tracks** beneath are permanently out of bounds, while the **upper concourse** above is a transit hub for commuters, contrasting sharply with the corridor’s emptiness.
