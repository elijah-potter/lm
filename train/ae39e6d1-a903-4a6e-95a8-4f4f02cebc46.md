The weight of the decision pressed against my chest like a stone.
*Should I turn back now?* The path ahead whispered promises—promises of safety, of warmth, of the familiar. But the shadows beyond the next bend pulsed with something darker, something that had been calling me for years. My hands clenched the handlebars tighter; the cold steel bit into my palms. The wind carried the scent of rain and possibility, and for the first time, I didn’t know which was the lie.

The fork in the road stretched before me, one side leading to the old mill where my mother still worked, the other to the forest where I’d hidden my first love’s letter. The sky above was a bruise of twilight, bleeding into the horizon. My breath fogged in the air, and suddenly, everything felt too quiet—too still to move. The future was a map I couldn’t read, and the past, a debt I’d yet to settle.

A voice, soft and distant, murmured: *"You’ve got this."* But the voice belonged to no one I knew. The road ahead was empty. The forest waited. And the question gnawed at my ribs like a rat: *What if I’m wrong?* What if I’m not? The choice was mine alone, and the weight of it felt like a sentence.
