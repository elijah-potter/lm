## Explain Like I'm Five: Why the Sky is Blue

Okay, imagine you have a big box of crayons. Lots and lots of crayons! Some are red, some are yellow, some are blue, and so on. Now, imagine the sun is shining its light – that's like the sun using all those crayons at once, sending out all the colors to us.

Now, the air around us, the air we breathe, is made of tiny, tiny pieces, much smaller than even a grain of sand. We can’t see them, but they're there!  When the sunlight travels to Earth, it bumps into these tiny pieces of air.

Now, blue and violet light (which is a kind of purple) are like bouncy balls. When they bump into those tiny pieces of air, they bounce *everywhere*! They bounce up, down, sideways – all around!  The other colors, like red and yellow, are more like bowling balls. They mostly go straight through.

Because the blue and violet light bounces so much, it spreads out all over the sky. That’s why when you look up, you see blue! It's like the blue crayons got scattered all over the room.

You might wonder, "If violet bounces even *more* than blue, why isn't the sky violet?" That’s a great question! The sun doesn’t send out as much violet light, and our eyes aren't as good at seeing violet compared to blue. So, we mostly see the blue light bouncing around.

Sometimes, when the sun is setting, it looks red or orange. That's because the sunlight has to travel through *more* air to reach us.  All the blue light has already bounced away, so we see the red and orange colors that were traveling straight through all along. It’s like the blue crayons bounced away, and now we can see the red and yellow ones!



Isn't that neat?
