The dim glow of the old lantern flickered as the door creaked open. Elias sat at the worn wooden table, fingers tracing the rim of his empty cup. Across from him, Mara shifted in her chair, her breath shallow, her knuckles white around the handle of her knife. The air hummed with unspoken tension, thick as syrup.

*"You’re leaving,"* Mara’s voice was a whisper, but it wasn’t needed.

Elias didn’t look up. *"Tomorrow."* He lifted the cup, empty again. The words hung between them like smoke—too heavy to say aloud.

Outside, the wind howled through the rusted shutters. The weight of what they couldn’t name settled between them, heavier than any argument ever could.
