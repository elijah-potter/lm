The elderly gardener, though unseen, planted a single rosebush in the cracked sidewalk beside the crumbling bench where children once played. Each morning, the soil was damp from the rain that no one noticed anymore, and the thorns became the first shelter for sparrows during storms. The city’s new urban planners missed the bench’s presence, but the rosebush’s roots—unseen by maps—fused into the cracks beneath a new pavement, a quiet rebellion of resilience. The act wasn’t grand; it was the kind of thing that doesn’t need to be saved, only endured.
