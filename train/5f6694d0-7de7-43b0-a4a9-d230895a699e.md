The wind carried the scent of damp earth through the cracked windows of the old barn, where the last embers of a dying fire flickered against the rusted beams. Inside, Elias sat cross-legged on the damp straw, his fingers tracing the edges of a half-finished sketch—a half-formed figure, its silhouette too vague to recognize. The ink had run black along the edges, smudging into the damp paper like tears.

Outside, the storm had picked up. Rain lashed against the tin roof, turning the slats into a rhythmic drumbeat. Elias didn’t look up. His breath came slow, measured, the way he always did when he was lost in something he couldn’t name.

Then—footsteps. Light, sure, but deliberate. The barn door creaked as it swung inward, revealing a figure in a patched raincoat, their face half-hidden beneath the brim of a battered hat. They carried a lantern, its glow dim but steady, casting long shadows across the floor.

Elias didn’t move.

The figure stopped a few feet away, the lantern’s light catching the hollows in their cheeks. They exhaled a breath of cold air, then reached into their coat pocket, pulling out a single silver coin—clink, clink against the wool—and tossed it onto the straw.

Elias didn’t look up. He didn’t have to. The coin landed in the dust, gleaming faintly in the firelight. The figure tilted their head, studying him, then turned and walked out, the lantern’s light fading into the night.

Elias exhaled slowly, then picked up his sketchpad. The figure was gone. The storm raged on. And somewhere, out there, another door opened.
