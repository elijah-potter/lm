When You Should Not Feed Your Dog Celery
While celery is generally safe for dogs, there are a few instances where it can be harmful. Some potential dangers include:
- Gastrointestinal blockage: Celery can sometimes cause gastrointestinal blockages in dogs. This is particularly true for large pieces of celery.
- Allergic reaction: Some dogs may have an allergic reaction to celery. Symptoms can include vomiting, diarrhea, and skin irritation.
- Salt toxicity: Excessive amounts of salt can be toxic to dogs and can lead to serious health problems.

How Can I Safely Give Celery to My Dog?

- Dog food: Celery can also be incorporated into doggie meals as a way to provide additional nutrients for your buddy, so check labels for any celery ingredients.

Frequently Asked Questions
How Much Celery Can I Give My Dog?
The amount of celery you can give your dog depends on the size and weight of your dog. Generally, it is recommended to give small amounts of celery as a treat or snack. Start by giving a few pieces or strips at a time and see how your pup reacts.
Why Can’t Dogs Eat Celery?
The only reason why dogs would not be able to eat celery is if they have an allergy or intolerance. If you are unsure, it is best to consult with your veterinarian before feeding your pup any new food.
Is Raw or Cooked Celery Better for Dogs?
Raw celery is better for dogs because it contains more nutrients and fiber than cooked celery. Raw celery also has a crunchy texture that can help clean your dog’s teeth.
Can Dogs Eat Carrots and Celery?
Yes, dogs can eat carrots and celery. These two vegetables are both low calorie and contain large amounts of fiber and nutrients. Carrots are also high in beta-carotene which is beneficial to your pup’s vision.
What Vegetables Dogs Cannot Eat?
Dogs should not eat onions, garlic, chives or any other members of the Allium family. They can be poisonous to dogs if ingested in large amounts. Other vegetables that are unsafe for dogs include raw potatoes, mushrooms, green tomatoes and raw rhubarb.
