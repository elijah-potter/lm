The fading echo of a name whispered in a language half-understood, etched into the margins of a notebook long since abandoned. A face, blurred by time, still lingers in the corners of half-closed eyes during the rare moments of lucidity. Objects—faded with age, their surfaces softened by dust—hold silent witness to a past that resists retrieval, yet lingers in fragments: a half-finished sketch, a broken chord on an instrument, the scent of an old book left open to a passage no one reads anymore. The memory isn’t fully gone, but it’s like a ghost in the machine—sometimes visible, sometimes not, always just out of reach. The brain’s attempt to reconstruct it leaves gaps, stitches of uncertainty that feel like a story unfinished.
