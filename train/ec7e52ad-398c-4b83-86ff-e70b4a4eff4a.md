## Municipal Humming Initiative Draws Mixed Reactions in Oakhaven

**By Bartholomew Finch, Metro Affairs Correspondent**

Oakhaven, a city previously known for its prize-winning zucchini and aggressively polite citizenry, has implemented a radical new municipal policy: the Strategic Sonic Resonance Program, or SSRP. The program, launched precisely at 7:00 AM this Tuesday, involves the city’s infrastructure – streetlights, park benches, even the municipal fountain – emitting a carefully calibrated, low-frequency hum. City planners assert the SSRP is designed to optimize civic contentment through subtle vibrational harmonization. Initial reports indicate a noticeable, if somewhat unsettling, ambiance across the urban landscape. 

The genesis of the SSRP stems from the work of Dr. Penelope Quibble, “Chief Vibrational Architect” at the Oakhaven Institute for Applied Serenity. “We’re not simply generating noise,” Dr. Quibble explained during a press conference held, naturally, within a soundproof booth. “We’re creating a resonant field. Think of it as a gentle, constant nudge towards a state of agreeable neutrality. It’s about removing the sharp edges of existence.” The frequency, she added, is “scientifically determined to be most effective in mitigating anxieties associated with late-season maple leaf discoloration.” 

The practical ramifications of the SSRP are already being felt. Local bird populations have reportedly migrated en masse to neighboring towns, citing “unpleasant sonic dissonance.” The Oakhaven Philharmonic Orchestra, renowned for its interpretations of Beethoven, has been forced to temporarily suspend rehearsals. “The vibrations interfere with the delicate interplay of the string section,” lamented Reginald Plumtree, “Principal Harmonizer and Curator of Aural Aesthetics.” “It’s as if the very air is subtly mocking our attempts at artistic expression.” More concerning, however, are the reports of increased synchronized swaying among pedestrians, a phenomenon described by Mildred Abernathy, “Director of Postural Equilibrium,” as “a fascinating, if somewhat alarming, display of collective responsiveness.”

The economic impact is also becoming apparent. Sales of earplugs have skyrocketed, while the local fidget spinner industry is facing a catastrophic decline. "People simply aren't feeling the need to fidget," stated Barnaby Butterfield, “Minister of Kinetic Diversion.” "It's quite disheartening, frankly. My projections for Q3 are now…untenable.” Meanwhile, the city's renowned zucchini crop, traditionally the cornerstone of Oakhaven’s agricultural identity, is exhibiting signs of premature ripening, prompting speculation that the vibrational field is accelerating the plant’s metabolic processes.

The Oakhaven City Council remains steadfast in its commitment to the SSRP. “We believe in the science,” declared Mayor Beatrice Bumble, “Guardian of Civic Resonance.” “A contented populace is a productive populace. And if a little synchronized swaying is the price we pay for that contentment, then I say, let the city sway!” The council is currently considering expanding the program to include the city’s dog park, with preliminary discussions centering on the optimal frequency for canine tranquility.
