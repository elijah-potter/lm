What do Volunteers do in charities?
Volunteers are the core of every operation done by a charity. Volunteering in a charity event means helping increase the quality of services or programs a charity provides.
If you think that volunteering is often disregarded and takes no account for their effort and effectiveness. Well, you’re wrong! Here are some facts about volunteers:
- Volunteers cut the cost of paying a full-time employee for the charity.
- Volunteers may have specialized skills that in-house staff may not have.
- Some charities can’t operate without volunteers.
- 90% of the charities have no paid staff and are run by volunteers.
- Some volunteers are willing to work even on holidays.
To answer the question, what do volunteers do in charities?
Formal volunteer labor, as you might assume, is varied in terms of abilities, ranging from simple, repetitive jobs to highly sophisticated ones requiring decision-making. To put it another way, formal volunteer work is quite comparable to paid work, with the same range of tasks and ‘hierarchies’ between skilled and unskilled people.
You might be wondering why volunteers are eager to work for nothing in return. Here are some reasons why volunteering is important, and hopefully they will persuade you to participate:
- Making new friends and getting a team with the same interests
- Develop social and practical skills
- Fulfillment and fun
- Volunteering can give you a healthy boost to your self-confidence, self-esteem, and life satisfaction.
They help without asking in return
All volunteers are highly prized by their organizations and they are incredibly grateful for the work that is undertaken. Being a non-paid member of the community and a truly reliable source of the workforce in every charitable organization, volunteers are truly the heroes of community service.
Remember that the most valuable skills you can bring to any volunteer effort are compassion, an open mind, a willingness to do whatever is needed, and a positive attitude. Why not step up to the challenge?
