In the dim glow of flickering streetlights, Elias sat on the rooftop of the abandoned bookstore, his fingers tracing the worn edges of an old book. The city hummed below—car horns, distant laughter, the occasional siren—but he had chosen silence.

A gust of wind carried the scent of rain and old paper. The city’s chatter faded into the background, replaced by the rhythmic *thump* of his breath against the cold metal railing. His phone lay abandoned on the ground, its screen dark. The last message from his sister still lingered in his mind: *"You don’t have to be here. You don’t have to do anything."* He had.

The book in his hands was a gift from his mother—*"For when the world feels too loud."* He opened it at random, a passage from a poem by a poet he’d never read. The words blurred into his thoughts: *"The quiet is not empty. It is the space where you learn to listen."*

His phone buzzed. He ignored it. The city’s noise still existed, but he had chosen silence. He closed the book, let the pages whisper against his palm, and leaned forward until his forehead touched the cold metal. The world outside was a storm of sound, but inside, there was only the stillness of his own breath.
