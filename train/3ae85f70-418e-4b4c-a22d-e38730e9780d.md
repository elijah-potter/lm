The library was silent except for the distant murmur of a clock striking midnight. Elias sat on the cracked edge of his father’s old chair, fingers tracing the grooves in the wood like a map of uncharted territories. Outside, the city’s glow flickered through the stained-glass windows, painting the room in hues of rust and gold.

His mother’s letters lay scattered across the desk—pages torn, ink smudged where she had cried herself out. *"You don’t understand, Elias. Some doors are not meant to be opened."* The last one, sealed with wax, bore her name and a date that felt like a warning: *June 12th.*

The air smelled of damp earth and old paper. Elias exhaled slowly, the breath curling into nothingness. What if he had never found that book in the attic? What if the name on the cover had been a lie, a shadow cast over a truth too heavy to bear?

He reached for the letter again, but his hand hovered. The words blurred—*You are the key. But who holds the lock?* The question hung between them, a ghost in the room, waiting for him to answer.
