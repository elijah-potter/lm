The old clock tower creaked as twilight painted the cobblestones in hues of violet and gold. Raindrops, slow at first, then relentless, carved their way through the damp air, turning the street into a mirror of forgotten dreams. The scent of damp earth and copper lingered, a quiet promise of something stirring beneath the city’s restless pulse.

The key in the lock turned with a groan, and the door swung open. Inside, the air was thick with the scent of old paper and unlit candles, their wax dripping onto the worn wooden floor like tears. The room was a shrine of half-finished projects—sketches of faces half-erased, a half-built model of a city skyline, and a single, trembling handwritten note tucked into the pocket of a chair where it had fallen.

She stood at the threshold, her breath fogging the glass of the single window, which overlooked the river, its surface shimmering under the last rays of the setting sun. The clock tower’s chime echoed like a heartbeat, counting down to the moment she would step into the room that had been waiting for her. The door closed behind her with a final, deliberate click.
