The **once-and-forgotten rule** was a simple directive in the old system: *"Never open the third drawer in the bottom cabinet."*

It was enforced for a single incident—when a security breach exposed classified files from a hidden compartment in drawer three. The rule was implemented immediately, but enforcement lapsed after the crisis passed. No one documented why it was there, or how it was discovered. Over time, the rule became a ghost in the system, neither codified nor challenged.

No one remembered the reason behind it. The cabinets were redesigned, the files were reclassified, and the rule faded into obscurity. It’s now buried in the archives alongside other forgotten directives, waiting to be rediscovered—or, more likely, ignored.
