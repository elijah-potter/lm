The clock tower struck midnight, its chimes ringing out like the last breath of an old world. Inside the abandoned library, Elias crouched by the cracked desk, fingers tracing the edge of the half-eaten book—*The Alchemy of Shadows*—his breath fogging the air around him. The book had been his only link to the past, but the pages were crumbling, the ink faded to a whisper of memory.

A rustle came from the floorboards. Something had moved.

The old oak door creaked open, revealing a figure wrapped in tattered cloak. The light from the moon spilled through the broken windows, casting long shadows. The stranger’s face was gaunt, lined with age, but their eyes burned with something far older than mortality. "You’ve been waiting for this, haven’t you?" the stranger whispered, voice like dry leaves skittering across stone.

Elias’ grip tightened on the book. "I’ve spent my life searching for it. What is it you want?"

The stranger reached into their coat, pulling out a small, polished metal hinge. It glinted under the moonlight, its surface etched with strange, spiraling symbols. "The hinge. The key to the door you’ve been trying to open." Their fingers trembled, not with fear, but with something colder. "And you’ve spent your life trying to open the wrong door."

Elias hesitated. The hinge in his hand was different—smaller, simpler. A child’s toy, really. But the weight of the moment pressed down on him. He reached out, fingers trembling, and pressed it into the stranger’s palm. The moment their skin touched, the air itself seemed to shift. The symbols on the hinge flared to life, casting eerie shadows across the room.

The stranger’s eyes widened. Then, slowly, they smiled. "Now we begin." The library doors behind them groaned open, revealing a corridor that stretched into darkness—one that had never existed before. The hinge in Elias’ hand burned, and for the first time in his life, he understood: he wasn’t just holding a door open. He was stepping into the hinge itself.
