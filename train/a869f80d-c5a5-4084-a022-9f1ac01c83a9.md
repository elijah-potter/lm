A **quantum dot metamaterial** in a vacuum chamber at cryogenic temperatures (~4K) exhibits **spontaneous emission suppression**—photons trapped in confined cavities emit less due to **quantum interference** ( Purcell effect suppression).

At room temperature, the same structure shows **superradiant scattering** where photon bunches (mode-locked lasing) emit **directional, coherent light pulses** with **time-reversed phase correlations**—a **non-classical interference pattern** that behaves like a **quantum vortex**.

In a **metamaterial slab with plasmonic resonators**, **hyperlensing** causes light to **bend around sharp corners** (e.g., a 90° turn) via **surface plasmon polariton (SPP) tunneling**, producing **unconventional diffraction** that violates Snell’s law. The output can be modeled as a **phase-only filter** with **negative refraction** at certain frequencies.
