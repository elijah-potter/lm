In a crowded room of strangers, their body hums with an unspoken tension—each step a silent question: *"Do I belong here?"*
The fluorescent lights cast long shadows across the faces of the strangers around them, while their own reflection in the polished metal of the elevator feels distorted, like a glitch in a mirror. The air smells of stale coffee and synthetic perfume, a mix of desperation and performative ease. Their mind races through the litany of things they *should* know: the unspoken rules of this place, the unspoken expectations of the people around them. They’ve forgotten how to read the room.

A chair scrapes against the floor behind them, a sound so mundane it’s almost cruel. They glance over their shoulder, but the stranger is already gone, swallowed by the throng. Their fingers tighten around the sleeve of their jacket, nails digging into the fabric like they’re trying to anchor themselves to something solid. They’ve spent years pretending they fit, but here—now—it’s all too real.

The door hisses open. The moment they step outside, the world tilts again. The noise outside is different: the distant hum of traffic, the laughter of people who don’t pretend to be anything but themselves. A child’s voice cries, sharp and unfiltered, and it’s like the sound of a key turning in a rusted lock. They exhale, slow and deliberate, as if exhaling the years they’ve spent holding their breath.
