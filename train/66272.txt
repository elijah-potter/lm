What is ultrasound?
It is used to describe sound waves as in “ultrasound,” which exceed frequencies the human ear can detect. Typically, the human ear can hear frequencies between 20 hertz and 20,000 hertz (1 hertz is one cycle per second). Ultrasound, used for imaging or treatment, can be used to analyze or treat medical conditions. It applies sound pressure cyclically that oscillates at frequencies higher than those detected by the human ear, namely 20,000 hertz, hence the name ultrasonic.

Use of ultrasound:
Ultrasound machines can be used on many objects, usually to penetrate them and evaluate the reflections that have occurred or to provide focused energy. The ultrasonic reflection signature can describe the internal structure of the rack in detail. The most well-known test performed on ultrasound devices is the obstetric ultrasound examination, fetal imaging. While many people only associate this applied science with obstetrics, ultrasound technology has many other uses. Today, daytime ultrasounds are performed in small offices and private clinics. Over the past decade, many endocrinologists and otolaryngologists have switched to performing ultrasound exams from the comfort of their offices.
The identification and diagnosis of many diseases using ultrasound imaging have significantly reduced mortality from various diseases and conditions. Medical diagnosis, which is essential in treating disease, is one of the main drivers of improved overall healthcare outcomes. Ultrasound devices have brought a huge step forward in medical diagnosis. The ultrasound machine can visualize problems in the patient’s organs and other tissues by bouncing ultrasound waves off them and using the computer to track multiple points received and represent these traces as images on the screen or print them on thermal paper.
