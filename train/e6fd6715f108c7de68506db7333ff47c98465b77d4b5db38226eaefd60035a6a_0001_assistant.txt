Poke is a Hawaiian word that means, to slice or cut crosswise into pieces. Poke originated with fishermen using the pieces cut off from their catch and eaten as a snack. This form of a fish snack became wildly popular in the 1970s. It was originally served with salt, seaweed, and roasted candlenut mean. This form is still eaten to this day. Modern versions of poke served in Hawaii include soy sauce, furikake, chili pepper, roe and wasabi. People often substitute Salmon or Tuna with Octopus or other various types of shellfish. Variations of the original dish may include ponzu sauce, teriyaki, sriracha, mayonnaise, avocado, onions, pickled jalapeno, edamame, and cucumber. Ahi tuna is the most popular fish in the dish followed by Yellowtail and Salmon.
