The shadows stretch long and jagged at dawn, casting jagged peaks of light against the ashen walls of an abandoned fortress. The stone is damp, its surface etched with ancient carvings that glow faintly under the dim light—some figures clutching weapons, others kneeling in silent prayer. The shadows here are not smooth; they cling to the battlements like living things, twisting into the shapes of half-eroded armor, half-buried corpses, and the ghostly outlines of a city that once was. The longer shadows belong to the deep ravines below, where the wind carries whispers of voices drowned in time. At dusk, they thicken, merging into a single, spectral pool where the air hums with static. The light never quite touches the floor—only the edges of shadows ever do.
