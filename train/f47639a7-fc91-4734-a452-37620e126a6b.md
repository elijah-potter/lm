The detective arrived at the crime scene to find the victim’s last words scribbled in blood on the wall: *"She was never a thief."* The prosecution’s case hinged on the stolen necklace, but the witness’s testimony contradicted the alibi—she claimed to have seen the victim with the necklace *before* the murder.

The jury’s verdict was a split decision: the defense argued the necklace was planted to frame her, while the prosecution insisted the victim’s confession proved guilt. The prosecution’s witness recanted under pressure, revealing the necklace was stolen from the victim’s own boutique hours before. The victim’s alibi was a lie—she’d been in a secret meeting with the necklace’s true owner, the corrupt mayor.

The trial collapsed into chaos when the mayor’s body was found in the river, drowned in the same night. The detective’s final report concluded: the truth was buried in the inconsistencies, and the misunderstanding—about the necklace, the alibi, and the mayor’s motive—was the only thing that kept them from realizing the victim’s death was an accident. The real killer was still out there, watching.
