The old mill stood abandoned on the edge of the village, its timbers creaking under the weight of forgotten years. The wind howled through the broken windows, carrying with it the scent of damp wood and rusted machinery.

Elias had always found comfort in the place—its eerie silence, the way the light filtered through the cracks in the ceiling like ghostly fingers. One stormy evening, as he sat on the rickety bench inside, he noticed something strange: a faint, rhythmic tapping on the floorboards.

Curious, he stepped closer. The sound grew louder, insistent. He followed it to the far corner where a small, half-buried box lay beneath a pile of dust. Carefully, he pried it open. Inside was a single, golden key.

The key fit into the lock of the mill’s old clock tower—a mechanism Elias had never seen before. He tried it. The gears whirred. The clock hands began to turn, though they stopped at 11:59 PM.

That night, the village woke to find the mill’s clock striking midnight. But the hands had stopped at 12:00. No one noticed the discrepancy until the next morning, when Elias returned and realized the clock had already passed midnight. The time had skipped backward.

By noon, the mill’s interior had shifted—walls had rearranged themselves, furniture moved without reason. The golden key glowed faintly, pulsing like a heartbeat.

Elias didn’t know what to do. The clock tower’s mechanism had altered reality itself.

The next morning, the village found the clock still ticking backward. Then the first child vanished, their shoes left neatly lined up in the hallway.

The mistake had spread.
