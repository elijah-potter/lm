In the cramped attic above the abandoned clock tower, Elias found a sealed envelope tucked beneath a loose floorboard. The wax seal bore the faded insignia of a 19th-century railway company: *"The Silver Arrow Express."* Inside was a single sheet of paper, ink faded with time:

*"Meet me at the Whispering Mill, midnight. Bring the key from the box marked 'X.' The river changes at dusk—find the stone that hums. If you’ve read this, you’re already late."*

Elias traced the cramped script with trembling fingers. Below, the skeletal remains of the mill’s machinery groaned, its gears rusted as if frozen in a forgotten moment. The key—worn but unbroken—lay half-buried in the cobblestones outside, half-sunk in a pool of stagnant water. He pocketed it as the wind howled through the broken windows, the tower’s old clock hands tick-tocking like a dying pulse. Midnight arrived, and the river’s surface shimmered unnaturally, reflecting not the stars but a shifting, liquid mirror. At the edge stood a woman in a tattered coat, her back to Elias, her voice a whisper: *"You’ve got three minutes."* She turned then, and her reflection in the water was wrong—her eyes were two separate orbs, flickering like fireflies in the dark. The stone hummed beneath her feet, vibrating in response to Elias’s presence. She held out a hand, and the key in it was not the one he held. *"This is a trap,"* she murmured. *"But you’ll never see it."* The last thing Elias saw was the river’s surface rippling, the water itself parting like a curtain to reveal something beneath: a black box, warm to the touch. The woman’s voice echoed: *"Now you know why."* The attic door slammed shut behind him.
