The dim glow of a flickering lamp cast long shadows across the cracked vinyl floor of the abandoned bookstore. Elias sat cross-legged on the worn leather couch, fingers tracing the spine of an old hardcover novel he hadn’t touched in months. The air smelled of aged paper and damp wood. Outside, the city hummed—a distant symphony of sirens, voices, the occasional car horn—but it was quiet in here, suffocating in its own way.

He exhaled slowly, watching steam rise from his lips like a ghost’s whisper. The screen on his wrist glowed faintly, displaying the time: 11:47 PM. He hadn’t checked it in hours. The last message from his sister, *You always say the wrong thing*, had burned itself into his memory like a brand. She’d been right—words had been his undoing. Now, they were just echoes.

His phone lay abandoned on the table beside him, silent. No notifications. No reminders. Just the sound of his breath, the creak of the floorboards, the occasional rustle of leaves outside the window.

He closed his eyes. The silence wasn’t empty. It was warm. It was alive. It was the only thing that had ever stayed with him when everything else had crumbled.

He reached for the book again, turning the pages until he found the one with the blank margins, the one he’d written in half a dozen attempts before giving up. His hand trembled slightly as he dipped the tip of his pen into the inkwell. The words were there, waiting. He didn’t know what they meant. He didn’t need to.

He let the pen hover over the page, and for the first time in years, he didn’t write. He let silence take the place of ink, the place of words. The room held its breath. The city kept its distance. And Elias? He was the only one who heard it.
