The rain hammered against the window like a thousand restless hands, each drop carrying the weight of unspoken fears. I sat cross-legged on the worn edge of my bed, fingers tracing the grooves of the old wooden floor. My phone buzzed—another missed call from *her*. The name alone made my throat tighten. I should’ve called back. I *knew* I should’ve.

The fridge hummed, its doors ajar like a wound that refused to close. Inside, the milk had turned to sludge, the bread was dusted with gray, and the coffee—if I was lucky—would taste like regret. I grabbed a crumbling sandwich from the fridge, the plastic wrapping peeling in my grip. The last time I’d eaten like this, I’d been younger. Younger and foolish enough to think I could fix this by myself.

My phone buzzed again—this time, a text from *her*:
*"I need you to stop ignoring me."*

I exhaled sharply, the sound caught in my chest. The words *her* had been a promise once. A shield. Now they were a knife in the dark.

I leaned back, staring at the ceiling where the paint had long since peeled away. The ceiling was a map of my own damnation—each crack a new way I’d fallen. What if I never got back up? What if I was already too broken to fix?

The door creaked open. My mother stood there, her face a mask of exhaustion and something else—something like the ghost of what she used to be. She didn’t say anything. She just handed me a coffee, the steam curling around my fingers like a ghost of her own.

I took a sip. The bitter, black liquid was the closest thing I had to her voice right now.

*"You’re not alone,"* she said, her voice barely audible over the drip of the faucet.

I should’ve believed her. But how could I, when the weight of everything felt like it was pressing down on my ribs, squeezing the air from my lungs?
