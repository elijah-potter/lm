When the shipwreck happened, I did what my parents told me to do and jumped onto a life raft. I was the only one on the same raft as me, and I wasn’t sure where my family or friends had gone. I had no idea how to get a life raft across the ocean, so I sat there with my life vest on and let the waves carry me around. The sun was hot, but the breeze was cool enough that I wasn’t sweaty. Luckily, I still had some water to drink and some snacks in my bag.

Soon, I began to see land in the distance. Luckily, the tides brought my raft closer to the shore with each gentle wave. As I got closer, I could tell I was not approaching the coast, but a small island. Eventually, I got close enough to where my raft would no longer float in the shallow water. I hopped off into the shin-deep waves and hauled the raft up onto the shore so it wouldn’t float away.

I immediately began to look for food. This island appeared to have lots of jungle, so maybe if I looked around, I would find a fruit tree or something similar. When I had only been searching for about half an hour, I began to smell something incredibly sweet. Strangely, the smell I noticed didn’t smell much like fruit, but more like fresh baked goods or candies.

Soon, I stumbled across what I would later name the Cove of Icing. This small cove on the opposite side of the island from where I landed had trees of all kinds that were covered in candies, cookies, cakes, and taffy. Each plant and flower was covered with a different type of sweet. This cove had everything sugary that you could ever imagine wanting to eat, from Pop-Tarts to ice cream and even assorted jelly beans.
