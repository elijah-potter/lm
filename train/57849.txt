Chemistry expert and showman CodyDon Reeder is no stranger to working with hazardous materials, like filling an isolated toilet with mercury to see how it flushes. He's also no stranger to taking care—a blood test after his little mercury project showed he was no worse for the wear.
Even with that in mind, it's more than a little stressful to watch him pour some actual, deadly sodium cyanide into a glass of water and then drink it. He's fine, of course, due to the low dosage of the poison, but it's still a nail-biter of a watch.
The point here, other than pulling a eye-popping stunt, is to illustrate that chemicals aren't toxic in and of themselves, but rather as a function of the dosage. Too much water, for instance, can kill you even if you really have to go out of your way to ingest that much. Caffeine, on the other hand, is both common and pretty easy to OD on if you have it in powder form.
But while sodium cyanide is a potent and deadly chemical for sure, there's still a minimum amount before it's a danger to your life. That amount is closure to a capsule-full than it is to a mere sprinkle diluted in a glass of water. (Still, obviously, don't drink cyanide water. Please)
As Cody notes, cyanide was a key ingredient in Zyklon B gas, used by Nazis in concentration camps during World War II. But it was also notoriously used in pill form by spies of various nationalities who wished to die before they could be interrogated, as well as by many senior Nazi officials as the Allies closed in in 1945.
Like any chemical or tool or atomic reaction, cyanide isn't inherently evil or deadly in all circumstances, though it certainly is if there's too much of it and/or if it's too close to your fleshparts. Still, please please please please don't try this at home. Or anywhere else. Because it also pays to be careful.
