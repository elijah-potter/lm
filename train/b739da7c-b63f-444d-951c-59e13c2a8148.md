The first light of dawn cast a pale hue over the crumbling walls of an old library, where the scent of aged paper and damp earth lingered like an echo of forgotten lives. You had come here not for knowledge, but for the quiet, the way time slowed when the world outside was a blur of noise and obligations. The library was a sanctuary, a place where the weight of the past could be momentarily lifted.

You found the book in the dimly lit corner behind the dust-covered stacks—a leather-bound volume titled *The Weight of Names*, its spine cracked from years of handling. The pages inside were filled with poetry, each line a confession, a whispered regret. The moment you picked it up, you realized you had no right to read it. It wasn’t yours to uncover, not when it belonged to someone else.

Your fingers trembled as you turned the pages, the ink still smudged with tears. The last entry was dated three years ago, written in a hand you recognized: your younger self’s. *"I should have said it. I should have stopped the lies."* The words felt like a knife pressed against your ribs, sharp and immediate. You had let your pride, your fear of being seen as weak, dictate your actions. You had chosen silence over honesty, and the cost had been paid in silence itself.

A knock at the door shattered the moment. Your name was called, and you knew—you had to leave. The library doors creaked open, revealing the morning’s first light and the familiar hum of the city beyond. You stuffed the book into your coat, the weight of it a reminder of the regret you would never speak aloud.

As you stepped into the cold, the weight of what you had missed felt like a physical presence, clinging to you. You hurried down the street, the book tucked away, but the regret lingered in the hollow between your shoulders, unspoken and unburied. The city moved on, indifferent. You would carry this secret with you, a quiet sorrow that no one would ever see.
