The clock ticked relentlessly as the steam hissed from the kettle, filling the small kitchen with a metallic tang. The kettle—an old, rusted relic from a previous life—had been left to boil since 7:00 AM. Each drop of water that evaporated stretched the silence into something thicker, like the pause before a punchline. The microwave timer beeped at 3:24 PM, but the minutes had been measured in seconds, each one a slow unraveling of time itself.

Outside, the wind howled through the trees, its voice muffled by the rain that had fallen since dawn. The windows were fogged, a half-formed barrier between the world inside and the one outside. The tea, still hot, sat untouched, its surface rippling like a lake in a storm. The mind, caught in this loop of expectation, had learned to exist in the space between the question and its answer. Time had become a weight, pressing down from all sides.
