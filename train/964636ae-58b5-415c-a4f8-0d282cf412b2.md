A skeletal concrete foundation juts from the earth, half-cemented into the ground, cracks spiderwebbed across its surface. Overgrown weeds and ivy cling to the rough edges, bending toward the sky like twisted fingers. Rust stains the steel rebar protruding from the broken slabs, some still half-buried. The exposed floor tiles, cracked and warped, are scattered like broken glass. Half-finished windows frame empty voids where glass once hung, their frames painted a faded, peeling green. A stairwell spirals upward but only halfway—halfway to the unfinished ceiling, which is a canvas of exposed joists and drywall still waiting for paint. Dust swirls in the air, trapped between the layers of abandonment. The scent of damp wood and old paint lingers, a mix of forgotten projects and forgotten time. The air hums with the quiet buzz of insects and the distant murmur of voices, either echoes from the past or whispers of those who never finished here.
