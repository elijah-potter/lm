**The Clockmaker’s Apprentice**

The city of Eldermere was built around a single, ticking tower that stood atop a mountain of rusted gears. Every night, the tower’s pendulum cast a shadow on the cobblestones below, marking the hours for those who dared climb it. Elias, a twelve-year-old with ink-stained fingers and a heart heavier than his worn leather satchel, had been tasked by his master, Old Man Harkin, with retrieving the last key from the tower’s heart.

The key was hidden inside the tower’s mechanism—a vault lined with whispers of forgotten languages. The door itself was a puzzle of interlocking gears and dials, each labeled with symbols Elias didn’t yet understand.

“You’ll need this,” Harkin had said, pressing a small, tarnished key into Elias’s palm. “The one that fits the lock beneath the clock’s face.”

Elias had followed the path up the mountain, past the ruins of the old observatory where astronomers had once measured the stars. The tower loomed ahead, its brass and iron face gleaming under the moon. At its base, a set of ancient stairs spiraled upward, their steps worn smooth by centuries of footsteps. At the top, a circular platform held a single, massive key, its teeth sharp enough to cut glass.

Below, the shadows stretched long and sinister. The clock’s hands moved in slow, deliberate motion, their ticking a drumbeat in Elias’s skull. He could hear them now—the distant *tick-tock* of the tower’s heart, the whisper of wind through the broken windows, and the distant laugh of the city’s guards, who had once been his friends.

A voice, smooth as aged parchment, called out from the darkness:

*“Turn left.”*

Elias hesitated. The key was heavy in his grip, its weight a promise and a curse. The options before him were clear:

1. **Climb the stairs to the platform**, where the key waited—but the path was slick with oil, and the gears below groaned like living things.
2. **Descend into the clock’s face**, where the dials pulsed with an eerie green light, their numbers shifting without cause.
3. **Press the small key into the tower’s mouth**, but the tower’s face was sealed with a lock that hummed with static.
4. **Run**, but the mountain’s slopes were too steep, and the guards would find him before he reached the base.

The clock’s hands stopped.

Elias’s breath hitched.

The tower’s mouth opened, revealing a mouthful of gears and teeth.

What will you do?
