I believe the aircraft you saw is the Scaled Composites Model 281 “Proteus.” It is a one-of-a-kind plane designed by world-renowned aeronautical engineer, Burt Rutan.

The Proteus first flew in 1998. Its intended purpose was to carry high-altitude, long-operation (HALO) telecommunication equipment. Essentially, it was supposed to be an alternative to orbital satellites. The long, thin wings and extremely lightweight airframe were designed for long-lasting missions at high altitudes. The plane can loiter for around 14 hours, so the engineers also built in a capability for it to be operated remotely or even sent off independently on preprogrammed missions. At the time, such capabilities were rare, especially in civil aviation.

Th HALO telecom project did not pan out, but the single completed Proteus was successful, so Scaled Composites began contracting it with NASA, the Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency (DARPA), the US Department of Defense, and private companies. The Proteus is one of the only aircraft on earth that can carry an external payload up to 60,000 feet altitude, so it is a very valuable research tool.
