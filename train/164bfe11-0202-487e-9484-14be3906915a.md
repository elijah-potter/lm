## TIL the “singing sands” phenomenon is likely caused by sand grain shape, not wind.

Today I learned that the mysterious "singing sands" – beaches where the sand emits a loud, musical tone when disturbed – are probably not caused by the wind's interaction with the sand, as previously believed. For decades, the prevailing theory attributed the sound to the wind vibrating across dunes composed of uniform grains. However, recent research, involving detailed acoustic analysis and laboratory experiments, suggests the sound originates from the shape of the sand grains themselves.

Specifically, the grains are typically elongated and rounded, with a specific ratio of length to width. When these grains rub against each other during movement – whether from footsteps, a landslide, or other disturbances – the friction creates a resonant vibration. This vibration, amplified by the surrounding sand, produces the characteristic singing sound.

The phenomenon is found in various locations globally, including beaches in Wales, the Arabian Peninsula, and the Namib Desert. While wind undoubtedly plays a role in moving the sand, it appears to be the unique morphology of the grains, rather than the wind’s action, that is the primary source of the audible tones. It’s a fascinating example of how our understanding of natural phenomena can evolve with improved scientific investigation.
