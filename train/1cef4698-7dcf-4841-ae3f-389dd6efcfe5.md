The grand hall of the Clock Tower was silent except for the whisper of wind through the broken stained glass. Every hour, the tower’s pendulum would still, as if holding its breath, before its weight would drag the hands forward in a slow, deliberate dance. At midnight, the last stroke of the bell would shatter the illusion, and the real time would begin again—unbroken, relentless, and inescapable.

In the cellar beneath, Elias crouched beside the rusted mechanism of a pocket watch, its gears still turning in the dark. He had spent years dismantling and reassembling it, not out of curiosity, but because he knew the moment it stopped—*he* would stop too. The watch had been a gift from his father, who had died in the same hour the watch had stopped, its second hand frozen at 11:59.

Elias pressed a finger to the glass. The numbers were blurred, but he could see the ticking still—just like his own heartbeat. The door creaked open. A woman stepped inside, her shadow stretching unnaturally long across the floor.

"Time doesn’t stop for anyone," she said, her voice like gravel. "But it doesn’t have to be cruel." She reached into her coat and pulled out a new watch, its face smooth and unblemished. "This one won’t let you stop."

She held it out. Elias took it. The moment he did, the second hand on his father’s watch snapped forward, the gears grinding violently. The woman smiled. "The clock never lies. But it doesn’t have to judge."

The tower’s bell tolled twelve times. The watch in Elias’s palm began to tick again.
