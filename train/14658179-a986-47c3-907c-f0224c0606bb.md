Alarms buzzed in vain before the sun rose past noon, casting an unnatural golden glow through the curtains. The coffee machine gurgled as it heated, steam curling lazily into the morning air. The first sip was bitter, the second weak—someone had already drunk half the pot.

The laptop screen flickered to life, a half-finished task list still cluttering the desktop. A single, unread email from yesterday’s meeting lingered in the inbox: *"Follow-up on Q3 budget adjustments."* The calendar was a chaotic mess—two meetings in one day, both starting at 9:00 AM, but with no buffer.

A text from the office group chat popped up: *"Late start?"* The reply was immediate: *"Worth it."* The answer was true, but not in the way it sounded.

By midday, the first client call had begun, the phone ringing like a distant alarm. The call ended with a rushed *"Let’s talk tomorrow"* instead of the agreed-upon *"See you at 3."* The afternoon stretched into evening, meetings rearranged, emails sent back, and the office door closing behind them as the sun dipped below the horizon—still not quite dark, but the kind of twilight that made time feel like it had been stretched too thin.

The kitchen clock read 11:17 PM. The first proper meal was a microwave meal, reheated from a box left in the freezer. The TV played a rerun of a show they’d seen a dozen times, the hum of the fridge the only sound in the quiet apartment.

As they lay in bed, the phone buzzed again—another late-night email: *"Can we reschedule the review for tomorrow morning?"* The reply was typed quickly, the night still catching up.
