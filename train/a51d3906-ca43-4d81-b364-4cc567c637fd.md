The first snowfall on the rooftop of the abandoned church, its crunch sharp underfoot as the wind carried the scent of pine and damp earth. The clock struck midnight; the old grandfather clock in the hall had been silent for years. The lantern flickered, casting long shadows across the cracked floorboards. She sat in the attic window, her breath visible in the cold air, and for the first time in decades, she laughed—not the bitter sound of memory, but the carefree exhale of a child. Outside, a single bird, startled by the sudden stillness, took flight in a spiral of feathers. The moment was fleeting, a whisper of warmth in the frozen silence, but it lingered like a half-remembered dream.
