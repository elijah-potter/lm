When the first inkblot on the table shifted into a dragon’s snout, I realized plans were just sketches—intended to be followed, but always prone to the chaos of human hands.

That afternoon, I’d spent three hours mapping out the escape route for our little operation: the hidden stash, the diversion, the signal. The map was precise, the numbers cold. But then the radio crackled—static, then a voice: *"You’re not alone."* The plan dissolved into a story as we sprinted through the alley, the flashlight beam bouncing off walls that had suddenly become a labyrinth of shadows and whispers. The dragon’s snout wasn’t a detail; it was a warning. The stash wasn’t a location; it was a memory of the girl who’d left her name behind in a café napkin, scribbled across the back of a receipt. The signal wasn’t a code; it was the sound of her laughter, half a block away, calling our names. Now, every step felt like an act of storytelling—each choice a new chapter, each risk a twist. The map was still there, but the story was what we made of it.
