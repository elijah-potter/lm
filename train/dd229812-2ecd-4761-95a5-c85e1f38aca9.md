To the silent ear that hears me,
who stands as far as you can stand
from these words yet still feels their weight,
I write not for fame nor fame’s hollow name—
only for the quiet, the unspoken thread
that binds us, though unseen, though unheard,
as if the dark itself were a language
you learn by listening to the spaces between
what you think you know and what you don’t.

If you read these lines, perhaps you’re like the others:
a shadow in a crowd, a name unspoken,
a question left unasked, a door left ajar.
I do not claim to understand what you are,
nor do I ask to be understood.
But if the words here find you, let them linger
as dust does in the wind—sometimes it settles,
sometimes it drifts away, but always it is there,
a ghost of something you might have said,
or could say yet.

I write not to convince, but to remember:
that silence is not absence, but presence,
a language of its own, one we learn
by watching the hands of others move,
by reading the cracks in the walls we build,
by listening to the hum of the unseen.

If you read this, perhaps you are the question
we all carry but dare not ask aloud.
I leave it here, then, as a gift:
not answers, but the space between them.
Let it be a door. Open it.
Let it be a door you never close.
