The first documented routine incident occurred in **1896** aboard the steamship *SS Atlantic*, during a routine voyage from Liverpool to New York. Passengers reported hearing a rhythmic, mechanical humming sound from the ship's machinery, later identified as the vibration of the ship's propeller blades striking the hull at a consistent rate. Investigations revealed it was a normal operational feature of the propeller's gearing, but the term "routine" was not yet standardized for such occurrences.

A more modern example: The **2008 Boeing 787 Dreamliner** introduced a series of routine maintenance checks after its maiden flight, where crew members followed pre-defined procedures to verify systems, including engine cycles, hydraulic pressure, and wing flap operations. The term "routine" was applied retrospectively to describe these standardized checks, distinguishing them from emergency procedures.
