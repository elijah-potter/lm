Where were you on August 6th?

As part of the student mobilization, I worked as a lathe operator in a place called Mukai Inada, about 6 kilometers from the hypocenter.

When the atomic bomb was dropped, the ceiling was blown off, the window glass broke, and the glass stuck in my finger. At first, I thought the machine had exploded. When we went outside, we saw a big mushroom cloud. At that point, we still didn't realize it was an atomic bomb, and everyone was talking about whether it was a gas tank explosion. The cloud was black, but the lower part of the cloud was white and the upper part was glowing pink.

On the 8th, we all went to dig up the food that had been buried in the air-raid shelter at my house. I thought I would finally find food, but instead what I found was a charred and deformed tea kettle and pitch-black ash. Because it was so close to the hypocenter, most of it had been carbonized at temperatures of thousands of degrees.

There, by chance, I was reunited again with my mother, who had come looking for my father. When she saw me surrounded by international students, she couldn't comprehend the situation and thought I was a ghost.

From that night on, my mother joined us in the camp. The international students seemed to have adopted my mother. They would happily join together and call out, "Mom, Mom."
