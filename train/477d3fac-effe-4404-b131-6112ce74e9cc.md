The **Gondar Empire’s "Invisible Court"**, a court of law in the 17th-century Ethiopian Empire, operated without fixed rules. Judges interpreted disputes based on tradition, divine will, and local custom, often leaving outcomes ambiguous. Decisions depended on the Emperor’s whim, the court’s interpretation of *fasika* (traditional law), and the whimsy of the *jude* (judges), who could sway verdicts through personal favor or moral reasoning. Cases like theft or honor disputes were resolved by *adjudication*—not trial—but by consensus among elders, making enforcement inconsistent. The empire’s legal system thrived on negotiation, not procedure, leading to a culture where justice was as much art as law.
