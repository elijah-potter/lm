The last day of summer was supposed to be a blur of lazy afternoons, forgotten homework, and endless ice cream runs. But for twelve-year-old Mia Carter, it was anything but.

Her dad had called at the crack of dawn, his voice raw with the kind of pain that didn’t sound like an accident—more like a slow, creeping betrayal. *They’re coming for you.* That’s all he said before the phone clicked dead. The words hung in the air between them like a ghost, heavier than the usual summer fog.

Mia’s mom didn’t speak until she’d packed a bag with just enough clothes to last two weeks. *They’ll find you,* she whispered, her hands trembling as she pressed the bag into Mia’s arms. Mia didn’t know what they were talking about. The only thing she knew for sure was that the moment the door shut behind them, her dad’s car engine roared to life, and her mother’s car pulled away, the world felt like it was spinning backward.

Now, Mia was hiding in her aunt’s old cabin, a place she’d never visited before. The cabin smelled of damp wood and old books, the kind of place that whispered secrets. The door was locked from the inside, but that didn’t stop her from listening outside. She could hear the distant rumble of a highway, the occasional shout of a truck driver, and once, a car alarm going off. It sounded like someone was looking for her.

Mia had spent the last few nights in the attic, where the floorboards creaked under her weight. She’d memorized every corner, every shadow, every sound the house made. She’d learned to recognize the difference between the creak of the old floor and the whisper of the wind through the broken windows.

On the third night, she heard it—a low, rhythmic *thump-thump-thump* coming from the basement. Her breath hitched. The cabin was supposed to be empty. Then, through the thin wooden walls, she could hear voices. Not the distant murmur of a neighbor, but someone talking in a language that sounded like it belonged in a movie.

She tiptoed down the stairs, her heart pounding. The basement door was ajar, a narrow slit of light spilling out onto the dark floor. Inside, she found a room lined with shelves that hadn’t been there before. Books—old, dusty books—shelved against the walls, their spines cracked with age. But what caught her eye were the people. They were sitting in a circle, their faces half-lit by the flicker of candles. They were all wearing dark, hooded cloaks, their hands wrapped around mugs of something steaming.

Mia’s stomach twisted. She’d seen enough horror movies to know what was happening. These weren’t just people. They were something else.

“You shouldn’t be here,” one of them said, their voice smooth and unfamiliar. “This is not your place.”

Mia’s fingers tightened around the edge of the door. *This is a nightmare,* she told herself. *You’re dreaming.*

But the voice didn’t sound like a nightmare. It sounded like the voice of someone who knew her.

She stepped forward, her bare feet silent on the cold floor. The room was too small, too dark. The people in the circle didn’t look at her. They just watched her, waiting.

“Who are you?” she whispered.

The leader—she had to be the leader, because she was the one who spoke—raised a hand, and the candles flared brighter. Mia’s eyes stung. She could see them now, not just as people, but as something else. Their faces were too perfect, too symmetrical, like they were made to fit together.

“You’re the one they’re looking for,” the leader said. “The one they called *The Lost.*”

Mia’s mind raced. *Lost.* Like she was lost. Like she didn’t belong anywhere. But that wasn’t true. She had a family. She had a house. She had a name.

“What do you want?” she asked.

The leader’s eyes gleamed in the dark. “You’re ours now,” they said. “And we’re taking you home.”

Outside, the wind howled through the broken windows. The cabin creaked. The basement door slammed shut behind her.

Mia sat up in her bed, the sheets tangled around her legs. The night was still, too still. She could hear her own heartbeat, loud and panicked. She pressed her palms against her ears, trying to block out the sound.

Then, from the hallway, she heard it—a whisper. Not from the people in the basement. From *her.*

“Mia?”

She turned. Her reflection in the full-length mirror wasn’t there. The mirror was empty, but the sound of her name was real.

“Mia?” she whispered back.

The whisper came again, closer this time.

She turned toward the sound. The hallway was empty. The door to the basement was closed. But the voices were still there, just beyond the edges of her vision, like something watching her from the dark.

Mia grabbed her backpack, slung it over her shoulder, and ran down the stairs. The basement door was still locked, but she didn’t need a key. She pushed it open, her breath coming in ragged gasps.

The room was darker than before, the candles snuffed out. The shelves were empty. The people were gone.

But the floorboards beneath her feet were moving. Slowly, like they were breathing.

Mia stumbled back, her hands flying to her mouth. She couldn’t believe she’d been so stupid. She’d thought they were ghosts. She’d thought she was dreaming.

But she wasn’t.

She was already one of them.
