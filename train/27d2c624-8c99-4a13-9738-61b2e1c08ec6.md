The server room’s fluorescent hum blurred through the half-lit haze, each flicker a whisper of neglect. A lone technician’s fingers danced over the keyboard of a terminal at the far end, but the screen’s red cursor blinked lifelessly, as if it had forgotten how to respond. A half-eaten sandwich sat on the desk, its crust cracked open, the cheese still warm but untouched. The server rack loomed behind him, its hum a steady, unthreatening drone, a chorus of ignored promises—data that should be sorted, logs that needed reviewing, and a thousand small failures that had been buried under more urgent fires.

Outside, the main floor buzzed with activity, but the server room remained a ghost town. The signal—a quiet ping from a distant database, a flicker of an alert—had been swallowed by the noise of the day, the way things were always done. The technician glanced at his watch, then at the screen, and shrugged. Another day, another half-done task, another moment of indifference.
