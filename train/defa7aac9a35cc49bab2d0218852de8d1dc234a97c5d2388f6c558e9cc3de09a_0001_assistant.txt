I shoot everything on Blackmagic cameras, but recently worked with a different crew that shot on Sony and Canon cameras. The Sony and Canon cameras take much longer to set up than the Blackmagic cameras. Sony and Canon use bespoke terminology and jargon to describe their various setting options, which makes it more difficult for people new to the cameras to figure out how to program and set them up properly. With Blackmagic, the menus and options are more intuitive and controlling for audio options through the camera is much easier and quicker to adjust the specific settings you'd like to record at.
One day, we were shooting with eight Blackmagic cameras when a critical piece of equipment for recording the data malfunctioned. This meant that we had to adjust the settings on all eight cameras to correct for the error. On other brands of camera, this could be an extensive process due to the complexity of menus and setting options, but on Blackmagic it was a very quick and easy process as the controls are all easily understandable and accessible.
