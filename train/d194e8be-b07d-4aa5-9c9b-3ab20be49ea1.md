**"The Last Letter"**

She kept it tucked in the pocket of her coat, a faded postcard from her mother’s handwriting—*‘Don’t forget to call’*—the ink still damp with tears she’d wiped away long ago. The ink had faded too, but the words still burned like embers in the dark.

Every night, before the alarm went off, she’d press the postcard against her chest, memorizing the way her mother’s script had twisted into loops, the way her mother had once told her, *"You’re not a ghost, you’re just waiting."*

Now, the waiting had turned to rust. The phone rang once—then twice. She didn’t answer. The message was left: *"Darling, I’m coming home. But first, let me tell you something. You’re not just waiting. You’re the one who taught me how to walk."*

The postcard was gone. The letter had never been sent.
