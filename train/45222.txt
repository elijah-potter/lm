Study: Binge-eating Latinos may be more obese, less educated
U.S. Latinos with high rates of binge eating may be linked to obesity and low levels of education, according to a new study by researchers at Cambridge Health Alliance, the parent of Somerville Hospital.
The study, “Prevalence and Correlates of Eating Disorders in Latinos in the United States,” concluded that although Latinos have the highest rate of any binge eating disorder, they have a low rate of anorexia and bulimia. Latinos born in the United States with a long lifespan show the greatest risk for eating disorders, which may be a result of obesity and a low education level.
Published in the June issue of International Journal of Eating Disorders, the study marks the first time researchers have collected and analyzed a compressive amount of data on eating disorders in adult U.S. Latinos.
Margarita Alegria, Director of the Multicultural Mental Health Research at Cambridge Health Alliance, along with more than five authors, concluded that although more research had to be made, binge eating is a serious health concern for Latinos.
