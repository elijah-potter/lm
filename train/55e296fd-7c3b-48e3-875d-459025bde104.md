A bustling marketplace in a historic district where the streets are paved with cobblestones and lined with old wooden stalls. The air hums with the scent of spiced tea, warm bread, and the faint metallic tang of coins. Vendors and customers move in quiet sync—no one shouts, no one rushes—each gesture carries implied weight. A nod signals agreement, a slight hesitation means hesitation, and a glance at the ground means "excuse me." The elders of the neighborhood sit on benches, their voices low, passing down unwritten customs: who may sit where, when to pause for others, the unspoken rules about respecting others' time. The market thrives because no one needs to say "please" or "thank you"—they are implied.
