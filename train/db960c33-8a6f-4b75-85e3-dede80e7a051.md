The clock on the wall seemed to stretch like taffy, each tick and toll a deliberate pause in the quiet hum of the room. The afternoon sun, once golden and crisp, now slanted through the half-drawn blinds, painting streaks of amber across the worn carpet where my shadow stretched unnaturally long. The coffee had gone cold, the first sip thick and bitter, the kind of taste that lingers like regret. Outside, the city’s usual rush was a distant whisper—traffic, distant sirens, the occasional laugh from a passing stranger. My mind wandered not to the next task but to the weight of the past few hours, each moment a puzzle piece I couldn’t quite fit into the shape of the day. The door creaked open, and for a heartbeat, the hallway outside was a blur of motion—footsteps, voices, the distant hum of life. Then silence. The moment felt like a film reel paused mid-scene, the frames flickering like a dying bulb. I exhaled, slow and deliberate, and realized how the hours had blurred into this single, aching stretch of time: waiting, not for anything in particular, but for the space between things to breathe.
