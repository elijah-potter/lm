In the quietude of an autumn evening, the city lights flickered like distant stars, painting the streets in hues of indigo and gold. I had spent months chasing shadows, convinced that if I could just catch them, I’d finally understand the weight of existence. The manuscripts I had written were scattered across my desk—some polished, some raw, but all the same: fragments of a truth I dared not face.

I had spent too long pretending I was something I wasn’t. The ink on my pages had dried with lies I had told myself to survive. The admissions I had avoided were not just about words or art—they were about the cracks in the mirror that reflected not my reflection, but the truth I refused to see.

Tonight, the last page I pushed aside was not a story but an act of surrender. The words were simple: *"I am not whole. I am broken."* And for the first time, I let it be true.
