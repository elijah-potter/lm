The old man sat on the cracked stool outside the general store, clutching his teacup like it was the last lifeline he’d ever need. *"The rain won’t stop,"* he muttered, swirling the black liquid. The wind howled through the pines, and the words echoed off the wooden signpost—*"The rain won’t stop"*—as if the sky itself were repeating them in a chorus.

A girl in a faded raincoat stepped onto the boardwalk, her boots splashing through shallow puddles. "You’re the one who says it," she said, her voice steady despite the drizzle. The old man’s fingers tightened around the cup. *"The rain won’t stop,"* he repeated, the words curling into the mist like smoke. She watched him for a long moment, then turned, her shadow stretching long across the dirt path as she vanished into the mist. The rain continued to fall, relentless, and the old man sighed, knowing he’d never hear her voice again.
