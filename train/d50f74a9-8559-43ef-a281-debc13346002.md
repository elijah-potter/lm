The neon hum of the city pulsed as Elias pressed his palm against the glass, the cold seeping through his fingers. The elevator doors hissed open, revealing a stairwell so narrow it could’ve swallowed him whole if he’d dared. His breath fogged the air above his sneakers—one minute left.

He slipped on the first step, skidding across the polished concrete. The sound of his shoes crunching on shattered glass filled the void as he rolled toward the exit, his body slamming into the wall. Pain flared behind his ribs, but he didn’t stop. His pulse roared in his ears, a symphony of adrenaline and desperation. The door at the top of the stairs was just a few steps away—if he could reach it.

He grabbed the metal railing, fingers trembling, and hauled himself up. The final step was the hardest. His fingers scraped against the door handle, his knuckles white. It swung inward with a groan. The hallway beyond was dark, but the glow of a flickering streetlight spilled through a broken window.

Elias exhaled, his chest heaving. The city’s heartbeat had stolen his breath, but he was alive. One minute. That was all he’d been given—and he’d used it.
