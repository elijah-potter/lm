The old watchtower creaked under the weight of forgotten warnings, its rusted brass gears turning in silent defiance of the storm that brewed outside. The wind howled through the broken windows, carrying whispers of the past—the kind that curled around bones long buried.

A lantern flickered to life in the corner, its dim glow casting long shadows across the crumbling parchment of the scroll propped against the wall. The ink had faded, but the words still stung:

*"Do not trust the ones who sing of peace before they strike."*

The door groaned as it swung open, revealing a figure draped in a cloak of black silk, their face obscured by a mask of bone and silver. Their voice was smooth, too smooth, like oil on water:

*"You should have listened."*

The lantern’s flame guttered out. Darkness swallowed the warning whole.
