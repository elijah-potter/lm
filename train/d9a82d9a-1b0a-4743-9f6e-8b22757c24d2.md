**A gradual shift in a single neuron's synaptic strength across a week:**

---
**Day 1:**
Spike A (pre-synaptic) arrives at Synapse 7 → weak depolarization (EPSP: 0.2 mV).
Spike B (post-synaptic) fires at 20 Hz → no immediate change; calcium influx in post-synaptic terminal is 100 pM.

---
**Day 3:**
Spike A → EPSP rises to 0.4 mV.
Spike B → post-synaptic terminal calcium: 120 pM.
Synaptic vesicle release probability: **0.10** (baseline).

---
**Day 5:**
Spike A → EPSP: 0.6 mV.
Spike B → calcium: 150 pM.
Vesicle release probability: **0.18** (due to *AMPA receptor insertion* in postsynaptic membrane).
*Glutamate reuptake* by astrocyte reduces extracellular concentration from 200 nM to 100 nM.

---
**Day 7:**
Spike A → EPSP: 0.8 mV.
Spike B → calcium: 200 pM.
Vesicle release probability: **0.25**.
*LTP marker* (CaMKII activation) stabilizes at 40% of baseline.
Membrane potential of postsynaptic neuron rises by 1 mV (increased excitability).

---
**Day 10:**
Spike A → EPSP: 1.0 mV.
Spike B → calcium: 220 pM.
Vesicle release probability: **0.30**.
New *NMDA receptors* recruited; postsynaptic calcium rises to 250 pM.
*LTP* consolidation begins; protein synthesis (synaptic tagging) activated.
Membrane potential: +1.5 mV; long-term potentiation (LTP) window open.

---
**Biochemical Note:**
- *Synaptic tagging* couples short-term changes to long-term memory storage.
- *Astrocytic buffering* stabilizes glutamate levels; *glutamate transporter* (EAAT1) efficiency increases.
- *Synaptic scaling* adjusts receptor sensitivity to normalize activity.

---
**Relevance to Learning:**
Incremental changes in synaptic efficacy (ΔE = 0.2 mV/week) accumulate to alter neural circuits over months/years.
