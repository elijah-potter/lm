No matter how digitally interconnected the world gets, cargo ships will always be necessary. The ocean will always separate the continents, so ships will always be needed to move physical goods, and the University of London's Energy Institute has created a gorgeous visualization of their movements and carbon footprint.
The visualization spans the year of 2012 and is broken down into the different elements of cargo: vehicles, liquids, dry goods. In a soothing British accent over calming piano, the narrator walks you through the wold, highlighting points of interest like the coast of China and the Suez Canal. The map gets its data from exactEarth and the Clarkson Research UK World Fleet Register, which added up to a quarter of a billion data points. The data visualization firm Kiln made sense of it all.
While the walkthrough is worth the click, it's by no means necessary. It's like hitting play on a guided tour of a museum. You'll gain more information, but the visualization stands on its own. Who knew just watching the sheer mass of global commerce could be so calming?
