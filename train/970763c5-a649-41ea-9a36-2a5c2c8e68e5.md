A newly synthesized peptide sequence, **KLNQDV**, demonstrated unexpected binding affinity to a previously overlooked mitochondrial protein, **Mito-ORP10**, with a Kd of **0.03 nM**. In vitro assays showed this interaction induced a reversible conformational change in the protein’s transmembrane domain, stabilizing a previously unstable intermediate state. Electron microscopy revealed this state correlated with a localized increase in membrane curvature (10% greater than control) within seconds of peptide addition. When expressed in human iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes, transient transfection of **KLNQDV** led to a 25% increase in action potential duration at 30 minutes, with no adverse effects observed over 48 hours. The discovery suggests a novel mechanism for tuning mitochondrial calcium handling, potentially offering a non-toxic approach to modulating cardiac repolarization.
