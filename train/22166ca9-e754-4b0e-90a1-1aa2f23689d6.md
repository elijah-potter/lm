A clause originally mandating a three-day notice for appeals becomes "a minimum of seven business days" through a routine policy update. The shift occurs in quiet moments: a memo from the compliance office is filed under "corrections," not "amendments," and the updated rule is published in an internal newsletter without public comment periods. Over time, the original intent—rooted in procedural fairness—is diluted into a bureaucratic convenience. Enforcement gaps emerge as staff interpret the rewritten rule loosely, and dissenting voices are absorbed into the administrative flow. The rewritten rule persists as an unspoken norm, its erasure from official documents invisible to those who depend on its new, less stringent form.
