Clang of distant church bells rising over the mist.
Steam curls from the old mill’s boiler, mixing with the damp earth’s musk.
Rotten bread and fish from the market stall—sour, salted, spiced with crushed pepper.
Woodsmoke curls low from the hearth, mingling with pine needles and damp wool.
Children’s laughter, sharp as broken glass, cut through the hush of the evening breeze.
Horses’ hooves pound the cobblestones, the scent of leather and sweat, mixed with wet hay.
The river’s whisper—cool, metallic, like wet stones—rushing past the stone bridge’s creaking timbers.
