A promise etched in fading ink on a crumpled napkin, the words *"I’ll be back"* trembling across the surface like a ghost. The night was thick with the scent of damp earth and distant rain, the moon a pale coin rolling across the sky. They sat on the porch steps, knees drawn to their chest, the weight of the words pressing against their ribs like a physical ache.

*"You’ll see me again,"* they whispered, but the words tasted like smoke in their throat. The last time they’d spoken like this had been in a hotel room, the sheets tangled, the air thick with the ghost of their own breath. They’d promised to stay. To hold on. To be there when the world spun into chaos. But the world spun on, indifferent, and the time they’d promised to stay had long since passed.

Now, the promise hung in the air like a promise made to the wind—unanswered, unheard, a flicker in the dark that might as well have never been. The years blurred together, each one a step further away, each one a chance they’d missed, a door they’d closed without turning the knob. They tried to write letters. To send them off like ships into the sea, but the ink would run, the words dissolving into the margins, and they’d never know if anyone read them.

The promise felt like a wound that never closed. Like a scar that refused to fade, stretching across the skin of their heart, raw and visible. They’d given themselves over to it, let it take root, and now it was too heavy to lift, too deep to dig out. Somewhere out there, they imagined, there was someone who had believed. Someone who had hoped. But hope was a fragile thing, and promises were meant to be broken. Eventually.
