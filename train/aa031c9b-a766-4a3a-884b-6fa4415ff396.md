A vast, cavernous chamber bathed in eerie, flickering bioluminescent moss that pulses like a slow heartbeat. The air hums with the distant, rhythmic drip of water from a single, frozen-over fissure high in the ceiling, its sound muffled by layers of sediment. The walls are draped in a shifting veil of mist that never fully dissipates, thick enough to obscure the edges of the horizon but transparent enough to reveal the skeletal remains of ancient structures—half-collapsed ruins where the stone itself seems to have absorbed the passage of time. Below, a vast, stagnant pool mirrors the sky, its surface so still it might as well be glass. The only movement comes from the occasional ripple of a deep-sea creature, its glow flickering erratically as it drifts just beneath the surface. The silence is so thick you can almost taste it. The only sound is the slow, deliberate creak of the chamber’s ancient, rusted hinges as something—or nothing—waits for the moment to move.
