The static hummed from the old radio as the last warning crackled through the static:
*"All hands on deck. Submarine alert. Depth charges approaching."*

Captain Elias Voss gripped the wheel, fingers digging into the leather grip. The *SS Mariner’s Dawn* lurked beneath the waves, its hull lined with rust and half-sunk in the frigid currents of the Arctic Circle. The icebergs loomed like giant teeth, their jagged edges scraping against the ship’s hull. A single torch flickered in the captain’s cabin, casting long shadows on the walls.

*"We’re running low on fuel,"* whispered the first mate, his voice barely audible over the creaking metal. *"If we don’t turn back soon, we’ll never make it."*

Elias exhaled sharply, the scent of salt and diesel thick in the air. The *Mariner’s Dawn* had set out three months ago, chasing rumors of a lost relic said to hold the secrets of the first explorers. But the ice had come earlier than expected, and now the ship was trapped in a maze of glaciers, its engines sputtering like dying embers.

The radio hissed again. A distorted voice—*coast guard*—cut through the static:
*"You’re not in danger, sir. Just… stay where you are."*

Elias’s blood ran cold. The coast guard didn’t issue warnings like that. They issued orders.

Outside, the ice groaned. A deep, resonant *crack* split the night. The ship shuddered as something massive shifted beneath them—an iceberg, half-buried in darkness, its surface slick with black water. The torchlight revealed a jagged hole in the hull, just below the waterline.

The first mate’s face paled. *"We’re taking on water."*

Elias turned to the helm. The *Mariner’s Dawn* was already listing. If they didn’t act now, the ship would capsize in hours.

*"Make for the nearest ice floe,"* he barked, voice raw. *"And pray it’s not the one that’s about to sink."*

The engines roared as the ship veered toward the last remaining ice shelf—a fragile, frozen island barely holding together. Behind them, the Arctic howled in protest, a storm of ice and wind that could swallow them whole.

Elias stared at the horizon. The *Mariner’s Dawn* would sink. And with it, the last hope of finding the lost relic—or the truth behind the warning.

---
**The warning wasn’t just an alert.**
It was a test.
