The rain tapped against the window like a restless crowd, each drop a whisper of the city’s forgotten ghosts. Elias sat at the cluttered desk, fingers tracing the edges of a half-finished poem, the ink still damp where he had hesitated too long. The typewriter’s keys had become a foreign language—each one a mistake, each word a half-truth.

Outside, the neon glow of a distant bar pulsed like a heartbeat, casting long shadows across the alley. He should have left hours ago, but the words had snared him. Now, the paper smelled of old coffee and something sharper—dust, perhaps, or the faint metallic tang of regret.

The door creaked open before he could finish the last line. The woman who stepped inside was too tall, her silhouette swallowed by the dimness. She carried a coat that had seen better days, its sleeves frayed at the cuffs. "You always write like this?" she asked, voice low, like she was afraid the words would vanish if she spoke too loud. Elias didn’t look up. "Like what?"

She exhaled, a gust of smoke curling from her lips. "Like you’re trying to outrun something."

He finally lifted his head, the weight of the words pressing down on his chest. The poem was a cage, and he’d built it for himself. "It’s not about running," he said, but his hands trembled. "It’s about staying."

She watched him for a long moment, then turned to leave, her shadow stretching behind her like a question mark. The door swung shut behind her, and the silence that followed was thick with the unspoken weight of the words left unsaid.

Elias stood, his fingers brushing the last lines—now just ink on paper, no longer his. He folded the sheet carefully, tucking it into his coat pocket. The city outside was already forgetting him, but for now, he carried the ghost of what might have been.

He walked away. The rain followed him, but it didn’t soak his pride.
