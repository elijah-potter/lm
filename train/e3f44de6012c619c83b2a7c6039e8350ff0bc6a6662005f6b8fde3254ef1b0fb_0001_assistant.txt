There is no sophomore jinx here. If there was any indication that Donna Duckling and the Wobbles would fail to match the chaos that was their first album, “In A Row…” then someone forgot to let these ducks know about it. If their first release was the aforementioned “chaos", then “Yellow and Fluffy” would be more akin to “controlled chaos with a side of wacky rationalization and sometimes unbelievable musical compositions”. If nothing else, this release will make a mark.

Donna starts the album with a nearly 3-minute acapella call and cry that rises and falls like the down that flies up when met with a table leg wrapped in a lime. It only gets weirder from there when the rest of the band kicks in with Webbed, a hard-driving march through the rain, and leads right into Lakeside Swim, which has a lot of splashes that seem uncontrolled, but fall in line with beak breaks that dive into waves of reverb. This frenetic pace continues for the next three tracks, Benches, Floppy Feet, and Squawkitty Squawk Squawk. Don’t let the innocent titles fool you - this is hard and intense.

Things appear to settle down when Fusia, the tiniest of the group, begins a sweet lullaby that is sonically interrupted by the splashing of webbed feet in Smelly Fish (The Return of the Tuna, Part 42). There are a couple of surprising attempts at a “commercial” sound, but these would best be considered “experiments” that should be seen as bridges between the more substantial parts of the record. The LP finishes off with Bread by Broadway, a sprawling, 23-minute epic that ranges from brilliant to confusing but is ultimately the sum of its very odd, feathered, and webbed parts.
