A nascent urban cluster emerging along a defunct railway corridor, its tracks now repurposed as a sunken bike lane. The air hums with the low drone of electric scooters and the occasional clatter of cargo bikes. Buildings, a mix of repurposed warehouses and new modular structures, spill out onto narrow, tree-lined streets. The skyline is dotted with solar-paneled canopies over pedestrian plazas where market stalls sell locally sourced goods, and the scent of fresh pastries drifts from open-air cafés. A community garden occupies the old train yard, where children’s laughter echoes over the crackling of compost bins. The place pulses with informal gatherings—music from makeshift stages, the sound of hand-built instruments, the occasional burst of laughter at a storytelling circle under the streetlights. The routine isn’t yet codified; it’s organic, shifting, and alive with potential.
