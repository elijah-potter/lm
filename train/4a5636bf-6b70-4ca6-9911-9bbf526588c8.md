A half-built concrete slab foundation sprawls across a sun-bleached lot, cracked and uneven, exposed to the elements. Steel rebar protrudes from the surface like skeletal fingers, some bent, others rusted at the tips. A rusted steel frame leans precariously against the edge, its windows and doors missing, the skeletal structure of a structure still taking shape.

In the center, a half-poured concrete floor slab, still wet in places, sits on a rough, unfinished base. Painted letters in faded orange peel finish—partially cracked and peeling—read **"WITHIN"** at the top, with the rest of the building's name partially obscured by dirt and debris.

Overgrown grass and weeds push through gaps between the foundation’s slabs, creeping up the sides of the unfinished walls. A rusted, tilted ladder leans against a corner, half-buried in dirt, leading to what was once a door that now stands ajar, its frame warped and swollen from moisture.

The roof section, if it was ever meant to be a roof, is a tangled mess of exposed beams and sagging trusses. A few shingles, if they were ever shingles, lie scattered around, half-buried in the dirt. The entire structure feels like it’s breathing—sinking, settling, waiting. The air hums with the quiet, unresolved energy of something that was meant to be more than just a foundation.
