The morning mist curled over the old bridge like smoke, thickening the air between the two halves of the town. I sat on the edge of the riverbank, my boots half-sunk in the mud, watching the fish dart beneath the surface. The sun hadn’t quite broken through the canopy of pines when I heard it—the first splash, sharp and sudden. I turned just as the man in the faded blue jacket dove into the water.

He surfaced moments later, gasping, his fingers clutching at the ripples. "I was just—" he started, but the words dissolved into a cough. His face was pale, his lips blue. "The house... it’s gone." His voice was raw, but the panic in it was real.

I should have asked more. I should have listened. But I was already counting the minutes until the boat’s engine roared to life, the way the sky would turn from gray to gold, the way my own chest would ache with the weight of something I couldn’t name.

I told him I’d be there in an hour. That was all he needed to hear. The boat pulled away before he could ask what I was doing here—or why the river had suddenly become my only escape.
