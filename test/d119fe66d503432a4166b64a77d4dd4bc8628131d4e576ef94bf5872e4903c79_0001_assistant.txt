The bars are slippery in his hands, but he pulls up, legs airborne for a moment, swinging to sit on the top. The playground is full today, the children like marbles racing over the bridges and down the slides. Henderson is nowhere to be seen. He must still be sick. George has a sinking feeling he'll be up here for the whole break. He ducks his chin, trying to not cry. He should try to have more than one friend so that when one is sick he is not alone. But what if both are sick? Then he'd need three. Four? He could never be friends with four people. Four people didn't even talk to him at school besides his teachers. George felt himself giving in, waiting to hear the bell ring, when something tugged on his jacket. Lusia smiled up at him. She motioned using the monkey bars, and George nodded. He didn't want to get in the way of her fun. He thought maybe he might as well sit by the door when she tugged his jacket again. She didn't understand her hand gestures, so he shook his head. She glowered and tugged him to the bars, motioning for him to go. He climbed up and thought maybe she could be his friend. He skipped the bars as he went across and heard clapping behind him. Lusia mimicked him and then went backward! George laughed and copied her. The bell rang too soon, but George couldn't wait to tell Henderson about his new friend.
