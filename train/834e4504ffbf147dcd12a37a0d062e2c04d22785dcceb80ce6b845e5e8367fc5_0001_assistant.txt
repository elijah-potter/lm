1) A Person's Age - Typically a person's blood pressure will rise as they get older. 
2) Racial Background - Hypertension tends to be more prevalent in the African-American community. African-Americans tend to get high blood pressure at an earlier age than other races.
3) Family and Genetics - If your parents or close relatives have high blood pressure, your odds of getting it typically increase
4) Your Weight - When you start to put on more weight than what is considered normal, the blood vessels in your body have to work harder, thus leading to an increase in blood pressure.
5) Sedentary Lifestyle - If you aren't getting much exercise, heart rates tend to increase and can coincide with your blood pressure rising. Exercising can increase the amount of oxygen your blood receives.
6) Smoking - Studies indicate that cigarettes and vaping correlate with hypertension.
7) Sodium and Potassium Intake - Higher salt intake in combination with low potassium can greatly impact a person's blood pressure. Sodium makes blood vessels work harder while potassium calms blood vessels down. 
8) Stress Levels - Stress typically impacts other areas on this list, which can in turn cause higher blood pressure in people. This is usually temporary, however.
9) Alcohol intake - Consuming alcoholic beverages can put a strain on the kidneys, which often leads to higher blood pressure. Alcohol can also cause liver issues which can impact fat breakdown.
10) Underlying conditions - Having a condition such as kidney disease or fatty liver disease can have a massive impact on blood pressure.
