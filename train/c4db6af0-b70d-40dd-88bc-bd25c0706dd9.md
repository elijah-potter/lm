The absence lingers like a half-remembered dream,
a hollow space where presence once resided—
not gone, but dissolved in the quiet ache of memory.
It’s not a gap, but a weight, a slow erosion
of what was never fully held, a silence that
fills the room like the scent of damp earth after rain.
Sometimes it’s a door left ajar, its handle cold,
other times a voice that fades before it’s spoken.
It’s the absence of a hand that once grasped yours,
the absence of laughter that was once a song,
the absence of the one who was always there—
not as a thing, but as the air you breathe,
the rhythm of the world you once knew.
It’s not a thing to be found, but the space it leaves,
a ghost that moves through your days like a shadow,
unseen but always there, watching, waiting,
to remind you that some losses are not
endings, but the way the world shifts when a light
goes out and never comes back on.
