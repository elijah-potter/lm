**The Last Light**
The old clock tower stood silent at dawn, its brass hands frozen midway between three and four. Outside, the city slept, but inside the church’s vaulted halls, a single candle flickered on the altar, casting long shadows of forgotten promises. The priest, a man who had spent thirty years whispering prayers to the empty pews, adjusted his collar and reached for the ledger he’d been writing in for decades.

*"They came back,"* he murmured to the dust, *"but only to ask for the same things again."*

A woman’s voice cut through the silence. *"Father, the last payment isn’t due until the end of the month."* She knelt beside him, her fingers tracing the worn edges of the parchment. *"You promised me they’d wait."*

He exhaled sharply, the scent of aged paper and candle wax mingling. *"I promised them nothing, child. I promised you."*

The ledger clattered open. Below the last entry—*"Debt settled: 1873"*—was a fresh line, scrawled in ink that hadn’t been used in decades:

**"Reminder: The last payment is due tomorrow at midnight."**
