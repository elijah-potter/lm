The rain pattered softly against the roof like a thousand hesitant fingers tapping out a rhythm no one could quite place. The kitchen light flickered—once, twice—before settling into a dim, persistent glow, casting long shadows that stretched like forgotten promises across the floorboards. The thermometer on the fridge read 68 degrees, a number that felt less like a temperature and more like a quiet agreement between the world and the walls.

Outside, the garden was a sea of overgrown weeds, their edges just visible through the curtains. The old radio in the living room, once a source of endless static and forgotten songs, now played nothing at all, as if it had finally run out of words. The clock on the wall moved forward, inch by slow inch, like time itself was being measured by something unyielding.

You sat at the table, stirring tea that had turned lukewarm in the saucer, the spoon tapping against the porcelain with a sound that was almost too familiar. The letter you’d received earlier that morning lay unopened on the coffee table, its envelope stiff with age and unanswered. The address inside was a name you’d almost forgotten—*Mira*, the woman who had once been someone else’s wife, though you’d never met her. The ink on the paper was faint, as if written in haste or sorrow. The words were simple: *"I’m not coming back."*
