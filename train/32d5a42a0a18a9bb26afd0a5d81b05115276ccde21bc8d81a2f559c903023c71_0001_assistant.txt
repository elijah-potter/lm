There are approximately 34,000 species of fish found in the world's fresh and salt waters. The majority of fish species are cold-blooded, with only one species of warm-blooded fish known: the opah (Lampris guttatus). Fishes have existed for over 450 million years. Over time, they've adapted and evolved many times to adjust to nearly all kinds of aquatic habitats found on Earth. Nearly all natural bodies of water host fish life, except for super-hot thermal pools and highly alkaline salt lakes like North America's Great Salt Lake or Asia's Dead Sea.
