There’s no historical evidence that Marie Antoinette, the last reigning Queen of France from 1755 to 1793, ever said the infamous line, “Let them eat cake” when presented with the fact that her people were too impoverished to afford bread. The first thing to consider is that the quote was mistranslated to begin with. Marie Antoinette had reportedly originally said, “Qu’ils mangent de la brioche,” which means, “let them eat brioche.” Brioche is a type of decadent bread with a rich flavor, but it’s not cake. However, the second consideration is that there's no evidence she actually said this quote.

Regardless of what Marie Antoinette uttered, it led to her overthrow and eventual execution because she was out of touch with the needs of her people. Rumor was enough when the people were informed of her supposed flippant response. Whether cake or bread, her kingdom’s families were too poor to afford either, so what she said conveyed ignorance about the plights of peasants.
