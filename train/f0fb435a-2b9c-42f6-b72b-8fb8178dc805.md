A dimly lit office, the air thick with the scent of old paper and stale coffee. The large meeting room door stood open, the faint hum of chatter from within echoing through the hall. Across the conference table, **Lena** adjusted her glasses, fingers tracing the edge of her notepad. **"We were going to execute this by tomorrow afternoon,"** she said, voice steady but edged with tension. **"But—"**

A sharp knock at the door interrupted. **"Excuse me, Ms. Chen,"** a uniformed security guard’s voice cut through the tension. **"There’s an incident at the docks. A container’s been compromised. Cargo’s gone missing—high-value, likely."**

Lena’s pen stilled mid-air. **"Missing?"** Her voice was barely above a whisper.

**"Yes, sir."** The guard hesitated. **"We’re already on alert. If you’d like to review security footage, I can—"**

**"We’ll be there in five."** Lena’s reply was clipped. She turned back to her notes, but her mind raced. The plan—**Project Orion’s final test phase**—had been designed to run smoothly, flawlessly. But now, the foundation of everything they’d built in secret was crumbling beneath them. The missing cargo wasn’t just misplaced data or a minor supply error. It was a **red flag**.

Outside, the city’s neon glow reflected off the rain-slicked streets as the team scrambled to adjust. Somewhere, a siren wailed in the distance. **The plan was dead. Again.**
