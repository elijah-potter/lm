A dim hallway flickered with the glow of a single bulb, its soft light casting long shadows. A worn wooden door stood slightly ajar, the latch whispering as it gave way. Inside, the room was bathed in silence—no creaking floorboards, no distant footsteps. The air smelled faintly of damp and old books, like a library left closed for decades.

A single candle burned low on a desk, its flame flickering against the backdrop of dust motes drifting in the dim light. On the shelf above, a stack of yellowed photographs lay untouched, their corners curled with age. The last light in the room was the reflection of the candle on the half-open window, a pale, wavering shape.

Outside, the wind had died down, the night settling into stillness. No voices, no laughter—only the occasional distant hum of traffic, muffled by the distant city. The room had been left exactly as it was, like a secret kept too long. The door closed quietly behind whoever had entered, sealing it once more.
