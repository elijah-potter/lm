The annual **Harvest Ceremony** at **Blackthorn Farm** marks the transition from the **Gathering Season** to the **New Year’s Cycle**. Each farmer—**Elias, Mara, and the old miller, Harkin**—stands in the dim light of a **candlelit hall**, their hands wrapped in woven linen. The air hums with the scent of **sun-dried grain, wild thyme, and the faint metallic tang of copper** from the sacred **anvil of the First Harvest**.

Elias, the eldest, strikes the **iron bell** three times, its clang echoing through the barns. Mara, the youngest, holds a **burning torch**, its flame casting long shadows across the **stone floor**. Harkin, with his **matted beard and calloused hands**, pours **boiled water** into a **molten brass cauldron**—the **First Offering**—while whispering the **ancient names of the seasons**:

*"To the Earth, who gives us life; to the Sun, who guides us; to the Moon, who holds the cycles."*

The farmers then **kneel**, pressing their palms to the **ancient oak table**, its surface carved with **runes of the old tongue**. Each takes a **small bundle of wheat**, **barley, and flax**, and **shares it in silence**. The bundles are wrapped in **leather from the last year’s beasts**, tied with **twine dyed in the blood of the first lamb born**.

At the heart of the ritual, **Elias lifts a **loaf of bread**, still warm from the **hearthstone oven**, and breaks it into seven pieces—one for each **element**: **earth (roots), water (rain), fire (lightning), air (wind), earth (soil), fire (flame), and earth (seed)**. The pieces are scattered into the **cornucopia of the harvest**, a **gourd filled with the day’s bounty**, which is then **buried beneath the **stone of the first frost**, marking the **end of the harvest’s power**.

As the flames flicker, Mara recites the **Oath of the New Year**:
*"From this day forward, we honor what we have, and what we must yield. The harvest is not just food—it is memory, sacrifice, and the promise of what comes next."*

The **candlelight flickers**, the **whispering wind carries the scent of damp earth**, and the farmers stand once more, their hands steady, their minds settled. The ritual is done. The New Year begins.
