Whispers of damp earth, rustling leaves like crumpled paper,
steam rising from hot pavement—earthy, metallic, smoky.
Far-off church bells toll, distant laughter spills from alleyways,
pizza boxes charred and crisp, their scent of garlic and tomatoes
clinging to the air, mingling with the faintest hint of gasoline,
a diesel truck’s exhaust hums like a slow, mechanical heartbeat.
Sunlight filters through broken glass, casting flickering gold on wet cobblestones—
the smell of old books, ink and damp paper, layered with the sharp tang of copper,
a stray dog’s bark, the skitter of rats, the occasional scream cut short.
The scent of rain on concrete, sharp and clean, mixed with the lingering
aftertaste of last night’s neon—burning plastic, sweet and sickly.
