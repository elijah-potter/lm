## TIL the "Great Emu War" was a real, and embarrassing, event for Australia.

Today I learned that in 1932, the Australian government declared war on emus. Seriously. It's a surprisingly bizarre and largely unsuccessful chapter in Australian history. 

Following the First World War, many veterans were given land in Western Australia to farm wheat. Unfortunately, an estimated twenty thousand emus, displaced by drought, began raiding these farms, destroying crops and damaging fences. Farmers, understandably frustrated, petitioned the government for assistance. 

The Minister of Defence, Sir George Pearce, authorized military personnel to assist, believing machine guns would quickly resolve the issue. Two soldiers armed with Lewis guns were deployed, but the emus proved remarkably elusive and surprisingly resilient. The birds scattered, regrouped, and utilized their speed and agility to evade the soldiers' attempts at control. After several days and thousands of rounds of ammunition expended, the "war" was officially called off, with minimal impact on the emu population and considerable embarrassment for the Australian government. 

It serves as a comical reminder of the challenges of wildlife management and the humbling power of nature.
