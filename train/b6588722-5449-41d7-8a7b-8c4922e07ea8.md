It wasn’t supposed to happen like this. The last time she saw him, the world still believed in grand gestures wrapped in lace and whispered promises. Now, his hands were rough, his voice a growl against the hum of the diner’s fluorescent lights. She had come for the coffee, not the memory of what could have been.

The jukebox played an old ballad, the kind that made her throat tighten. His fingers brushed hers—just for a second—before he pulled away, leaving her staring at the condensation on his coffee cup like it held the answers she’d forgotten to ask. The waitress slid a cigarette across the table, its glow like a warning she’d ignored. Outside, the city’s heartbeat thrummed, indifferent.
