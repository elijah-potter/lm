The wind whispered through the hollows of the old oak, its bark cracked like ancient parchment, its roots coiled tight around the earth like a serpent’s embrace. The moon hung low, a silver coin turned to dust, casting long, trembling shadows across the moss-stained stones. She sat on a broken bench, her fingers tracing the cracks in the wood, each one a story left unspoken.

Outside, the river hummed a lullaby of its own—slow and steady, unbroken by the weight of the world. She had come here to forget, to let the silence drown out the voices that had carved their way into her bones. But the river did not listen. It only carried on, indifferent, as it always had.

She closed her eyes and let the wind carry her away, just for a moment. When she opened them again, the air smelled of damp earth and something sweet, like wildflowers blooming after rain. The question lingered in her chest, unanswered: *What if the only thing that ever asked her was the one she tried to outrun?*
