A rusted metal box, half-buried in the overgrown garden of an abandoned house, contained a folded newspaper clipping dated **1978** from *"The Daily Gazette"* with the headline: **"LOST: A Letter from 1952"**.

Inside, a single typed page with ink smudges:

---
**DEAR FRIEND,**
If you’re reading this, you’ve found my message. My name is Elias Carter, and I wrote this in a storm in 1952 after my ship, *SS Aurora*, vanished near the Bering Strait. The last thing I remember was a woman’s voice—cold, urgent—saying, *"They’ll take you. Don’t come back."* Now, thirty years later, I’m gone too. But perhaps you’ll find the truth in the black box at the bottom of the ocean. It holds my journal, my sketches of the woman, and a key to the door that wasn’t there before.

—E.C.

The box’s seal was torn, as if someone had tried to open it and failed. A single photo peeking out: a woman in a long coat, her face half-lit by candlelight, her eyes wide with something like terror. The edges were singed, as if burned by heat or flame.
---

*(End of found text.)*
