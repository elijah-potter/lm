The wind whispered through the old oak’s gnarled branches, carrying the scent of damp earth and forgotten secrets. Inside the crumbling church, the stained glass windows cast shifting hues over the pews, like fragments of a dying light. Father Elias sat in his study, his fingers tracing the edges of a half-finished prayer book, its pages brittle with age.

*"Why does the sky remember the past but not the future?"* he muttered, staring at the cracked mirror above his desk. Outside, the church bells tolled an hour too late, their echoes bouncing off the stone walls like hollow laughter.

The last candle had burned down, leaving only embers to flicker in the dark. Somewhere, a child laughed—not in joy, but in the quiet desperation of those who had lost everything. Elias leaned forward, his breath fogging the air between them.

*"What happens when the last light goes out?"* he whispered, his voice trembling. The answer, if there was one, was lost in the silence between the walls and the stars.
