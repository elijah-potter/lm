WORCESTER — Results of studies by University of Massachusetts Medical School scientists, published Sunday in the journal Nature Medicine, suggest that a prime cause of the most common form of inherited mental retardation may be an imbalance that causes elevated protein production in the brain.
The UMMS researchers, led by Joel Richter, a professor of molecular medicine, showed that correcting one genetic mutation with another resulted in normal brain function in mice with a version of Fragile X Syndrome, which in addition to retardation is the most frequent single-gene cause of autism.
"Biology works in strange ways," Mr. Richter said in a press release Sunday. "Mutations in each gene result in impaired brain function, but in our studies, we found that mutations in both genes result in normal brain function."
According to the release, Mr. Richter's team showed that knocking out a gene important for messenger RNA translation in neurons restores memory deficits and reduces behavioral symptoms in mice with the syndrome.
