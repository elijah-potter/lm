It has been at least 30 years since the first "Top Gun" film was released and propelled Tom Cruise to stardom. The first movie was legendary in its own right and inspired numerous kids and young people to become fighter pilots in the military.

Now, after 30-plus years, "Top Gun: Maverick" hits screens and allows a new generation of viewers to recapture the magic that surrounded the first movie. Cruise returns for the sequel (as the title bears his character, Pete "Maverick" Mitchell's name) as well as an appearance by Val Kilmer, who played "Iceman" in the first film. The cast is rounded out by stellar performances from newcomers to the series, including Miles Teller and Jon Hamm, among others.

"Maverick" follows the story of Cruise's Maverick, now a Captain in the United States Navy, who is assigned to become the new instructor of the Navy's Top Gun school for elite pilots. The mission this time: teach the new pilots to fly a dangerous mission behind enemy lines and disable their weapons capabilities before they decide to strike domestic soil. Upon his return to Top Gun, Maverick rekindles a long-lost love in the movie's new female co-lead, Penny, played by acclaimed actress Jennifer Connelly. But the relationship with the most contention is between Maverick and one of the new mission pilots, Rooster, played by Miles Teller, who just happens to be Maverick's best friend Goose's son, a callback to the first movie.

The film features high-flying camera work that puts Cruise and company in the cockpits of the planes they are depicted to be flying, creating a one-of-a-kind cinematic experience best seen on the big screen.

Based on a scale of one to five stars, I give "Top Gun: Maverick" an undeniable five out of five.
