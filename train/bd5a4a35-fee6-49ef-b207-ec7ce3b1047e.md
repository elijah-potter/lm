The hollowed-out cathedral’s stone walls pulse faintly with warm, rhythmic light from embedded lanterns, shifting like a slow heartbeat. The air hums with static—whispers of voices trapped in the cracks, some coherent, others barely audible, as if the space itself breathes. The floor tilts subtly, as if the ground is swaying to unseen music. Shadows move independently, forming shapes that dissolve when observed too closely. At night, the stained glass windows glow with a faint, eerie luminescence, refracting into patterns that seem to shift in real time. The scent of damp earth and old wood lingers, thick with the scent of forgotten prayers. Inside, a single, ancient clock ticks backward, its hands frozen mid-second, as if frozen mid-moment of time. The walls whisper in a language that shifts with the air, leaving only fragments behind.
