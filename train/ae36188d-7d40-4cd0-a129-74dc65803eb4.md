**The Last Light**

The city slept beneath a veil of frost, its neon ghosts flickering like dying stars. I stood at the edge of the park, my breath curling in the cold air, the camera strap tight across my chest. The old man with the lantern had warned me—*"They come at dusk, when the shadows stretch too long."* I ignored him. Some things are worth the risk.

The streetlights hummed, their bulbs casting jagged shadows on the pavement. I adjusted the settings on my camera—slow shutter, high ISO, the kind of settings that make noise but capture the magic. My fingers trembled as I framed the shot. The buildings loomed, their windows like hollow eyes, watching.

Then—movement.

A figure stepped from the alley, cloaked in darkness. Not a person. Something *else*. Its limbs moved too fluidly, too *wrong*, and its silhouette shifted like smoke. The camera’s lens fluttered, but I held steady. I wanted this. I needed this.

The light caught it.

A burst of color—a fireworks display from the old clock tower, its chimes ringing out in time with the creature’s steps. The image blurred, streaked with light and shadow, the beast’s form dissolving into the chaos. The shutter clicked.

**Image:**
A split-second freeze-frame of neon-lit alleyways, where a shadowy figure moves with unnatural grace, its form flickering between human and something *else*. Behind it, the clock tower explodes in a burst of light—a single, perfect second where the city’s glow merges with the creature’s movement, the lens capture frozen mid-flutter. The composition: low-angle shot, foregrounded by frost-covered pavement, the beast’s head slightly tilted, eyes reflecting the fireworks like trapped stars. The image is raw, chaotic, and haunting—raw enough to make the viewer *feel* the moment.
