The alarm buzzed at 6:17 AM, its shrill tone barely piercing the fog-laden silence of the kitchen. The coffee machine’s steaming hiss was the only sound until the coffee was brewed, the steam curling like ghostly fingers through the morning air. The first sip was bitter, the world outside still blanketed in the remnants of last night’s haze.

The train arrived at 7:43 AM, a metallic groan cutting through the rain-soaked streets. The seat next to me was occupied by a woman in a trench coat, her headphones buried in a podcast about quantum physics. She didn’t look up until the train lurched into the next station.

By 8:52 AM, the office email inbox was already overflowing—urgent messages from clients, last-minute edits for the quarterly report, and a single attachment labeled *"Urgent: Client X’s proposal—review ASAP."* The meeting with the team had been pushed to 9:15 AM, but the first draft of the proposal was due by 9:00 AM.

The meeting ended at 11:12 AM with a half-finished decision on the new marketing campaign. By noon, the lunch break was interrupted by a call from the HR department—someone had submitted a complaint about the new policy on flexible hours.

The day’s last task: a 10:45 PM deadline for the final client presentation. The slides were half-finished, the voiceover still needing tweaks. The coffee was cold, the screen’s glow the only light in the dimly lit office. The first draft of the presentation was saved as *"v1.0 (Incomplete)."*
