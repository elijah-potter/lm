**BREAKING: New Energy Boom in Solaria Capital Sparks Economic & Environmental Shift**

Residents of Solaria, the city known for its golden skyline and historic canals, are reeling after the discovery of the **Solaria Energy Core**, a naturally occurring geothermal deposit beneath the city’s downtown. Environmentalists, however, warn of immediate risks. The drilling process has triggered minor seismic tremors near the **Arboretum**, prompting the city council to issue an emergency evacuation order for the **Lower Canal District**, where homes along the shore have been identified as at risk of flooding. Mayor Elara Voss declared a state of emergency, directing all non-essential personnel to report to shelters by sunset. The city’s water supply, which relies on the same geothermal infrastructure, is also under scrutiny as engineers test for potential contamination.

Critics of Solaria’s rapid industrialization accuse the government of prioritizing economic growth over safety, while labor unions demand higher wages for workers hired to oversee the project. Meanwhile, a **black-market trading ring** has emerged, offering "premium energy credits" to citizens willing to sell power back to the grid—raising concerns about market manipulation. The city’s stock exchange, which had been suspended for "unforeseen operational delays," is expected to reopen tomorrow under new regulations.

With the energy core still in its early stages of assessment, Solaria faces a crossroads: can the city balance its financial and environmental ambitions—or will the rush to exploit this discovery leave scars deeper than the tremors?
