The rusted hinge on the old door creaked as Emma turned it, but the latch wouldn’t catch. She stepped back, hands on her hips, and stared at the empty space where the family photo album had once hung on the wall—now just a jagged hole, the frame half-buried under dust. Her mother’s voice, sharp with frustration, echoed from downstairs: *"Another thing we’ll have to replace!"* Outside, the wind howled through the broken windowpanes in the kitchen, carrying the scent of damp wood and something faintly metallic. The kitchen clock struck five; she’d been gone too long.
