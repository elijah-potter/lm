The first time Liam noticed the strange glow in the hallway mirrors, he thought it was his imagination. But the next morning, as he brushed his teeth, the reflection in his bathroom mirror flickered—not with his face, but with a shadow of something ancient and hungry.

His best friend, Mia, found him sitting on the floor of his room, staring at the wall where the mirror once hung. "You okay?" she asked, handing him a coffee. He took a sip, but the warmth didn’t reach him. "No," he said. "Something’s changed."

That night, Liam dreamed of a city built from bones, where the streets were lined with skulls and the people were hollow-eyed. When he woke, he found his backpack had vanished. Inside, instead of textbooks, he found a single, folded note: *"They’re coming for the light."*

The next day, at school, Liam saw it too—the same flickering in the bathroom mirror, now in every mirror in the school. Then came the whispers. Voices in the walls, humming in a language that wasn’t English. The principal called an emergency meeting, but the meeting room’s windows darkened, and the people inside started laughing in unison, their faces stretched too wide, their mouths too big.

Mia found him in the library that afternoon, hunched over a stack of books. "What are you doing?" she asked. "Reading," he said, but his fingers trembled. She pulled a book from the pile—a dusty, leather-bound tome with strange symbols carved into the spine. The moment she opened it, the pages glowed, and the symbols pulsed like living things.

That night, Liam and Mia stood outside the school, watching as the mirrors in the halls began to weep. Drops of liquid light cascaded down, pooling on the pavement. Mia grabbed his hand. "We have to stop them," she whispered. "We have to find out what’s feeding them."

They followed the light into the school’s basement, where they found the source—a cavernous chamber lined with rusted pipes and ancient machinery. At its center stood a figure, half-buried in the floor, its body woven from blackened wood and metal. Its mouth was a maw of jagged teeth, and its eyes burned like embers. It turned its head slowly, and the pupils were empty, but the irises pulsed with an unnatural light.

Liam stepped forward. "You’re not real," he said. "You’re just… something that feeds on fear." The creature hissed, and the ground trembled. The light in the chamber flared brighter, and the walls seemed to breathe. Mia grabbed his arm. "We need to get out."

They ran, but the school itself seemed to rise after them. The doors slammed shut. The lights flickered out. Then, the whispers came again, louder this time, filling the halls like a chorus of voices. The mirrors in the school began to spin, their reflections twisting into faces that weren’t human.

Liam clutched Mia’s hand as they stumbled through the darkness. They found a door labeled "Fire Escape" but the words warped, rewriting themselves as they spoke. The door opened, and they tumbled outside into a storm of black rain. The sky was a void of stars that weren’t there before, and the wind howled with voices.

They ran until their lungs burned. Then, they collapsed onto the pavement, exhausted. Mia wiped tears from her eyes. "We’re alive," she said. "We just have to figure out how to stop this."

Liam looked at the storm, at the sky, at the world that had changed in the blink of an eye. The light in the mirrors had faded, but the whispers still lingered in the air. Something had changed. And they were the only ones who could change it back.

The next morning, the school reopened. The mirrors were normal again. The whispers were gone. But Liam and Mia knew the truth—they’d only delayed the storm. Now, they had to find out who—or what—was behind it, and how to break the cycle before it was too late.
