## Project Chimera: A Cooperative Research Game

**A Game for 2-4 Players**
**Ages 14+**
**Playing Time: 60-90 Minutes**

### Introduction

Project Chimera places you in the roles of specialized researchers at the Global Response Initiative (GRI), a clandestine organization tasked with containing a rapidly evolving biological threat. A genetically engineered virus, initially intended for agricultural advancements, has escaped containment and is now exhibiting unpredictable mutations and accelerating global spread. Your team must race against time, utilizing limited resources and facing difficult choices, to understand the virus, develop countermeasures, and prevent a global pandemic. Success depends on meticulous research, strategic resource allocation, and unwavering cooperation.

### Components

*   **Research Board:** A central game board depicting various global regions, each with associated research opportunities and escalating threat levels.
*   **Researcher Cards (8):** Each card represents a unique researcher with specialized skills and starting resources.
*   **Virus Mutation Cards (40):** These cards dictate the virus's evolving traits, introducing new challenges and modifying existing mechanics.
*   **Research Tokens (60):** Representing data points collected during research, used to advance on research tracks.
*   **Resource Tokens (120):** Representing Funding (blue), Personnel (green), and Equipment (yellow).
*   **Threat Tokens (50):** Placed on the Research Board to indicate escalating viral presence and potential outbreaks.
*   **Outbreak Markers (10):** Placed on regions experiencing uncontrolled viral spread.
*   **Containment Markers (10):** Used to temporarily mitigate outbreaks.
*   **Dice (3):** Six-sided dice used for research rolls and event resolution.
*   **First Player Token:** Designates the active player.
*   **Rulebook (this document).**

### Setup

1.  Place the Research Board in the center of the playing area.
2.  Shuffle the Virus Mutation Cards and place them face down in a designated draw pile.
3.  Place the Research Tokens, Resource Tokens, Threat Tokens, Outbreak Markers, and Containment Markers in accessible supply piles.
4.  Each player chooses a Researcher Card and takes the starting resources indicated on the card.
5.  Place three Threat Tokens on the designated starting regions on the Research Board, as indicated on the board.
6.  Draw the top three Virus Mutation Cards and place them face up beside the draw pile. These represent the initial viral traits.
7.  Determine the first player randomly and give them the First Player Token.

### Turn Structure

Each player's turn consists of the following phases, performed in order:

1.  **Draw Mutation Card:** Draw one Virus Mutation Card and add it to the face-up Mutation Cards. If there are more than five face-up Mutation Cards, discard one of your choice.
2.  **Action Phase (2 Actions):** You may perform two of the following actions, in any order. You may perform the same action twice.
    *   **Research:** Choose a research track on the Research Board. Roll two dice and add the result to your Researcher’s relevant skill (indicated on their Researcher Card). If the total meets or exceeds the target number indicated on the research track, place one Research Token on that track.
    *   **Gather Resources:** Choose one type of Resource Token (Funding, Personnel, or Equipment). Take two tokens of that type from the supply.
    *   **Contain Outbreak:** If an Outbreak Marker is present in a region, you may spend two Personnel tokens to place a Containment Marker on that region, temporarily mitigating the outbreak.
    *   **Move:** Move your Researcher to an adjacent region on the Research Board.
    *   **Share Resources:** Give one or more Resource Tokens to another player in the same region.
3.  **Viral Spread Phase:** Roll one die. Consult the Viral Spread Table on the Research Board. Place Threat Tokens on the indicated region(s), as directed by the table. If a region already has the maximum number of Threat Tokens, place an Outbreak Marker instead.
4.  **Pass the Token:** Pass the First Player Token to the player on your left.

### Win/Loss Conditions

**Winning the Game:**

You win the game if you successfully complete all research tracks on the Research Board. This signifies the development of a viable countermeasure and containment strategy.

**Losing the Game:**

You lose the game if any of the following conditions are met:

*   **Global Pandemic:** Five Outbreak Markers are present on the Research Board simultaneously.
*   **Resource Depletion:** The supply of any one type of Resource Token is exhausted.
*   **Researcher Incapacitation:** All Researcher Cards are discarded due to negative effects from Virus Mutation Cards.

### Difficulty Scaling

*   **2 Players:** Reduce the number of starting Threat Tokens to two.
*   **3 Players:** Play with the standard setup.
*   **4 Players:** Increase the number of starting Threat Tokens to four.

### Advanced Variants & Mechanics

1.  **Uncertainty Trade-Off: Mutation Prioritization:** When drawing a Virus Mutation Card, the team must collectively decide whether to immediately apply its effects or to discard it and draw another. Discarding a card provides a temporary reprieve, but also potentially delays the introduction of vital information about the virus. This forces a strategic trade-off between immediate risk mitigation and potential long-term benefits.
2.  **Resource Allocation Dilemma: Containment vs. Research:** At the end of each round (after all players have taken a turn), the team must decide how to allocate a limited number of Funding tokens to either Containment efforts (reducing the number of Outbreak Markers) or to Research (providing a bonus to research rolls). This creates a tension between immediate crisis management and long-term progress.
3.  **Risk Assessment: Specialized Research:** Certain Research Tracks require a “Specialized Research” roll, which demands a player with a matching skill on their Researcher Card. If no player possesses the required skill, the team must collectively spend Personnel tokens to attempt the roll, representing the recruitment of temporary expertise. Failure results in a setback, forcing the team to re-evaluate their strategy.



### Designer's Note

Project Chimera was designed to be more than just a game; it's intended as a thought-provoking exercise in collaborative problem-solving. The escalating crisis and the difficult choices presented throughout the game are meant to simulate the challenges faced by scientists and policymakers responding to real-world pandemics. The mechanics emphasize the importance of communication, resource management, and the acceptance of uncertainty. We hope that Project Chimera fosters a deeper appreciation for the complexities of scientific research and the vital role of teamwork in confronting global challenges.
