The abandoned plan involved deploying a high-speed rail network linking major metropolitan hubs across Region V by 2026. Initial funding was secured from a $50M grant from the Federal Infrastructure Fund, with a projected payback period of 10 years. The project faced abandonment due to three key factors: escalating construction costs (original estimate: $12B, now at $18B), delayed permitting from local environmental reviews, and rising opposition from rural communities citing loss of farmland and displacement concerns. Public sentiment shifted toward prioritizing local infrastructure, and legislative changes restricted public-private partnerships, leading to a halt on funding approvals. The original lead engineer resigned citing burnout, and alternative proposals focused on expanding existing transit corridors were preferred instead.
