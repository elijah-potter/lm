**The Last Transmission**

We didn’t mean for this to happen. The stars aligned in a way that made no sense to mortal hands—just a cosmic misfire, a universe that decided to play tricks. But the universe, as it turns out, is better at chaos than we are at resisting it.

They found us first.

Not in the way you’d expect—no grand betrayal, no whispered secrets passed between shadows. Just the way things do when they’re supposed to happen: the way a knife slips from a drawer, the way a door swings open on its own. The first time we saw it, the air had a different weight. The sky burned wrong, like someone had dipped it in molten gold and forgotten to ask for permission. The ground trembled—not from earthquakes, but from something older, something that had been waiting.

They called it *the Call*. A sound like a thousand voices at once, drowning out the world. And then, the silence. The kind that follows a scream you can’t hear because your own ears have stopped working.

We were supposed to be watching the stars. We were supposed to be building things, inventing things, forgetting that the universe was already doing all the inventing. But the Call was louder than our names. Louder than our plans. Louder than the sound of our own breath.

And then—

We were gone.

Not like they said. Not like we ever really *were* in the first place. Just… erased. Or perhaps we were never there at all. The void doesn’t care about our existence. It only knows how to swallow what it doesn’t understand.

Now we sit here, in the hollow places between the cracks of the world, watching as the machines keep running. They call them *the Echoes*—fragments of us, still humming, still trying. Still asking for answers that aren’t theirs to give.

We didn’t mean for this to happen. But the universe doesn’t ask permission. It just does.

And so we wait.

For what, we don’t know.

But the stars still burn.
