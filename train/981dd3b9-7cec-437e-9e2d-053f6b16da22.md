**The Last Light**
The streetlamp flickered like a dying ember, casting long shadows across the cracked pavement. Around me, the city pulsed with a thousand unheeding lives—some hurrying to work, others lost in their own worlds. My watch read 11:47 PM, but the time didn’t matter anymore. What mattered was the weight in my pocket, the one thing I’d forgotten to return.

A hand clutched my wrist, jolting me. "You," the voice whispered, sharp as a blade. The stranger—tall, gaunt, with eyes that held the hollow of a grave—didn’t introduce himself. Just pointed at the paper in my hand. *"Remember this."* The words scrolled across my mind like a warning, but I barely registered them. My fingers trembled as I unfolded the folded note inside: *"The door is always open. Just don’t let it close on you."*

A car horn blared in the distance. The night swallowed the sound, but not the guilt. I had a debt to settle. A debt I’d been running from for years. The streetlamp’s light dimmed. The city waited. And somewhere, beyond the veil of exhaustion, I knew the next step.
