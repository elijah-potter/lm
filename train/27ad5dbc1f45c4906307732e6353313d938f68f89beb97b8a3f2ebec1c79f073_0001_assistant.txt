I found some information about the Legend of Faust you might be looking for. 

Faust, often called Faustus, is a scholar that made a deal with the devil in exchange for his soul. Although the literature is fictional, it's based on a German astrologer and alchemist from the fifteenth century. 

Turning away from theology, he becomes tempted by magic and makes a deal with the devil. Power and knowledge at the price of eternal damnation of his soul. The question at the core of this is: Is it possible for a human to hold the powers of God, yet resist the temptations of the Devil? 

The legend has grown large inspiring a range of great writers, musicians, and other artists throughout the years. The earliest known published sources for the legend are the Wolfenbüttler manuscript which was released around 1580-1587. As well as a printed chapbook in 1587 (the Spies edition) which was intended for popular sale.  

If you want two works that focus on these themes check out Christopher Marlowe's The Tragical History of Doctor Faustus and Johann Wolfgang Von Goethe's Faust. 

While many find Goethe's Faust to be the official one, there is no definitive story. There are thousands of variations that exist in theatre, music, film, poetry, and other literary works.
