The ruins of **El Dorado’s Last Shadow**, a forgotten citadel lost in the heart of the **Copper Peaks**—where legends claim the last gold-smiters of the lost empire buried their hoard beneath the **Whispering Stones**. The ruins stand in a place called **"The Dying Eye"** now, a crater where the sky meets the earth in a fractured gaze. Locals whisper that if you stand at the center of the arching stone slabs, the wind carries the echo of a thousand voices singing the same song: *"We were never lost. We were waiting."*

The **Black River**, once a river of silver, now flows through the skeletal remains of a dock city where ships with no names were built for a trade that never arrived. The water is thick with silt and rust, carrying secrets of lost deals and forgotten gods. Fishermen catch **silverfish**—creatures that glow faintly in the dark, their bodies covered in the same dull gold as the ruins’ crumbling towers.

At the heart of the ruins, beneath a dome of blackened coral, rests the **Chamber of Echoes**. No one enters. Those who have tried say the walls hum with the voices of those who tried to find what was never there—cursed by the same ghost that haunts the **Bone Bridge**, a span of skeletal fingers stretching over the river, where the water beneath is always still. Some say it’s the last bridge to the **Underwater City of the Forgotten**, where time runs backward, and the past and future bleed together. Others claim it’s just a story to keep people away.

The only constant is the **Song of the Last Lighthouse**, a melody played by the wind through the ruins’ chimes. It plays itself, never stopping, never fading. Some say it’s the echo of the last lighthouse keeper, still searching for the ship that never came. Others say it’s the universe’s way of reminding us that some places aren’t lost—they’re just waiting to be found again.
