I was standing outside a dimly lit alley, waiting for my shift to end at the local diner. The air smelled of rain and old cigarette smoke. A car horn blared suddenly, jarring me from my thoughts. Through the haze of my coffee, I saw the back of a woman pushing a stroller, her coat fluttering in the wind. She turned, and her face lit up as she spotted me.

"Oh, you must be the guy from the diner," she called, her voice echoing slightly. "I’ve been looking for you all day."

She grabbed my arm and pulled me closer. The stroller squeaked as she maneuvered it through the alleyway, her hands trembling slightly. "My husband’s car broke down at the service station near the highway. I’ve been driving all day, and I just couldn’t stop. I didn’t want to leave my kids behind."

She leaned in, her breath warm against my ear. "You were the last person I saw from the diner. I just couldn’t resist. I hope you don’t mind?"

I swallowed hard, glancing at the stroller. "Of course not. What can I do?"

She hesitated, then pulled out a crumpled note from her pocket. "This is our address. We’re staying with my sister until we figure something out. Please, come by tomorrow. I’d really appreciate it."

She pushed the stroller ahead, and I watched her go, my chest tight. The night was quiet except for the distant hum of traffic. I turned back to the diner, my mind racing. That note was all I needed. I’d make sure she and her kids were safe.
