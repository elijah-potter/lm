**Eldermere, December 15, 2023** – The annual **Grand Harvest Festival** in Eldermere’s historic **Market Square** drew over 1,200 attendees today, despite a mild rain that dampened some spirits. Local farmers showcased their latest crop varieties, including a rare **black corn** strain rumored to be resistant to drought, sparking interest among urban gardeners. The festival’s signature **Midnight Feast**—a communal meal of slow-cooked meats, spiced pumpkin soup, and honeyed apples—was a hit, with lines wrapping around the square by 6 PM.

The city’s **Eldermere Farmers’ Guild** reported a 12% increase in local produce sales since the festival’s launch last year. Mayor Elias Voss praised the event’s cultural revival, noting, *"We’re proving Eldermere’s roots are as strong as ever."* A minor incident occurred when a vendor’s stall collapsed under the weight of a stacked barrel of cider, injuring two spectators. Emergency crews arrived within minutes, and no further damage was reported.

Tonight’s **street performers**—a trio of musicians playing traditional fiddle and accordion—were praised for their spontaneous rendition of *"The Old Harvest Song."* Residents urged the council to consider permanent funding for the festival, citing its economic benefits. The next event, the **Winter Solstice Lantern Walk**, is scheduled for January 21, with early registrations already at capacity.

No major disruptions were reported outside of the festival, though a minor traffic jam formed near the **East Bridge** due to pedestrian crowds. The city’s **Eldermere Historical Preservation Society** plans to analyze festival data to expand programming in 2024, focusing on sustainable agriculture and community storytelling.
