The rainwater in the cracked sidewalk pool evaporated into morning mist by noon, leaving behind a thin, barely visible film of dew. The city’s air smelled like wet concrete and old pavement, but for one man in a worn-out raincoat, it was the quiet proof that winter still held secrets—like the way the sun, after three days of gray, finally turned the steel of a rusted fire hydrant from dull gray to a faint, unnoticed bronze. He’d wiped it clean yesterday. That was his victory: a single, invisible act of care in a world that only noticed when the fire hydrant bled red. The rest of the city walked by, unaware.
