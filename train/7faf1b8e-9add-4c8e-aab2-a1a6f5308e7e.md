**The Last Letter**

The ink smudged across the page like a forgotten ghost, the quill still trembling in my grip. I had written this letter a week ago, but the time had blurred, and now it lay untouched beside me, half-empty coffee stain spreading like a stain of regret.

*"Dearest [Name],"* the words had begun, but they stopped mid-sentence when the phone buzzed—a text from an old friend: *"You forgot your birthday party. Don’t be late."*

I’d typed a hasty reply: *"Of course I’ll be there. You know me."* But the lie had settled like a stone in my chest, heavy as the silence between us now.

The clock struck midnight. My alarm would go off in an hour, but I’d already overslept twice this week. The reminder on my wristband buzzed again—*Breakfast at 8:30 AM*—but my stomach had forgotten hunger.

Outside, the city hummed, indifferent. I’d left the door unlocked, the key still in my pocket, but the house felt colder now, the air thick with the weight of unfinished things.

I reached for the letter again, fingers brushing the faded ink. *"If you’re reading this, it means I didn’t make it."* The words had been half-hearted, but now they felt final. The pen hovered over the page, then fell silent.

A door slammed upstairs. My mother’s voice, sharp with exhaustion: *"Eat. Now."*

I exhaled, the breath shallow, the letter forgotten in the rush of the moment. The reminder on my phone chimed again—*Meeting at 9:15*—but I didn’t look. The past was a story I’d never finish, and the future was already writing itself in ink too thick to erase.
