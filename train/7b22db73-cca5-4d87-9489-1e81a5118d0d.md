The wind carried whispers of secrets from the old mill, its stone foundations whispering back as if echoing the past itself. Clara pressed her palm against the cold metal door, the key turning slowly, reluctant to let go of what it held.

*"Why do they say the mill still breathes at night?"* she asked aloud, her voice lost in the hush of the forest. The answer, if there were one, was not in the words she spoke but in the silence between the cracks of the old walls. The water in the basin below the door was still, unnaturally so, reflecting neither sky nor shadow. A single spider spun its web across the surface, its legs trembling as if holding its breath too.

She turned back toward the fire pit, the embers glowing faintly. The last of the evening’s meal was gone, and the kitchen was too quiet. The old clock on the wall—its hands frozen at 11:11—ticked like a drumbeat of something waiting.

*"Did you see it too?"* she whispered, her reflection in the cracked mirror above the hearth staring back blankly. The question hung between them, heavier than the air itself. What had been left behind in the mill’s final moments?
