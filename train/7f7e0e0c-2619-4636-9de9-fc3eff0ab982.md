A tiny ring, once worn by a soldier’s mother, now sits on a shelf—its engraved date marking the day her son returned home. The metal bears faint scars where a lockpick once cracked it open; the key was his, not hers. The ring, though worn thin, holds the weight of unspoken love and the quiet pride of a mother who never asked for the battle she lost. To touch it is to remember: some things are meant to be kept small.
