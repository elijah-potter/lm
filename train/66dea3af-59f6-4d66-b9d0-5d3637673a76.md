The dim glow of the flickering CRT screen cast long shadows across the worn leather couch. The coffee cup, half-empty, sat untouched beside a half-smoked cigarette, its ash scattered over the ashtray. A slow drip from the tap behind him sounded like a metronome counting down time.

Across the room, the TV hummed a static-filled channel, the sound of a news anchor’s voice muffled by the TV’s static. The door creaked open, then shut again silently. Outside, a distant car alarm blared, cut short by the sirens of police sirens.

"Breaking news—" The voice crackled, then faded into static.

A hand reached for the remote. The screen went blank.
