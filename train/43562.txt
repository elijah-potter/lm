Addiction Treatment – What Exactly Is it?
Addiction treatment refers to interventions that are made to help those that are struggling with substance addiction stop substance use. It also helps to improve their lives, work, family, and general well-being.
Sometimes, some persons may take the initiative to seek out this treatment themselves once they realize that they cannot help themselves to quit, whereas some do it because they have been mandated to. Whichever the case, addiction treatment is a good call, and most times, the outcome is always positive.
A holistic approach typically involves several therapeutic therapies and treatments combined including:
- Behavioral therapies
- Medical detox
- Support groups
- Individual and group counseling
- Family therapy
- Social services like financial, legal, and housing services
How these services will be combined, and the treatment’s duration is often based on the patient’s particular needs. Often, the needs will change with time, and when it does, the approach being used would also be evaluated and adjusted or changed to fit the changing needs.
Addiction happens to be a chronic condition and like other chronic conditions, the patient can relapse even after the rehab program is complete. A release does not mean the program didn’t work. However, sometimes, it means that the approaches used may need adjustment with time so the recovery pace can be maintained.
What To Expect
When you start a drug rehab, the treatment’s intensity would be based on your needs. Below is the level of treatment care according to The American Society for Addiction Medicine:
- Outpatient services
- Partial hospitalization or intensive outpatient
- Inpatient services or residential
- Intensive inpatient
The professional typically will recommend an ideal plan for the patient with these levels in mind. This would be based on several assessments including support, individual risks, as well as other needs.
Throughout the process, several approaches would be taken, and they may include individual or/and group therapies, medications, and sometimes, a mix of both.

Detox refers to substance withdrawal in a supervised environment with a management period that allows the patient’s body to stabilize in the early stages of the treatment. The person typically would be kept as safe and comfortable as possible so that their body can remove the substances it.
