It wasn’t supposed to happen like this.

The first time I saw her, the world had already tilted.
Her laugh—sharp, like glass breaking—cut through the static hum of the neon-lit alley,
and I was already wrong. Wrong about the way my fingers trembled before I reached for her hand,
wrong about the way her skin burned under my palm,
wrong about the way she didn’t look back.

She called me late that night, voice thin as sugar,
*"You’re not supposed to find someone like this,"*
but I knew better than to believe the first words
that slipped from a mouth that had already lied.

She was a ghost in my blood,
a shadow that didn’t fade.
And the night after that,
I found her name on a receipt—
her address, her phone number—
written in ink so dark it could’ve been her own blood.

Now the house is too quiet.
The walls remember the way her breath hitched
when I pressed her against the cold pavement,
how her nails dug into my shoulders
as the stars above us
were just another promise she never kept.
