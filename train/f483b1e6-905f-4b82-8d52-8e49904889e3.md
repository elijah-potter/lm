The collector spent hours poring over vintage postcards, meticulously noting the faded ink, cracked edges, and marginalia—hidden messages, doodles, and erasures—each holding a story. Their desk was a chaotic mosaic of receipts, pressed flowers, and newspaper clippings, each item labeled with a tiny handwritten note: *"Tiffany’s 1978 haircut"* or *"The day my grandfather’s watch broke."* They kept a pocket notebook for "small wonders," jotting down the sound of rain on a tin roof, the weight of a particular key, the way a streetlamp flickered at 9:17 PM. Their friends called them a hoarder, but they saw the world as a museum—every detail a clue, every shadow a clue to something unseen. One night, they found a lost coin in the gutter, not for its value, but for the inscription on the back: *"To my future self—keep looking."* They kept it in their wallet, alongside a single, crumpled business card from a stranger who’d left their number on a napkin.
