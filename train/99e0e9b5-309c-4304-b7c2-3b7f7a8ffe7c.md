A vast, glass-enclosed chamber hangs suspended in a misty valley, its floor a cracked, rust-stained carpet of ancient stone. The air is thick with the scent of damp earth and ozone, the only sounds the distant hum of machinery and the occasional whisper of wind through the jagged edges of the ceiling. The walls pulse faintly with a low, unnatural light—like a heartbeat—reflecting in pools of water that have collected in the depressions of the stone. Above, the sky is a heavy, overcast expanse, its edges blurring into the chamber’s own oppressive glow. Every movement seems to pause, as if the world is waiting, holding its breath, for something to break the stillness.
