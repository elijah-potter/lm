•	Grizzly Bears. 
These bears can weigh up to 1,500 pounds, especially before hibernation. They fatten up through summer to prepare for the long sleep of winter. 

•	Tiger Sharks. 
These sharks can swim down to depths of 1,100 feet or more, which is a cold, dark place for sea creatures. These sharks can reach over 1,000 pounds to insulate themselves for warmth. 

•	Elephant Seals. 
These seals can reach weights of 3,000 to 9,000 pounds, depending on their environment. The warmth of accumulated blubber protects them from cold climates, while their plush bodies protect them from the aggressive nature of other elephant seals. 

•	African Elephants. 
These elephants are the largest land animals in the world. They can grow up to 16,500 pounds, which comes in handy for protecting their young from predators in the wild. 

•	Blue Whales. 
These whales are the biggest known animals in existence. Their typical weights hover around 330,000 pounds, but there have been record cases of blue whales that reached 380,000 pounds. 

•	Hippopotamus. 
Hippos are massive and aggressive by nature. They are some of the largest, most dangerous animals on the planet, weighing in at 3,000 pounds to 6,800 pounds. 

The aforementioned six animals, as well as many other animals on land and in the seas, weigh 1,000 pounds or more for a wide range of reasons. The most common reason is warmth in colder climates, while the second reason is protection from predators. The bigger an animal is, the less likely they are to be targeted by a more dangerous animal in the wild.
