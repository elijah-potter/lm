The hallway’s dim glow cast long shadows as the older woman knelt beside the half-empty coffee mug on the floor, her knuckles white around the edge of the broken ceramic. The loose fragment of the saucer—half the porcelain still glinting—sat atop a crumpled napkin, its once-white surface now stained with tea and something darker. She traced the jagged rim with trembling fingers, the sound of her breath sharp against the chipped enamel.

"Damn it," she muttered, dusting off her coat. The missing piece of the broken mug lay in the recycling bin, half-packed with yesterday’s trash. She’d found it there earlier—sliced thin, the shape of her favorite teacup.
