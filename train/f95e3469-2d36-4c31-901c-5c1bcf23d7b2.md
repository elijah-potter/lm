The rain hammered against the grimy windows of the dimly lit bar as two strangers—**Daniel**, a sharp-witted ex-cop in a stained jacket, and **Lena**, a sharp-tongued journalist in a leather jacket—slumped into adjacent booths, arms crossed. A neon sign flickered *LOST IN TRANSLATION* above them.

Daniel’s voice was low, bitter. *"You really think you can just walk into a place like this and ask questions without a damn warrant?"*
Lena smirked, swirling her whiskey. *"I don’t need one. I’m not here for warrants, Daniel. I’m here for answers—and you, apparently, are the only one who’s got them."*

A beat. The clink of glass. Then, outside, a cop car’s siren wails—too close. Daniel’s phone buzzed. **Unknown number.** He swiped it away. *"You’re lucky I’m not the cop you’re looking for."*

Lena’s laugh was sharp. *"Lucky? I’m the one who’s been tracking this case since before it was a case. You’re the one who’s been hiding in plain sight."* She leaned forward. *"You’re selling something—drugs, maybe? Or worse, that damn tech you were talking about before you disappeared."*

Daniel’s fingers tightened around his glass. *"It’s not drugs. It’s not tech. It’s just… a mistake."* He exhaled through his nose. *"And if you’re not careful, you’ll make it worse."*

The booth creaked as Lena’s chair tilted. *"Mistakes don’t pay the bills, Daniel. And neither do you."* Outside, the sirens faded. Then—footsteps. Too many. Too fast.

Lena’s eyes narrowed. *"You heard them. Someone’s coming."* She grabbed her bag. *"We need to go."*
