*"The Great Library of Alexandria Fire of 1783: A Local Incident Turned Into a Myth"*

On the evening of **November 24th, 1783**, a single candle accidentally left unlit in a scholar’s workshop at the **Alexandria Public Library** (then a makeshift facility in the old **Bibliotheque Nationale** basement) ignited the adjacent **papyrus scroll storage**. The fire was contained within minutes due to nearby **olive oil lamps** and a rapid evacuation of the staff, but the damage was extensive. Only a dozen scrolls—mostly historical letters and minor texts—were irreparably damaged, though some were salvaged by librarian **Sofia Marinos**, who used her father’s **waterproof leather bindings** to preserve fragments.

The event was largely forgotten in records, but rumors spread among **Jewish traders in Jaffa** who claimed the fire was "divine punishment" for a failed **Egyptian tax protest** the previous year. A single **anonymous manuscript** (now lost) later surfaced in **Naples**, detailing how a **"sacred fire"** had consumed the library’s **"eternal texts"**—a claim later dismissed as a **satanic hoax** by the local inquisitor. No official report was filed, but the incident became a cautionary tale in **Arabic oral history**, where it was retold as *"The Night the Stars Burned"*—a tale of a world where knowledge was fragile and fire was both a thief and a judge.

*(Note: No historical documents confirm this event. All details are speculative fiction.)*
