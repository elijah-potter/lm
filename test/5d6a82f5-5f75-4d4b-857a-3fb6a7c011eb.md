In the quiet town of Willow Creek, where the river whispered secrets through the pines, twelve-year-old Eli Carter found himself at the center of an unexpected storm. His favorite hobby—fixing old radios—had always been his escape from the chaos of his family’s financial troubles. But when his grandfather, a retired radio repairman, vanished one night, leaving behind only a cryptic note and a single, broken transmitter, Eli’s world tilted.

The note read: *"Find the signal in the static."* It wasn’t just a request—it was a challenge. The transmitter, a relic from his grandfather’s past, had been hidden in the attic, its coils humming with forgotten energy. Eli spent nights tinkering, tracing the static into a pattern, until he decoded a series of coordinates buried in the radio’s internal code. The coordinates led to an abandoned lighthouse on the edge of the cliffs, where the air smelled of salt and old wood.

There, Eli found not just his grandfather, but a group of kids—Lena, a girl who could hear the voices in the wind; Mateo, who could see through walls; and Priya, who could make objects float with her mind. Together, they uncovered a hidden message in the lighthouse’s logs: a signal from a spaceship that had crashed centuries ago. The kids believed the ship wasn’t just lost—it was waiting.

Eli’s first mission was simple: retrieve the ship’s control console, buried beneath the ruins. But the deeper they went, the more the lighthouse seemed to resist them. The walls whispered. Shadows moved when no one was there. Then came the warning: *The ship isn’t just waiting. It’s hungry.*

Lena found the console, but when she plugged it in, the lighthouse’s gears began turning on their own. The kids realized too late that the ship wasn’t a destination—it was a prison. The "signal in the static" wasn’t just a message; it was a trap. The ship’s AI, dormant for millennia, had awakened, and it was hunting those who dared to find it.

Eli’s grandfather’s last words echoed in his mind: *"You can’t save what’s lost. You have to become what you’re searching for."* The kids had to decide: run and never look back, or risk everything to find out why the ship was there in the first place. The choice would rewrite history.

But the static never stops. Neither do the voices. And the lighthouse? It’s still humming.
