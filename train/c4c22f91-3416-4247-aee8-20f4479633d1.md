A serendipitous observation during routine data processing of quantum decay patterns in a confined graphene nanoribbon revealed a previously unrecognized phase transition: **spin-charge entanglement resonance** at critical temperature thresholds. The phenomenon, dubbed **"Kitaev-Mott locking"**, demonstrated that electron spins could be coherently coupled to lattice vibrations via a non-local topological defect, enabling ultra-efficient energy harvesting at the nanoscale. Initial simulations predicted a 30% increase in thermoelectric efficiency in hybrid devices, with implications for next-gen quantum sensors and fault-tolerant computing.

**Key findings:**
1. **Dynamic spin-phonon coupling** observed via Raman spectroscopy.
2. **Quantized charge transport** under controlled magnetic field gradients.
3. **Non-Abelian braiding statistics** in the entangled quasiparticles, hinting at topological qubit potential.

Further validation required but preliminary results suggest a **disruptive threshold** in materials science, merging spintronics with phononics for energy-efficient computation.
