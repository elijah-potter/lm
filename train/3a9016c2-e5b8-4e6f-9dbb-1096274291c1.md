The fire crackled low in the hearth, casting flickering shadows across the worn tapestry. Elias sat hunched over his mug, the steam curling into the dim light. Outside, a winter storm howled, rattling the windows. Across from him, Mara shifted in her chair, her fingers tracing the rim of her glass.

"Listen to this," she murmured, her voice barely above the crackling fire.

Elias leaned forward. "What?"

"Once, there was a man who walked into a tavern in Black Hollow," Mara began, her fingers weaving tales in the air. "He sat alone, his cloak damp, his face etched with exhaustion. The barkeep, a grizzled old man with a scar running down his cheek, barely glanced up as Elias ordered a drink.

The man—let’s call him Jace—didn’t speak. He just stared at his ale, the liquid swirling lazily before him. The barkeep, sensing something amiss, asked, ‘What’s your story, stranger?’ Jace exhaled sharply, like he’d been holding his breath for years.

‘I’m looking for my sister,’ he said, voice rough. ‘She’s been missing for three winters. Every town I’ve visited has the same answer: she’s not here. But I keep coming back anyway.’ The barkeep’s eyes flickered with something like pity.

Jace’s fingers tightened around his mug. ‘She was the last person to see my brother alive. The last person to say he’d be back before dawn.’ He paused, swallowing hard. ‘I’ve been walking for three years. Every step brings me closer to the truth—or to the end of it.’

The tavern fell silent. The only sound was the clinking of glasses and the distant wail of the wind. Then, the barkeep said, ‘You’re not the first to ask. The townsfolk say she’s still out there—somewhere. But they won’t tell me where.’ He leaned in, his voice dropping. ‘They say she’s hiding.’

Jace’s jaw clenched. ‘Hide from what?’

‘From the men who came looking for her.’ A beat of silence. ‘They didn’t find her. But they didn’t leave.’

Jace’s eyes darkened. ‘Who?’

‘The Hollow Men,’ Mara whispered. ‘The ones who live in the caves beneath the old church. They don’t speak. They only watch. And they remember.’

Elias’ breath hitched. The firelight danced on Mara’s face, sharp and unreadable. She was quiet for a long moment, her gaze fixed on the embers. Then, softly: ‘They say the Hollow Men don’t take. They just… remember.’
