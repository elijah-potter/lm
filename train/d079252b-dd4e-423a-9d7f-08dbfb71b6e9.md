**INT. COFFEE SHOP – DAY**

A mid-30s man in a rumpled sweater sits at a corner table, laptop open, half-drunk coffee in front of him. His posture is slumped—one elbow on the table, the other draped over the keyboard—head tilted slightly, eyes half-closed. Across from him, a woman with sharp features and a no-nonsense expression leans forward, arms crossed, voice low and deliberate.

**WOMAN:** *(sighs)* You really think you can just *pretend* this will go away?

He exhales sharply through his nose, fingers drumming restlessly on the keyboard. His jaw tightens, but he doesn’t look up.

**MAN:** *(flat)* No.

A beat. The hum of the shop—conversations, clinking mugs—fades into the background. She leans in closer, voice dropping.

**WOMAN:** You *know* what I’m saying. You *know* how this ends.

He swallows hard, fingers still tapping. Then—slowly—his head snaps up. For a fraction of a second, his eyes meet hers. Something unspoken passes between them. Then he slumps back, voice a rasp.

**MAN:** You’re not listening.

Silence. The weight of the unspoken hangs between them. The woman exhales, frustration bleeding into her next words.

**WOMAN:** *(cold)* Then don’t bother.

Outside, a car horn blares. The scene cuts to black.
