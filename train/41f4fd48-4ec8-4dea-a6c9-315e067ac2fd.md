In the hollowed-out core of an old library, where books still whispered secrets to the wind, Professor Elias Voss found his last notebook. The ink had faded, but the pages held the remnants of his final theory—one that defied the laws of time itself. *"What if memory isn’t stored in the brain but woven into the fabric of the stars?"* he had scribbled, his trembling hand barely managing the words. Outside, the clock tower struck midnight, its chime echoing like a funeral bell.

Elias stood before the grand clock, its gears rusted with age. The hands moved slowly, as if in protest. *"Why do we remember what we forget?"* he asked aloud, though no one answered. The air smelled of damp paper and something older—something that tasted like the universe itself. His fingers traced the edge of the page where he had penciled the coordinates of a star no human had ever mapped: *HD 191089*. A red giant, cold and distant, yet his pulse quickened at the thought of it. *"Is it watching us?"* he whispered, and the library’s shadows seemed to answer.
