I stood frozen in the rain-soaked alley, my coat damp and my breath fogging the air. The streetlights flickered, casting long shadows that twisted into shapes I didn’t want to see. A group of men—some I recognized, others not—were gathered around a body slumped against a dumpster. One of them, a burly guy with a scar running down his cheek, was shouting something about "fucking cops" while another, smaller man with a trembling hand, clutched a cell phone. The third, a guy who looked like he’d been drinking, was laughing too loudly, his eyes glazed.

The sirens were getting closer. I wanted to walk away, but my feet wouldn’t move. The stench of alcohol and something metallic was thick in the air. The smaller man’s phone buzzed—a text from someone named "Dad." He pocketed it quickly, then glanced at the body. His face paled.

Then, the sirens were right there. The sirens were too close. I ran.
