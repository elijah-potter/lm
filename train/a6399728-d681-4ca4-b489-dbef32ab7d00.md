In the quiet hum of the old clock tower, where the hands moved like shadows, I found myself confessing something I’d never shared—just a small truth, like a seed in the wind.

I had been keeping it for years, buried deep in the corners of my memory, afraid it would drown me if I let it out. The truth was this: I had once stolen a book from a library because I wanted to keep it. Not because it was mine to take, but because I needed to read it again and again, and the rules of borrowing made it impossible.

The librarian had never found me. But that didn’t matter. What mattered was the way my hands trembled as I folded the pages neatly back into the spine, as if I could erase the act by pretending it never happened. I had lied to myself for so long that the lie had grown roots. Now, as I stood there, confessing it aloud, the weight of the words felt like a stone in my chest. The truth didn’t change anything—it only revealed how much I had loved the story, how much I had wanted it to stay mine.
