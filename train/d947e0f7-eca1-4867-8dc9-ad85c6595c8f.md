The neon hum of the abandoned subway tunnel pulsed through the cracked concrete like a dying heartbeat. Dust swirled in the stale air, thick enough to choke a man. You shouldn’t be here, but here you were—clutching a crumpled receipt from the last café in the district, the one that still served coffee before the storm took it.

A rusted metal door creaked behind you, the sound sharp as a blade. The fluorescent lights flickered, casting long, wavering shadows across the floor. The walls were lined with graffiti—names scrawled in black marker, faces frozen in time, their mouths stretched in silent screams. You pressed your palm against one, the ink still warm. It was *you*.

A voice, thin as paper, whispered from the darkness. *"You shouldn’t be here."* The words slithered from the shadows like smoke. Your pulse hammered in your throat. The door behind you groaned again, the hinge groaning as if it, too, remembered the weight of what came next.

The air smelled of ozone and something older—something like rain that never fell. The subway was a grave, and you were the first body buried beneath the tracks. The last thing you saw before the darkness swallowed you whole was the receipt in your hand, the ink smudged, the words blurred: *"Last call. Do not enter."*
