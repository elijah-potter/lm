The morning sun spilled through the cracks in Emma’s bedroom window, painting stripes of gold across the floor. She groaned, pushing her pillow into her face, but the familiar ache in her head wouldn’t budge. *Another night of waking up with a headache.* She groaned again, rolling over to check her phone. The alarm had gone off at 6:15 AM—just like always. Her fingers flew over the screen, scrolling through the endless stream of TikTok videos before she finally clicked the snooze button. *Five more minutes.*

Outside, the neighborhood was already alive with the hum of cars, the clatter of bikes, and the distant shout of a neighbor’s dog. Emma’s mom had already been up for hours, making breakfast while her dad dozed in the armchair. She didn’t need to be up yet. Not today.

But then she heard it: her little brother, Jake, crying in the bathroom. Emma’s stomach twisted. She hadn’t heard him cry in days. The sound was sharp, desperate, like he was in pain. She sat up, rubbing her eyes, and made her way down the hall. The bathroom door was ajar. She pushed it open, stepping inside.

Jake was curled up on the floor, his knees pulled to his chest, his breath ragged. A small, bloodstained bandage was wrapped around his forehead, but it didn’t stop the way he was shaking. “I’m sorry,” he whispered, his voice cracking. “I just… I don’t know what happened.”

Emma’s heart pounded. She had seen Jake get into fights before, but this wasn’t like him. Not like *him*. She knelt beside him, pulling him into a hug. “Shh,” she murmured. “It’s okay. Tell me what happened.”

Jake swallowed hard, his eyes burning with unshed tears. “Last night, I was at the park with my friends. We were playing baseball, and then—” He cut himself off, shaking his head. “I don’t know. Something went wrong. I hit someone. I don’t even remember. But when I woke up, I was covered in blood.”

Emma’s hands trembled. She remembered the way Jake had always been careful, how he’d avoided getting into trouble. This was different. *So much different.*

“You don’t have to tell me,” she said gently. “But I’m gonna take you to the doctor.”

---

Later, at the urgent care clinic, Jake sat stiffly in the waiting room while Emma sat beside him, her fingers tangled in her lap. The doctor, a kind woman with silver hair, examined Jake’s head and hands. She didn’t ask about the fight. She just asked, *“How long have you been like this?”*

Jake’s shoulders slumped. “Since last night.”

The doctor nodded. “We’ll get some scans, just to be sure. But I think we need to talk about what happened.” She turned to Emma. “Have you noticed anything different in your brother lately?”

Emma hesitated. “Not really. He’s always been quiet. But he’s always been careful.”

The doctor’s eyes flicked to her. “Careful is one thing. But if he’s been avoiding people, that’s not normal.”

Emma’s stomach twisted. She had seen Jake’s friends avoid him. They’d been mean. *“Jake’s a crybaby,”* one of them had called behind his back. *“He doesn’t know how to fight.”* Emma had wanted to fight for him, but she was scared.

“It’s not his fault,” Emma said softly.

The doctor smiled. “No. It’s not. But if he keeps avoiding things, it could get worse.”

---

That night, Emma sat in the kitchen, staring at the open box of Jake’s baseball gloves on the table. She had bought them last week, just in case. But she hadn’t used them.

Her phone buzzed. A text from her mom: *“You’re late. Dinner’s getting cold.”*

Emma sighed, typing a quick reply: *“On my way.”* She packed Jake’s gloves into her backpack, along with the doctor’s note and a new, smaller bandage. She didn’t want to ask too many questions tonight. She just wanted to get him home safe.

---

The park was louder than usual. The air smelled of hot dogs and cheap beer, and the scent of sweat and dirt filled her nose. Jake’s friends—all of them—were already gathered around a game of kickball. Emma hesitated, her fingers tightening around her backpack. She should’ve stayed inside. But she was here now.

Jake saw her first. His face paled. “Emma,” he said, his voice small. “What are you doing here?”

She swallowed. “I brought you something.”

He glanced at her backpack, then back at her. “You don’t have to—”

“*I do.*” She held up the gloves. “I thought you might need this.”

Jake’s eyes widened. He took the gloves, examining them. “This is for me?” His voice cracked.

Emma nodded. “It’s for fighting.”

Jake looked at her like she was crazy. “You think I need to fight?”

Emma hesitated. “I don’t know. But I think you should try.”

Jake’s shoulders shook. He didn’t say anything for a long time. Then, slowly, he nodded. “Okay.”

Emma watched as Jake stepped forward, his hand trembling as he took his place on the field. The other kids laughed when he didn’t run to the plate. Emma wanted to scream, but she stayed silent. She couldn’t let him fail again.

---

Three days later, the clinic called. The scans were clear. Jake was fine. But the doctor had something else to say.

“There’s no medical reason for your brother’s behavior,” she said, her voice firm. “But I think he’s hiding something.”

Emma’s stomach dropped. “What?”

“He’s been avoiding people. Avoiding fights. And now, he’s avoiding *me*.”

Jake’s face was pale. “I’m sorry.”

Emma’s hands clenched. “He doesn’t have to be sorry. But he *has* to talk about it.”

The doctor nodded. “I’ll give you both a referral to a therapist. But I think the first step is for him to face his friends.”

Jake’s breath hitched. “Face them?”

Emma swallowed. “Yeah. Face them. And tell them the truth.”

Jake’s eyes filled with tears. “I don’t know what to say.”

Emma reached for his hand. “You don’t have to say anything. Just stand up for yourself.”

---

The next morning, Jake sat at the kitchen table, his backpack on his lap. Emma watched him from the doorway, her heart heavy. She had told him what to say. *“I’m not a crybaby,”* she had told him. *“I’m not weak. And neither are you.”*

Jake took a deep breath, then stood up. He didn’t run to the field. He didn’t even look at his friends. He just walked straight toward them, his shoulders straight.

The first guy laughed. “Hey, Jake. Thought you were done.”

Jake didn’t flinch. “I’m not.”

The others hesitated. Then, one by one, they looked at him. “What do you want?” one of them asked.

Jake met their eyes. “I want to know what you’re talking about.”

The guy smirked. “You hit someone last night.”

Jake’s face burned. He took a step forward. “I didn’t mean to. But I won’t let anyone else hurt me.”

The others stared at him. Then, slowly, they started to back away.

Jake didn’t chase them. He just stood there, his fists clenched at his sides.

Emma’s throat tightened. She had never seen Jake like this. Never.

---

Later, as they sat on the couch, Jake didn’t talk. He just stared at the TV, his hands folded in his lap. Emma knew what he was thinking.

*What if I’m wrong? What if I’m not strong enough? What if I can’t fix this?*

She reached for his hand. “You’re not wrong,” she said gently. “You’re *right*. You were scared. And that’s okay. But you don’t have to be alone.”

Jake looked at her, his eyes wet. “Thanks.”

Emma smiled. “For what?”

He shrugged. “For making me fight.”

She nodded. “For making you *you*.”

---

The days that followed were harder. Jake’s friends avoided him. The schoolyard was quieter. But Jake wasn’t the same. He still got angry sometimes. Still cried when he was scared. But he was standing up for himself. And that was enough.

One evening, as Emma was walking Jake home from school, he turned to her. “I don’t know what happened,” he said softly. “But I’m glad you were there.”

Emma smiled. “I’m glad you’re okay.”

Jake nodded. “Me too.”

And for the first time in a long time, Emma believed it.
