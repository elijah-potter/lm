- A 4-in-1 screwdriver. This tool has a set of regular and Philips heads on two bits that store inside the semi-hollow stem. This should be enough to tackle most minor repairs and maintenance, but if you find the bit sizes a little too small or large for what you need, consider a set of screwdrivers that either have more individual tools or a larger set of bits. Keep in mind that having a longer stem on a single tool may help you get into narrower areas. 
- A hammer. These come in various shapes and sizes, but a basic claw-style hammer will get most jobs done. If you need to hang some decorations or artwork, think about getting a set of nails made for this purpose. They are usually gold in color and tend to have rounded, more decorative hardware. 
- A tape measure or ruler. How long is that mini-blind in the den? How much space is in the living room? Answer these questions and more with a retractable tape measure. A yardstick can also be helpful for medium measuring that is too large for a ruler. Getting the right measurements will ensure you have the right fit for curtains, carpets, and more.
- Tapes and adhesives. Electrical tape is useful for more than just wrapping wires. The use of duct tape beyond heating systems is almost legendary. Glue or rubber cement can be handy for quick repairs as well. Masking tape is not only used for painting but can be used to hang a quick note as well.

These are the basics, but there are certainly many more tools out there for just about any repair or project you can think of. Hardware stores often have “how to” classes or materials and are a good source for any extra tools and advice you may need.
