A sunrise painted in gold and pink, bleeds through the curtains of my bedroom. I reach for my alarm, but it’s already buzzing. The world wakes up in a rush of emails, meetings, and the relentless hum of a day that refuses to slow down.

By the time coffee brews, the morning news scrolls with crises that feel too vast to handle. At the office, deadlines loom like shadows, each one demanding more than I can give. A conference call turns into a marathon, voices overlapping, tasks piling up.

Lunch is a fleeting pause—salad, a bite of bread, a quick walk outside where the sky is a bruised blue. Back at my desk, the afternoon drags with reports, meetings, and the constant pressure of *what ifs*. By five, my body aches, my mind is foggy, and the evening feels like a distant promise.

Home, the kitchen table is a warzone of open books, half-finished meals, and a to-do list that stretches into tomorrow. The TV flickers on, a distraction from the endless cycle, but even that feels like a chore.

Dinner is a quiet affair—perhaps takeout, a glass of wine, a show that doesn’t hold my attention. The bed feels like a foreign place, but sleep eludes me, the day’s weight pressing down. Tomorrow starts again, and the cycle repeats.

A day that feels too short, stretched thin by the weight of everything that must be done, the time that slips away like sand through fingers.
