How to Make Children Understand the Power of Positive Thinking?
We’re all aware that life doesn’t always go as we’d hoped it would. Ups and downs, successes and failures, positives and negatives are all part and parcel of life. It is hence only natural if you start thinking negatively at times. However, as you all know, a negative attitude is like standing against a giant wall of negativity stuck in a rut. It prevents you from learning, growing, and achieving happiness. This makes it important to embrace positive thinking and maintain a positive outlook in life.
But how to teach the importance of positive thinking to kids? Their young and tender minds are too naive to understand what’s negative and what’s positive. So, let us delve deeper into the topic today and explore various ways to help your kids develop a positive outlook towards life in this article below. Read on.
- Set a good example to follow – Reflect positivity and encouragement
We, at K.R. Mangalam Global School, one of the top IB schools in Gurgaon, firmly believe that optimism is contagiousness. As a result, if your child sees you achieving success, they are more likely to want to do the same. Explain that positivity promotes happiness and increases the probability of achieving any goal. After all, seeing is indeed believing, and there’s no one better to prove this to your kids than you.
- Create a positive environment at home
To inspire optimism, you need to create a positive mood. Make a bulletin board in your child’s study room at home that’s decorated well with positive quotes. Another fun idea is to use this craft to turn kitchen magnets into positive quotes. A positive environment can do wonders in enabling the kids to embrace the challenges and grab the opportunities.
- Instill a winning attitude
We, at K.R. Mangalam Global School, Gurugram, firmly believe that the best way to instill a winning attitude in your kids is to encourage them to visualize the positive outcome of each scenario. The regular setting of goals with your child is essential as it clearly defines what success looks like when you’re doing it. What will it be like to achieve the goal? What’s in it for them? What does being successful mean? It will encourage the kids to work in a more focused manner towards the goal and remind them of the positive outcomes they must look forward to as a result of their efforts.
Eliminate negative words from your child’s vocabulary
It’s essential to take a step back when your child tells you, “I can’t do it.” Bring your child’s attention to these negative statements. Help the kids take a closer look at what exactly is stopping them from giving out their best. Ask them, “Why can’t you do it?” or “What is holding you back?” Have a conversation about how to turn “I can’t” into a “we can” attitude by showing your child that you are all in this together.
Let them be rewarded for the positiveness
In the face of defeat or a sense of failure, it’s not uncommon for children to feel demotivated. But this is where your role as their parents, their biggest supporters, comes into the scenario. Let them know that efforts are more important than the outcome. Kids must be taught that when it comes to accomplishing any task, what’s actually significant is whether they poured their efforts with their full potential or not. This will help them understand that efforts are crucial. Also, make sure to reward their effort to encourage optimism in their approach.

A positive person has the potential to face even the biggest challenges in life with a beaming smile. Hence, help your kids practice positivity, and you will notice how well they bloom in not only their academics but in all the varied spheres of life.
