The rain pattered against the grimy windows of the alleyway, each drop a reminder of how quickly life could turn sour. I stood near the corner, my coat damp from the drizzle, watching the chaos unfold. A group of men in rumpled suits had gathered near the flickering neon sign of the dive bar down the block. One of them—a burly brute with a scar running from his temple to his jaw—was arguing with the bouncer, a wiry man with a broken nose and a permanent scowl.

The fight erupted before I could move. The bouncer threw a punch that sent the brute sprawling. The others lunged, fists flying, and soon, the alley was a storm of shouts and crunching bones. The bouncer, though battered, kept his feet, dodging blows with a mix of desperation and grim determination. The crowd outside the bar had grown restless, some throwing debris to disrupt the fight. A kid no older than twelve, clutching a stolen soda can, tried to intervene but was shoved aside by a drunken man.

Then—silence. The men collapsed, groaning, blood seeping through their clothes. The bouncer wiped blood from his mouth, his knuckles raw but unbroken. The kid, now shaking, stared at the mess between them. A woman in a torn jacket, her face streaked with tears, approached. She pulled the kid away, whispering something I couldn’t hear. The alley fell still.

I exhaled slowly, my breath fogging in the cold air. Somewhere, a siren wailed in the distance, but it was too late. The fight had been forgotten, swallowed by the night. The bouncer limped toward his car, his shoulder aching. The kid and the woman walked away, the kid’s eyes still wide with the weight of what they’d seen. The alley returned to its usual quiet, but not for long.
