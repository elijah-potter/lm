The old clock in the hallway chimed seven, but the noise faded into static as my fingers traced the worn edges of my father’s journal. The page was yellowed with age, the ink smudged where he’d written notes in a hurried scrawl. *"Plan B"* was scrawled across the top in what looked like coffee rings.

Memory of his last words: *"You’ll need this."* The kitchen table was cluttered with maps—some folded, some torn—and a small, tarnished compass that had belonged to my grandfather. The walls of our apartment were lined with photographs: me as a child, laughing with my sister, our parents’ faces half-obscured by the grain of the film.

I flipped through the journal. The first entry was dated two years ago: *"First step: find the old lighthouse. Second step: wait for the tide."* The second entry was dated a month later: *"They’re coming. I’m not sure if it’s the coast guard or the military. I’ve hidden the documents—"* The page ended there, torn, the rest of the sentence cut off.

Outside, the wind howled through the cracked window. I grabbed the maps and the compass. The first one was a detailed sketch of the coast—markings for hidden caves, tunnels, and places marked *"A."* The second was a timeline of dates, each with a corresponding location where something had gone missing over the years. *"January 1987—"* I traced my finger over the names: *Lena, Daniel, Maria.*

The plan wasn’t just about the lighthouse. It was about the others—the ones who’d vanished, the ones who’d disappeared into the sea or the forest, their stories buried like the journal’s last entry. I needed to find the cave where they’d hidden the documents, but first, I had to find the right tide.

The journal’s final note was scrawled in the margins: *"You’re the last one left. Don’t trust anyone."* The compass spun wildly in my hands. It wasn’t pointing north anymore. It was pointing to the memory of a promise I’d made to my father: *"You’ll be safe."* But safety wasn’t a destination—it was a map, a plan, a memory I had to follow before it faded like the ink on the page.
