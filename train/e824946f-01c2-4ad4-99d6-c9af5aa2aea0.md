The old clock tower stood silent against the twilight, its brass face etched with the hands of time that had long outrun its purpose. Inside, Elias sat with a half-empty coffee cup, the steam curling into the dim air like forgotten ghosts. His fingers traced the rim of the cup, the weight of years pressing down like the weight of unspoken truths.

He had spent a lifetime chasing dreams—dreams that had turned to dust in the corners of his mind. The art he’d once loved was now just another memory, a flicker in the void. The woman he’d thought would be his everything had moved on, leaving him with more questions than answers. And now, here he was, alone, staring at the cracks in his life like they were the only things keeping him from falling.

He exhaled sharply, the sound hollow. *"I always thought I knew what I wanted,"* he muttered, *"but I was just chasing shadows."* The words hung in the air, heavy and true. Outside, the wind whispered through the empty streets, carrying away the last remnants of his resolve. Somewhere, a door slammed. Somewhere, someone laughed. The world went on without him. And for the first time, Elias didn’t care.
