Wood Trellis Structures
Whether freestanding or attached to a wall, a wooden trellis makes an attractive and functional addition to your garden. A range of trellis shapes and designs makes these structures useful tools to support and grow flowers, vines, fruits and vegetables. Although other types of trellis materials are available, a wood trellis blends naturally and organically into almost any landscape.
A trellis helps protect plants from damage, pests and weeds, and also makes it easier to spray or dust when insect and disease control is required. Starting vertical-growing plants on a trellis makes an efficient use of space, especially handy in small gardens, and can result in increased yields of food crops. A trellis can serve as the decorative centerpiece of a garden or a backdrop for a colorful shrub. Trellises increase privacy when used as boundary accents to your yard, and they are cheaper than a fence. An overhanging trellis with a fast-growing deciduous vine can add shade to patios or homes, and a hinged trellis can easily be lowered when you need to do home maintenance.
A trellis can be made from almost any type of wood, even twigs and branches. A wood's durability depends on the ratio of sapwood -- the lighter colored outer portion of a tree -- to heartwood, the darker colored interior portion. White cedar and black locust trellis posts made primarily from heartwood can last up to 50 years. Pressure-treated lumber will also last many years, but some homeowners don't like the idea of using lumber treated with toxic chemicals in pressure treatment. Without treating, certain species of wood aren't very durable, such as redwood or pine posts that only last three to six years.
Styles and Designs
The design of a trellis is limited only by your imagination when you create one from scratch, and commercially available wood trellis panel styles include fan-shaped, rectangular, arching, those with convex or concave tops, or "winged." Many basic trellis designs for climbing plants such as beans and tomatoes use a pyramid or obelisk shape, or an even simpler A-frame or cage. The planter box design, with a soil-filled plant container at the base of a small trellis, is ideal for a balcony or even indoor use.
Untreated wood trellis supports are vulnerable to fungi that break wood down. This is common in areas with a lot of rain and humidity, and less of an issue in arid regions. Termites and carpenter ants are also attracted to wood structures such as trellises. Using wood treated with water-repellent preservatives helps protect against fungi and rot, while pressure-treated wood is resistant to fungi and insects. If termites or carpenter ants are likely, treat the soil around the base of the trellis with an insecticide. Reduce wear and decay on less-permanent trellis structures by dismantling them or moving them into a garage or other storage area after the end of each growing season.
