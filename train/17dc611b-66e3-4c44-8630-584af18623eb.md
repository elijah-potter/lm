A decision feels like a leap when the path forward is uncertain, and the future is a blank canvas. The mind hesitates—each choice feels like an unknown, a potential fall or a daring ascent. The fear of failure is tangible, a shadow that stretches long before the first step. Yet, the hesitation itself becomes a kind of resistance, a refusal to commit to the weight of possibility.

For every risk there is a reward, though neither is easily measured. The leap is not measured in distance but in the courage to trust the act itself, even if the outcome remains a mystery. Sometimes, the leap is the only way to find out what you’re truly made of. The ground beneath may not be solid, but the absence of certainty is the only thing that feels real in the moment. The leap is not the fall—it’s the choice to keep going.
