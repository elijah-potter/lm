Solar eclipses occur during the New Moon phase when the alignment of Earth, the Moon, and the Sun takes place. However, not every New Moon results in a solar eclipse. 

For a solar eclipse to occur, the New Moon must be near a lunar node. Lunar nodes are the points where the Moon's orbital path intersects with Earth's orbital plane around the Sun. These conditions create the potential for the Moon to align precisely with the Sun, resulting in a solar eclipse. 

Solar eclipses are limited to specific periods known as eclipse seasons, which happen approximately every six months and last for around 34.5 days.
