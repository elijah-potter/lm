The neon glow of the abandoned theater flickered against the rain-streaked windows. The sign above the door read *"Closed Forever"* in faded letters, though the glass was cracked and the letters sagged like old bones. The air smelled of stale beer and something metallic—blood, maybe, or rust.

You shouldn’t be here.

Your footsteps echoed off the empty stage, the only sound apart from the distant hum of the city. The last performance had been three years ago, but the place still held the weight of forgotten dreams. A stagehand had been found dead in the dressing room, his face pressed into a pile of props, his breath never coming again. The police called it suicide, but no one believed it.

You’d heard the rumors before: whispers about things that moved in the dark, about voices that called from the wings. But you’d come anyway. The offer was too good to pass up—a job, a chance to leave your life behind. The owner had been charming, warm, too warm. *"Just for a little while,"* he’d said. *"We could start over."*

Now, the door creaked open as you pushed it with your shoulder. The scent of dust and something older—something that clove the air like a curse—filled your lungs. The stage was set up as if no one had touched it in decades: a single spotlight, a half-burned candle on the piano, a half-eaten sandwich on the ledge.

Then you saw them.

The figures in the shadows, half-hidden behind the curtain, their faces blurred by the dim light. They didn’t move. Didn’t breathe. But you could *feel* them watching.

You shouldn’t be here.

A voice, low and familiar, whispered from the darkness:

*"You’re late."*

The figures stepped forward.
