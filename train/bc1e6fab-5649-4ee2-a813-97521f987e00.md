## Bookstore Serendipity (Seattle, WA)

The rain was falling steadily that afternoon, a soft drumming against the awning of Elliott Bay Books. I was browsing the poetry section, drawn to a collection by Mary Oliver, when I noticed you. You were examining a volume of Rilke, your brow furrowed in concentration, and a shaft of afternoon light caught the auburn highlights in your hair. There was a quiet intensity about you, a peacefulness that resonated with the atmosphere of the store. I remember the comforting aroma of aged paper and leather, a familiar and welcome scent. We didn’t speak, but I felt a connection, a shared appreciation for the quiet solace found within those pages. I lingered, hoping for a reason to speak, but you moved on. I walked away, feeling a peculiar sense of loss for a moment that never truly began. 

Do you recall a woman with dark hair, wearing a navy blue coat, near the poetry shelves?
