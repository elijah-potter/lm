In dim, flickering candlelight, the gathered throng traces **three intertwined spirals** on the floor with ash—each loop representing the past, present, and future of a forgotten vow. The elder, now clad in a robe of patched fabric, recites lines from a script no one recognizes: *"Where the earth meets the sky, the balance shifts."* The youngest child, their hands trembling, drops a single seed into a cracked urn of blackened glass. No one knows if it’s wheat, willow, or something else entirely.

The priestess, her voice a whisper on the wind, presses a hollowed bone to their lips—its edges worn smooth from centuries of use. *"Speak the truth,"* she murmurs, *"but let it rot."* The crowd murmurs back, repeating words they’ve heard as children, as adults, as elders: *"The cycle turns, the world endures."* The urn is smashed. The spirals are erased. A single, unlit match is tossed into the fire—no one knows why.

The ritual’s meaning dissolves like ash in rain, yet the act persists. The ash remains on the floor. The candles burn low. The urn, now a vessel for nothing, sits empty. The world outside the circle continues, indifferent.
