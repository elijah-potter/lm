A leap of faith is often measured not in the magnitude of the risk but in the quiet certainty that the alternative—staying rooted—is a compromise of the self. That night, the choice came when the map’s last unmarked path led to a place neither compass nor common sense flagged as safe. The map was torn from a journal, its edges frayed with ink that hadn’t dried yet, the name of the destination a whisper: *"Beyond the Blackthorn Woods."* The voice that urged me to go was not one’s own—it was the echo of a stranger’s voice, half-faded on a wind that carried the scent of wet earth and something older, something like regret. The decision wasn’t about the stars above or the stars I might lose if I turned back. It was about the stars that had been buried under the weight of what I *should* have done, and the fear that if I hesitated, the sky would forget to shine for me again. The leap wasn’t the jump itself but the trust that the ground beneath my feet, though unseen, would hold. And so, I stepped forward, not into the unknown, but into the space between the known and the unknowable—where the only certainty was that I would either fall or rise, and the choice was already written in the way my heart had begun to pound, not with fear, but with the rhythm of something daring.
