**"The Last Light"**

Beneath the cracked ceiling of an old lighthouse, the tide hissed like a dying beast. Captain Elias adjusted the brass dials, fingers trembling—not from age, but from the weight of the world above. The horizon flickered, a ghostly smear of amber and blue.

*"Another hour,"* he muttered, though he knew it was already too late.

The storm rolled in without warning, turning the water black as oil. The wind screamed through the ragged sails, but Elias had learned long ago that fear was just the sea’s way of reminding him he was mortal. He lit the last lantern, its glow bleeding through the rusted glass like a dying star.

Then silence.

The ship groaned as the waves swallowed her whole.

Elias stood on the deck, watching the darkness swallow the lighthouse too. The final beam cut through the storm like a knife, steady, eternal. The sea breathed in the silence.

He walked to the railing and whispered:

*"Goodnight, world."*
