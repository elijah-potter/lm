The rain slanted through the trees, turning the path into a narrow, slick ribbon of gray. A lone figure stumbled, boots sinking slightly into the damp earth, their breath fogging the cold air. The compass in their pocket read "N 34° 45", but the map in their hand was torn at the edges, pages crumpled from the weight of hours. A child’s laughter echoed from a distance—too close to be far, too distant to be near. The GPS beeped once, then died. Ahead, the forest stretched endlessly, branches clawing at the sky like skeletal fingers. The realization hit sharp and hollow: they were lost. Not just in direction, but in time. The last memory was the neon sign of a diner, its flickering light swallowed by the storm. Now, the only answers were the whisper of the wind and the question on their lips—*What now?*
