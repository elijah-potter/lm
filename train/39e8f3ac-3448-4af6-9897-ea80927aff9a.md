**The Hollow Chair**

The wind howled through the skeletal trees, their branches clawing at the bruised sky. Inside the abandoned theater, the stage was a graveyard of tattered curtains and half-erected props, but the auditorium still held a faint, lingering scent of old sawdust and damp wood.

Eleanor sat on the edge of a cracked velvet seat, her fingers tracing the jagged cracks in the wooden floor. The last performance had ended with the curtain falling—never to rise again. The orchestra’s remains lay scattered like broken glass, their instruments half-played, half-forgotten.

A single, flickering bulb cast long shadows across the stage. The only sound was the distant hum of the city beyond the broken windows.

Then—footsteps.

Slow. Deliberate.

The door creaked open. A figure stood framed in the doorway, silhouetted against the streetlamps outside. Their coat was too dark, their boots too still.

Eleanor didn’t look up.

The stranger moved forward, past the wreckage of the stage, until they stood beside her. The air between them felt charged, not with dread, but with something older.

*"You’re the last,"* they said, voice low, like gravel.

Eleanor exhaled. *"The last of what?"*

The stranger didn’t answer. Just watched her.

And then—

The lights went out.
