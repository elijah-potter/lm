According to a Chinese legend, tea was first discovered in 2737 BC when the then-emperor Shen Nung was sitting beneath a tree when leaves accidentally fell into the drinking water one of his servants was boiling. The leaves were from the Camellia sinensis tree which is now known as the tea plant. While the truth of this story is up for debate, there is no debate that tea originated in the regions around modern-day China thousands of years ago and was enjoyed by other nearby countries. However, it wasn’t until hundreds of years later that global trade routes brought tea to the rest of the world. Now tea comes in many different varieties. The main categories of tea that come from the tea plant are white, green, and black tea. The difference in these teas is in the oxidation process. Green tea is steamed after harvesting, maintaining the green color, while white tea is made by allowing the leaves to oxidize for a short amount of time. Lastly, black tea is allowed to undergo full oxidation, which allows its aroma to develop and gain its black-brown appearance.
