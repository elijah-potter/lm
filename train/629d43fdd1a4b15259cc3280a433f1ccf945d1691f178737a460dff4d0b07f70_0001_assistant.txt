Utilitarianism is the belief that life should be lived in such a way that one maximizes happiness and minimizes sadness. However, while this is a great way to live to obtain pleasure, this path will ultimately lead to pain. In the pursuit of the greatest happiness, or the greatest pleasure, people inevitably try to gain more happiness and more pleasure until they become numb to it and have to go to further extremes to obtain it. For instance, people who enjoy the taste of food may end up on a path to obesity if their pursuit of happiness through food leads them to excess intake. People who look for physical pleasure in the form of sex may become lustful in their search for more pleasure, and they may also begin to entertain more perverse and twisted sexual fantasies. Still, others may be adrenaline junkies who love the thrills of bungee jumping, skydiving, or traveling fast in a car, but as they attempt to push themselves to find bigger and better thrills, they ultimately put themselves in dangerous and deadly situations that could end their lives.

In contrast to utilitarianism, living a healthy life and avoiding excesses is a better way to live a happier, more fulfilling life. There may still be some discomfort or pain in the pursuit of a healthy life (i.e. dieting, exercising, demonstrating discipline), but these short-term discomforts pale in comparison to the long-term benefits of living longer and being healthier. One would argue that those who practice a lifestyle of moderation - like those who travel the "middle road" of Taoism - end up going with the flow of life, careful to balance in the middle of its scales rather than leaning to one side and tipping those scales in one direction.
