Two scientists, Dr. Chen and Dr. Park, stand in a dimly lit lab, their laptops glowing under fluorescent lights. A half-eaten sandwich sits untouched on a nearby desk. The air hums with the low murmur of a failed experiment.

Dr. Chen’s monitor flickers with data: a quantum anomaly in a superconducting lattice. "This isn’t possible," they whisper, tracing a red line across the screen. "The equations predict no such deviation."

Dr. Park leans over, fingers tracing the same numbers. "Unless... unless we’ve been measuring the wrong thing." Their breath fogs the glass. The machine beeps erratically—too erratic.

A server fan spins violently. The screen cuts to static. Then, a single pixel glows red. The data files disappear.

Outside the lab, a storm rips through the campus, lightning striking the old observatory. Inside, the two realize: the anomaly wasn’t in the equations. It was in *them*. The experiment wasn’t wrong—it was *right*. The universe had just answered in code.
