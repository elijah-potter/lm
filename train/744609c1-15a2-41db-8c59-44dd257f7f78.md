The village had always been quiet at dusk, the kind of quiet that settled over time like dust, until the next morning. I stood at the window, watching the streetlamps flicker to life, their golden light dancing over the cobblestones like fireflies trapped in glass. The old clock tower, its face etched with numbers long since worn smooth, chimed the hour—twelve. It was the hour when the markets closed, when the bakers took their last loaves home, and when the children would soon be returning from their errands, their laughter already fading into the evening breeze.

I turned away from the window, the warmth of the room wrapping around me like a blanket. The scent of coffee and woodsmoke lingered in the air, a reminder of the day’s warmth. My fingers traced the edge of the table, where a half-empty cup of tea sat, its surface still damp with condensation. The morning’s paper was spread out, its headlines a blur of numbers and dates, but I didn’t need to read them—my mind already filled in the rest.

A knock at the door made me jump. I exhaled slowly, my breath steadying as I made my way to the entrance. The door creaked open, revealing the familiar silhouette of Elias, his dark hair streaked with the first hints of silver. He carried a basket, its contents hidden from view. "Thought you might be hungry," he said, his voice low and warm, the kind of voice that made you forget the world outside for a moment. "Took me longer than I expected."

I stepped aside, letting him in. The basket was heavy, and as he set it down, I could almost feel the weight of what was inside—the fresh bread, the cheese wrapped in cloth, the apples still warm from the oven. "You should eat," he said, handing me a slice of bread. "It’s not often we get this late."

I took the bread, my fingers brushing against the warm crust. The silence between us was comfortable, like the quiet before a storm. I didn’t mention the reason he’d been late—the news he’d been carrying, the things he’d seen. Instead, I let the moment unfold, letting the warmth of the evening and the simplicity of the food fill the space between us.

Elias sat across from me, his expression unreadable, but his eyes held something I couldn’t quite name. "You look tired," he said, his voice breaking the quiet. I nodded, my throat tight. "The past few days have been… long."

He reached into his pocket, pulling out a small, worn book. "I thought you might like this," he said, handing it to me. It was the kind of book you found in attics or forgotten corners, its pages yellowed with age. The title was faded, but the words still read: *The Hollow Years*. "I read it once," he admitted. "It was the only thing that made sense."

I took the book, its weight familiar in my hands. The pages were brittle, the ink smudged in places where time had seeped through. I flipped through them slowly, my fingers tracing the lines of text. Some of the words were unreadable, swallowed by the passage of years, but others clung stubbornly to the page, like ghosts refusing to fade.

I closed the book with a quiet sigh. "It’s beautiful," I said, though I wasn’t sure if it was the words or the way they had been written. "It speaks of things I’ve never known."

Elias watched me, his expression unreadable. "There are things you don’t know," he said softly. "Things that should have been left buried."

I looked away, my mind racing. The book was heavy in my hands, its weight a reminder of the past I couldn’t shake. "I don’t know what to do," I whispered.

Elias stood, his silhouette stretching against the dim light of the hallway. "You don’t have to," he said. "Sometimes, you just have to let it go."

The door clicked shut behind him, leaving me alone with the weight of my thoughts. The book lay open on the table, its pages filled with stories I didn’t understand, but the knowledge that they were there, waiting, made my chest tighten. The evening was quiet, the kind of quiet that felt heavy with unseen burdens. I picked up the book, turning the pages slowly, my fingers tracing the words that had been written long before my time.

Somewhere outside, the wind whispered through the trees, carrying with it the distant sound of a church bell. It was the kind of sound that made you pause, that made you wonder what was waiting beyond the edge of the world. I didn’t look up, but I could almost feel the weight of it, the unseen forces pulling at the edges of my reality.

I turned back to the book, my eyes tracing the lines of text. The words were familiar, a language I understood but didn’t fully grasp. They spoke of a time before my own, of a world that had been shaped by things I couldn’t begin to imagine. The pages were filled with stories of love and loss, of triumph and despair, all wrapped up in a tale that felt like a whisper from the past.

I closed the book with a quiet sigh, its weight in my hands a reminder of the things I couldn’t change. The evening was quiet, the kind of quiet that felt heavy with unseen burdens. I let the silence settle around me, letting the weight of the past settle into my bones.

Somewhere, a clock struck midnight. The world outside was still, the kind of stillness that felt heavy with the weight of what was to come. I stood, my hands empty, my mind filled with questions I couldn’t answer. The book was gone, its pages filled with stories I didn’t understand, but the knowledge that they were there, waiting, made my chest tighten.

I walked to the window, watching the streetlamps flicker to life, their golden light dancing over the cobblestones like fireflies trapped in glass. The world outside was quiet, the kind of quiet that felt heavy with unseen burdens. I didn’t know what would come next, but I knew that the night was only just beginning.
