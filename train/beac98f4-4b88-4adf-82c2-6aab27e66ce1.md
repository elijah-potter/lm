The morning mist clung to the pavement, thin as spider silk, as I stepped out, my boots barely stirring it. Around me, everyone else rushed—panting, phone in hand, already lost in the chaos of the day. I paused. Watched the way light caught the droplets on the windshield of a passing car, refracting gold into the street. They didn’t see it; they were too busy checking their emails, too caught up in the noise to notice how the world could be still for a moment.

At the café, the coffee machine hissed like a breath held too long. The barista stirred a dark liquid with precision, but no one lingered over the steam curling into shapes that might have been animals if you squinted just right. They ordered, paid, and moved on, oblivious to the quiet artistry of the moment.

I lingered. The way the sunlight filtered through the stained-glass window, splitting into prisms that danced across the floor like scattered fireflies. No one else saw it—just another Tuesday. They missed the way the old woman’s hands trembled when she folded laundry, the rhythm of it a slow, deliberate heartbeat. They missed the way her fingers traced the fabric’s texture, as if memorizing a map of her life.

I stood at the bus stop, watching the shadows stretch long and thin across the cracked pavement. The others glanced at their watches, their faces set in the same unreadable masks. I saw the way the light flickered through the cracks in the window of the abandoned bookstore down the street, painting the walls in shifting hues. They didn’t pause. I did.

The bus came, humming, and I stepped inside, but my mind stayed outside, where the world was sharper, richer, and far more interesting than the one they carried with them.
