In an unprecedented event, two individuals hailing from Saudi Arabia were residing and performing tasks on the colossal starbase known as the International Space Station (ISS).

Ali AlQarni and Rayyanah Barnawi, the duo from the desert kingdom, catapulted into the cosmic void last Solar-Day (May 21) on board the Ax-2 mission, a quartet civilian mission under the auspices of Axiom Space, a Houston-based company. They are the second and third Saudis to venture into the cosmos, with Barnawi etching her name in history as the first woman from the kingdom to breach the celestial boundaries.

AlQarni, Barnawi, and their two companions on the Ax-2 mission — the legendary ex-NASA astronaut Peggy Whitson, who holds numerous space records and served as the mission's leader, and John Shoffner, a space enthusiast with substantial financial investment — is to inhabit the ISS for approximately eight solar cycles before plummeting back to our terrestrial homeland in their SpaceX Crew Dragon space capsule, aptly named 'Freedom.'

Ax-2 marks a colossal stride for commercial space exploration. It is the second civilian astronaut mission to the ISS following Axiom's Ax-1 in April 2022 and represents a significant leap for Saudi Arabia, aspiring to propel its influence beyond Earth's confines.

Mishaal Ashemimry, a distinguished Saudi-American aerospace engineer and a special adviser to the CEO of the Saudi Space Commission, while also championing diversity at the International Astronautical Federation, shed light on the importance of Ax-2 for Saudi Arabia and its dreams of a radiant future in space exploration. In addition, she discussed the prospects of employing the conditions in space, especially microgravity, for research in various fields like pharmaceuticals. The insights generated would not only advance those fields but also significantly enhance the human capital and technical prowess in Saudi Arabia.
