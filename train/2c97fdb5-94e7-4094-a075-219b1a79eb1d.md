**The Last Light**

The city hummed like a thousand trapped bees, their wings pressed against the glass of skyscrapers, their voices a chorus of static and laughter. I stood at the edge of the park, my fingers digging into the cold pavement, the scent of damp earth and exhaust fumes thick in my lungs. The streetlights flickered, casting long shadows across the pavement where the lost wandered—those who had stopped believing in the future before it had even begun.

A woman passed, her coat too big for her, her hands tucked into the pockets like she was already too cold to move. She didn’t look up. She never did.

*"Why do you stand here?"* I thought, but didn’t say it. The question was too heavy, too tired. The answer was a whisper of regret, a half-remembered promise.

Then the air changed. Not the kind of shift that comes with a breeze, but something deeper, something that pressed against the ribs of the world itself. The hum of the city softened—no longer a chorus, but a single, mournful note. The streetlights burned brighter, not with warmth, but with something colder, sharper. Shadows stretched longer, not just from the lamps, but from the weight of what had been lost.

The woman stopped. She turned. Her eyes were the same, but now they held something different. A flicker of recognition, maybe. Or fear. She reached out, as if to touch something, but her hand passed through the air like it was made of smoke. *"You were supposed to leave,"* she said. Not a question. A statement. *"But you stayed."*

I didn’t answer. The city had changed again. The traffic slowed. Cars came to a halt. The woman’s voice was the only sound left.

*"Why?"* she whispered.

I don’t know. Maybe because the world had finally decided to let go. Maybe because I had stopped believing in the way things were supposed to be. Maybe because the last light was going out, and the dark was coming, and I had no choice but to stand here with her, watching.

The wind picked up. It wasn’t cold. It wasn’t warm. It was the kind of wind that doesn’t exist in the stories we tell ourselves. It carried the weight of something that had always been there, waiting. The woman stepped closer. Her breath was shallow, but steady. *"You’re not alone,"* she said. *"Not anymore."*

The sky split open. Not rain. Not snow. Something else. Something that glowed. The world held its breath. And then—

The city was gone.

Not erased. Not destroyed. But *changed*. The buildings stood, but they were no longer buildings. The streets were no longer streets. The woman was gone. I was gone. And the light was still there, pulsing like a heartbeat in the silence.

I don’t know what happened next. Maybe I was asleep. Maybe I was awake. Maybe I was never here at all.

But the light was still there.

And it was waiting.
