A crimson lantern flickers on the edge of the village square, its light casting long shadows over the cobblestones. A child’s voice, rough with excitement, calls out, *"The river will be dry by tomorrow’s dawn!"*—a promise whispered by the old miller’s daughter to her younger sibling, clutching a half-eaten apple. The elder watches, arms crossed, as the girl’s eyes dart between the distant river and the half-melted snowbank where the promise was made. A single leaf, still green, clings to the branch of an oak, like a last stubborn oath.
