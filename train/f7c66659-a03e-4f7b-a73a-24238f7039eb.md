The artist stood at the canvas, fingers trembling as they traced the first hesitant strokes of light on the dark canvas. The subject was not a face—it was the void between the stars, the infinite black between galaxies, where time bends and identities blur. They had been drawn to this subject before, but never had they dared to capture it as fully as they did this time.

The painting began with a whisper—just a faint suggestion of a void, a space where nothing was certain. The artist let the brush move on its own, guided only by the ache in their chest. They knew, deep down, that this was not about the void itself, but about the fear that the void was all there was. The strokes grew bolder, more defined, and then—oh, then—it began to feel like something more than paint on canvas. It felt like a confession.

The final stroke was an admission: *I am not alone in this fear.* The void was not empty. It was full of the echoes of all the questions left unanswered, all the dreams left unfulfilled. The artist looked at their work and realized that the void was not the enemy. It was the space where the truth was waiting to be found—if only they could face it.

The canvas hung in silence. The artist left, but not before whispering to the void: *"I see you."* And for the first time, the void seemed to answer.
