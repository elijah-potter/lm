**The Last Light of the Forgotten Harbor**

At the edge of the world where the river bends into a narrow, winding arm, the harbor once bustled with activity. Now, the wooden docks rot in silence, their once-bright paint peeling into the salty air. The crumbling stone piers, carved with faded names of ships that no longer sail, are the only remnants of a time when this place was a hub of trade.

The buildings—now skeletal husks—stand half-submerged in the tide, their windows shattered like eggshells. The harbor master’s house, once the center of stories, now hosts only the whispers of gulls and the occasional seagull. Locals tell tales of the lanterns that once floated on the water, marking the safe passage of merchants; now, only the occasional glow of an oil lamp flickers in the windows of the last remaining tavern, a place where the last of the old guard still drink the same stale ale that tastes of memories.

The river’s current carries stories downstream—of the missing sailors, the forgotten contracts, the bargains struck in haste that were never kept. Some say the ghosts of the dock workers still stand at the water’s edge, watching the waves. Others claim the harbor is haunted by the spirits of those who never returned from voyages that vanished into the mist.

The only thing that remains constant is the wind, carrying the scent of salt and decay, a reminder that even the most vibrant places fade into the abyss of time. The harbor is no longer remembered as a place of commerce or adventure, but as a symbol of what was lost—what was forgotten before the world moved on.
