The **Chronoscope Tower**, perched atop an ancient stone archway in the heart of **Timelands**, functions by measuring time through **lunar reflections** rather than clockwork gears.

At its base lies the **Eclipse Well**, a deep fissure where water flows upward in reverse—each drop a mirrored image of a past moment. Observers stand in a semicircular chamber lined with **glass slabs** that refract light into **holographic threads**, each thread a spiral of time showing the precise moment of origin for the observer’s current age. The tower’s "clock" is a rotating **crystal orb** that pulses when a visitor’s soul aligns with a past event, measured in **"echo-seconds"** (a day’s worth of time divided by the number of past lives recalled).

Astronomers in Timelands call it a **lunar astrolabe**. Pilgrims bring **time-seed vials** to trade, filled with grains that grow or shrivel based on the discrepancy between their local time and the tower’s measurement. The city’s laws are enforced by **time-keepers** who judge disputes by the angle of the observer’s shadow against the **Celestial Mirror**, a polished obsidian disc that projects a distorted future. The tower is said to be **sensitive to emotional resonance**—those who lie experience their past selves "unfolding" in the mirror, a physical manifestation of the paradox.

The locals whisper that the tower was built by a **time-traveling architect** who left behind a warning: *"The only true clock is the one that doesn’t run."*
