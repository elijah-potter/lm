In the quiet coastal town of **Marina Haven**, residents were left stunned by a dramatic discovery near the abandoned fishing pier on **Blackshore Road** yesterday. Locals reported finding a series of ancient artifacts buried beneath the tide line, including intricately carved wooden plaques depicting maritime battles from what appeared to be a long-departed civilization. Archaeologists from the **Haven Historical Society** confirmed the artifacts were likely from the **Vaelthari Dynasty**, a maritime empire that thrived over 2,000 years ago, though their exact purpose remains speculative.

Among the finds was a **bronze compass** with markings suggesting it was used to navigate storms, and a set of navigational charts depicting coastal routes along the eastern coast. The discovery sparked immediate debate among historians about whether these relics could offer new insights into Vaelthari’s naval tactics or even their eventual decline. The town’s mayor has announced a temporary closure of the area to prevent looting, while the **Marina Haven Museum** is preparing to exhibit the artifacts upon official approval.

Local fishermen, who have long speculated about hidden treasures beneath the waves, now see their folklore as more than myth. "We’ve been telling stories about this place for generations," said fisherman Elias Carter, "but now we know the truth." The discovery has also reignited interest in the town’s abandoned **Vaelthari Lighthouse**, a structure rumored to be connected to the artifacts. Authorities warn that further excavation without proper permits could damage irreplaceable evidence. The town’s first official archaeological team will begin systematic digs this week, with plans to collaborate with regional universities to study the artifacts in depth.
