**The Last Light**

Beneath the cracked ceiling of the old lighthouse, the last beam of the dying lantern carved a jagged path against the storm-wracked sky. The waves, restless and hungry, lashed the broken stone steps like a living thing, their foam curling into shapes no one recognized. The wind, a ghost of the autumn before, carried the scent of salt and decay, whispering secrets only the drowned could hear.

Inside, the air was thick with the scent of old oil and forgotten dreams. The glass panels, once polished to perfection, were now splintered mirrors of the past, reflecting not the world outside, but the ghosts of those who had stood here before—the sailors, the lighthouse keeper, the lovers who had come to watch the horizon fade into myth. One of them, a girl in a tattered coat, had sat here long ago, her fingers tracing the rim of an empty cup, waiting for something to appear.

Now, she sat still, her breath a slow rise and fall against the damp stone. The lantern’s light flickered, then died entirely, leaving only the dim glow of the fire in the hearth. The storm outside roared, shaking the foundations of the structure, but the girl did not move. She was no longer afraid. Fear had dissolved into something quieter, something like acceptance.

Then—

A shift. The air itself seemed to ripple. The wind stilled. The fire burned brighter, its flames twisting into shapes no one could name. The girl’s fingers tightened around the cup, and when she looked up, the horizon was no longer an expanse of endless water and sky. Instead, it was a city of glass and steel, its lights pulsing like a slow, steady heartbeat. The lighthouse beam now pointed not at the sea, but at a towering structure that rose from the earth—a cathedral of light, its spires piercing the clouds.

And there, at the top, stood the girl. She was no longer alone.
