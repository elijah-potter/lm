What are the different patterns of alopecia areata?
The most common pattern is one or more well-defined spots of hair loss on the scalp. There is also a form of more universalized loss of hair referred to as diffuse alopecia areata throughout the scalp. Sometimes, all of the scalp hair is lost, a condition referred to as alopecia totalis. How is alopecia areata diagnosed?
The characteristic finding of alopecia areata is a well-circumscribed area or areas of hairless skin in normally hair-bearing areas. Sometimes, it may be necessary to biopsy the scalp to confirm the diagnosis. Other findings that can be helpful are the appearance of short hairs that probably represent fractured hairs, yellow areas of skin deposition at the follicular orifice, short thin hairs, and grey hair present in a bald area. Other reason of hair loss are usually excluded from the consideration by history and clinical evaluation.
