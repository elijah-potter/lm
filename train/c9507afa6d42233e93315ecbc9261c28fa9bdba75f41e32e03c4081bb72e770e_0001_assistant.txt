The modern Olympics consist of three types of games: The Summer Olympics, the Winter Olympics, and the Paralympics.

1. The Summer Olympics: This is the longest-running version of the modern Olympic Games. The first Summer Olympics took place in Athens, Greece, in 1896, marking the revival of the Olympic Games after their discontinuation in ancient times. The event occurs every four years and consists of various sports traditionally played in warmer weather or indoor venues.
2. The Winter Olympics: These were introduced to accommodate sports that are performed on snow and ice, which couldn't be included in the Summer Olympics. The first Winter Olympics were held in Chamonix, France, in 1924. Like the Summer Games, the Winter Olympics are held every four years but are staggered with the Summer Olympics, resulting in an Olympic Games every two years.
3. The Paralympics: Established in 1960 in Rome, Italy, the Paralympics are designed for athletes with a range of disabilities. The Paralympics follow shortly after the Summer and Winter Olympics and are held in the same host cities. The Paralympics have various categories based on athletes' different physical abilities. They represent a significant movement toward inclusivity in global sporting events.
