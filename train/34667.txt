Tracey Curtis-Taylor withstood everything the elements could throw at her during her 13,000km (8,000-mile) flight in an open cockpit biplane.
But she achieved her dream of following in the footsteps of Lady Heath, who in 1928 became the first person to fly alone across Africa.
Severe weather proved a constant challenge to Ms Curtis-Taylor during her eight-week adventure from Cape Town in South Africa to Goodwood in West Sussex.
‘Getting back across Europe, battling strong headwinds and crosswinds, I lost a third of my ground speed,’ said the 51-year-old, who landed in appalling conditions in Goodwood. ‘I felt very vulnerable.’
She flew in the Spirit of Artemis, a classic 1940s Boeing Stearman biplane. It has a top speed of 95mph and a maximum altitude of 3,000m (10,000ft), although Ms Curtis-Taylor flew as low as 4.5m (15ft) over Lake Natron in Tanzania, allowing her to enjoy Africa’s wildlife and dramatic terrain.
One of her worst journeys was over Egypt. ‘It was getting colder, the plane was getting slower and I was almost out of fuel,’ she said. ‘I was really anxious and I became almost hypothermic.’
Her 40-leg trip is being made into a TV documentary.
