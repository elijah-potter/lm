A dimly lit warehouse hummed with the distant sound of machinery. Dust coated every surface, thick and silent. The last worker, Elias, wiped his brow with a rag, his fingers trembling slightly as he adjusted the rusted latch on the ancient safe. Inside lay a small, ornate pocket watch—its brass face cracked with age, the hands frozen at 11:11.

Elias had found it abandoned in the corner of the warehouse months ago, marked with faded graffiti: *"For the hour that time forgot."* He had kept it, half-expecting it to mean nothing, but curiosity had driven him back to it every few days. Now, his breath caught as the watch’s hands spun backward, then forward, then *stopped* again.

A sharp knock echoed from the door. Elias turned, his heart pounding. Standing there was Lina, the warehouse’s last employee, her eyes wide with the same unspoken dread. She held a single, silver locket in her hands, its chain frayed. *"It’s not just a watch,"* she whispered. *"It’s a key. And we’re not supposed to find it."*

Outside, the warehouse doors creaked open, revealing a group of men in dark coats—someone had arrived. The air thickened with tension. Elias and Lina exchanged a glance. The watch pulsed faintly in his palm, its hands now spinning wildly.

*"We have to fix it,"* Elias muttered. *"Before they do."*
