In dimly lit alleyways of an abandoned city, a silhouette hunts through the mist, fingers tracing cold pavement cracks. The scent of damp concrete and old oil mixes with a faint, unnatural metallic tang—like rusted gears dissolving into air. A whisper in their mind, a voice neither heard nor spoken, guides their steps. The object they seek isn’t held, isn’t touched; it’s felt in the hollow between ribs, the way the air hums just beyond reach, the ghost of a touch that wasn’t. The map is a memory, a loop of fragments: a key left in a door that never opened, a name whispered by someone who vanished mid-sentence. Shadows stretch like tendrils, coiling around ankles, but the thing they chase isn’t trapped—it’s the *absence* of itself, slipping through cracks like smoke through a broken window.
