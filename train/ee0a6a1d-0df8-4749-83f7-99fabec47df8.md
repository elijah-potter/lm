In a dimly lit apartment, flickering fluorescent lights cast long shadows across the cluttered floor. A hollow-eyed figure kneels beside an old wooden desk, fingers tracing the grain of a half-empty coffee cup. The scent of stale air and something faintly metallic lingers in the air.

A whispered, urgent voice—*"It’s not here"*—echoes through the crackling static of a broken radio. The person’s breath is shallow, hands gripping the edges of a torn map, its edges frayed with time and unanswered questions. They press their palm against the wall, fingers brushing dust motes suspended in the stale air, as if the intangible—*the thing they seek*—might emerge from the walls themselves.

A single, cold drop of rain taps against the broken window. The search continues.
