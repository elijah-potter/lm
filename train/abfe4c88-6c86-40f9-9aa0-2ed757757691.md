A lone figure stood at the edge of the cliff, rain slicing through their soaked coat as they clutched the broken remnants of a rusted lantern. The wind howled through the skeletal trees, carrying whispers of the past. Below, the river rushed black and unyielding, its current swallowing the last remnants of the promise carved into the wood: *"I will return."*

Their breath came in ragged gasps as the lantern’s flame guttered out, extinguished by the relentless storm. The compass in their pocket spun wildly, its needle frozen mid-swing, as the last threads of resolve frayed. The old man’s voice—once steady—now cracked like dry branches: *"You swore you’d stay."* The figure’s fingers dug into the cold stone, nails biting through leather gloves. *"I couldn’t."*

The lantern fell, shattering into a thousand fragments that scattered into the storm. The promise, once indelible, was now but a memory—erased by the tide of doubt and the weight of what no longer could be.
