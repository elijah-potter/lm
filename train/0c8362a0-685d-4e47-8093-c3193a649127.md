The alarm buzzed at 3:17 AM, its harsh tone cutting through the stillness of the empty apartment. Clara stepped out of the shower, towel wrapped around her hair, her reflection in the fogged mirror flickering with the light of the streetlamp outside. She grabbed her phone, fingers trembling slightly as she scrolled through the messages: *"You’ll do it. Now."* The text was from her boss, the one who had been calling her every night for the past three weeks, demanding answers about the data breach at the company’s AI division.

She hesitated, then typed a reply: *"I’m on my way."*

The elevator doors opened onto the crowded subway platform, a cacophony of voices and beeps. Clara squeezed in, her pulse quickening. The train lurched to life, and she clutched her bag tighter. The station was packed—people rushing to work, students late for classes, the usual chaos of a city waking up.

Outside, the first light of dawn seeped through the windows of the office building. Clara pushed through the glass doors, her heels clicking against the polished floor. The lobby was empty except for a single security guard, his eyes scanning the room like a hawk. She ignored him, her mind racing.

The hallway was lined with closed doors. She knew the layout: the data servers were in the basement. She took a deep breath, dialed the number in her pocket, and said, *"I’m here."*

The door to the server room creaked open. Inside, the hum of the machines filled the air, a low, mechanical drone. Clara moved past the racks of servers, her breath shallow. The breach had been in the encryption protocol—someone had slipped a backdoor into the system months ago. Her boss had found it, but they hadn’t had time to fix it before the hackers hit.

She pulled out her laptop, the one she’d smuggled in, and tapped the password into the terminal. The screen flickered to life, the data breach log appearing before her. She had been given just enough time to recover the files before the servers went offline at 3:30 AM.

*"It’s done,"* she whispered.

The door behind her clicked shut. The guard was gone. She exhaled, her hands steady now. The city outside was waking up, unaware of the storm brewing in the shadows. But Clara was ready.
