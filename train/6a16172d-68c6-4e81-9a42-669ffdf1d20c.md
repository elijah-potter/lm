The morning light slipped through the curtains like smoke,
softening edges of a world half-remembered.
A voice—no, not a voice, but the hum of a thousand questions—
whispered through the static of the mind:
*"What if?"*

You stood at the edge of a cliff, fingers tracing the damp stone,
the wind carrying fragments of a dream: a child’s laughter, a name,
a promise that had never been spoken. The air smelled of rain
and something older, something like earth after a storm.

Then—
the light flickered.
A breath.
The name dissolved into the crowd.
The cliff crumbled into the ground,
not with sound, but with the quiet erosion of time.

The moment lingered, a ghost in the mirror,
a half-remembered sigh. You reached for it,
but it slipped away—
not broken, just *gone*.
And in the silence, the only thing left was the weight of what was never said,
the echo of a voice that had never been heard.
