The **One-and-Done Rule** was introduced in the 2013 *NFL Draft* to prevent teams from signing players for multiple seasons in a single year, limiting their contract to one year unless re-signed by the same team. While it was intended to curb excessive offseason spending, its enforcement was inconsistent, often overlooked in favor of more visible incentives like rookie bonuses. Teams like the **49ers** exploited loopholes by signing players to multi-year contracts under "supplemental" clauses, bypassing the rule’s intent. After scrutiny, the NFL relaxed the rule in 2016, allowing teams to re-sign players for a second year if they met performance benchmarks. The rule’s legacy remains a cautionary tale: well-intentioned policies can be undone by ambiguity, regulatory drift, or the persistence of unchecked power dynamics.
