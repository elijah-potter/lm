Quantum dots in semiconductor nanocrystals exhibit size-dependent photoluminescence with non-classical emission properties (Fluorescence Quantum Yield: ~70% at 550 nm, ~85% at 650 nm).
At low temperatures (below 5 K), these dots exhibit single-photon emission and superposition states in quantum dots (e.g., CdSe/ZnS core-shell structures with ~6 nm diameter).
Quantum interference effects observed in coupled quantum dots show anti-bunching in spontaneous emission (G-concentration: ~0.95 for FWHM < 500 fs).
Dopant-induced defect states in perovskite nanocrystals exhibit defect-mediated energy transfer and persistent photoluminescence with long decay times (τ > 100 μs) at room temperature.
