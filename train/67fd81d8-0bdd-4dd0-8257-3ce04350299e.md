A dimly lit office at 3:17 AM, fluorescent buzzing like a trapped hummingbird. The monitor casts a sickly green glow across the desk—keyboard keys feel like they’ve been pre-lubed for this exact sequence. The mouse darts across the screen in a loop, pausing only when the cursor hovers over "Undo" in the same way it did last Tuesday, last Thursday, the Tuesday before. The phone rings. The same ringing tone, same delay before it cuts to silence. "Call me back," the voice says, but it’s not yours. The voice sounds like a recording you’ve heard once before, in the same context, at the same time of day. Outside, the city holds its breath, a metronome counting down. You’re not doing anything here. You’re just *standing*. The chair creaks as you adjust it, the same creak you’ve done since the last time you sat here, the one before that. A file folder slides open with the same whisper. Inside, a document titled "Rehearsed Moments: Entry 47" waits, its edges frayed like old fabric. The ink on the page reads: *"This is how it always goes."* The screen flashes a notification: "Next rehearsal in 5 minutes." Your fingers tap the mouse again, not to move it, but to *feel* the resistance. The city exhales, a slow sigh of static.
