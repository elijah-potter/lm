It wasn’t supposed to happen like this.

The first time I saw the storm clouds gather over the old mill, I thought only of the river’s whisper—how it had once carried me down the same path without a care. But the wind changed, sharp as a knife through flesh, and the water rose like a wall, swallowing the bridge before it could be crossed. The mill’s creaking gears turned in silent grief, their rusted teeth grinding against the weight of the unknown. That night, the rafters groaned like dying men, and the floorboards split beneath my feet, though I never dared step back.

Now, the mill stands as a tomb, its windows shattered, its doors hung with black cloth. The river’s song is gone, replaced by the hiss of steam from the old boiler—still running, though no one comes to tend it. The clock above the hearth ticks backward, counting down the years until the world forgets this place exists. Some say the river remembers. That the water remembers. That the stones remember. I do not know. But I know the mill remembers, and it is waiting.
