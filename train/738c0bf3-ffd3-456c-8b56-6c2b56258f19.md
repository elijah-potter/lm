In the quiet hum of a midnight café, the scent of stale coffee and pastries clung to the air like a forgotten memory. Across the table, a woman with silver-streaked hair traced the rim of her empty espresso cup, her fingers pausing just before the edge.

She had come here for solitude, but the café was alive with the whispers of strangers—conversations that blurred into the static of a thousand half-heard stories. A child’s laughter, muffled by a stained paper napkin, drifted from a booth at the back, lingering like a ghost.

Outside, the neon glow of the city pulsed against the dark, painting the window in fractured light. A single, crumpled ticket—red and faded—fell from her pocket, landing with a soft *thud* on the floor. She didn’t notice until she bent to pick it up, her breath catching. The name on the back was scrawled in ink that had long since faded: *"For You, Always."*
