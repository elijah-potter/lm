**"The Last Transmission"**

It wasn’t supposed to happen like this.

The radio crackled in his pocket before he’d even turned on the engine. A static hiss, then—*static*—a voice, breathless, whispering through the interference: *"Run."* The transmission ended before the words made sense. His heart hammered in his chest as he floored the gas, the tires screaming against the cracked asphalt. Behind him, the forest burned. Not from the storm—from *him*.

The road twisted into the mountains, higher and steeper than he’d planned. The GPS had failed before he’d even left, but he’d memorized the coordinates from the old map in his glove compartment. *No one should ever need this.* The words were half-truths, half-accusations. His father had said that. His father had always said things that were never meant to be true.

The cabin loomed ahead, its windows shattered, smoke curling from the chimney. His boots splashed through water that shouldn’t have been there. The door was locked. He kicked it in anyway.

Inside, the air smelled of mildew and something metallic—blood? His pulse roared in his ears. The last thing he saw before the lights went out was his own reflection in the broken mirror: hollow-eyed, sweating, *alive*.

The radio was still on the desk. Static again. Then—

*"They’re coming."*
