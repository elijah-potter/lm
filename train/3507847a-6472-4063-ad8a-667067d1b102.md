The dim glow of a flickering streetlamp cast long shadows across the cracked pavement of an abandoned alley. A lone figure crouched against the cold concrete, hands clenched around a half-empty cup of bitter coffee, steam rising lazily into the damp night air. Their breath formed small, invisible clouds in the chilled evening. The air smelled of damp earth and distant rain, and the distant hum of a city’s pulse—car horns, distant laughter—felt distant, muffled, like a dream half-remembered.

A pocket watch ticked, slow and deliberate, its brass face half-lit by the streetlamp’s flicker. The figure had arrived here hours ago, but no one had come. No one *needed* to. The waiting was the only thing they had ever known. Somewhere, a door swung open. The sound was muffled, swallowed by the wind. The figure stood, shoulders stiff, and stepped into the alley’s hollow darkness, where the next wait would begin.
