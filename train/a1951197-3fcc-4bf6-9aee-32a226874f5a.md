**BREAKING:** Major Flooding Hits New Haven’s Riverfront District After Unpredictable Storm Surge

New Haven residents scrambled as severe flooding swept through the Riverfront District late last night after a sudden storm surge caused by a rare tropical weather front converging with unusually high tides. Emergency services reported over 400 calls for assistance, with at least 12 homes submerged up to waist-deep. The city’s newly renovated waterfront promenade—once a major tourist attraction—now resembles a flooded canal, with debris drifting toward downtown.

Authorities declared a state of emergency, activating all available flood barriers along the Thames River. The National Guard was deployed to assist search-and-rescue efforts, though no fatalities have been confirmed as of this morning. The city’s transit system, including the historic river ferry, was shut down until further notice, causing significant disruptions for commuters.

Mayor Eleanor Carter issued a statement urging residents to avoid the area until further notice, warning of potential landslide risks along the riverbanks. Schools and businesses have been ordered to close until at least tomorrow afternoon, while volunteers from local fire departments and the Red Cross are coordinating cleanup efforts. The city’s disaster response team is investigating whether this flooding could be linked to climate change patterns, though initial reports suggest the storm was not part of a long-term weather trend.

Meanwhile, nearby residents in the suburbs reported heavy rain but no significant flooding. The National Weather Service has issued a prolonged warning for the region, advising residents to prepare for additional rainfall and potential secondary effects like power outages.
