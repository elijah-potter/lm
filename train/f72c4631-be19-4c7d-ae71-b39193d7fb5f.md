The final light of dusk bleeds through the cracked windows, painting the crumbling stone walls in hues of burnt copper. The air hums with the scent of damp earth and old wood, thick with the ghost of laughter and the faint tang of something sweet long since forgotten. Dust motes swirl in the dim glow of a single flickering lantern, its flame weak, casting long shadows that dance across the warped floorboards. A half-eaten sandwich crumbles in the corner, its crust still crisp despite the passage of time. The scent of coffee, bitter and warm, lingers in the air like a relic—someone’s last lingering touch.

The floorboards groan underfoot, each step echoing like a confession. A door, once sturdy, now hangs askew, its hinges rusted to silence. A painting of a younger time hangs crookedly on the wall—faces blurred, eyes hollow. The last notes of a song, half-played on a keyboard, still vibrate in the walls.

The weight of what’s left is pressing down. The silence is too loud. The end is coming, and the place remembers it all.
