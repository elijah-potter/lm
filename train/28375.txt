A Link Between Deodorant and Breast Cancer?
There is no proven link between antiperspirant or deodorant use and breast cancer. In fact, according to the U.S. Food and Drug Administration, which regulates food, medicines, and cosmetics in the United States, the ingredients in antiperspirants and deodorants are not known to be cancer-causing.
One large study supported this claim. It looked at 813 women with breast cancer, and 793 women without breast cancer. These women were asked to report on their use of underarm antiperspirants and deodorants, and whether they used these products within one hour of shaving with either blade razors or electric razors. The study found no increase in breast cancer risk among women who used antiperspirants and deodorants, even when these products were applied within one hour of shaving, compared with women who reported not using deodorants and antiperspirants.
Regarding the risk of specific ingredients, there has been some discussion of preservatives known as parabens (p-hydroxybenzoic acid), which are found in many antiperspirants, deodorants, and cosmetics. While parabens may mimic the effect of estrogen in some cells in test-tube studies, this effect has never been shown in humans, and there are no data showing that exposure to parabens increases the risk of breast cancer in humans.
