A skeletal concrete foundation clings to the ground, ribs of unformed steel rebar jutting from the rough surface like the ribs of a half-cooked skeleton. The walls, exposed internal forms still in place, are cracked in places where the plaster has fallen away, revealing the raw, rusting wood and metal studs beneath. A single, tilted doorframe leans against the wall, its hinges bent from years of neglect, the wood warped and split. The ceiling above is a chaotic tangle of exposed ductwork and electrical conduits, some cradled in plastic, others dangling precariously like the bones of some unfinished beast. Dust blankets everything, a thick, gray shroud, while the air smells of damp, mildew, and the faint metallic tang of unchecked rust. The place hums with the ghostly sound of a broken AC unit, a single bulb flickering weakly, casting a sickly yellow glow over the desolate remains. The floor is uneven—some areas sagged under its own weight, others are still supported by makeshift wooden braces. The place is a testament to half-hearted efforts, a place that should have been abandoned years ago but still stands, a silent, half-built monument to half-finished dreams.
