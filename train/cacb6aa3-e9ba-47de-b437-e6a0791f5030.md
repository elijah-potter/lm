The silence of a room left empty after you’ve left
is not absence—it’s a hollowed-out space
where the air hums with the weight of what you were
before you walked out. The coffee cup, half-drunk,
stands like a silent accusation.
The bed, unmade, holds the shape of your absence,
a ghostly imprint of how you used to lie there.
The mirror shows nothing—no reflection,
no trace of the person who once stared back.
The absence is in the way the light catches
the edges of the room differently now,
as if the world has adjusted to your absence
and found a new way to bend around it.
You’re not there, but you’re still here,
in the way the doorframe feels different,
in the way the last note on the piano
lingers, half-sung, half-forgotten.
