A damp corner of the old warehouse’s wooden floor creaked underfoot as Elias crouched beside the rusted iron door. His fingers traced the chipped paint where the handle had once been—now half-buried under layers of dust. The fluorescent light flickered overhead, casting long shadows that stretched across the cracked concrete.

He had come for the keys. A single key, lost months ago in the same spot where he’d left them—*the key to the safe*, the one that would unlock the family’s heirloom. His mother had begged him to find it, saying it was the last thing keeping their name alive in the family’s long-neglected business.

But the key wasn’t there.

His breath hitched. He pulled out his phone, fingers trembling. The screen read: *"404: Key not found."* A text from his sister, sent hours ago: *"I can’t wait to hear what you found."*

Elias’s phone slipped from his grip. It clattered against the floor, shattered glass spraying the dust. He knelt, fingers digging through the wreckage—until his thumb brushed something cold and smooth. A small, tarnished silver locket, its clasp broken. Inside, a faded portrait of a young woman in a ballgown, her eyes staring back at him. No name, no date. Just a whisper of a memory.

His hands trembled as he opened it again. Inside, a single silver thread—barely visible—was wrapped around a small, polished stone. The kind of stone that belonged in a watch, not a locket. His blood ran cold. He knew what this meant.

He pocketed the locket. The keys were gone. But this—this was something else entirely. Something that didn’t belong to the past. It belonged to the future.
