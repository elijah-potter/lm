The moment was quiet—just the hum of the city outside, the faint scent of rain on pavement. **She** stood at the edge of the crowd, arms crossed, watching the protest march. Her phone buzzed—a reminder: *"Tomorrow, the rally starts at 8."* The text felt like a ghost whispering in her ear: *What if you’re wrong? What if they’re right?*

Then she saw **him**—a kid, no older than sixteen, with a backpack slung over one shoulder, his hands trembling as he held up a sign. The words were scrawled in ink that hadn’t yet faded: *"Stop the war."* The crowd murmured, but she heard his voice above the noise: *"I’m doing this because I’m scared, but I’m not leaving."*

A choice had been made before she even realized it. The weight of it settled in her chest—not regret, not doubt, but something heavier, like the first sip of coffee on an empty stomach. The crowd parted, and she stepped forward. Not because she had to. Not because she was brave. Just because she was there.

The world didn’t stop. The rain didn’t wait. But for that moment, she chose. And the act was hers.
