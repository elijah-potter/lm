The space is a void where no light passes—walls of obsidian blacken under unblinking starless eyes. Shadows here do not move; they *are* the walls, the ceiling, the floor, all at once. The air hums with a frequency that makes ears bleed unless muffled by thick, damp wool. No sound remains; even silence is a ghost that lingers too long. Maps here do not exist; the mapmaker’s ink turns to mist the moment it touches paper. The only constant is the thing that never was: the absence of a horizon, the absence of time, the absence of *you*—though you might think you’re still here, because the walls whisper your name in a language that dissolves as soon as you try to name it.

The place refuses to show itself.
