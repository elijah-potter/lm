A flickering neon sign read **"DO NOT ENTER"** in jagged letters, though the glass was cracked. The air smelled of ozone and something older—rust and damp earth. The warning was written in blood, though no one had seen it there before this night.

The old man in the corner of the bar didn’t look up. His fingers traced the rim of his glass, a slow, deliberate circle. *"You shouldn’t be here,"* he murmured. His voice was a whisper, but the bartender—who had been listening—swallowed hard.

The door behind them groaned. A figure stepped into the dim light, cloaked in black fabric that swallowed sound. The warning on the sign pulsed like a heartbeat. The bartender’s hand trembled as he slid a glass toward the stranger. *"First drink’s on you."*

The stranger tilted their head. *"I know why you’re here."* Their voice was smooth, like honey poured over ice. *"But the choice is yours."* The sign above them flared, then went dark.
