A lulling mist curled over the cracked pavement of an old alley, damp and soft beneath the faint glow of a single streetlamp. The last of the evening’s din had faded—whispers of laughter from the nearby diner, the distant clatter of a bus. A child’s laughter, muffled and muffling, escaped from a closed door. The wind carried the scent of rain and old wood. The air hummed with silence, thick and warm, like the last breath of a fire.

On the cracked concrete, a small, half-empty glass jar sat beside a tattered bookmark. Inside, a single, faded photograph—a woman’s face, blurred and ghostly—fell out, landing on the ground. The bookmark was a yellowed page torn from an old journal, its edges frayed, the words smudged: *"Sometimes the end is just the quietest part."* The jar’s lid was loose, dust motes swirling inside.

A shadow moved near the window of the abandoned storefront. A man in a worn coat, his hands tucked into his pockets, leaned against the frame. His breath was slow, even. The door creaked open, sealing the world outside with a sigh. The light from the streetlamp flickered, casting long, shifting shadows across the pavement. The scene held still, suspended in time.
