A dimly lit office at 3:17 AM. The fluorescent lights flicker as the last of the staff exits, leaving only the hum of the printer and the soft, rhythmic tapping of a keyboard. **Mark** leans back in his chair, fingers tracing the edge of his open notebook, the last entry crossed out and rewritten twice. His phone buzzes—a silent notification from his wife’s number, a text she never reads aloud: *"You’re late."*

Across the desk, **Lena** adjusts her glasses, her peripheral vision catching the way his shoulders tense just before he exhales. She doesn’t need to say it. The question lingers between them like a half-remembered dream.

The door creaks open. **Daniel**, the night shift’s newest hire, slips in, his tie half-done, a coffee cup still steaming. He glances at Mark, then Lena, and the unspoken words—*"You’re both here because of me"*—are swallowed by the silence. He tosses the coffee onto the desk, the cup shatters. The sound is final. No words are spoken. The room absorbs the weight of what’s understood.
