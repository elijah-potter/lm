The old wooden box lay half-buried in the damp earth of an abandoned farmhouse’s cellar. The rusted hinges groaned as I lifted it, revealing a crumpled paper inside, sealed with a wax stamp bearing the initials **"V.E.K."** in faded ink. The handwriting was scrawled across the front, illegible but urgent:

*"If you find this, know the river’s whispering secrets. The stones remember names—mine included. Meet at the old mill on the third, before dawn. Bring what you fear most. —V.E.K."*

A torn photograph slipped from beneath the wax seal—a blurred portrait of a woman with silver-streaked hair, her gaze fixed on something unseen. The edges of the frame were singed, as if caught in flames. The mill’s clock tower, standing skeletal against the storm clouds, loomed over the crumbling barn. The air smelled of rain and copper. I knew then what I had to do.
