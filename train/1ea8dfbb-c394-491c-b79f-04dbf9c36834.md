**The Hollow House**

The rain had stopped by the time she found the door, rusted half off its hinges, half buried in ivy. The key turned like a promise—old, smooth, and stubborn. Inside, the air smelled of damp wool and something sour beneath, like old tea left to ferment.

She stepped in and the floor groaned beneath her boots. The walls, once white, were now paper-thin, peeling in places where the light didn’t reach. A single candle flickered in the corner, casting long shadows that danced like things watching.

The room was a cage, but not for her. It was for the voices that whispered in the cracks between the walls, for the things that sat in the corners, half-eaten by time.

She had come for answers. But the answers were already here.

The door creaked behind her.

Then—footsteps.
