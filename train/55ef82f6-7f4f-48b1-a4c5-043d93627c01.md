A small, rustic barn in rural Iowa was transformed into a surrealist extravaganza. Locals, dressed in mismatched vintage attire, lined up with mismatched instruments—a theremin, a kazoo, and a stolen guitar from a roadside garage—performing a medley of folk and electronic music. A lone DJ played looped vinyl records while a DJ booth’s speakers crackled with distorted feedback.

The centerpiece was a "time capsule" constructed from salvaged materials: a weathered radio, a cracked clock, and a glass jar filled with preserved letters from a failed 1990s rock band. A group of teenagers in neon scrubs danced around a holographic projection of a 1980s neon sign that flickered with static.

Guests sipped from mismatched cups labeled with handwritten notes like *"For the one who still believes in magic"* or *"Warning: May cause existential crisis."* The food was a chaotic buffet of homemade pies, stale donuts, and a single uncooked chicken leg labeled *"Unsafe to eat"* in bold, childish scrawl.

A fire pit in the middle of the barn crackled with discarded CDs, their plastic melting into a bubbling mess. A man in a top hat stood on a stool, reading a speech about "the fleeting nature of human connection," while a woman in a raincoat sang a hauntingly off-key version of *"Don’t Stop Believin’"* into a microphone attached to a bicycle bell.

The event was organized by a group calling themselves *"The Chronically Unstable"*—a loose collective of misfits, conspiracy theorists, and people who had once worked at a failing bookstore. They claimed the barn was a "time capsule of lost dreams," though no one could explain why the celebration happened at 3 AM on a Tuesday.

A single, discarded party hat lay on the floor, half-crushed under a stack of crumpled napkins. The crowd parted as a child in overalls ran past, shouting, *"Did you see the ghost in the corner?!"* before vanishing into the shadows.
