**The Last Light**

The café’s neon sign flickered like a dying star, casting long shadows over the empty tables. Outside, the rain hammered against the windows, each drop a silent whisper of the city’s forgotten secrets. I sat at the bar, my fingers tracing the rim of an empty espresso cup. The waitress passed by, her heels clicking against the worn linoleum, but I didn’t look up. The clock on the wall had already lost two hours.

A text message from my mother’s name appeared in my pocket—*Darling, the lights are out in the attic again. Come home.* My thumb hovered over the screen, then hesitated. The attic had been a tomb for years, a place where memories and dust had long since buried what was left of me.

I exhaled sharply, the breath fogging the glass. The reminder was written in my bones: *You can’t outrun what you’ve buried.* But the past was a ghost that only answered when you let it.

The door creaked open. The rain had stopped. Somewhere, a siren wailed.

I reached for my phone again.
