Ephemeral mist curls around the abandoned church’s stained-glass windows at dusk, filtering light into golden, shifting patterns. The wooden pews creak in unison as the wind whispers through the gaps, each note a ghostly echo of past sermons. The old brass organ pipes, frozen mid-register, seem to hum with residual vibrations, as if still playing a hymn no one has heard in decades. The bell tower’s clock hands stop at 11:11, their tick-tock now a deliberate, rhythmic pulse, as if counting down to an event only it can predict. The air hums with static—faint, like static on an old radio—but the voice isn’t coming from any speaker. It’s in the hollows of the stone, the spaces between the cracks in the floorboards, the way the shadows on the walls stretch *just so* as if drawn by unseen hands. At night, the windows glow faintly blue, reflecting the glow of a distant streetlamp that never seems to fade, as if the light is trapped here, waiting. The trees outside are skeletal, their branches forming a living lattice that filters the moon’s light into prismatic, shifting constellations on the pavement below. The scent of damp earth and old wood lingers, thick enough to taste, like the lingering aftertaste of something spoken in hushed tones long ago. The ground beneath your feet *moves*—not visibly, but in the way your boots press into the dirt, as if the earth itself is holding its breath. The silence here isn’t absence; it’s a breath held, waiting.
