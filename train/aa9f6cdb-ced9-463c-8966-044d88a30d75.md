Silence wrapped around the room like a curtain at dusk, thick and unbroken. The clock on the wall chimed midnight, each toll sharp and distant. Outside, rain drummed against the windows, a steady, rhythmic pulse—unheard, unnoticed.

Daniel sat at the kitchen table, fingers tracing the edge of the half-empty coffee cup. His mother’s voice had faded into static, swallowed by the TV’s muted static. She’d said nothing for over an hour, not a word, not a glance, not even a sigh. The silence was the only thing that moved.

His phone buzzed—a text from his sister: *"You’re still here?"*

He didn’t respond. The screen remained dark.

The door creaked open. A gust of cold air seeped in, carrying the scent of wet pavement and something metallic—his own breath. His mother stepped into the room, her coat damp, her eyes shadowed. She didn’t speak. Just stood there, arms crossed, watching.

Daniel exhaled, slow and deliberate. The silence between them was a promise.

He picked up the cup, the coffee cold. Sipped. Let the first drop linger on his tongue before swallowing.

*"You know what you want,"* she said, voice low.

He met her gaze. *"I do."*
