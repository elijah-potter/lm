When the last light of evening faded into the quiet street, Maria sat at her kitchen table, clutching a half-empty coffee cup. The decision she’d made earlier in the day still lingered like a ghost—one she’d initially relented to out of fear, but now, the weight of hesitation settled into her chest. The call from her estranged sister, who had moved across the country, had been brief but charged with a plea: *"We need to talk."* She’d hesitated, not because she didn’t want to, but because the weight of the past felt heavier than the future.

Now, her fingers traced the rim of the cup as she replayed the conversation in her mind. Her sister’s voice had been raw, her words sharp with the ache of silence between them. *"You’re the one who left,"* she’d said, *"and now you want to fix it?"* Maria had wanted to tell her yes, but the words stuck. The guilt, the unfinished business, the fear of drowning in it all—it had paralyzed her. Then, a knock at the door shattered the silence.

Her neighbor, an older man who always left her coffee, stood there, his face lined with exhaustion. *"You look like you’ve seen a ghost,"* he said, handing her a fresh cup. She exhaled, the tension in her shoulders easing. The decision wasn’t about him, but it was a reminder that change, like life, wasn’t always clean or easy. As she walked back inside, her sister’s words echoed: *"You’re stronger than you think."* The coffee was cold, but the decision to pick up the phone was warm.
