For some time now, the alfalfa was in bloom and rocked the bees back and forth on the dawn wind. Leaves brushed across my cheek and danced above the flattened crop where I lay. I swatted them away and brushed off dry dirt coating the right side of my face that was there from sleeping on my side. I scrunched my brow as the bright sun shined in my eyes and raised my palm to block the bright light. Field bugs were crawling over my shins but I was too bewildered to really notice, as I took in the overgrowth where the walls of my bedroom should be. I was on cold ground. Not in my bed. But as I lay there, something moved quickly by my feet. There couldn't be any other people here for at least a mile, and there was no noise but my own and the song of a bluebird perched in the nearby woodline. 

I sat up. A scrape across my knee burned as I moved my legs closer to me, though there was no blood. I stood on all fours and leaned over to look into the field growth before me. It was a groundhog hole, or once was. Any number of creatures could inhabit this residence now. I must have tripped on it. The whole of yesterday felt like a nightmare, and as I stared down into the hole, my mind ran blank from the present moment. I was safe, for now. But in my peripheral, two amber eyes lined in black watched me cautiously in the shadow of the tall foliage. I blinked at them, and they blinked back. It cocked its head to the right as if it had asked me a question. I sighed in relief. But the fox turned and leaped back through the alfalfa, his bushy tail gracing the violet blooms. I'd better get going, too, I thought.
