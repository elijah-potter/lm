The air smelled of damp earth and pine, thick with the scent of decay and something older—warm, like honey left too long in a jar. The river below whispered, its surface rippling with unseen currents, reflecting the last rays of a setting sun.

The docks were empty, save for the rusted remains of old boats, their hulls worn smooth by time and water. A few boards sagged, leaning precariously over the edge, as if the entire structure were tilting toward the water. The wind carried the scent of salt and something metallic, like old blood drying in the sun.

A single lantern hung from a rusted beam, swaying gently in the breeze. Its glass was cracked, but still, it cast a flickering glow over the moss-covered stone steps that led down to the water. The steps were slick with rain, their edges worn away by decades of footsteps.

Beyond the docks, the forest stretched endlessly, its canopy dense and unbroken. The trees stood tall, their trunks blackened by smoke or fire, their leaves a muted green, as if they had lost their vibrant color long ago. A few birds chirped, their calls sharp and distant, like distant thunder.

The sky was a bruise of colors—purple at the edges, bleeding into the last remnants of the day. It was the kind of sky that made you feel small, that made you wonder if you were the only one who had ever looked at it this way.

The last time I saw this place, I felt like I was being pulled toward something unseen, something beyond the river, beyond the trees. I carried this feeling with me, like a shadow that couldn’t be shaken off.
