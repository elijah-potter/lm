Cast of long, narrow valleys where light bends like a river’s edge—soft at dawn, sharp as knives by midday. The north walls bear deep, ancient shadows, crumbling like stone teeth, while the south slopes stretch thin and restless, flickering like smoke in a hurricane. Midday’s rays dance across the floor like trapped fireflies, forming geometric labyrinths where the light itself seems to weave.

At twilight, shadows swell into living things—dragonflies with wings of shadow, their bodies flickering between earth and sky. The air hums with the weight of their movement, a low, resonant frequency that makes the ground vibrate beneath bare feet. Some shadows linger too long, pooling like stagnant water, whispering secrets in a language of static and silence. Others vanish abruptly, as if dissolved by the first breath of wind. The wind, too, is a shadow—thin, shifting, and always hungry.
