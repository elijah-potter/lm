A corporate meeting where every participant had memorized the script—microphones crackled as the CEO delivered a polished, pre-recorded speech, the slides projected flawlessly in sync with the timing. Attendees nodded in unison, eyes scanning the document in front of them, not the room. The air smelled of stale coffee and the faint, synthetic tang of a meeting room designed to minimize distractions. The pause after the CEO finished was the most natural part of the whole performance, yet it felt like a calculated moment, a deliberate misdirection to let the audience breathe before the next pre-approved transition. A single phone rang in the corner, its ringtone a jarring interlude—someone had forgotten to mute their own device. The rest of the room, like a well-oiled machine, resumed as if nothing had interrupted. The moment was a loop, replayed thousands of times, until even the participants forgot they were acting.
