A sleek, silver smartphone lay on a wooden coffee table, its screen glowing softly blue. Across the room, a woman in a faded denim jacket adjusted the strap of her backpack, her fingers brushing the edge of a small, black pouch. She turned, walked toward the table, and placed the pouch on the glass surface beside the phone. A man in a worn button-down—his hands calloused—reached out, picked up the pouch, and slipped it into his own backpack. The moment his fingers closed around it, the pouch vanished into the folds of his fabric, leaving only the faint scent of old leather behind.
