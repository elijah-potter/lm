The city streets feel smaller after years away—narrower alleys, quieter traffic. The old bookstore on the corner still has its dusty shelves, though the second-floor window is boarded up. The café where I used to meet friends now serves coffee in mismatched mugs, and the barista remembers my order but doesn’t know my name.

The park bench where we used to sit has a new coat of paint, faded where we sat together. The lamppost still casts long shadows, but the tree roots beneath it have spread differently, now creeping toward the sidewalk.

Home feels like a story I half-remember, one where every detail—from the scent of the bakery’s croissants to the way the subway entrance creaks—makes it feel like I’m returning to something that’s been waiting for me.
