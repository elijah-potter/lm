Groundbreaking research on the brain's ability to shield vital parts of itself from the destructive damage caused by a stroke has revealed the exact causes of the phenomenon.
According to a new journal published in the journal Nature Medicine, cells switch to a 'survival mode' that causes them to produce a protein called harmartin, which forces cells to conserve energy.
The study builds on observations made over the past 85 years that suggested some areas of the brain are able to withstand the shortage of oxygen and sugar caused by strokes, although the specifics were not fully understood until now.
Professor Buchan of Oxford University, who conducted the research, said: 'We have shown for the first time that the brain has mechanisms that it can use to protect itself and keep brain cells alive.'
Scientists hope to replicate the effects of harmartin in the future in order to devise a drug capable of forcing the entire brain to conserve energy in the event of a blood clot.
