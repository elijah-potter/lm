The remnants of **Blackwood Hollow** still whisper in the wind. A forgotten valley where the old-timers say the last family left in 1923 after the **Blackwood Incident**—a fire no one could explain, or the bodies of three children found mid-dance, their hands still clutching a single, tattered doll. The trees there grow twisted, their bark cracked with patterns like scars. The locals avoid the place, but children still dare to play near the **stone circle**, where the elders claim the old ones gather at dusk.

The **Blackwood Ledger**, a yellowed ledger found in an attic, records transactions of the past century: payments in whiskey, favors, and debts in kind—mostly rendered by those who vanished. The last entry, written in 1978 by a woman who signed only **"M"** (her name was **Magdalena Voss**), reads:

*"They did not die. They just… stopped being here. The river remembers them. The earth remembers them. But no one does."*

A single **silver locket**, engraved with a crescent moon and a skull, was found in the hollow’s deepest ravine in 2009. The inside was empty. The outside bore only this inscription:

*"You were never gone. Only waiting."*
