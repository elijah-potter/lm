**"The Last Light"**

She found the book in the attic—its spine cracked like old bones—because the floorboards had creaked just once too often. The ink on the pages was smudged with time, with fingers that hadn’t touched it in decades. The title was faded: *The Weight of Silence*.

She read it in the dark, the way people read love letters they knew were never meant for them. The words slithered past her like snakes, each one a confession she’d never dare voice aloud. The author had written of the way the world hums when you’re alone, of the way the stars don’t blink because they don’t need to. Of how some things are not meant to be shared, not even with yourself, because the truth is always softer when whispered in the dark.

At the end, there was only this: *"I am the ghost of what I was not. And that is enough."*

She folded the pages carefully, as if the paper might still hold something she could reclaim. Then she burned them. The ashes blew away before they could settle into her hands.

She was not sorry for it.
