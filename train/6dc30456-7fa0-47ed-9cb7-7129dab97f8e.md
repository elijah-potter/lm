A lone figure stood before a cracked mirror, the shards of its reflection shattered like glass at dawn. Their shadow stretched long across the floor, a serpent coiled around a half-eaten apple. The room smelled of damp stone and old paper, the walls lined with faded maps of a forgotten kingdom.

The figure reached into their pocket, pulling out a single coin. It was silver, worn smooth by time, but still gleaming faintly. It was the same coin they had found on the floor of their childhood home, a place they swore had burned to the ground.

Their breath fogged the air as they turned—back to the mirror. The figure smiled. "Another day, another circle," they whispered. The coin clinked against the edge of the table. The mirror’s reflection flickered once, then settled into the same stillness as before. The apple sat unnoticed, half-eaten, its seeds now sprouting green tendrils on the floor.

The figure stepped forward, the coin warm in their palm. The kingdom’s maps whispered in the wind, and the stone walls seemed to breathe. The mirror’s cracks split wider, revealing a path not seen in centuries. The figure took one last breath, then vanished into the dark. The coin fell into the apple’s hollow center. The seeds grew into roots, and the floor beneath them pulsed with ancient, hungry life.
