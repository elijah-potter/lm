The decision to adopt open-source software for the company’s internal tools was initially framed as a straightforward cost-benefit analysis—savings on licensing fees versus the risk of custom development. However, the complexity arose from unanticipated dependencies: the legacy ERP system required integration with proprietary middleware, and third-party compliance audits exposed hidden vulnerabilities in open-source libraries. The team’s resistance stemmed from fear of technical debt and the lack of long-term support guarantees, while leadership emphasized scalability and innovation. Ultimately, the decision was deferred to a committee, delaying the rollout while internal debates raged over autonomy versus efficiency.
