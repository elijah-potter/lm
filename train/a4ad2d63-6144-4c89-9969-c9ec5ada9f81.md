**The Hollow Between Two Worlds**

A cavernous mouth yawns beneath cracked stone, its jagged edges illuminated by a sickly green glow from bioluminescent moss. The air hums with static, the scent of damp earth and rusted iron clinging to the damp walls. Here, the air feels *wrong*—too dense, like it’s thickened with unseen weight. The floor tilts subtly, a slope leading toward what might be a door, but the wood is warped, half-buried in the earth, its hinges long rusted beyond use. Shadows stretch unnaturally, long and writhing, as if something is *waiting* beyond.

A single step forward, and you might step into something—or someone. A whisper, faint and distant, echoes from the darkness: *"You were never meant to find this."* The light flickers, and the walls dissolve into shifting patterns of memory: a child’s laughter, the hollow ache of a lost language, the slow, creeping stain of time. The threshold is not a door, but a *breach*—and crossing it might mean becoming part of it.
