The waiter bowed slightly as he placed the teacup on the table, but the nervous tremble in his hands made the delicate porcelain wobble. "Just a moment," he muttered, his voice cracking when he added, "before we proceed with—" The customer, a woman in a sharp blazer, stared at the cup’s edge, where the tea had splashed onto the tablecloth. "I beg your pardon," she said, adjusting her glasses, while the waiter’s face turned an unnatural shade of red. He wiped the teacup with a napkin, but the stain remained. "The service was impeccable," he stammered, "but—uh—perhaps you’d like another?" The woman tilted her head. "I’d like to see if the tea still tastes like tea," she replied, before turning to her friend. The waiter, now sweating, whispered to the manager, who sighed. "Next time," he muttered, "we’re hiring someone who doesn’t turn into a human flamingo."
