In a city of towering skyscrapers with holographic billboards flickering between retro-futuristic neon and organic solar panels, autonomous drones hover above streets where traffic lights pulse in real-time adaptive traffic grids. Sidewalks feature embedded touch-responsive paving that shifts between tactile paths and smooth digital surfaces. People navigate through augmented reality overlays, adjusting their pace to avoid glitching elements that appear in the air—AI-generated projections of weather, historical data, or speculative futures. Buildings grow and reconfigure midday, their facades morphing via robotic facades to host temporary exhibitions, cafes, or emergency shelters. Public transport runs on magnetic levitation loops, while personal pods glide through underground networks or sky lanes. The hum of constant evolution is the only constant—each generation inherits a world that outpaces their understanding of how it was built.
