A rusted iron gate creaks open under the gentle sway of a windblown tree, its branches casting shifting shadows across the weathered path. At the far end of the lane, a lantern hangs from an oak beam, its glass face still intact despite the decades. Inside, a hearth crackles, casting flickering light on a table draped in a single, embroidered cloth. A parchment lies open, its edges curled from age, where a calligrapher’s quill has traced words in a language older than the village itself:

*"The silver key lies buried where the old well’s stone cracks—take it, and the door opens."*

A child’s laughter echoes from the kitchen, where a woman’s voice murmurs, *"It’s a promise, my dear. But promises are like seeds—sometimes they grow, sometimes they rot."* Outside, the lantern’s glow reflects on the promise left unspoken in the air between them.
