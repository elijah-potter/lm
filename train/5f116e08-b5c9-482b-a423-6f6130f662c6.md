The air smelled of damp wood and old coffee, thick with the scent of yesterday’s rain clinging to the walls. Broken glass glinted in the dim light filtering through the shattered panes of the north-facing window, where a storm had rattled the frames earlier. The floorboards beneath cracked underfoot, one splintered plank jutting upward, half-hidden under a tattered red blanket that once hung on a hook by the bed.

A single candle—its wick unlit—sat in the ashtray beside the half-empty coffee mug, its wax dripping onto the worn rug, a faded floral pattern now smeared with ink. The bedspread, once crisp, now sagged in places, the sheets tangled around the edge of the mattress where a pillow had been tossed aside. A half-eaten sandwich sat on the nightstand, its crust cracked, the paper wrapper crumpled into a ball beside it.

The closet door was ajar, its contents spilling out—shoes rolled in a heap, a coat draped over the doorknob, a pair of scuffed boots left behind in a hurry. A half-packed suitcase lay open on the floor, its contents spilling onto the floor: a frayed sweater, a torn map, and a single photo frame with its lens broken.

The desk in the corner was untouched, its surface gleaming under the single desk lamp, but the chair behind it had been pushed in, its legs scraping against the floor. A stack of papers—some crumpled, others still neat—was half-hidden beneath a stack of books, their spines worn from frequent handling. A half-filled water glass sat on the desk, its rim still damp with condensation.

The floorboards groaned faintly underfoot, as if something heavy had been moved recently. The only sound was the distant hum of the city beyond the windows, muffled by the rain-streaked glass.
