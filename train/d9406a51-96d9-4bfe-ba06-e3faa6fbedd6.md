A single step—tossing a crumpled napkin into the trash instead of tossing it on the floor—can ripple through a room, altering the scent of dust and the hum of idle conversation. The act of *not* leaving a trail of litter might not seem profound, but it’s the kind of quiet decision that, when multiplied by millions, transforms cities from dumping grounds into cleaner spaces. The same principle applies to habits: a single morning meditation session might not change your life overnight, but it’s the seed of consistency. That seed, planted daily, grows into a garden of resilience. Small actions—repairing a worn-out chair instead of replacing it, saying "thank you" instead of assuming kindness—are the threads weaving together a tapestry of intentional living. Change, like a fire, often starts small, fueled by the embers of repetition. The difference between stagnation and progress? One is a single, deliberate step. The other is a thousand.
