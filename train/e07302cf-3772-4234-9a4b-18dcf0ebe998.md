A flickering candle in an empty chapel, its wax pooling unnaturally on the altar. The incense burns too slow, its scent thick with dust. A hollow chime rings once, then stops. A stone slab beneath the altar has no mark, though it was once inscribed with symbols. The priest’s robes are too long, frayed at the hem, their embroidery faded. The last offering—a rusted silver coin—is wrapped in a tattered cloth, its edges singed. The congregation stands in rows, silent, their faces blurred by the dim light. A single voice whispers from the shadows: *"This was never for us."* The ritual ends when the candle burns out, leaving only the echo of a voice that no longer belongs.
