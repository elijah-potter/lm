Pit pat pit pat. The rain falls down onto my nylon windbreaker and rolls onto my face. At least it masks the tears. The city is nothing but grey. I can’t tell if it’s me or if it’s actually like that. I’m walking along the Schuylkill River trail. I look to my left and see the rain crashing into the river. I stare at it for a bit, absorbing and taking in how the raindrops each create little ripples that bounce off each other as they land. Much like how we humans bounce around the endless expanse, bumping into each and uniting, only to leave each other with no notice.

I hate how that can happen, one day you cross ripples and you’re together, you’re happy, and you’re sharing cheesecake. The next she’s rippling off into the expanse with all of her stuff and you’re stuck paying full rent on an apartment you agreed to split despite only having your name on the lease. Then you struggle to find a way to make that money, so in an attempt to take your mind off the turmoil, you go on a walk, even though it’s pouring rain. But it just makes you think about it all more.

The clouds cover the buildings, I see the tower that is the Comcast building, but not the top. The light peers through the clouds, reminding me it’s still there, just hidden for the day. I continue walking north towards the art museum. 

We had our first date at the art museum.

I need to stop thinking about her. I can’t walk toward the art museum, instead, I get off the trail and head east toward Broad Street. I wonder if anything is playing at the Kimmel Center? That could be a fun activity. She never did let me see plays once things got serious. Always said we needed the money for other things. Am I just doing this to spite her? Regardless, I need something to do. Something that doesn’t remind me of her. 

But I wonder if that’s even possible? She’s everywhere.

Whatever. I’ll just take it as it comes.
