AITA for insisting my sister pay for her daughter’s wedding after promising to do so for fifteen years?

Okay, this is… complicated. It started back in 2009. My sister, Eleanor, and I were both young, relatively carefree, and living in Chicago. Eleanor was engaged to David, and the wedding planning was in full swing. I was working as a graphic designer at a small firm in River North, earning a decent wage, and she was a paralegal downtown. During a particularly emotional conversation at a cafe on Wells Street, she tearfully confessed she was struggling to afford the wedding she envisioned. I, feeling incredibly generous and wanting to ease her stress, impulsively offered to cover the cost – essentially, to pay for the entire wedding. I was 24 then, and frankly, a bit naive. I believed I’d always have the means to fulfill that promise.

Fast forward to 2014. I’d moved to Denver for a new job and a change of scenery. Eleanor and David had divorced in 2016, a messy situation involving infidelity on his part. She’s been single since then, working as a legal secretary in Indianapolis. She started seeing Mark in 2020, and they're now engaged. This time, she's planning a wedding for her daughter, Chloe, who is 28. 

Chloe’s wedding is scheduled for October 27th, 2024, at a vineyard in Napa Valley. The estimated cost is $45,000. Eleanor has been dropping hints for months that she’s strapped for cash and needs help. Initially, I brushed it off, assuming she's just being dramatic. Then, last Tuesday, July 16th, she explicitly asked me for $20,000 towards the wedding. I gently reminded her about my earlier promise. She became visibly upset, claiming I “never specified a dollar amount” and that “things are different now.” She pointed out that I’m now married with a mortgage and two young children, and she’s facing financial hardship. 

My husband, Ben, thinks I should just let it go. He argues that fifteen years is a long time, and my financial situation has drastically changed. He says I shouldn’t feel obligated to fulfill a promise made when we were both significantly younger and less responsible. He’s also worried about the strain it could put on our own finances. He’s not entirely wrong – $20,000 is a significant sum.

Eleanor's fiancé, Mark, has weighed in as well. He’s been subtly pressuring me, implying that I’m being selfish and that my sister is counting on me. He’s framed it as a family obligation. I feel manipulated by both of them. My parents are caught in the middle, telling me to “do what my heart tells me.” 

I’m torn. Part of me feels guilty for breaking a promise, even one made so long ago. But another part of me feels resentful, especially given Eleanor’s current lifestyle – she enjoys expensive vacations and designer clothes, which seems inconsistent with her claims of financial hardship. I’m also concerned about setting a precedent – if I cave now, will she expect me to fund every major life event in the future? 

I’m seriously considering just sending a generous gift instead, but I fear that will only escalate the drama. I'm feeling overwhelmed and honestly, a little taken advantage of. ESH, probably, for both sides. My sister for expecting this after all this time, and me for making a promise I couldn’t realistically keep.

AITA for refusing to pay for my niece’s wedding?
