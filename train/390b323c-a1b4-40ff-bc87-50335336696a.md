The last light of dusk spills over the cracked pavement of the old square, painting the broken cobblestones in hues of copper and rust. The air hums with the scent of damp earth and distant fire, mingling with the faint metallic tang of the river that meanders through the ruins beyond. The church tower, its gargoyles weathered into silent sentinels, leans slightly over the edge of the world—its stained-glass windows shattered, only fragments of swirling colors still visible in the void where the rest once hung. Children’s laughter echoes from the crumbling schoolhouse, their voices too thin, too far away, like ghosts of a time that never was. The market stalls, though still standing, bear no sign of the vendors who once traded here. The scent of garlic and spices is gone, replaced by the acrid stink of rust and old stone. The last time I walked these streets, there were more people, more life. Now, it’s just the wind and the memory of what used to be.

The river’s banks are lined with willow trees, their branches gnarled and twisted, their leaves whispering secrets to the water. The water itself is restless—rippling too fast, too violently, as if the earth itself is mourning. The docks, once bustling with activity, are now overgrown with ivy and wildflowers. The last boat that passed through here carried something away: a secret, perhaps, or a promise left unkept. The ferry slip is empty, the ropes frayed and tangled in the muck. The last time I came, the river’s reflection was sharp and clear. Now, it’s just a dark, distorted blur.

Beyond the ruins, the forest has swallowed the last of the city. The trees are taller here, their trunks twisted into ancient, sentient shapes. The air smells of damp earth and pine, thick with the weight of something older than memory. The last time I walked into the woods, I heard a sound—something between a cry and a laugh, echoing from the canopy. Now, it’s silent. The path I took once was wide and well-trodden, but now it’s a narrow ribbon of moss and twigs, leading nowhere. The last time I followed it, I thought I saw my own reflection in a puddle. It was wrong. It wasn’t me. It was something else.

The last time I stood at the edge of this world, I thought I could hear my name called from the distance. I turned, but there was no one there. The wind carried it away, and the sound faded into the static of silence. The horizon is a blur of color and light, the sun setting in a way that doesn’t feel like sunset anymore. It’s like watching the end of something beautiful from afar, too far to stop it from happening.

The last thing I remember seeing was the sign at the edge of the square, its paint long faded: *"Here lies the last place you’ll ever need to return."* It was a lie. But the world doesn’t care. It keeps moving, keeps changing, keeps forgetting what was here before. And I am just another ghost in the wreckage.
