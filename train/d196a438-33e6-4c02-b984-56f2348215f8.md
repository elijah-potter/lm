## Explain Like I'm Five: Why the Sky is Blue

Okay, imagine you have a box of crayons, right? And in that box, you have all sorts of colors: red, yellow, green, blue, purple, and so on. Sunlight is kind of like that box of crayons – it’s actually made up of *all* those colors mixed together! We can’t see them all separate when sunlight shines on us, it just looks white or yellow.

Now, imagine you're playing with those crayons, and you're bouncing them off a bumpy trampoline. Some colors bounce more easily than others. Blue and violet are like bouncy, energetic crayons – they bounce *a lot*! Red and orange are more like sleepy crayons; they don’t bounce as much.

The air all around us, the stuff we breathe, is full of tiny, tiny bits, much smaller than even a grain of sand. When sunlight comes down from the sun, it bumps into those tiny bits of air. 

Just like the crayons bouncing on the trampoline, the colors in sunlight bounce off those tiny bits of air. But blue and violet bounce the *most*. They bounce all around the sky!

Because blue light is bouncing everywhere, that's the color we see when we look up. It's like the sky is covered in tiny, invisible bouncy balls of blue light!

Sometimes, when the sun is setting, it looks orange or red. That’s because the sun has to travel through *more* air to reach us. All the blue light has bounced away already, so we mostly see the red and orange colors that didn't bounce as much. 

So, the sky is blue because blue light is the bounciest and spreads out the most when it hits the air! Isn't that neat?
