The last light flickered into silence before dawn’s whisper
through cracked glass—just a breath of rusted metal
and the scent of damp earth clinging to the walls.

The door had always been a lie,
a promise carved into wood that bled
when left open too long. Now it stood
half-swallowed by the garden’s overgrown roots,
the keys still wrapped in the old man’s apron,
their edges dull as his patience.

He sat in the chair where he’d sat last,
the same cup of tea, the same half-eaten biscuit,
but this time, he didn’t reach for the door.
Instead, he wrote his name in the dirt
with a stick, the same way he’d learned
as a child, tracing the letters
of a world that had long since stopped talking.

The letters formed a map—
not to a place, but a list.
The first was *beginning*,
then *again*,
then *what was lost in the turning*,
and finally, *the one who wrote them all.*

Outside, the garden shifted.
The roots coiled tighter.
The door groaned—not open, but *unlocked*,
as if it had always been waiting.
