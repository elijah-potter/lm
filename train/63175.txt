Police officers who work with a dog, known as a canine officers, handle their animals and help to train them on tasks that humans cannot, such as drug, bomb and illegal substances detection. This highly demanding job requires specialized training in addition to traditional police work. The U.S. Customs and Border Protection, Homeland Security, Transportation Security Administration and the Department of Alcohol, Tobacco and Firearms also utilize canine officers.
Handling and Obedience
A canine officer’s main duty is to handle and ensure obedience from his dog; this comes through extensive instructional programs and off-duty training. Canines often live with their handlers, who are responsible for maintaining their training on and off-duty. Handlers use code words that their dogs understand, and they feed them a strict, healthful diet.
Just like regular police officers, canine officers are also responsible for apprehending criminals. Though they do not do regular traffic stops, canine officers patrol the streets and ensure the safety of public and property. Canine officers may find criminal suspects by searching buildings and open areas.
Canines have a keen sense of smell. Therefore, officers are responsible for handling canines during tracking. Tracking is used to find criminal suspects, missing persons and evidence. Canines are able to track and locate faster and safer than police officers alone.
Often, canine officers are called in to assist with drug and bomb detection. Canines are trained to detect heroin, methamphetamines, cocaine, marijuana and other narcotics. They search cars, homes, businesses and yards. A canine officer may be called in to locate drugs or bomb materials before a search warrant is issued by a judge.
