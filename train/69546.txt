Why Do Pimples Hurt So Bad?
Pimples are a part of life that can cause physical and emotional discomfort. They can arise for a variety of reasons and the pain associated with them can range from mild to extreme. So why do pimples hurt so bad?
Hormones play a major role in the development of pimples. During puberty, the body’s hormones fluctuate as it prepares to become an adult. These fluctuating hormones can cause the skin to become more oily and more prone to breakouts. When the skin gets too much sebum, which is an oily substance, it can clog pores and cause breakouts. When this happens, the body’s natural inflammatory response kicks into gear and causes swelling and pain in the affected area.
Pimples can also be caused by infection. Bacteria that lives on the skin can get into the pores and cause inflammation. This is often the case with cystic acne, which is a deep form of acne caused by bacteria. This form of acne is often more painful than a regular pimple due to the presence of bacteria.
While it’s impossible to completely avoid pimples, there are steps that can be taken to reduce the discomfort associated with them. Here are a few tips on how to prevent painful pimples:
- Keep your skin clean: Washing your face twice a day with a gentle cleanser can help reduce bacteria and sebum buildup.
- Use topical acne treatments: Over-the-counter topical treatments can help reduce the inflammation and pain associated with pimples.
- Moisturize: Moisturizing can help reduce the appearance of dry, flaky skin, which can make acne worse.
- Avoid touching or picking: Touching or picking at pimples can cause the bacteria to spread and make the situation worse.
Pimples can be both unsightly and painful, but understanding why they are so painful can help you take steps to prevent them. While it’s impossible to completely avoid pimples, taking steps to keep your skin clean and moisturized and avoiding touching or picking can help reduce the discomfort associated with them.
Why do pimples leave scars?
Pimples can leave scars because of the skin’s natural healing process. When skin is damaged, the body creates new collagen fibers to repair the damage. If too much collagen is formed, it can lead to a raised scar. Additionally, some people are more prone to scarring than others due to genetics.
Why are some pimple scars more pronounced than others?
Pimple scars are more pronounced depending on a few factors: genetics, depth and type of acne, and skill of treatment. Genetics play a role in the tissue texture of facial skin and how much scarring from acne is likely to remain. Cysts and nodules, two deeper types of acne, are more likely to leave visible scars because the lesions are larger and go deeper within the skin. Treatment options can also affect the appearance of acne scars. If a person opts for
dermabrasion or laser therapy, the results may be more noticeable.
Additionally, people with darker skin are more likely to have more noticeable scars, since the contrast between the acne scar and the skin color is more pronounced.
