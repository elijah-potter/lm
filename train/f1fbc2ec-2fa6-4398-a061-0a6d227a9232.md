The rain fell in silent sheets over the old mill, its rusted gears groaning under the weight. Elias wiped the sweat from his brow with a damp cloth, his fingers trembling slightly. He had been apologizing for the past week—not for the mistakes he’d made, but for the weight of them.

His father had died two years ago, leaving behind only a ledger of debts and a half-finished bridge. The mill was in ruins, the bank had called in the loan, and the village had turned its backs. Elias had been the one left holding the pieces.

Tonight, he stood at the edge of the village square, staring at the empty cobblestones. The mayor had asked him to speak first, a sign of respect for his father’s name. But Elias only wanted to disappear.

*"I apologize for failing you,"* he muttered to the wind. *"I apologize for the silence in my heart. I apologize for the bridge that will never cross."*

The crowd shifted, murmuring among themselves. A child’s laughter cut through the damp air, bright and indifferent. Elias exhaled sharply, his breath misting in the cold.

The mayor cleared his throat. *"Elias..."*

The story was already written in the cracks of the pavement. He just had to walk into it.
