Choosing to wait was not an act of surrender but a deliberate pause to reclaim agency. The clock ticked, but the mind refused to rush. A stranger’s voice—soft, hesitant—asked, *"Why do you stand?"* The answer was in the silence: patience was a form of resistance, a quiet rebellion against the rush of expectations. The hands of the clock became a mirror, reflecting not time’s passage, but the weight of decisions deferred. Somewhere, a tree stood still, its roots deep, its branches unhurried—proof that stillness could be strength. The wait was not empty; it was the space where decisions were measured, where hesitation became a choice. The future, though distant, was not lost—it was being shaped, one unhurried moment at a time. The pause was not defeat; it was preparation. The act of waiting was the first step toward reclaiming control.
