Waiting is a quiet rebellion against the rush of time. A stranger sat in a café, fingers tracing the rim of their coffee cup, lost in a book about the ocean’s secrets. Outside, the city hummed with urgency, yet here, the silence was a promise. They waited not for the next move but for the right one—one that felt like home, not a sprint. The train’s whistle carried a message: patience is its own kind of courage. Waiting isn’t absence; it’s the space between choices where stillness becomes strength. A door clicked open. They stepped forward, lighter.
