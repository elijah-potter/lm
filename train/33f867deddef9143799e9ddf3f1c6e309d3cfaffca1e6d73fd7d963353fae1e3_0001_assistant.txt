The rabies virus, like most deadly viruses, can change the way they act in response to environmental changes. The rabies virus is transmitted by an infected animal biting another animal because that is the way the virus evolved. The number of people bitten by rabid animals and the number of people dying from rabies has dramatically declined over the past 30 years. However, scientists are concerned that the rabies virus could, through natural mutation or genetically engineered mutation, change into a virus that would turn infected people into "zombies". Wild animals, such as foxes, skunks, raccoons and vampire bats usually transmit rabies in about a ten-mile area. Unfortunately, there are no natural barriers, such as forests or rivers, to keep infected animals from entering our crowded cities. It would be easy for a wild animal carrying the mutated rabies virus to bite a person and kick off a zombie apocalypse. Like any rabid animals, infected people would bite and infect those around them, thus creating a race of zombies. A simulation of such an event projected that it would only take about a week from the first bite before most of the US population became zombies. For people living in remote areas of Nevada or Montana, it would only take a month before the zombies found them and turned them into zombies.
