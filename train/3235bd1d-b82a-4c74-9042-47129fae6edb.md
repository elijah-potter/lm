A hollowed-out oath, its words like brittle glass—shattered easily but never truly broken. The promise was meant to be a bridge across a storm, its promise of shelter and warmth woven with care, yet the weight of it grew heavier than the rain itself. The keeper spoke of change, of transformation, of a life reshaped by their commitment. But change, as they said, was not a straight path—it was a labyrinth of detours, of choices that unraveled the thread of trust.

They spoke of trust, of faith, of something deeper than mere words. Yet the deeper they dug, the more the ground shifted beneath them. The promise was never about the destination but the journey, and the journey became a gauntlet where every step felt like betrayal. The storm raged, and the keeper’s hands were tied, their arms stretched out in supplication, but the winds only howled louder, twisting the words into something they could never keep.

They swore on a map of lies, where every landmark was a mirage, and the compass spun wildly, pointing not to safety but to ruin. The keeper’s voice cracked with each retelling, each confession a knife in the wound. The promise was a lie from the start, a lie that grew into something monstrous, something that devoured the giver and the taken alike.

The world outside was a mirror of what they promised, but the reflection was wrong. The storm never ended, and the bridge they spoke of was never built—only a skeleton of its promise, standing half-collapsed, its beams twisted into something unrecognizable. The oath was broken not by the weight of the words, but by the weight of the world they refused to change.
