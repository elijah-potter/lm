The dim glow of a flickering desk lamp cast long shadows across the cluttered study. A half-empty coffee cup sat beside an open notebook, its pages filled with scribbled notes and half-finished equations. The fingers of the searcher, stained with ink and time, traced the air above the worn leather-bound atlas, tracing the coordinates they’d memorized from a dream half-remembered.

The words they sought—*the pulse of the old clockwork*—were never written down, never pinned to the wall. They were a whisper in the static of the radio, a number in the margin of a textbook no one had read. The searcher knelt by the antique desk, its surface worn smooth by decades of restless hands, and whispered aloud, *"Where is it?"*—not into the void, but into the fabric of the room itself, as if the answer might seep from the walls like steam.

The clock on the wall—its hands frozen mid-second—ticked slowly, as if counting down. The wind rattled the windowpanes, but the glass was thick, heavy, and unyielding. The searcher exhaled sharply, the breath curling like smoke in the cold. Outside, the city slept, unaware. Inside, the hunt continued, blind and endless.
