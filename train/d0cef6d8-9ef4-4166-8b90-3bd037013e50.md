The first recorded routine was likely the development of agricultural practices around **10,000 BCE** in the **Fertile Crescent**, marking the shift from nomadic hunting-gathering to settled farming, which became a foundational routine for early civilization.

Earliest documented routines include:
- **Cuneiform tablets** (Mesopotamia, ~3000 BCE) describing agricultural cycles, temple rituals, and labor assignments.
- **Ancient Egyptian scribes** recording daily work schedules for the Nile’s flooding season (~2000 BCE).
- **Roman law codes** (e.g., *Twelve Tables*, ~450 BCE) standardizing public and private routines.

Key early routines:
1. **Sunrise–sunset labor cycles** (e.g., water distribution, harvest).
2. **Temple/state-mandated rituals** (e.g., offerings, tax collections).
3. **Seasonal festivals** (e.g., Assyrian agricultural festivals, Greek *Thesmophoria*).

Later, industrialization (1700s) formalized modern routines: clockwork schedules, factory assembly lines, and bureaucratic hierarchies.
