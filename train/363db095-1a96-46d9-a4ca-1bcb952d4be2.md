The first sip of the whiskey was cold on his tongue, bitter as the weight of what he’d done. The phone call had been brief—just enough to confirm the details of the deal, the time, the location. His hands trembled, but he forced himself to stay seated, the weight of the gun still warm in his palm. Outside, the neon glow of the alley flickered like a dying ember.

He knew it was wrong. He’d said it a thousand times in his head—*She’d never forgive you*—but the moment passed, swallowed by the rush of adrenaline and the thrill of the game. The plan had been simple: take what wasn’t his, disappear into the crowd like smoke. But now, as he watched the distant figures moving in the dim light, he wondered if he’d just become another shadow himself.

The regret was a whisper, sharp and fleeting, but it lingered in the hollow between his shoulder blades. He exhaled, slow and steady, trying to erase it with every breath, every step toward the exit. The city hummed on, indifferent. He was just another man lost in its noise.
