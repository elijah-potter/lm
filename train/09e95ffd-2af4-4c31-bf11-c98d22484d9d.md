The ruins of **Tir Na Nog**, a once-thriving valley in the highlands of **Ard-Cairn**, now lie half-buried beneath moss and forgotten stone. The locals whisper of its name as if it were a ghost: *"Land of the Evergreen."* Once, it was a place of song and trade, where the rivers ran silver and the air hummed with the chimes of ancient bells. But the war that razed the northern passes took its toll, and the valley was swallowed by the encroaching **Black Spire**, a towering monolith where the earth itself seems to sigh in grief.

The few who remember describe it in fragments: the **Whispering Trees**, their bark etched with symbols that shift like liquid mercury; the **Silent Lake**, where the water reflects not the sky but a mirror of faces that no longer live. The **Drowned Choir**, a cathedral where the arches are stained with the hues of a thousand sunsets, now crumbles into dust. Some say the wind still carries the last words of its inhabitants, though no one can hear them unless they listen with their eyes closed.

The only evidence left is the **Candle of Last Light**, a brass torch embedded in the old well, still flickering faintly at dusk, as if burning for those who pass. The locals avoid the area at night, but children sometimes drag pebbles from the riverbed, each one smoother than the last, as if polished by unseen hands. The **Black Spire’s shadow** stretches long over the land, a reminder that some places are not meant to be forgotten—only remembered.
