The neon glow of the abandoned clinic pulsed through the cracked window like a dying heartbeat. Outside, the rain hammered the rusted roof, turning the alley into a slick mirror of the city’s forgotten corners. We didn’t mean for this to happen, but the night had other plans.

Inside, the air smelled of mildew and something older—something that clung to the walls like dust. The door had been left ajar for weeks, and now it stood open, as if the building itself had been waiting. The fluorescent lights flickered weakly, casting long shadows that stretched like fingers across the floor.

She found him first. His back was turned, hunched over a desk piled with crumbling files and half-empty bottles of something dark and viscous. His fingers, thin and pale, moved with deliberate precision, though his eyes were closed. She thought he might be asleep, but when she reached out, her hand brushed something cold against her palm—a needle, sharp and abandoned. His breath hitched. She stepped back, heart pounding.

“You shouldn’t have come,” his voice whispered, rough and unsteady. The words were barely audible over the rain’s relentless song.

She should have left. But the moment she hesitated, the door behind her swung shut with a creak that made her jump. The clinic’s ancient alarm blared, a shrill, mechanical wail that cut through the gloom. The lights died. Darkness swallowed them whole.
