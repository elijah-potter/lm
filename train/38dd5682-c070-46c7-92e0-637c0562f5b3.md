In the dim glow of a neon-lit alleyway, the old clock struck twelve. The air smelled of rain and stale beer. Dr. Elias Voss adjusted the straps of his coat, his fingers trembling slightly as he reached for the door handle. His wife’s voice echoed from the apartment above: *"Elias, I need you. Please."*

The hinge of the door squeaked as he pushed it open, revealing a cramped living room. His wife lay on the couch, her breath shallow, her fingers clutching a small, tarnished ring. The device in her palm pulsed faintly, a beacon of something ancient and dangerous.

She whispered, *"The key is in the ring. Open it."* His pulse roared in his ears. The moment he turned it, the world dissolved into a swirling vortex of light. When it settled, he stood in a hallway lined with mirrors—each reflecting a different version of himself.

In the third mirror, his younger self smiled. *"You did it. Now what?"* The ring in his grip glowed brighter, and the air hummed with energy. The voice in his mind, that of the entity inside the device, whispered: *"Choose. The door in the mirror, or the one behind you."* The hinge of fate turned.

He chose the door behind him. The moment he stepped through, the alley vanished. He was standing in a vast, endless library, shelves stretching into infinity, each book a potential fate. A hand clutched his shoulder. *"The choice was yours. Now, live it."* The ring fell from his fingers, dissolving into dust.

The last thing he saw was the clock striking thirteen.
