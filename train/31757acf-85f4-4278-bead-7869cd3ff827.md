The river hummed a lullaby of silver light,
where willows bent like poets in the breeze.
Dawn whispered secrets to the morning dew,
a slow unraveling of the world’s first breath.

The city’s clockwork heart still ticked,
but the clock itself had paused to listen—
to the distant train’s last sigh,
to the crackle of embers in the hearth,
to the way the light through the window
turned the floor into a map of stars.

The storm had passed.
No longer did the wind scream its old refrain,
no longer did the walls hold the weight of forgotten wars.
The sky, once a canvas of chaos,
now cradled the sun in a velvet glow,
soft as a lover’s goodbye.

Calm returned like a sigh—
not the forced quiet of exhaustion,
but the quiet of understanding.
The world had learned its rhythm again,
and so had we.
