A hidden alley in an old town, where the pavement cracks tell stories of unspoken laws. The right side of the street yields to the left, though no signpost says it. A shared bench between two families—one with children, one with elders—ensures silence when the elders speak, and laughter when the children do. The butcher’s stall stands empty when the market is closed, though no bell rings. The older generation knows where to walk with their dogs, and younger ones learn by watching. The library’s oldest books are kept in the corner, not because of a rule, but because the librarian *would* notice if they weren’t. The tavern’s "quiet hour" starts when the first lanterns flicker, though no clock is posted. The rule is in the unspoken rhythm of the place itself.
