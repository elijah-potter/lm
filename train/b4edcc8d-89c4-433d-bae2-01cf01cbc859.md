A dimly lit tavern’s murmur thickens as the fire crackles low. A grizzled old man leans over a wooden table, his fingers tracing the rim of a half-empty mug. His voice, rough as gravel, carries the weight of decades.

*"You ever wonder where stories come from?"* He pauses, letting the question hang between them like smoke. *"I’ve heard tales from men who’ve walked this earth longer than most, and the oldest ones? They’re not mine. They’re older than memory itself."*

He exhales smoke, then begins to spin the tale of the first hunter who lured a beast to the edge of the forest by whispering the names of the stars. The beast, bound by the weight of forgotten words, fell into his trap—not with claws or fangs, but with the hunger of a storyteller’s magic. The hunter, though he did not realize it, had already told the tale when he named the moon *"Silver Veil"* and the river *"Song of the Wild."*

*"The beast was the first to remember,"* the old man continues, *"because it was the first to listen. And that’s when the stories started to live."*

Outside, the wind howls through the tavern’s broken windows, carrying echoes of a world where words are both weapons and prayers.
