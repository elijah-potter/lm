The streets smelled of rain and rusted metal after years of neglect. Neon signs flickered weakly, their colors faded by time, casting long shadows over the cracked pavement. The café’s windows were boarded up, but the old sign still hung—*Morning Brew*—though the letters were half-erased. Inside, the counter groaned under the weight of decades; the coffee machine hissed, its steam rising in lazy spirals through the dust-choked air. A single, battered stool held a half-empty mug, its rim stained with years of forgotten coffee. The barista, if he could be called that, sat hunched over the register, fingers trembling slightly as they typed. The music—if it could be called that—was a loop of static and old vinyl cracks, the kind that played in empty rooms. The scent of stale coffee, burnt sugar, and the faint metallic tang of age filled the space. Outside, the distant hum of a passing truck carried the sound of the city’s pulse, though it felt distant, like something far away. The place was still here. But it had changed.
