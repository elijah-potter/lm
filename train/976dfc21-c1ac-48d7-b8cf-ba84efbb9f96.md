The hum of distant traffic filtered through aged wooden windows, each one cracked and whispering secrets of time. The air smells like damp earth and old incense, drifting from a forgotten shrine in the corner, its brass lamps casting flickering shadows that seem to pulse in sync with unseen breath. The walls, covered in moss and lichen, breathe slowly, shifting with the weight of unseen hands—someone’s, or something’s. The floorboards groan underfoot, each step a rhythm that matches the slow, deliberate sway of a doorframe that doesn’t quite close. A low, rhythmic *click-click-click* echoes from beneath a table, like teeth gnawing at the dark. The walls themselves seem to exhale—subtle, warm breaths that linger like a sigh before vanishing. Shadows stretch and twist, not like light, but as if something is shaping them, pulling them into shapes that don’t quite align with the room’s corners. The silence is loud. Every sound is magnified, every breath felt. The air hums with a static charge, like the static on a radio tuned to nothing. And sometimes, when the wind howls just right, the whispers start—soft, like voices speaking in a language that isn’t quite language at all. It’s not a place that *hears*. It’s a place that’s *listening*.
