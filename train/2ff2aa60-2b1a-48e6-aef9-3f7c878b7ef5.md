**Character:** Elias Veyne, a scholar of arcane lore, stood before the cracked archway of his ancestral library, fingers tracing the dusty spines of forgotten tomes. The air hummed with the weight of centuries—each whisper of parchment a ghost of debates long since settled.

The *Book of Echoes*, his most prized possession, lay open on the table before him. Its pages glowed faintly, as if alive with the remnants of a forgotten language. Elias had spent years deciphering its secrets, but now, as he leaned closer, the ink *moved*. Not ink—*writing*. The words rearranged themselves into a new sentence:

*"You seek what was never yours to claim."*

His breath hitched. The book had always been a mirror of his own thoughts, but this… this was *judgment*. The library itself seemed to lean in, the shelves whispering like a chorus of disapproving spirits.

He turned, his reflection in the glass-walled chamber not quite his own—distorted, younger, a boy standing in his place. The boy smiled, but his eyes were hollow, and in them burned the same accusation:

*"The past is a debt. You will pay it."*

Elias clutched the book tighter. The library doors creaked open behind him, revealing the same boy—now an older man, his face lined with the same sorrow that had been his since childhood. *"You were always meant to keep,"* he said, voice rough with age. *"Not understand."*

A storm raged outside the window. Lightning split the sky, casting Elias’ shadow three times—once as a scholar, once as a child, once as the hollow-eyed boy in the reflection. The air smelled of ozone and old paper.

The book’s glow pulsed. The last line appeared, final:

*"Choose now."*

The room spun. When it settled, Elias stood alone in the dark, the weight of centuries pressing down on him—not as a scholar, but as a man who had spent his life chasing answers when the truth was the only question that mattered. The library was silent. The boy was gone.

Only the book remained, its pages blank again. But the ink was waiting.
