The garden was quiet, a small space between the cracks in the world, where the sun’s slow, golden fingers traced the edges of a carefully tended plot. The tomatoes—plump, unassuming—hung like overripe jewels, their skins soft as morning breath. The bees, lazy at first, began to stir at the faintest scent of blooms, their wings a whisper against the stillness. The earth remembered how to hold things, how to wait, how to yield. Sometimes, the best things are the ones you don’t notice until they’re almost gone.
