The skeletal remains of a crumbling stone bridge span a forgotten river, its archways half-buried under moss and ivy. The walls, once grand, now show cracks like veins of age, some patched with rusted metal that has long since corroded. A single, overgrown garden plot beside it bears the faint scent of damp earth and wildflowers that refuse to die. The path beneath the bridge, once polished stone, is now uneven, splintered planks jutting from the decay. A small, half-finished statue of a bird, its wings half-carved, sits abandoned near the center—its face smooth but the rest unfinished, as if paused mid-sculpture. The air hums with the occasional rustle of leaves, and the water below mirrors the bridge’s decay with ripples that never settle. No one comes here anymore, but the wind still carries whispers of what was once built here—maybe a temple, maybe a watchtower, maybe just a dream left to rot. The place exhales.
