The air smells of damp stone and old incense, thick with the scent of centuries. The altar is draped in a single, faded tapestry—its colors faded to muted blues and grays, the figures almost lost in the fray. The pews, once rich in polished wood, are now splintered and worn, their surfaces etched with the names of long-departed worshippers.

The organ rests, its pipes hollow and silent. The clock on the tower’s facade ticks, but time feels heavier here, each second dragging. The bells, once so sharp, now chime only once, then stop. The last echo fades into the echo chamber of the nave.

Your breath fogs in the cold, the church’s high ceiling pressing down. You look down at your hands—calloused, tired—and realize how quickly this place has become a refuge. How quickly it has become everything.

The door at the back creaks open. The wind howls through the empty aisles, carrying with it the ghosts of the past. You don’t know if you’ll ever return. You don’t know if you’re even coming back.

The last light dies. The cathedral stands alone in the dark.
