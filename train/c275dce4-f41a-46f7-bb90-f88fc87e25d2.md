The storm clouds gathered unnaturally fast, blotting out the sun as the wind howled through the skeletal branches of the old oak. Rain lashed against the glass of the abandoned library, turning the once-clear pages into a blur of waterlogged ink. Inside, the last student sat hunched over a worn desk, a single half-finished poem in their trembling hand—the words were a storm of unspoken fears, each line a tempest of unresolved conflict. The air smelled of damp earth and ozone, thick with the weight of forgotten dreams.

Outside, lightning split the sky in jagged, silvered streaks, casting eerie reflections on the rain-soaked pavement. The wind carried whispers of the past—the echoes of laughter, the muffled sobs of those who had come before, their stories lost to the elements. The student looked up at the storm, their breath fogging in the cold, and for the first time, they realized the words were no longer just ink on paper—they were a storm of their own making, and the sky was watching.
