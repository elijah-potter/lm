A flickering neon sign cast eerie shadows on the cracked pavement outside *The Last Drop Tavern*, its bulb dimly pulsing like a dying heartbeat. Inside, the air smelled of stale beer and warm coffee, thick with the murmur of a dozen voices—some low, others raised in hushed arguments. The only light came from the single bulb in the corner, casting long, twisting shadows across the worn wooden tables.

A group huddled near the bar, their faces half-lit by the glow of a phone screen, its glow bleeding through the cracked screen like a dying star. The bartender, a grizzled man with a scar running from his temple to his jaw, wiped down glasses with a rag soaked in something dark. His hands shook slightly, but his voice was steady when he called out, *"Another round for the crew. And don’t forget the old lady in the corner—she’s got the last can of beer left."*

The power flickered again, sending a surge of static through the room. A woman in a faded leather jacket clutched her coffee cup, her breath shallow. *"Another outage, another night,"* she muttered, but her words were swallowed by the sudden silence that followed.

A child—no older than eight—clutched her mother’s hand, eyes wide. *"Mommy, what’s happening?"* The mother’s face was pale, her lips pressed thin. *"We’re just waiting for the lights to come back,"* she lied, her voice trembling. Outside, the wind howled through the broken windows, rattling the glass like a warning.

At the far end of the bar, an old man sat alone, reading a newspaper by the dim glow of a single candle. His fingers traced the words slowly, as if each letter were a promise. When he looked up, his eyes were hollow, but his smile was sharp. *"Best days are the ones that don’t end,"* he murmured. The room held its breath.
