The clock struck midnight in the old town square, its chimes echoing through the frozen streets like distant whispers. The baker’s daughter, Clara, knelt beside the hearth, her fingers tracing the edges of a scroll she had unrolled from the chest beneath her bed. The ink was still damp, and the words pulsed with a faint, unseen energy. Outside, the wind howled through the skeletal trees, but inside, the house hummed with a warmth only Clara knew existed.

She had always known this moment would come. The scroll was the key to the door beneath the floorboards, the one that led to the forgotten things—the things the world had forgotten to ask about. The words on the page were not of this time, not of this place, but of a time before time, before the first breath of a world that had yet to remember itself.

Clara opened the chest carefully, her breath shallow. The key was cold, its surface etched with symbols she recognized—stars and rivers and a single, unbroken line that curled into infinity. She inserted it into the lock with trembling hands and turned. The mechanism groaned, then clicked.

The floor beneath her shifted, and the world dissolved into a void of shifting colors and sounds. She was not falling. She was being lifted. Above her, the sky was not blue but endless, a vast ocean of swirling light that stretched into the dark. The air tasted of something like honey and ash.

A voice, not a sound but a presence, filled her mind. *"You have opened the door, but you have not yet begun."*

Clara’s eyes flew open. The room was hers again, the scroll still in her hands, the chest closed. The clock had struck again, but this time, the chime was different—soft, resonant, as if sung by many voices at once.

She unfolded the scroll, her fingers trembling. The ink was dry now, but the words were still there, waiting. She read them aloud, not knowing what they meant, but knowing they would.

*"The beginning is the end, and the end is the beginning of the beginning."*

Clara stood, her feet no longer touching the floor, and walked toward the door. Outside, the town slept, unaware. But inside her, something had awakened—a hunger, a memory of a world that had been before her time and would be after hers. The scroll was not a map; it was a promise. And now, she was ready to walk through the door again.
