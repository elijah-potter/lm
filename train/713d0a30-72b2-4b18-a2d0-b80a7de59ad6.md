In the quiet hum of a midnight café, where the scent of espresso mingles with the faint whisper of distant traffic, I sat across from an empty chair. The city outside pulsed with life—laughter, neon, the relentless rhythm of existence—but here, in this corner of the world, time had slowed to a whisper.

I had been chasing something, a shadow that moved too fast, a voice that demanded answers I wasn’t ready to give. But the coffee, warm and slow, had finally lulled me into stillness. The first drops of rain began to tap against the window, a steady, comforting rhythm. Outside, a street musician played a melody that didn’t demand attention, just let it wash over you like the tide.

A friend had once said, *"Calm is not the absence of noise—it’s the absence of fear."* And for the first time in weeks, I believed them.

Now, the city breathes. The world keeps turning. And I, for once, am just listening.
