The first time a routine was observed, it was in a controlled laboratory setting analyzing neuronal spike patterns in a rat’s brain during a simple behavioral task. Researchers recorded consistent, predictable sequences of neural activity that aligned with specific actions—e.g., movement or sensory response—over multiple trials. The repetition of these sequences suggested an emerging, self-reinforcing process that deviated from pure randomness, marking the transition from stochastic to structured behavior.

Early theories linked this to a form of procedural learning, where the brain developed a "routine" to optimize efficiency, though the mechanisms (e.g., synaptic plasticity, reinforcement learning) were still being mapped. The term "routine" was later adapted to describe such repetitive, optimized behaviors in both biological and artificial systems, reflecting their role in efficiency and adaptability.
