The first light of dawn painted the sky in hues of gold and crimson, as Elias sat on the edge of his grandmother’s old rocking chair, the scent of jasmine clinging to the air. His fingers traced the worn edges of the book in his lap—*The Silent Years*—its pages yellowed with time. Outside, the village slept, its whispers muffled by the rustling leaves.

He had come here to find answers, but the questions had outnumbered the answers. His mother’s voice still echoed in his mind: *"The well doesn’t run dry, Elias. It just changes."* But what did that mean? Did it mean the past was still here, waiting to be drawn from the depths like water from a well that refused to empty?

The wind picked up, carrying the distant hum of a church bell. Would it ring for him? Would it call him home, or would it be the last sound he’d ever hear? The book slipped from his hands, landing with a soft *thud* against the wooden floor. He didn’t pick it up again.
