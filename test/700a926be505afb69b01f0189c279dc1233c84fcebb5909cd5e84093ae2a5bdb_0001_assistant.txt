My name is Desti. Not Dusty. Not Desi. And no, it’s not short for Destiny. I’m just Desti. I am a nurse at Clearwater Valley Community Hospital, and I’ve been working to help manage coma patients for the past two years. I’m currently on the night shift, which involves monitoring my patients’ vital signs, keeping an eye on the ventilators, and giving medications. It is usually very quiet, which is how I like it. 

It started out like any other night—nothing out of the ordinary. Trina, the nurse who was responsible for updating me when I arrived, had nothing to report. There had been no changes with any of the seven patients we were monitoring, so it looked like it was going to be an easy night. When Mike Clarke came in at 9 p.m. to say goodnight to his wife, I waved to him as he headed down the hall toward her room. He had checked in downstairs, and I knew him well. He had been coming to the hospital five nights a week, Monday through Friday, for as long as I had been working there. 

As far as I knew, he had never missed a night, at least when I was on duty. He usually stayed about half an hour, read Julie a story or just sat with her and held her hand, and left. it was so sweet and also heartbreaking. She had been in a coma for over two years, but he never gave up hope. I was surprised when he came to the nurses' station after only a minute, and even more surprised by his following question:
“Where’s Julie? She’s not in her room.”
