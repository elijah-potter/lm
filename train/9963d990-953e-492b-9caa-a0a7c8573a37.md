The warehouse door creaked open under the weight of the forklift, revealing a dimly lit interior. Stacks of crates, labeled with faded logos, were arranged in an irregular pattern—some near the ceiling, others leaning precariously on the floor. A single worker, arms laden with a pallet of half-processed goods, paused mid-step, glancing at the partially sealed shipment marked **"DO NOT OPEN"** in bold red ink. The foreman’s voice crackled over the PA system, *"Team, the new shipment is delayed—no one is to handle it until the proper inspection."*

The worker hesitated. The rules were clear: no unauthorized access, no bypassing security protocols. But the pallet was already open, the seal torn. The goods inside were unmarked, yet the packaging bore the same supplier’s logo as the others. The worker reached into their pocket, pulling out a slip of paper with a single line: **"Just a heads-up—no harm intended."** They stepped back, letting the forklift driver take over. The shift ended as it always did, but this time, the foreman’s gaze lingered on the open crate—unnoticed, as usual.
