Once upon a time in the old town square, a rule was carved into stone:
*"No one may light a fire in the evening without permission."*

It was simple, practical—fire in the streets meant chaos. The town elders enforced it strictly, but after a decade, the rule faded from memory. The first night a young smith, eager to finish his forge, lit a fire without asking. The townsfolk noticed the smoke, then the flames, then the chaos. The elders were called, the rule was recalled—but it was too late.

Now, the town square still has the stone rule, but no one remembers it. The smith’s fire became legend, a cautionary tale of hubris and forgotten wisdom. The rule was written, but not remembered. The lesson was learned, but not preserved.
