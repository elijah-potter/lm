The shouting stopped abruptly. A man’s knuckles bled against the glass of a café window, fingers pressing deep. Across the table, a woman’s coffee cup shattered, splinters glinting under the fluorescent lights.

“You’re the one who called me a liar!” she hissed, voice trembling. Her dark eyes darted between the shattered remains and the man’s furious face.

He wiped his hands on his jeans, his voice rough. “I *was* a liar. But not for the reason you think.” He gestured to the coffee stain on his sleeve. “I didn’t steal that from you. I *paid* for it. But the bill was a fake. You were the one who—”

“You were the one who *threatened* my sister,” she cut in, hands clenched. “And now she’s in rehab because of *your* family. Your *damaged* family.”

A beat of silence. The café’s lone customer, a woman with a newspaper, glanced up. The man exhaled sharply, fingers still twitching toward the door.

“I’m sorry,” he said. “But you don’t know what you’re dealing with.”

Outside, the wind picked up. A distant siren wailed.
