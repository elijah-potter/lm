Golden dawn spills across mist-laced fields, painting the earth in amber and crimson.
Sky’s veins glow with molten silver, streaked by auroras—soft violets and electric blues bleeding through twilight’s cracks.
Shadows stretch like ink, but light bleeds through cracks, refracting into prismatic dust motes suspended in air.
At dusk, the lake mirrors the horizon, its surface rippling with liquid fire—flickering like dying stars.
Forest edges pulse with bioluminescent whispers: moss glows pale green, fungi pulse a faint phosphorescent glow, and fireflies ignite like scattered constellations.
Midnight whispers: city lights bleed through the veil, forming constellations of neon ghosts—cobalt, magenta, and ghostly blues, warping through smog into distorted constellations.
