The moon hung low, a silver coin in the black velvet sky, its reflection rippling across the calm lake. The old lighthouse stood sentinel, its beam cutting through the mist like a blade of light, carving the darkness into sharp, geometric edges. Inside, the lantern room was empty save for the flickering glow of the single candle—its wick burning low, casting long shadows that danced like specters at the edges of the room.

Outside, the wind whispered through the skeletal branches of the willow, their leaves rustling like pages from a forgotten book. A child’s laughter, sharp and bright, echoed from the village below—just for a moment—before dissolving into the quiet hum of the night.

Then, the painting.

It hung on the wall, its frame carved from dark oak, its surface smooth as glass. The artist had captured the same scene: the lake, the moon, the lighthouse beam. But this time, the water was not still—it pulsed, a living thing, swirling with colors that shifted like oil on water. The lighthouse stood at its center, its beam now a spiral, wrapping around the world as if drawing it into the light. And in the painting, the child’s laughter had become a song, a melody that played on the wind, carried by the currents of the lake.

The candle burned out.

The painting began to glow.
