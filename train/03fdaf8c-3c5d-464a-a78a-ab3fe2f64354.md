The sun rises early, but the day stretches endlessly like a stretched-out rubber band, snapping back at its own weight. By seven, the coffee is cold, the news cycle demands your attention like a hungry ghost, and your to-do list is already taller than you are. Breakfast is a hurried bowl of cereal, each spoonful a battle against the clock, the kitchen a maze of half-empty containers and abandoned cups.

Work begins with a jolt of caffeine and a prayer that this hour will stretch into eternity. Emails arrive like a relentless tide, demanding answers, corrections, and the illusion of progress. The afternoon drags—meetings where time vanishes into the ether, reports that need endless revisions, and the weight of unanswered questions pressing down like a physical force. The lunch break is a brief reprieve, but the sandwich is cold, the salad wilts, and the walk outside is just another chore.

By five, the day has morphed into something surreal—hours blurred into a single, suffocating block. The commute feels like a marathon, the traffic a slow, suffocating current. Home is just another place to collapse into exhaustion, the evening news a distraction from the quiet despair of a day that never ended.

Tomorrow promises the same, another loop of obligations, another hour to feel like a lifetime. The night is just another empty stretch, and the morning comes again, indifferent and unyielding. The world moves on, but for you, the day feels like a slow, suffocating suffocation.
