A cavern humming with low-frequency vibrations, walls etched with glowing mineral veins pulsing faintly like slow breathing. The air is thick with the scent of damp earth and something older—rust, maybe, or the scent of forgotten time. Overhead, the roof arches like a colossal, skeletal hand, supported by roots of black stone that split and twist in impossible patterns. A narrow, spiraling path descends into darkness, its edge marked by the faintest whisper of movement: the slow, deliberate steps of something not quite human. The floor is uneven—some places feel like they’ve been worn down by centuries of weight, others seem to rise up from the ground like the first stirrings of something buried. At the center, a circle of stones lies unbroken, though they shimmer faintly, as if reflecting a memory that hasn’t quite solidified. The threshold here isn’t just between light and dark; it’s between the world as we know it and the one that’s waiting to be crossed.
