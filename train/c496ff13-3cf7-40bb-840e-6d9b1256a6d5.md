## Project Chimera: Rulebook

**A Cooperative Game for 2-4 Players**

**Designer’s Note:** *Project Chimera* is designed to explore the complexities of crisis management and the difficult choices inherent in scientific advancement. The game aims to foster collaborative problem-solving, strategic planning, and an appreciation for the ethical considerations that arise when facing unforeseen global challenges. Players will grapple with limited resources, incomplete information, and the potential for unintended consequences, mirroring the real-world dilemmas faced by researchers and policymakers. The game is intended to be challenging and thought-provoking, encouraging discussion and reflection even after the final card is played.

### 1. Components

*   **Crisis Board:** A central board depicting a global map with designated research locations and escalating threat levels.
*   **Researcher Cards (12):** Each card represents a unique researcher with specialized skills and starting resources.
*   **Event Cards (60):** Cards detailing escalating crises, unexpected discoveries, and global reactions. Divided into three tiers of increasing severity.
*   **Discovery Tokens (40):** Represent breakthroughs in research, categorized by scientific discipline (Biology, Chemistry, Engineering, Data Science).
*   **Containment Tokens (20):** Used to mitigate the spread of the Chimera outbreak.
*   **Resource Tokens (80):** Represent Funding, Personnel, and Equipment.
*   **Chimera Markers (30):** Represent the escalating global spread of the synthetic organism.
*   **Threat Level Tracker:** Indicates the overall severity of the crisis.
*   **First Player Marker.**
*   **Rulebook (this document).**

### 2. Setup

1.  Place the Crisis Board in the center of the playing area.
2.  Place the Threat Level Tracker at its starting position (Level 2).
3.  Shuffle the Event Card deck and place it face down near the Crisis Board.
4.  Place all Resource Tokens, Discovery Tokens, and Containment Tokens in accessible piles.
5.  Each player chooses a Researcher Card and takes the starting resources indicated on that card.
6.  Place three Chimera Markers on the starting locations indicated on the Crisis Board.
7.  Determine the first player randomly and give them the First Player Marker.

### 3. Turn Structure

Each player’s turn consists of the following phases, completed in order:

1.  **Event Phase:** Draw the top Event Card and resolve its effects. This may involve placing Chimera Markers, increasing the Threat Level, or introducing new challenges.
2.  **Action Phase:** You have three Action Points to spend. You may perform any combination of the following actions, spending the indicated Action Points:
    *   **Move (1 Action Point):** Move your Researcher to an adjacent location on the Crisis Board.
    *   **Research (2 Action Points):** Attempt to acquire a Discovery Token. Draw two Discovery Tokens at random. You must discard one. The discarded token may be of any type.
    *   **Contain (2 Action Points):** Remove one Chimera Marker from your current location.
    *   **Gather Resources (1 Action Point):** Gain one Resource Token of your choice.
    *   **Share Resources (1 Action Point):** Give one Resource Token to another player at your current location.
3.  **Discard Phase:** If you have more than seven Resource Tokens, discard down to seven.
4.  **Pass the Marker:** Pass the First Player Marker to the player on your left.

### 4. Mechanics – Forced Trade-offs Under Uncertainty

1.  **Discovery Draw:** When Researching, the random draw of Discovery Tokens introduces uncertainty. You must discard one, potentially losing a crucial breakthrough. This forces players to prioritize which disciplines are most vital, based on incomplete information about future Event Cards.
2.  **Resource Scarcity:** The limited number of Resource Tokens necessitates careful allocation. Players must decide whether to invest in Research, Containment, or movement, knowing that neglecting any area can have severe consequences. The ability to share resources mitigates this but requires coordination and trust.
3.  **Event Cascade:** Event Cards often present choices with no clear “correct” answer. Resolving one event may trigger a chain reaction, making subsequent decisions more difficult and forcing players to accept trade-offs between short-term gains and long-term stability.

### 5. Win and Loss Conditions

**Win Condition:** Players win the game if they successfully research a Cure (acquire one Discovery Token of each scientific discipline – Biology, Chemistry, Engineering, Data Science) *and* reduce the Threat Level to Level 0.

**Loss Conditions:**

*   The Threat Level reaches Level 5.
*   Chimera Markers are placed on all designated locations on the Crisis Board.
*   The Event Card deck is exhausted.

### 6. Difficulty Scaling

*   **2 Players:** Begin with the Threat Level at Level 2. Reduce the number of starting Chimera Markers to two.
*   **3 Players:** Standard setup as described in the Setup section.
*   **4 Players:** Begin with the Threat Level at Level 3. Increase the number of starting Chimera Markers to four.

### 7. Advanced Variants

*   **Ethical Dilemmas:** Introduce a separate deck of Ethical Dilemma cards. At the end of each round, draw an Ethical Dilemma card and have the players collectively decide how to resolve it. These decisions can have positive or negative consequences, impacting the game’s overall trajectory.
*   **Hidden Agendas:** Assign each player a secret objective at the beginning of the game. These objectives may conflict with the team’s primary goal, creating internal tension and strategic complexity.
*   **Specialized Events:** Create a custom deck of Event Cards tailored to a specific scientific field (e.g., virology, genetics) to simulate a more focused crisis.
