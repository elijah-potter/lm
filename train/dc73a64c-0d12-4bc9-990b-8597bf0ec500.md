The void of interstellar emptiness stretches endlessly, a vast, silent expanse where cosmic dust swirls in eerie, whispering currents. Black voids swallow light like black holes, their edges blurred by the sheer absence of matter—only the faintest, distant glows from distant galaxies punctuate the darkness, their colors muted by the distance and time. Stars are sparse, isolated points of light, their names lost in the abyss. The silence is absolute, broken only by the distant hum of a dying pulsar or the occasional crackle of radio static from a dead satellite. The horizon curves impossibly, a seamless arc of infinity that defies human perception, while the weight of the cosmos presses down on the soul, making even the most mundane things—like a single rock, a whisper, a fleeting shadow—seem infinitely significant. Time itself seems to stretch thin, bending into something that feels neither past nor future, just a vast, unending now.
