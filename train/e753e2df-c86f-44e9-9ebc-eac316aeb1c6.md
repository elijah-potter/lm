In the quiet of dawn, the first light spilled through the cracks of the world like slow honey, painting the edges of the earth with gold. The air smelled of damp earth and something metallic—rusted iron and the faint, sweet tang of wildflowers. The trees stood like sentinels, their branches whispering secrets against the rustling wind, each leaf a whisper in a chorus of forgotten things.

A child, no older than seven, knelt in the grass, her fingers tracing the veins in a dandelion’s stem. The world was hers today—unburdened, untouched by the weight of tomorrow. She didn’t know it yet, but the sky was a canvas waiting to be filled, and the stars were already dreaming of her name.

Then came the storm.

It didn’t come as a roar but as a sigh, a slow unraveling of the sky itself. Thunder cracked like distant gunfire, and the rain came down in sheets, turning the world to a mirror. Lightning split the heavens into jagged, electric shards, illuminating the chaos beneath—a river of silver cutting through the dark. The child’s eyes widened, and for a moment, she was lost in the storm’s embrace.

After the storm, the sun rose again, but it was different. The sky was a bruised canvas of deep purples and sickly greens, streaked with veils of cloud that clung like forgotten sorrow. The horizon was a line of ash and smoke, where the earth’s breath still trembled. Above, the clouds pulsed like a slow, uneven heartbeat, their edges shimmering with the last remnants of storm’s fury. The sun itself seemed to waver, a fragile orb caught between day and night, its light filtering through the remnants of the tempest like a ghost.

Dawn had ended. The world slept in the hush of its own aftermath, and the sky remained a silent witness, its canvas still wet with the tears of the storm.
