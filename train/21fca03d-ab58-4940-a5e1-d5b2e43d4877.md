**BREAKING:** Mayor’s Office Announces Emergency Water Restrictions in Rivermere as Reservoir Levels Plummet to Critical 23%

Rivermere, October 12 – In response to a sudden and alarming decline in Lake Rivertown’s water levels—now at just **23% capacity**—Mayor Elias Voss has declared a **Stage 2 Water Emergency**, effective immediately. Residents are advised to reduce usage by **30%** starting October 15, with stricter limits in place for industries and agriculture.

City officials blame a **week-long heatwave** and **unauthorized drilling** near the reservoir’s intake pipes, though local environmental groups claim the dam’s aging infrastructure has been underfunded for years. Authorities have already suspended **non-essential watering** of public parks and mandatory **indoor showering** for city employees.

The Rivertown Water Board has activated emergency rations, allocating **5 gallons per household per day** (up from 7 previously). Schools are implementing **mandatory outdoor learning** to conserve indoor water use. A **24-hour toll-free hotline (888-8RIVERME)** has been activated for reports of leaks or violations.

*"This is not just a crisis—it’s a warning,"* said Environmental Commissioner Dr. Priya Kapoor. *"The next few weeks will determine whether Rivermere survives this drought or becomes another cautionary tale."*

Protests have already erupted in downtown, with groups calling for the resignation of the Water Board chair, **Senator Daniel Mercer**, over alleged conflicts of interest in the last dam upgrade. Mayor Voss has urged patience, but warns: *"If the rains don’t return by November 1, we’ll be forced to implement a **Stage 3 cutoff**—meaning no water at all."*
