The clock tower’s pendulum swings erratically, drifting between ticks and ticks again. Shadows stretch diagonally across the cobblestones, as if the sun moves in reverse. A child’s laughter echoes from the alley—too sharp, too long—while the streetlamp flickers between day and night, casting eerie blue and yellow glows.

At the café, the coffee brews itself, pausing mid-brew, then resuming, though the beans are fresh. The waitress’s hands move through a dish of soup as if frozen mid-gesture, then snap back. The diner’s conversation stutters, phrases repeating in loops: *"I should’ve left earlier."* The walls hum a melody that repeats in perfect, identical loops for hours.

The train tracks bend backward, leading to a station that doesn’t exist on the map. A man in a trench coat walks toward you, his boots clicking *forward* in reverse. The air smells like wet stone and old tea, but the tea cup is always empty, yet the handle is warm.

The last thing you remember is leaving—now you’re standing at the same door, watching your reflection age in real time, but only *partially*. The mirror’s glass is cracked, and the fragments whisper in voices from other timelines.
