The firelight flickered against the cracked porcelain of the old kitchen table, casting long shadows over the half-eaten pie. Her fingers traced the rim of her teacup, the warmth of the liquid seeping into her veins as she remembered the night it all began.

She had been sixteen, standing in the dim glow of the streetlamp outside the diner, the neon sign casting a sickly yellow hue over the cobblestones. A boy—no, not a boy, a man—had walked toward her, his boots kicking up gravel. He held a half-smoked cigarette between his fingers, the ember glowing like a dying star.

"You promised," he whispered, his voice rough with something unreadable. "I just wanted to remind you."

The words hung in the air between them, heavy with unspoken meaning. She had forgotten, of course. How could she remember a promise from a boy who had vanished the next day? But the memory clung to her like a second skin, sharp and painful.

Now, years later, she sipped her tea, the memory surfacing like an old wound. The taste of the pie—sweet, slightly burnt—reminded her of the first time she had eaten it with him. The way he had laughed when she had spilled sugar on his shirt. The way he had held her hand when the world felt too big to bear.

The table creaked under her weight. Outside, the wind howled through the empty street, carrying away the remnants of her youth. But inside, the promise remained, a quiet flame that refused to die.
