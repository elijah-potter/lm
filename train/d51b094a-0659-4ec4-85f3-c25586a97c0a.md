**Part 1:**
The old lighthouse keeper, Elias, had spent his life watching the storm-lashed waves. One evening, as a ship’s distress beacon flickered across the horizon, he hesitated before pressing the bell. The wind howled through the lantern room, and the beam cut through the fog like a blade. The ship’s captain, weary and cold, knelt at the edge of the plank, clutching a child’s doll.

**Part 2 (First Ending):**
Elias pressed the bell. The beam caught the ship, guiding it into port. When the captain returned, he handed Elias the doll—a tiny, weathered porcelain figure of a sailor. "For the night the stars were kind," the captain whispered. The next morning, Elias found the ship’s log entry: *"Saved by the light of a stranger’s kindness."* The lighthouse burned on, but the keeper’s eyes were always closed.

**Part 2 (Second Ending):**
Elias did not press the bell. The beam turned black. The ship’s crew rowed for hours, their lanterns extinguished. When they reached shore, the captain buried the doll at the base of the lighthouse. Years later, a storm tore the beam free—it landed in the captain’s garden, still glowing faintly. The child’s doll sat there, its eyes filled with saltwater. The keeper’s final words were whispered: *"Some things don’t need saving."*
