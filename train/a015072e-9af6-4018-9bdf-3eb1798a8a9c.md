AITA for refusing to let my sister use my inherited vacation home?

Okay, this is a bit complicated, and I'm honestly stressed about it. It involves my sister, Eleanor (42), my parents, and a property that’s been a source of family tension for years.

My parents, Richard and Margaret, passed away in July 2022. They owned a small, charming cottage in Kennebunkport, Maine. They purchased it in 1998, intending it as a retirement property, but they always prioritized family visits over personal use. They generously allowed my sister and I, along with our respective families, to use it for week-long vacations each summer. I’m 45, and my husband, David, and I have two children, ages 12 and 15. Eleanor has one daughter, age 18, who is now away at university.

The will, drafted in 2020, stipulated that the cottage was to be split equally between Eleanor and me. It also stated that we were to discuss how to manage it moving forward. I assumed we’d sell it, split the proceeds, and be done with it. Eleanor, however, had a different idea.

On August 10, 2022, just weeks after the funeral, she called me, suggesting we keep the cottage and rent it out. I was hesitant; managing a rental property felt like a significant responsibility. But she argued that it would generate income and preserve a cherished family memory. I agreed, albeit reluctantly, figuring we could hire a property management company to handle the day-to-day operations.

For the past two years, things have been… fraught. Eleanor has consistently attempted to dictate how the rental income is distributed, despite the will’s ambiguity. She wants a significantly larger portion, claiming she’s "more emotionally attached" to the cottage. I've repeatedly pointed out that we both inherited it equally and that her emotional attachment shouldn’t translate to financial advantage. 

This year, things escalated. On May 2, 2024, she emailed me requesting to use the cottage for two weeks in July – specifically, July 12-26 – for her and her daughter’s vacation. I was initially open to it. We typically block out a week each year for our own family’s use, and I thought letting Eleanor and her daughter have the remaining time would be a nice gesture. However, she then added that she expected to use it *rent-free*, citing the “emotional burden” of managing the property.

I responded on May 2, explaining that we both need to contribute to the upkeep and rental income, and that free usage is simply not feasible. This prompted a lengthy phone call on May 10, during which she accused me of being “greedy” and “insensitive” to our family’s history. She brought up past instances where I’d declined her requests for financial assistance, implying I was deliberately holding back to spite her. I countered that those were separate issues, and that her expectation of free accommodation was unreasonable.

David got involved, reminding her that we both agreed to share the responsibilities and financial obligations. She dismissed him, saying he was "taking her sister's side" and that I was clearly prioritizing money over family. My mother’s best friend, Patricia, who is also Eleanor's godmother, called me yesterday, May 15, to express her disappointment. Patricia believes Eleanor is grieving and that I should be more accommodating. She implied I was being unnecessarily harsh. 

I’ve explained my position to both of them, but they seem to view my stance as a personal attack on Eleanor’s grief. I feel like I’m constantly being pressured to concede to her demands, even when they’s financially unsustainable. I'm exhausted from the constant conflict. I’ve even considered selling my share of the cottage just to be rid of the stress, but I know that would likely upset my father’s memory. 

AITA for refusing to let my sister use the cottage rent-free?
