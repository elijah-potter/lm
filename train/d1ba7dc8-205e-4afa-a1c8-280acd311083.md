A dim lamp flickered against the cracked wallpaper in the cramped apartment, casting long shadows. The door creaked open with a slow exhale of cold air. **Javier** stood in the doorway, his hands shoved deep into his pockets, fingers tracing the edges of his worn leather jacket. The silence was thick enough to cut—he could almost taste it, like dust settling into the air.

He turned, his gaze drifting over the room. The couch, half-collapsed under a heap of forgotten books, the coffee table stained with years of spilled beverages. A single photograph of his mother, framed but cracked, sat on the kitchen counter. She was always smiling, her hands gentle on his shoulder when he was a child. He didn’t remember much of those days, but the memory of her voice—soft, warm—lingered like a ghost.

His phone buzzed. A text from his brother: *"You gonna be late?"* The message was simple, but the question hung in the air like a challenge. Javier exhaled through his nose, watching the smoke curl into the dim light.

He walked to the window, fingers pressing against the glass. Outside, the city pulsed—laughter, sirens, the distant hum of traffic. But here, in this room, there was only the weight of silence. He closed his eyes. Let the world outside fade. Let the noise of it all dissolve into the quiet.

Then, slowly, he turned back to the photograph. The photo stayed. The silence, though, was his choice.
