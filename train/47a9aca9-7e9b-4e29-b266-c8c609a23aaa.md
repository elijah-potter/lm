I was just passing by the old warehouse district when the lights flickered out. No sirens, no chaos—just the hum of the city dying down. A flicker of movement caught my eye. The door creaked open, and in stumbled a figure wrapped in black, their breath fogging the air like mist. They stumbled past me, their boots thudding against the cracked pavement. No one else was around. The streetlights had gone dark, but the glow of the distant neon signs cast long shadows.

I stood there, frozen, as the figure moved toward the alley. Then I heard it—a wet, rasping sound. A car engine roared to life. The figure turned, their face half-lit by the car’s headlights. I didn’t see their eyes, but the way they moved, like something wasn’t quite *them* anymore. The car swerved, tires screeching. The figure vanished into the shadows.

I turned away before I could think. The alley was empty. The lights were back on. The city kept moving. But somewhere, in the dark, I swear I heard a laugh.
