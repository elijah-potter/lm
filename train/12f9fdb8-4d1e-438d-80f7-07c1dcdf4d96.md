**The old wooden desk**—its grain cracked with age, the half-empty coffee cup still warm—was the only place where her father’s voice lingered like a ghost. The **promise** she’d made him, whispered in the dead of night when the world outside was a blur of rain and neon: *"I’ll be back by dawn."* The clock on the wall ticked like a countdown, but time had warped—each second stretched into a lifetime.

She traced the scar on her palm where the blade had grazed her skin, the one she’d given him for safekeeping. The **promise** was etched there too, unspoken but unbroken: *"I won’t let you down."* Outside, the city slept, but the **promise** still hummed in the walls, a current of silent resolve.

She picked up the letter she’d written that night—her own words, her own blood—folded and sealed in wax. The **promise** wasn’t in ink, but in the way her fingers trembled as she pressed it to her lips. *"I’m sorry,"* she’d written. *"I’m coming."* The last line was unread, the paper damp with her tears.

The **promise** wasn’t a thing she’d said, but the thing she’d become. And now, every morning, she woke to it.
