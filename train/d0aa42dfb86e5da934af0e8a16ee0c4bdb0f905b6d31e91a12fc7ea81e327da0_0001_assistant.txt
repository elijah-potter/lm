The demon that infiltrated our house on the night of November 17, 1915, unleashed such a cacophony of chaos and malevolence that I regret not leaving it undisturbed and retiring to bed. Its arrival instigated a series of misunderstandings that led my mother to hurl a shoe through a neighboring house's window and culminated in my grandfather firing a fatal shot at a patrolman. I ruefully admit that my attention to the haunting footsteps proved to be a grave mistake.

The eerie echoes began around 1:15 a.m., a rapid and rhythmic cadence encircling the dining room table. Upstairs, my mother slumbered in one room, my brother Herman in another, while my grandfather occupied the attic, resting on the same old walnut bed that had once tragically claimed my father's life. Just as I had stepped out of the bathtub and begun toweling off, the footsteps invaded my senses. They were unmistakably the footsteps of a man, briskly traversing the dining room downstairs. The dim light from the bathroom cast its glow down the back staircase, directly into the dining room. I could faintly discern the shine of plates on the plate rail, but the table remained hidden from my view. The footsteps persisted, circling the table in a ceaseless loop, occasionally punctuated by the creaking of a trodden board. Initially, I assumed it was my father or brother Roy, who was expected home from Indianapolis at any moment. My next conjecture veered toward a possible burglar. But it never occurred to me until later that it was an insidious demon haunting our abode.

After the spectral pacing persisted for about three minutes, I tiptoed to Herman's room, urgently whispering his name in the darkness and shaking him awake. In a somber, resigned tone reminiscent of a despondent beagle, he murmured, "Awp." I revealed my identity and whispered, "There's something downstairs!" Reluctantly, he rose from his bed and followed me to the top of the back staircase. Together, we listened intently, but there was no sound. The steps had ceased. Herman regarded me with growing trepidation, only to find me clad in a bath towel wrapped around my waist. He yearned to return to the safety of his bed, but I clutched his arm firmly, insisting, "There's something down there!"

Suddenly, the footsteps recommenced, racing around the dining-room table as though pursued by an unseen force, then ascending the stairs towards us with an ominous weight, two steps at a time. The feeble light continued to cast a pallid glow on the staircase, yet no figure materialized before our eyes—only the haunting sound of the steps reverberated through the air. In a panic, Herman retreated to his room and slammed the door shut. I hastily sealed the door at the top of the stairs, bracing my knee against it. After an agonizingly long minute, I cautiously reopened it, expecting a horrifying apparition. But to our relief, there was nothing there. Silence enveloped the house. None of us ever encountered the demon again, but the lingering memory of that night's terrors would forever haunt our souls.
