The office clock chimed again, marking another monotonous shift. She adjusted the strap of her bag, fingers lingering on the worn leather as she scanned the crowd. There were always *them*—the ones who sat at the exact same desk every Tuesday at 3:17 PM, their coffee mugs identical, the crumbs from yesterday’s sandwiches still on the table.

She leaned forward, elbows on her knees, and traced a finger along the edge of the window. The pattern wasn’t subtle. The first time, it was just a coincidence—one of the men, the one in the red tie, was always the last to leave. But then came the second time. The woman in the fluorescent shirt, the one who always ordered the same breakfast burrito at 6:47 AM, now worked the night shift. The third time, it wasn’t just them—it was *everyone*. The same faces, the same routines, the same unspoken rules carved into the rhythm of the building like a city’s veins.

She exhaled, watching the pattern emerge from the noise. The numbers on the wall—someone had marked them with a Sharpie, a jagged line running down the center. Thirty-six. That was the number of days she’d been here before she noticed. Before the world slowed, before she could see the threads connecting the chaos. The pattern wasn’t in the people. It was in the way they moved. The way they repeated. The way they *expected* something to be different tomorrow.
