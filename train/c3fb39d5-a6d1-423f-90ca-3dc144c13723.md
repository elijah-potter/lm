Here’s a short piece incorporating a list and a confession:

---

The morning paper lay open across the kitchen table, its headlines stark: *"New Yorkers Struggle with Rising Costs."* I stared at the list in my hand—*groceries, rent, the gym membership I never used*—as if reading it aloud could make it disappear. The truth, though, was simpler: I had spent the night in my old apartment, watching reruns of *Friends* until 3 AM, and then, unable to sleep, I’d written this list in my journal. It wasn’t just a budget breakdown. It was a confession.

I had stopped going to the gym because I didn’t *want* to lose the way my body felt after a full night’s sleep, after a bottle of wine and too much Netflix. I had stopped running because I was tired of the way my knees ached when I bent over to pick up my phone, tired of the way my reflection in the mirror looked like a ghost of someone else. But I kept the gym membership anyway, like a debt I couldn’t afford to call in. And rent? I couldn’t afford to leave either.

I had spent years pretending I had it all together. The perfect job, the perfect house, the perfect life—until one evening, when I came home and found the apartment empty. My things were gone. The list in my hands was the only thing left. The confession was written in the margins of a book I hadn’t touched in months.

I didn’t know what to do with it.
