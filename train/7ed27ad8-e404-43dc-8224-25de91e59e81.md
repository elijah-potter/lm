The crowd at the café was a blur of muted tones—earthy browns of coffee-stained tables, the soft blue of the stained-glass window, the constant hum of chatter beneath the low murmur of rain. I sat alone, fingers tracing the rim of my empty cup, watching as someone else—someone who *noticed*—leaned in, adjusting their sleeve with deliberate care. The fabric had frayed at the hem, a small but deliberate detail, like a signature. They were aware of the way the light caught the edge of their cuff, the way the sleeve bunched just so when they stood. They didn’t miss the way the café’s fluorescent hum made their glasses sparkle like cheap jewelry.

I, however, was lost in the quiet rhythm of the city: the way the wind outside carried the scent of damp pavement and old books from the bookstore down the street, the way the café’s air smelled like stale sugar and something older, something like nostalgia. I saw the way the woman at the next table’s coffee cup had a single tear, dried now, but still there, a faint smudge on the rim where she’d wiped it away. She didn’t notice it was there, but I saw the way her fingers trembled when she picked up her latte. A stranger’s hesitation, a fleeting thing, but I held it like a secret.

The bell above the door chimed, and a new customer—someone who *knew* how to exist in the world—slipped in. They took the seat across from me without a glance, as if they’d been born here. They knew the weight of the afternoon, the way the café’s air had a temperature of its own, thick with the quiet despair of those who’d been waiting too long. They didn’t need to look; they *felt* it.

I, on the other hand, was still watching the world through the cracks in my own perception. The way the streetlight flickered, casting long shadows that stretched like ghosts across the pavement. The way the woman’s hands moved when she poured her coffee—too precise, too deliberate, like she was performing a ritual. She didn’t need to; she was already doing it.

Outside, a car backfired, the sound sharp and sudden. The crowd around me didn’t flinch. They didn’t notice the way the sound had cut through the city like a blade. But I did. I felt the way the world had shifted, just for a second, into something raw and unfiltered. And I wondered: what did they see? What did they *really* see?
