The clock tower’s pendulum swung past midnight when the last child in the village vanished into the mist. No one saw them leave, but the next morning, the village elder found a single, unbroken acorn in his cup—a seed with no roots, yet bearing the faintest whisper of sapling growth.

The farmer’s oxen pulled the plow into the field where the old oak had stood, and where the roots had been, the earth now pulsed with new green. The baker’s oven, cracked from years of neglect, began to bake bread on its own, the scent drifting through the alleyways like a promise.

One winter’s eve, the children returned—not as ghosts, but as a single, unbroken line of light, tracing the shape of the village’s future. The first one stepped into the snow, and the others followed, forming the shape of a child’s hand, fingers curled into the future. The acorn had grown into a tree, its branches now sheltering the new ones. The village elder knelt, pressing his palm to the bark, and whispered:

*"The ending is a beginning."*
