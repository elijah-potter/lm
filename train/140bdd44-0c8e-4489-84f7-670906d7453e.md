A lone figure, their coat slightly askew, steps past the threshold of the dimly lit café where they’d lingered for hours—poured over the same half-eaten pastry, sipped the same stale coffee, let the hum of the city seep through the cracked windows. Their backpack slung low, a single book tucked under one arm, they didn’t turn back. The door creaked shut behind them, the sound swallowed by the rush-hour traffic. The café’s lone customer, a stranger who’d been watching from the booth, watches them go, a half-smile unguarded on their lips. The city exhales, indifferent.
