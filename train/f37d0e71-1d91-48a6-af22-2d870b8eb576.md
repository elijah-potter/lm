The skeletal structure of a vast, abandoned orbital station hummed with residual energy, its segmented walls shimmering with faint, eerie static. Panels hung in jagged disarray, some fused shut by decades of neglect, others cracked like glass in a high-gravity field. The air—thin, stale, and charged with the scent of ozone and rust—circulated lazily through the open ventilation shafts, revealing nothing but endless void. The central hub, a towering circular node, dominated the scene, its hollow core a chasm of darkness where only the ghostly glow of a single, failing laser pointer pulsed weakly against the black. The floor, a fractured metal grid, stretched endlessly outward in every direction, its uneven surface etched with the imprints of countless footsteps, some fresh, others worn smooth by time. The silence was deafening, broken only by the occasional metallic groan of a support beam under its own weight, as if the structure itself was holding its breath, waiting for something—or nothing—to return.
