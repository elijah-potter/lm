The daily ritual of checking social media began as a way to stay connected, but it soon spiraled into compulsive behavior, consuming 4+ hours daily. Initially, it provided fleeting dopamine hits from notifications, but over time, the habit devolved into mindless scrolling, ignoring work deadlines and family time. The anxiety of missing updates or notifications intensified, leading to insomnia and irritability when offline. Cognitive dissonance set in as the habit undermined self-respect for prioritizing productivity over dopamine. Eventually, it became a psychological dependency—withdrawal caused intense cravings, and attempts to quit failed due to emotional attachment. The cycle trapped the user in a paradox: the habit was both a crutch and a curse, eroding focus, relationships, and well-being.
