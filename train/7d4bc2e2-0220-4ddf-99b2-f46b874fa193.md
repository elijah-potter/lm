The last words echo softly through the dim office, each line a ghostly whisper: *"You said—"*

Lena’s fingers pause over the keyboard, the cursor blinking like a dying ember. The screen’s glow casts long shadows across her face—eyes half-lidded, jaw clenched. *"You said something about the old house?"*

Her throat tightens. The words come in fragmented bursts, each one a half-truth, a memory half-buried under dust. *"The night the wind howled through the attic..."* Her voice cracks. *"You called it a ghost, but I told you—it was just the furnace."* The keyboard clatters as she smashes Enter, the words flooding the screen in jagged, unreadable text. *"I don’t remember what we said."*

A silence. The hum of the server blades beneath her desk, mechanical and unfeeling. Then, the sound of her phone—static, a single missed call from her mother. *"Remember?"* The voice on the other end is distant, as if it’s already fading.

Lena exhales, her breath shallow. The screen flickers to black.
