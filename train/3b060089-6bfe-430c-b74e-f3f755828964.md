The city council’s recent budget cuts to the emergency medical services (EMS) department have sparked rumors that **critical life-saving equipment—including defibrillators and advanced trauma kits—could be removed from public access stations** within the next fiscal quarter. Locals claim to have overheard a senior EMS supervisor at a private meeting discussing a **reallocation of funds** from community outreach programs to hospital-based reserves. Anonymous sources suggest that **automated external defibrillators (AEDs)** installed at schools and parks might be decommissioned if funding is diverted. The council’s spokesperson denies these claims but has not confirmed a timeline for audits of public safety assets. Some residents are organizing vigilante checks to ensure compliance.
