**The Last Light**

The clock struck midnight in the hollowed-out town square, where the gas lamps flickered like dying stars. Old Elias sat at the wooden table, his fingers tracing the edge of the half-finished sketchpad. The ink had dried in places, but the lines still held the promise of something—something he couldn’t name.

Outside, the wind howled through the skeletal trees, carrying the scent of damp earth and forgotten prayers. His breath fogged the glass, and in that moment, he swore he saw a shadow move where there shouldn’t have been one. Not a tree. Not a man. Something *between*.

He exhaled sharply, then picked up the pencil again. The image was simple: a figure standing at the edge of a river, its back to the viewer, arms outstretched as if embracing the dark. The water shimmered unnaturally, reflecting the glow of a distant, unseen lantern.

Elias closed his eyes. The paper felt warm against his skin. When he opened them again, the figure had vanished—but the sketch remained. He pressed his palm to the ink, and the lines pulsed like veins.

A knock at the door made him jump.

The door creaked open, and in stepped a woman in a tattered cloak. Her eyes were hollow, her smile too wide. "You finished it," she said, voice like rusted hinges.

Elias didn’t look up. "The lantern’s still burning."

She stepped closer, her shadow stretching unnaturally long. "Then it’s time to see it."

The lantern—an old brass thing, half-buried in the riverbank—glowed faintly in the dark. Its light stretched across the water, painting the figure in gold.

Elias stood, the sketch slipping from his fingers. The woman’s hand brushed his arm, and for a second, he swore he felt something *pull* him toward the water.

He reached for the lantern.

The river was cold. The light was warm.

And the figure turned.
