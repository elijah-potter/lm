A fleeting sunset painted the horizon in hues of molten gold and crimson, its light trembling like a breath held too long. The air smelled of damp earth and wild thyme, carrying the faintest trace of distant rain. A child’s laughter, sharp and bright, cut through the silence as they chased butterflies across the field, their tiny hands clutching a cracked glass jar.

In that moment, the world was soft, unguarded—no weight, no rush. The past was a whisper behind them, the future a shimmering mist ahead. But then, as if summoned by some unseen force, a gust of wind stole the moment entirely: the jar shattered, the laughter faded, and the sunset dissolved into the night like sugar in water.

No replay. No second chance. Only the echo of what was.
