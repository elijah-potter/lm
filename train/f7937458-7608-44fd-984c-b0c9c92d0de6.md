The air smelled like damp wool and old secrets, thick and cloying, as if the world had exhaled its last breath into the autumn wind. I stood at the edge of the field, my boots sinking into the cool earth, fingers trembling slightly as they traced the cracked pavement of the old road. The house loomed ahead, its windows like empty eyes watching us from the past. I could hear the distant murmur of voices inside, but they were muffled, indistinct—like ghosts who had forgotten how to speak.

I wanted to turn back. Not because I feared what lay ahead, but because I was afraid of what I was becoming. The doubt coiled in my stomach, a thing I’d never known until now, twisting like a snake in the dark. I had spent my life following orders, doing what others expected, but here, in this quiet moment, I realized: I didn’t even know what I wanted anymore. The weight of it pressed against my ribs, a question that had no answer, only the hollow echo of my own hesitation.

The door creaked open before I could decide, and the light spilled out like a promise. Inside, the room was warm, too warm, the kind of warmth that felt like a trap. My pulse hammered in my throat as I stepped forward, the space between us stretching like a river that had forgotten how to flow. She was waiting for me, standing near the window, her back to me, her silhouette sharp against the fading light. I could see the way her shoulders tensed, the way her breath hitched when she turned, but she didn’t say anything. Just stood there, as if she had already decided I wasn’t worth an explanation.

I reached for her hand, and she took it, her grip firm, unyielding. But her fingers were cold. Too cold. I pulled away, my pulse roaring in my ears. "What if I’m wrong?" I whispered, my voice raw. "What if this isn’t what you need?" The words hung between us, heavy as stone.

She didn’t answer. She just looked at me, really looked at me, and for the first time, I saw the truth in her eyes: she didn’t know either. And that was worse. Because in that silence, in that unspoken understanding, I realized something terrifying. We weren’t just two people making a choice. We were two people who had already chosen to be afraid.
