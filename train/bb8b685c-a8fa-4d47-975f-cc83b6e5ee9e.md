**The Last Light**

The city hummed under a sky too bright to sleep. Neon signs flickered like dying stars, casting long shadows on pavement that had seen too many footsteps. She walked past the empty café, its windows still smudged with coffee stains that hadn’t washed out in years. A newspaper lay open on the table, half-eaten by a ghost of a meal long forgotten. The headline glared at her:

*"Community Event: Free Food & Stories Tonight—All Are Welcome."*

She hesitated, fingers brushing the rim of her empty coffee cup. The reminder on her phone buzzed—a text from her mother, *"Remember: The doctor’s appointment is at 7. Don’t skip it."* Her stomach twisted. The clinic was three blocks away, but the night air felt heavier than the city’s pollution.

A street musician played jazz on a corner, his voice thin but familiar. She reached out, fingers curling into the air, and for a second, the world paused. Then she exhaled, stepped forward, and let the evening swallow her whole.
