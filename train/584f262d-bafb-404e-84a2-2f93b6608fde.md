**"The Weight of Silence"**

She found the journal tucked behind a loose floorboard, its pages yellowed with time and the ghost of her own words. The first entry was dated three years ago, a confession scrawled in trembling script:

*"I don’t know why I do this. Sometimes, I just… stop."*

The entries that followed were fragments—half-thoughts, fragments of regret, the unspoken things she’d never say aloud. She traced the words with her fingertips, as if memorizing them would erase them. But the ink had already seeped into her bones.

One night, she wrote:
*"I used to think I was brave. Now I think I was just afraid, and the courage was the thing that scared me."*

The last entry was written in a different hand, smudged and hasty:
*"I don’t know if I’m done. I just know I can’t go on pretending."*

She closed the journal and let the pages fall to the floor. The weight of it settled over her like a shroud. Some things, she realized, were too heavy to carry alone.
