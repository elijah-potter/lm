The clock struck midnight in the dimly lit office of Dr. Elias Voss, his fingers trembling as he double-checked the last line of the quantum algorithm. "Perfect," he muttered, "this time."

His assistant, Mara, adjusted her glasses. "The error margin is still too high for the critical deployment," she said, pointing to the red flag on the screen.

Elias exhaled sharply. "Then let’s fix it. Again."

He made a mistake. The wrong variable was swapped in the loop, a typo in the initialization condition. The system crashed with a single, ominous error: **"DECOHERENCE DETECTED."**

Outside, the city hummed with life—neon signs flickered, voices carried on the wind. Elias stood in the middle of the office, staring at the blank wall where the console had been.

Mara’s voice cut through the silence. "You said *this* time."

A cold wind howled through the alley behind them, carrying the scent of rain and something older—something like the first whisper of the storm before the thunder. Elias turned, his breath fogging in the chill air.

He had one chance. One chance to fix it before the algorithm became the world’s first sentient error.

And he didn’t even know if he’d done it right.
