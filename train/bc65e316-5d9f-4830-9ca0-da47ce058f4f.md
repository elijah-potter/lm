After the storm’s relentless drumbeat fades, the air hums with the faint, rhythmic pulse of cicadas—only a few, distant, like the slow breath of a dying storm. The silence isn’t empty; it’s thick with the weight of what was and the quiet tension of what lingers.

The leaves on the oak sway lazily, each one a whisper of the wind, no longer fighting against the sky. The pavement glistens, a mirror of forgotten rain, reflecting the muted light of a setting sun that has turned the world into something softer—amber and golden, like a half-remembered dream.

A child’s laughter, once sharp and bright, now dissolves into the background, swallowed by the quiet. The only sound is the occasional rustle of a twig underfoot, the distant call of a rooster breaking the eerie calm. The world feels smaller, more fragile, like a breath held just long enough to realize how fragile it is.

Even the shadows stretch longer, casting long, shifting lines across the ground, as if the absence of noise has given them new shape. The quiet isn’t absolute—it’s the space between the cracks, the pause before the next sound, the moment when the world exhales.
