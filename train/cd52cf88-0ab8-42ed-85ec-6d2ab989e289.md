The city glows with neon veins—pulsing crimson, cobalt, and electric blue veins threading through the skeletal streets, their flickers casting prismatic shadows on the damp pavement. Streetlamps hum like distant drones, their amber glow bleeding into the mist that curls like smoke from alleys, obscuring figures moving in the dim.

Sunrise over the harbor paints the water in molten gold, reflecting like liquid fire. The docks, lined with rusted cranes, are bathed in a sickly greenish hue, the mist rising from the water’s surface like a slow, breathing thing. A single lighthouse stands at the edge, its beam slicing through the fog in a jagged, white serpentine, carving through the dusk like a blade through ink.

Midday sunlight cuts through the canyons of glass and steel, turning the skyline into a fractured mosaic—windows like scattered mirrors, their reflections shifting with the wind, trapping fleeting light in trapped, shifting pools. Shadows dance in long, thin streaks across the pavement, stretching like living things, shifting with every breath of air.

Dusk turns the forest into a labyrinth of golden threads—oak leaves catching the fading light in amber shards, the trees standing like sentinels wrapped in fire. The forest floor glows faintly, the damp earth absorbing and re-emitting light like a slow, humming glow.

Moonlight over the desert is a slow, creeping thing—soft silver veins tracing through the dunes, casting everything in a ghostly pallor. The stars above are scattered like scattered diamonds, their light barely reaching the parched earth below, where the sand sifts into the shadows like dust from a dying world.

At night, the city’s underbelly flickers with the eerie glow of flickering street signs, their letters dissolving into static, casting jagged, flickering light across the pavement. The air hums with the faint glow of distant lights—neon signs, flickering like dying embers in the wind. The night is alive with light, both soft and harsh, painting everything in hues of the unseen.
