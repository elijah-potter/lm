Dear Sisters,

I hope this letter finds you well. There's an urgent matter concerning our sisters in Iran. The Iranian authorities have recently announced their plan to install cameras in public spaces intending to identify unveiled women and arrest them. As you know, the hijab law in Iran requires women to cover their hair, and failure to comply can lead to arrest.

This decision comes after the tragic killing of Mahsa Amini by the morality police last year, which sparked widespread protests against the mandatory dress code. Many brave women in Iran have started to stop covering their hair, standing up for their right to choose how they dress.

According to a statement released by the state-aligned Tasnim News Agency, the Iranian police claim that using smart cameras in public places is an innovative measure to prevent tension and conflicts in enforcing the hijab law. Once an unveiled woman is identified, she will receive warning text messages about the consequences of violating the law.

The statement goes on to further emphasize the preservation of values, protection of family privacy, and the mental health and peace of mind of the community. It states that any behavior, individual or collective, that goes against the law will not be tolerated.

This news is deeply concerning and highlights the challenges faced by our sisters in Iran who are fighting for their rights and freedom of expression. We need to stand in solidarity with them, raise awareness about their struggles, and advocate for their rights to be respected.

Let us support our Iranian sisters in their pursuit of equality and justice. We must continue to push for change and work towards a world where every woman can exercise her right to self-expression without fear of persecution.


With love and solidarity,

Your sister
