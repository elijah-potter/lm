The clock struck midnight, but the silence was deafening. The promise hung between them like a half-burned candle—half-hearted, half-finished, the kind of thing you say to make someone feel better, to ease the weight of the unsaid. *"I’ll figure it out."* It was the kind of promise that started with a flicker of hope, a spark in the dark, but quickly dimmed into something duller, like a candle guttering out before the wax had a chance to melt.

It wasn’t about the *how*. It was about the *will*. The how was always just beyond reach—too far, too late, too tangled in the mess of what had already happened. The will, though? That was the thing that kept slipping away, like sand through fingers, or a door left ajar in a storm. One minute, you were standing there, shoulders squared, teeth gritted, ready to turn the impossible into something—maybe not possible, but at least *possible-ish*. The next, the ground had shifted, and the ground was no longer there at all.

They said promises were just words, but words had teeth. Or at least, they did when you believed them. This one was a hollow echo, a ghost of a vow that had outlived its purpose. The moment it was made, the weight of it had settled into your bones, a cold, unyielding thing you couldn’t shake. You tried. You *tried*. You told yourself you were stronger than this, that you could do more than you could ever say. But the truth was simpler: you couldn’t keep a promise that felt like a lie in the telling. And the lie, in the end, was the only thing that stayed true.
