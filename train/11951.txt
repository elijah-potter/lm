What is flatulence?
Flatulence (gas problem) is also commonly known as farting. Flatulence is basically the passing of the intestinal gas through the anus.
Where does the gas come into the body?
- You swallowed excessive air.
- It is produced by the bacteria resident inside the intestine.
What are the signs and symptoms?
- Abdominal bloating
What are the causes?
Main cause for flatulence or gas problem is:
- Creation of hydrogen/methane gas by the bacteria present in the intestine. These gases are generated when food digestion of sugars, starch and cellulose is taking place.
- Lactose intolerance: If you have lactose intolerance, sugars like lactose, sorbitol and fructose are not digested properly.
- Indigestion of starches: Some starches especially from cabbage, wheat, potato and oats are not digested properly leading to intestinal gas.
When to seek medical advice
You should seek medical advice if you are constantly passing gas (more than 20 times a day).
What are the methods for screening and diagnosis?
Following diagnosis can be performed by the doctor:
- The doctor will ask for a medical history.
- A diet history will also be asked to eliminate the cause of flatulence due to intolerance to certain foods.
- An abdominal ultrasound.
- An X-ray will confirm the presence of excess air in the stomach and the intestine.
- CT scan and MRI scan
- Hydrogen/Methane breath tests: Test the bacterial overgrowth in the small intestine.
- Maldigestion and malabsorption tests: To test if any particular food is not getting digested properly.
- Gastric emptying studies: To study the ability of the stomach to empty its contents.
What is the treatment?
- If flatulence is caused due to maldigestion of some particular fruit and vegetable, you will be advised to stop consuming them.
- In case of lactose intolerance, enzymes similar to those present in the small intestine are added to the milk. This will break down the lactose so it can be digested properly.
- In case of malabsorption of specific sugars like fructose etc, your food should not contain them.
- Bacterial overgrowth in the small intestine can be controlled by antibiotics.
- If a physical obstruction is present in the intestine, surgery is required.
How to prevent flatulence?
Several home remedies can be tried initially to reduce flatulence:
- Drink lots of water but slowly. Do not gulp it down.
- Instead of 3 large meals, eat 4-5 small meals in a day.
- Eat a high fibre diet.
- Avoid chewing gum.
- Exercise regularly.
- Avoid carbonated drinks.
- Avoid smoking.
Even after trying out the home remedies, you get no relief from flatulence contact your doctor immediately.
