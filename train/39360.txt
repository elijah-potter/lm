The Sabbath mode on modern ranges is a feature designed to allow observant Jews to use the range and oven, with certain restrictions, on the Sabbath and Jewish holidays, such as Yom Tov, in accordance with Jewish law.
Cooking on Sabbath and Yom Tov
Jewish law forbids cooking on the Sabbath, although cooked food may be kept warm. Cooking is allowed on Yom Tov, although the heat used must be "transferred" from another source rather than initiated. For example, a match may be lit from a burning candle to light another candle, but a match may not be struck. These laws have implications for the functioning of modern ranges and ovens.
Orthodox Jews observe two consecutive days of Yom Tov at several points in the Jewish calendar. If the days fall immediately before or after the Sabbath, that creates a three-day period when a range or oven may not be turned on. Most modern ranges and ovens have an automatic shutoff feature that initiates after 12 hours, but the Sabbath mode allows the oven to keep food warm for the entire Yom Tov.
On Yom Tov, but not on the Sabbath, temperatures may be raised and lowered to cook the food properly but only from an existing flame. For gas ranges, this means that the burners must be lit from a type of pilot light rather than an electric spark. Ranges with a Sabbath mode feature allow the user to do this and also to raise or lower the oven temperature without affecting the digital panel readout.
Automatic Lights and Digital Readouts
Sabbath-mode ranges and ovens allow the user to defeat many automatic functions, such as digital readouts of temperature displays or automatic oven lights. Ironically, the Sabbath mode functions as a kind of reverse technology, making modern ranges and ovens less "smart" than they could be and facilitating their use on the Jewish Sabbath and Yom Tov.
