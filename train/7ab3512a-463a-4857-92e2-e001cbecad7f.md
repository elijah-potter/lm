The journey began at dawn in an open plain where the horizon blurred into mist. The first destination: a crumbling stone tower near the old riverbank, marked by a single rusted key on a weathered post.

After three days, the key led to a hidden cave where the walls whispered of lost cities. The new destination: a black-market bazaar beneath the city’s ruins, selling artifacts with names like *"Memory of the First Dawn"* and *"Echo of the Storm Before Time."

The bazaar owner, a woman with eyes like polished obsidian, traded the key for a vial of liquid silver. The next stop: a floating library suspended between clouds, its pages rearranging themselves with every visit. The book’s title changed daily—today: *"The Shape of Infinite Steps."*

A storm rolled in, and the library vanished. The only clue: a trail of blood-red snow leading to a frozen lake. The destination now: a door beneath the ice, its frame carved with spirals that shifted underfoot. The door was not a portal but a mirror—reflecting not the traveler, but the next destination.

Inside, a man in a tattered coat stood waiting. He held a map that had no coordinates. *"You’re not lost,"* he said. *"You’re always right where you’re meant to be."* The journey resumed, this time toward the place where the map’s edges dissolved into the wind. The final destination? The moment the first step was taken.
