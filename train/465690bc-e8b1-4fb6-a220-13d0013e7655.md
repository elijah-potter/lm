The old clock tower creaked at midnight, its pendulum swinging like a weary soul counting down to another endless night. I stood beneath its shadow, my fingers tracing the cracked pavement where the city’s forgotten dreams had once been etched in mud. My name was whispered by the wind—*Elias Vey*—but none knew it well. Not even me, half the time.

The letters had come first. A single sheet, folded twice, slipped under my door like a thief’s apology. *"Meet me at the docks at dawn. Bring what you stole."* The ink was smeared, as if written by someone who’d been crying. I didn’t know what to steal. Just that I was supposed to.

The docks were worse than I’d imagined. The air smelled of salt and rust, of something older—something that had been waiting too long. A figure emerged from the mist, taller than any man I’d seen before. His coat was too dark, his gloves too clean, as if he’d never touched anything but gold. He held out a hand. The grip was firm, the fingers long, like they’d been filed to perfection.

"You’re Elias," he said. "They’ve been looking for you."

I didn’t answer. My throat was too dry.

He turned to the others, the ones who’d been waiting in the shadows. They were different. Not men, but something else—shaped by fire and hunger. They moved without sound, their eyes black as ink on parchment. One of them reached into his coat and pulled out a single key. It was heavy, old, and *wrong*. The kind of thing that didn’t fit any lock.

"We need you to find something," he said. "Something they don’t know they’ve lost."

The docks were alive with whispers. Some said it was a debt. Others, a curse. I wanted to run. But the key in my pocket felt like a promise, like a spark in the dark.

He smiled. The kind of smile that didn’t reach his eyes. "Come on, then. The night’s long, and we’ve got a job to do."
