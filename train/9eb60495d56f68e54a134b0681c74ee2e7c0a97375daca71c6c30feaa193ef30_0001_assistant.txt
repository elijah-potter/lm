A pharmacist named Dr. John Stith Pemberton invented Coca-Cola syrup on May 8, 1886. He brought the syrup to Jacobs' Pharmacy, which started selling the product for five cents each. Dr. Pemberton's bookkeeper, Frank M. Robinson, came up with the name "Coca-Cola," thinking that the two Cs made the beverage marketable. Once The Atlanta Journal advertised the drink, Coca-Cola sold an average of nine drinks per day in the first year. Dr. Pemberton later sold the brand to businessman Asa G. Candler before he died in 1888, never knowing how famous Coca-Cola would become.
