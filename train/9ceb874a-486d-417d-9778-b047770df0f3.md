The cityscape of Neo-Edenville was a labyrinth of hyperloop tubes and autonomous drone-pods, their neon-lit pathways humming with data streams. Streets were repurposed every few minutes by AI planners, shifting between pedestrian zones, car-only lanes, and vertical farms overnight. Buildings, once rigid concrete, now flexed like living organisms with embedded smart-glass that shifted opacity, color, and even structural support based on real-time environmental needs. Skyscrapers sprouted new floors midday, their foundations reinforced by 3D-printed carbon-fiber composites. The population, though dense, was fragmented into nomadic micro-communities, each housed in modular, self-assembling pods that rearranged like jigsaw pieces based on social algorithms. The air carried the scent of lab-grown citrus and ozone, while holographic billboards flickered with ads for quantum computing, bioengineered meat, and "time-traveling" VR experiences. Bridges suspended between floating platforms connected by magnetic levitation cables, and traffic moved at variable speeds dictated by congestion prediction AI. The people—some wore augmented reality visors, others communicated via neural implants—struggled to keep up, their routines rewritten daily by unseen algorithms. The tension was palpable: *How do you belong when the world outgrows you?*
