Waiting—patient, deliberate, unhurried—is the quiet art of surrendering to the process. A train slows outside the station, its wheels turning in the empty corridor, before finally pulling into a station that has been waiting for this moment. The person inside, once rushing, now stands still, watching the door open. The train does not rush to move; it lets the weight of its presence settle, as if each second is a pause in the storm of haste.

They could have jumped onto the next one, but the rhythm of the first train feels right. The door swings open, revealing a space that has been waiting for them, too. The air smells faintly of rain and damp pavement, a scent that lingers in the gaps between moments. The train’s interior is half-empty, the seats unoccupied because someone chose to wait for this moment to arrive.

The passenger steps on, not in a rush, but with the quiet confidence of someone who knows that the delay was not wasted. The train begins to move, slow at first, then steady, carrying them forward—not against the current, but with it. The journey is not about speed; it’s about the space between the train and the station, the pause before the next step. Waiting is not idle time; it’s the space where decisions are made, where connections are forged, and where the universe, in its infinite patience, aligns just enough to make the moment feel right.
