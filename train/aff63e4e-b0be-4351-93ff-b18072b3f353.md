The concept of a "shortcut that is not shorter" refers to a metaphorical or logical paradox where the apparent goal of reducing effort or distance is paradoxically made longer or more complex. For example, a poorly designed detour might appear to save time on the surface but actually adds unnecessary steps, making the original route more efficient. Similarly, in software, a "shortcut" like a complex macro or script might initially seem efficient but can become convoluted and harder to maintain. The irony lies in the irony itself: the "shortcut" fails to achieve its supposed goal, highlighting inefficiencies in design or intent.
