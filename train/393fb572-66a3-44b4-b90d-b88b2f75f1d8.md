The holographic projection flickered to life over the neon-lit bar, casting eerie blue hues across the faces of the gathered crowd. A half-dozen figures huddled around a central table, their expressions a mix of speculation and dread. One man—tall, clad in a tailored coat that seemed slightly too tight—leaned forward, his fingers tracing the edge of a folded paper.

*"They’re calling it the Great Reckoning,"* he murmured, his voice barely audible over the low hum of the bar’s speakers. *"Not a date, not a year—something broader. A reckoning of sorts. The old systems are failing, the algorithms are… unraveling."*

A woman beside him exhaled sharply, her fingers tightening around her glass. *"You think we’ll still be standing when it comes?"*

*"Maybe,"* he replied, *"or maybe we’ll be the ones who decide we’re no longer worth keeping."*

A murmur rippled through the crowd. The air smelled of stale beer and distant ozone, the kind of tension that made the walls feel heavier. Outside, the city’s skyline pulsed with flickering lights, a chaotic dance of static and promise.

*"What do you mean, ‘decide’?"* a voice asked, cutting through the quiet.

The man’s eyes flicked to the stranger, then back to the paper in his hands. *"They’re saying we’re the ones who broke it. The ones who made it so fragile."*

The room went still. Somewhere, a siren wailed in the distance.
