The vast expanse of a silent, unmanned observatory chamber hums with residual energy from a defunct laser grid. The walls, slick with condensation, curve seamlessly into an infinite black void, their surfaces etched with faint, ancient symbols—no longer legible, lost to time. A single, flickering LED in the center casts a dim, uneven glow, casting long, shifting shadows that dance like living things against the floor, a cracked, metallic carpet worn smooth by centuries of disuse. The air is still, thick with the scent of ozone and something metallic, like old blood. Beyond the chamber, the cosmos stretches on—endlessly, unbroken—no horizon, no edge, just endless darkness and the whisper of unseen galaxies.
