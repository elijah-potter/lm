Today was another day of waking up before the sun, coffee that tastes like it’s been sitting in the cup for a week, and the same old routine of pretending I don’t see the way my hands shake when I grab my keys. The news still makes me feel like I should be doing something—maybe running a marathon, maybe starting a revolution—but I’m just trying to get through the day without burning out from trying too hard.

Work was okay. I kept my head down, did my job, and didn’t let myself think about how much smaller my life feels compared to the things people talk about on TV. My boss says I’m "good at this," but I wonder if that’s just because I don’t ask for too much. The commute was quiet, just the hum of the train and the way my mind drifts to all the things I could be doing instead of being here.

At home, the same old conversations: the neighbor’s dog barks too much, the neighbor’s cat steals their food, my partner jokes about how I’m still waiting for the day that doesn’t feel like a mistake. I tried to read, but my brain kept wandering to the things I *could* have done differently, the things I *should* have done differently. The mirror still tells me the same thing: my face is tired, my hands are calloused, my life is a blur of small, ordinary moments.

I didn’t even realize I was crying until I wiped my face and looked down at my hands. They’re just hands. They’re the same hands that washed dishes yesterday and typed out the same sentences today. But for some reason, the way the rain splattered against the window made me feel like maybe, just maybe, I’m not the only one who’s been waiting for something to change.
