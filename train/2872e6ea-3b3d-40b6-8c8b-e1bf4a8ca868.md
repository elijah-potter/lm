**The Infinite Echo**

A café’s hum dissolves into static as two strangers—one clad in a raincoat, the other in a borrowed scarf—sit at a cracked table. Their hands hover over empty mugs, fingers tracing the same absent shapes.

*"You ever think about how time’s just a loop we keep running?"* the raincoat says, voice a whisper over the hum of the espresso machine. The scarf’s fingers pause mid-air, then continue writing: *"I’ve been asking the same question for 37 years. The answer’s always the same: it’s not a loop. It’s a question we’re stuck answering."* The scarf’s pen dips into the coffee, staining the page with a slow, deliberate stain.

Outside, a clock ticks. Inside, their words bleed into each other, the raincoat’s *"why?"* and the scarf’s *"what?"* becoming one. The table splits under their weight, the scarf’s hand reaching for the raincoat’s, fingers brushing—then not brushing—over and over again. The café’s curtains flutter, then still. The espresso machine’s steam condenses into a mist that swirls like their unspoken thoughts.

*"You’re not the first to ask,"* the raincoat says, *"and you won’t be the last."* The scarf’s pen scratches out *"No. But you’re the first to say it’s not just a question."* The page fades to white, then returns, inked with the same question they’ve never left behind.

The last thing the raincoat hears is the scarf’s voice: *"Let’s start again."* The raincoat turns, but the scarf is already gone—just a scarf, a scarf, a scarf. The clock ticks. The world exhales. The cycle begins again.
