The first thing you will need to figure out is the amount of money you are able to spend on your vacation. Stressing about your money beforehand will not only show you the realistic price of gallivanting around before returning to work, but it is also going to keep you from overspending once you hit the road.

Use these tips to help save money on your trip:

* Instead of renting a car, use the car you already have.
* Even though you are on vacation, it is still a cost-effective idea to prepare the majority of meals at “home.” Home may be the car, hotel room, or campsite, but if you cook your own meals instead of going to restaurants every time you are hungry, it will save you money.
* Speaking of, camping is a great way to get to know the areas along your route more intimately and is almost always cheaper than renting a hotel room.

Next up, you will need to decide where to go. This is likely going to be a group effort to find a vacation everyone is on board for. Choosing some aspects from this list of destination descriptors may help narrow down where you will ultimately point your car:

* Oceanside: Do you want to be next to the ocean on your trip?
* Mountainous: Would you prefer to be in the mountains?
* Warm Weather: Depending on the season you are traveling in, you may want to find warm weather to defrost from the long winter season.
* Cold weather: Otherwise, if it is too warm in your area and you are looking to switch things up, pick somewhere that is going to supply you with hotter temperatures.

Finally, take note of how long you can afford to be gone. Like many, one or two weeks out of the year seem to be reserved for pleasure while the rest are full of work or school—or both. Knowing how long your trip will last will let you plan how far from home you can drive in the allotted time, where you can stop along your route, and how long you can stop there.

Once you have checked off all those boxes, get out and have fun because you are ready to hit the road!
