**The Last Transmission**

It wasn’t supposed to happen like this.

The radio crackled at 3:17 AM, static hissing through the static-dampened speakers of the *SS Aurora*, as if the very air had been holding its breath. Captain Elias Voss adjusted the dial, fingers trembling—not from cold, but from the weight of what he’d just heard.

*"They’re not coming alone."* The voice was muffled, distorted, but the words were clear. *They.*

The ship’s logs had been scrubbed. The crew had been given the same orders: *Do not open the hatch. Do not listen. Do not breathe the air in Sector 9.*

But Elias had been listening anyway.

The hull groaned as the *Aurora* lurched into the abyss of the black hole’s gravitational pull, its engines screaming like dying beasts. The last transmission had been from Dr. Lien—her voice raw, her breath coming in short, panicked gasps. *"They’re not the ones we feared. They’re the ones we made."*

Outside, the void swallowed the ship whole. Inside, the air grew thick, pressing against his face like a thousand unseen hands. The radio hummed, then died.

Then—movement.

Something had slipped through the gap in the hull.

Elias didn’t wait to see what followed. He grabbed the emergency oxygen tank, the one he’d been saving for a *real* emergency, and shoved it into his mouth. The last thing he heard before the darkness took him was the distant, rhythmic *click-click-click* of something scraping metal.
