The morning light spilled through the cracked blinds like slow liquid gold, pooling on the coffee-stained table where my hands trembled over the half-finished sketch. The words—*the past is a foreign country*—had been scrawled in jagged loops across the page, but now they blurred into something softer, like the edges of a dream dissolving in the humid air. The phone buzzed again, a notification from an old contact, a name that felt like a wound rather than a connection. I exhaled sharply, the breath fogging the glass as I stared at my reflection in the window: hollow cheeks, the same tiredness etched into the lines of my face.

The clarity had been sharp as a blade at first—the way the notes had unfolded, how the argument about time had settled into something almost poetic. But now, the edges were fraying. The phone in my pocket buzzed once more, a reminder of the world that didn’t pause for thought. The sketchbook, abandoned halfway through, sat open on the floor, the page where the words had been fading into the gray of the paper. Outside, the first raindrops tapped against the window, each one a question mark over what had been.
