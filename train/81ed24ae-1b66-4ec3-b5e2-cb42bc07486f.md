A dimly lit forest path, overgrown with ferns and tangled roots, curves away into the mist. The lone traveler, clad in worn boots and a hooded jacket, clutches a frayed map in one hand, the other gripping a rusted compass that spins uselessly. The sun hangs low, casting long shadows over moss-covered boulders and the distant, indistinct shapes of trees. A cold breeze whips through the underbrush, carrying the scent of damp earth and pine. The traveler halts abruptly, gripping the map with shaking fingers as realization dawns: the trail ends here. The GPS signal flickers to life—a single, faint dot on a map that’s no longer matching landmarks. A voice in their head whispers, *"You’re not on this path."* The path itself seems to twist, spiraling farther into the unknown.
