Pomegranates, which grow on shrub-like trees, are hard, smooth, round fruits of reddish-brown color (about the size of a navel orange). Inside a pomegranate are hundreds of red juice-filled, edible seeds (arils) and the white, inedible mesocarp. They're in season during winter and are less affordable than other typical fruits by weight. Mexican, Turkish, Greek, and other cuisines rely on pomegranates, and some people even use the juice for dye.
