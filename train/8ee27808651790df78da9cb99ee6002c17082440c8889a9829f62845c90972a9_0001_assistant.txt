The "Vitruvian Man" is one of Leonardo da Vinci's most recognized sketches, named after the ancient Roman architect Vitruvius who postulated that the human body could be made to fit inside a circle (symbolizing the spiritual cosmos) and a square (symbolizing the material earth), representing a sense of symmetry and proportion.

In this drawing, da Vinci illustrated the correlations of ideal human proportions with geometry, as described by Vitruvius: the span of a man's outspread arms is equal to his height, a palm is the width of four fingers, a foot is the length of four palms, a man's height is 24 palms, and so on. The "Vitruvian Man" also possesses eight heads tall, which was another common measure of proportion during the Renaissance.
