A whispered vow between the crack of dawn and the weight of midnight,
where every word was a half-truth wrapped in sugar,
and the only thing keeping it from shattering was the fear
that keeping it would mean drowning in the same tide
that had already washed through the hands that made it.

The clock was a liar—time stretched like wet silk,
and the promise was a bridge over the abyss,
its foundation built on the bones of lies told to oneself.
You promised to stay,
but staying was a word for people who had already learned
how to breathe underwater.

They say you can’t keep promises you don’t believe,
but the ones that break are the ones that know you *will*.
The moon was a witness, but it never judged,
and the stars didn’t ask why the sky was too heavy
to hold the weight of what you swore you’d never let go.

Now the wind carries the echoes of a vow
that has outlived its own meaning—
a ghost in the hollow of the heart,
still whispering promises
to ghosts who don’t remember what they wanted.
