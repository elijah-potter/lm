A candle flickered in the dim glow of an overcast evening, its flame dancing like a whisper of the unseen. The wooden table beneath it trembled slightly, as though it too held the weight of forgotten promises. On the table lay a small, velvet-lined box, its surface etched with intricate engravings. A single, blood-red rose rested beside it, its petals still damp with dew.

In the corner, a child—no older than six—leaned against a crumbling bookshelf, their breath shallow. Their eyes were wide with the kind of fear that comes from knowing the world might not be as kind as it seemed. The adult beside them, their face lined with exhaustion, clutched a worn pocket watch in their trembling hands. The second hand had stopped, frozen mid-motion, as if caught in a moment of time long past.

*"Open it,"* the adult whispered, their voice raw. *"Just open it."* The child’s fingers trembled, but they reached out anyway, the tip of their little thumb brushing the lid. With a sharp *clink*, it lifted—revealing a single, golden key within, its surface gleaming faintly. The child’s eyes locked onto it, then onto the adult’s face, searching for something they couldn’t name. The adult exhaled, a sound half-hysterical, half-hopeful. *"It’s the only thing left,"* they murmured. *"The only way out."* The child swallowed hard, their gaze flickering between the key and the box, then to the horizon where the first stars were beginning to pierce the clouds.

The adult reached out, their fingers brushing the child’s shoulder. *"Take it,"* they said. *"Take it and run."* The child’s hands shook as they lifted the box, the key glinting in the dim light. The adult’s voice cracked. *"Promise me you’ll find out what’s inside. Promise."* The child’s lips moved, but the words were lost in the silence. The candle burned lower, its flame flickering out as the last drop of wax fell onto the table. The box lay open, the key still within. The child held it, and the air between them seemed to hold its breath.
