**Passage through the central plaza of a medieval European city:**

A mist curls from cracked cobblestones, reflecting the low autumn sun. Groups move along the broad, arched walkways lined with stone benches where locals sip from mismatched mugs. Vendors push carts of warm pastries near the fountain, its water still trickling despite the dry season. Children dart between adults, their laughter mingling with the distant clatter of horses and the occasional call of street performers.

Market stalls along the south side display woven textiles, copperware, and dried goods, their wares arranged in neat piles. The air smells of roasting meat and freshly baked bread, with the distant scent of the river crossing the city’s outer walls.

A grand archway ahead leads to the fortress gates, its stone battlements worn smooth by time. A few soldiers patrol the perimeter, their uniforms faded by centuries of wear. Beyond the arch, the city’s walls stretch into the distance, marking the boundary between the old and the new. Some travelers pause at the arch, their eyes drawn to the distant spires of the cathedral rising above the rooftops. Others hurry, their hooves or feet pounding the stone.
