**The Last Light**

The clock struck eleven when she found him—half-drunk, his breath sour against the cracked floorboards of the abandoned lounge. His fingers, once steady, now trembled as he reached for the glass of whiskey, its amber liquid swallowed in one shuddering gulp.

*"You shouldn’t have come,"* he whispered, voice thick with regret. His eyes were hollow, like the space between the cracks in the ceiling. *"I never should’ve let you in."*

She didn’t speak. Instead, she pulled the flask from her pocket—the one she’d carried since last summer—and pressed it to his lips. His hand shook as he took it, but he didn’t refuse. The burn of the burn, the memory of the night before, when he’d laughed too loud in the back of a cab, when the world had been just a blur of neon and stolen kisses.

The air smelled of damp concrete and old perfume. The streetlights flickered, casting long shadows that stretched like skeletal fingers across the pavement. She watched him swallow, watched the way his jaw tightened, the way his breath hitched.

*"You’re leaving,"* he said, almost a demand. *"Again."*

She didn’t answer. Instead, she let the door swing shut behind her, the click of the lock echoing like a gunshot. The night swallowed her whole.

She didn’t look back.
