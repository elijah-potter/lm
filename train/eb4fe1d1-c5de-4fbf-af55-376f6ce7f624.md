In the quiet, sunlit kitchen of the old farmhouse, **Lena** wiped her hands on her apron, the fabric rough against her palms. Outside, the wind howled through the skeletal branches of the willow, rattling the panes. She had promised her father she’d keep the farm running—no matter the cost—when the storm hit.

The lightning split the sky in jagged silver, followed by a deafening crack. Rain lashed against the windows, turning the kitchen into a glassy maze. The generator’s whine cut out, plunging them into darkness. Lena’s breath hitched—she could hear her father’s labored breathing from the loft, the only light a flickering candle on the counter.

A child’s scream echoed from the barn. **Mira**, just eight, had wandered off while fetching water. The storm had torn the barn door apart, and the wind screamed through the gap like a living thing. Lena’s fingers tightened around the candle, her stomach churning. She had promised to protect Mira, to keep them safe.

She grabbed her father’s lantern, half-finished, and dragged it upstairs. The loft creaked under her weight, the floorboards groaning. The candle guttered, casting long shadows. Her father’s voice was hoarse with exhaustion: *"Lena, don’t—"* She didn’t answer. Mira’s voice came from the barn now, shrill and desperate: *"Mama! Help!"*

The lantern slipped. She caught it just as it shattered against the wall. The barn was a nightmare of debris, the storm’s fury unspooling into the dark. Mira screamed again, her voice raw. Lena dove in, her boots sinking into mud. The wind howled like a beast, but she moved faster than fear could name. She found Mira tangled in broken branches, her small hands clutching a torn rag doll.

*"I’m coming,"* she whispered, her voice raw. The lantern was gone. She had promised her father the farm would endure. She had promised Mira she’d never let her fall.

The lantern was lost. The promise was tested.
