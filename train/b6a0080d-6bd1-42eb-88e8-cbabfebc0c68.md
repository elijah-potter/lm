The air smelled of damp wool and old leather. A half-lit chandelier cast long shadows across cracked, sun-bleached linoleum. The fireplace’s embers glowed faintly, casting flickering patterns on a desk cluttered with half-empty mugs, a half-eaten sandwich, and a crumpled sketchbook. The bedspread was rumpled, one pillow tossed aside, its once-white pillowcase now stained with what looked like tea rings. The window, slightly ajar, revealed a rain-soaked street below, the distant sound of sirens muffled by the wind. A single framed photo—perhaps of a child—lay on the floor, its glass cracked and smudged with fingerprints. On the bookshelf, a few volumes were open to random pages, their pages slightly bent from being dragged across the shelf. The floorboards creaked underfoot, but the only other sound was the distant hum of a neighbor’s TV.
