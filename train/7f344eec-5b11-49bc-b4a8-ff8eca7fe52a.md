An infinite void stretches endlessly, void of reference points or boundaries. Light flickers faintly, either from distant stars or a distant sun, casting eerie reflections across smooth, featureless surfaces. The silence is absolute, broken only by the occasional, unnatural hum—perhaps residual energy from some forgotten machinery or the whisper of an unseen entity. Shadows stretch unnaturally long, defying the curvature of the space, as if the horizon itself bends away from you. The air, if there is any, is thin and stale, carrying a faint metallic tang. Objects seem to exist in a perpetual state of disarray, as if the space itself is a living thing, rearranging things without purpose. Gravity, if it exists at all, is weak and inconsistent—sometimes pulling you toward one point, then suddenly away. The walls, if walls there are, recede into a haze, never quite meeting, as if the space is designed to make you feel insignificant, lost in an endless, indifferent void.
