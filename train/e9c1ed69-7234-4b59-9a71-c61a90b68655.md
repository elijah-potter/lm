A flickering emergency light casts long shadows across the cramped living room. The hum of a generator fills the quiet—its low, mechanical drone the only sound besides the distant *thump-thump* of a nearby window rattling in the wind. The air smells like burnt plastic and old coffee from the stove that still sits unplugged, its coils glowing dully.

Lena leans against the kitchen counter, her fingers tracing the edge of a half-eaten sandwich. Her phone buzzes—a text from her neighbor: *"Broke something? Can’t open door. Stay safe."* She doesn’t reply, her gaze fixed on the flickering TV screen, where a news ticker glitches in and out: *"Power outage reported across Zone 5. Residents advised to use generators sparingly."*

Outside, the streetlights have died, plunging the sidewalk into near-darkness. A car horn blares nearby, its owner presumably trying to signal someone in the dark. The generator’s engine coughs, stalls, then revs again, its light casting jagged beams across the room. The ceiling fan above spins slowly, its blades whirring like a dying insect.

Mira walks in, her coat damp, her breath fogging in the cold air. She carries a thermos of coffee and a bag of frozen meals. "Took me three tries to get this door open," she says, tossing the bag onto the table. "Found a neighbor’s keys under the porch mat. Figured you’d need supplies."

Lena doesn’t look up. "Generator’s dying. We’re out of fuel." She opens the thermos, swirling the black liquid. The generator’s light dims again.
