**The Storm’s Curtain**

The sky was a bruise of midnight, cracked by lightning that split the heavens like a child’s drawing of a fist. Rain lashed the windowpane in jagged sheets, each drop a whisper of despair coiled in the wind. The air smelled of damp earth and something older—rust, perhaps, or the slow rot of secrets left unspoken.

Inside, the house held its breath. The grand piano, half-forgotten, stood like a sentinel, its keys dulled by neglect. A single candle flickered on the desk, its flame a trembling ember against the chaos outside. The curtains, once crisp and tailored, were now damp and askew, caught between the weight of the storm and the weight of the silence.

A figure sat at the kitchen table, their fingers tracing the edge of a half-filled glass. The window framed the storm—blackened branches clawing at the clouds, the distant howl of the wind, the thunder’s slow, deliberate pulse. The mood was a storm too, one that had been raging for years, buried beneath layers of silence and half-hearted attempts at repair.

Outside, the rain grew heavier, the wind howling like a ghost. The house groaned, as if it had been listening all along. Inside, the figure exhaled slowly, the breath catching in the hollow of their throat. The storm outside was a mirror—every tear, every whispered regret, every half-finished sentence—all of it visible now, raw and unfiltered.

The candle guttered. The world outside did not yet know the end was near.
