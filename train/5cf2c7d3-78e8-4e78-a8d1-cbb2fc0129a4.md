The warehouse’s fluorescent lights flickered as the forklift driver, mid-lift, hesitated. A single loose pallet in the corner—marked "Do Not Handle"—had been abandoned by the previous shift. The foreman’s clipboard, left open on the counter, showed a "10% margin" note next to the "Safety Violation" box. The driver adjusted the grip, lowering the pallet just enough to bypass the forklift’s sensors. The guardrail at the loading dock was slightly bent from a previous incident; the foreman had joked it was "old enough to be flexible." Inside, a new shipment of unmarked boxes waited, their labels smudged with ink that matched the foreman’s signature—*not* the approved one. The stack of invoices on the desk had a misplaced "2023" in the bottom right corner. A single receipt, dated one day earlier, sat atop the stack. The camera feed was disabled for the night, and the only visible alarm was a half-empty coffee mug on the foreman’s desk. The warehouse’s air smelled of old wood and the faint metallic tang of the foreman’s forgotten cigarette butt in the ashtray. The driver’s radio crackled with a message: *"Team, no one’s watching."* The pallet was loaded, the invoice signed, and the foreman’s face, half-lit by the dim glow of the exit sign, was already set in the chair by the door, his tie loosened. The system was designed to fail.
