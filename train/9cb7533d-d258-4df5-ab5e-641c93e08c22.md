**The Last Transmission**

We didn’t mean for this to happen.

The radio crackled to life in the back of the old truck, static hissing like teeth bared in the dark. The driver’s hands gripped the wheel tighter as the engine coughed, then settled into a slow, deliberate groan. Outside, the neon glow of the city pulsed like a heartbeat, but inside, the cabin felt suffocating—thick with the weight of something unspoken. The passenger, arms folded across the seat, stared at the screen in the dash, fingers tracing the faint, distorted outline of a message: *"You’re not alone."*

The words had come from nowhere.

The night before, she’d been working late, her fingers dancing across the keys of her old laptop. The screen had glitched—just a second, but long enough to make her jump. Then silence. Nothing. No error codes, no warnings, just the hum of the machine dying. She’d rebooted, but the message remained: a single line, repeated in a voice that wasn’t hers. *"We didn’t mean for this to happen."* The text flickered, then vanished. The next morning, she woke to find her desk empty.

The truck had been waiting for her.

The driver pulled over at the edge of the highway, where the guardrail loomed like a skeletal finger. The passenger stepped out first, their breath fogging in the cold. The driver followed, the radio still broadcasting the same message, looping like a lullaby.

*"We didn’t mean for this to happen."*

The ground beneath them trembled. The sky above split open, not with rain, but with something else—lightning, sharp and silver, cutting through the clouds like a blade. From the cracks in the sky, shapes emerged: faces, blurred and shifting, their mouths moving in perfect synchrony.

*"You’re not alone."*

The truck’s tires screeched as it lurched forward, but the road dissolved beneath them. The passenger’s hands shot out, fingers weaving through the air, pulling threads of something—dust, maybe, or stardust, or nothing at all. The driver watched as the world unraveled around them, colors bleeding into each other, shapes dissolving into smoke.

The radio’s voice filled the silence again.

*"We didn’t mean for this to happen."*

The passenger smiled. The driver’s eyes widened. The truck’s engine died.

Outside, the city breathed—once, twice—then still.

And somewhere, in the quiet between stars, something watched.
