A lone gardener in a forgotten courtyard noticed a single acorn in the cracked earth—one that had been missed by years of neglect. They nurtured it with water from a cracked jug, tucking it into a hollowed-out terracotta pot, shielding it from the wind that had once howled through the empty space.

Months passed. The acorn sprouted into a stubborn sapling, its roots burrowing deeper than the soil’s original depth. One evening, as the first frost touched the courtyard, the gardener stood beneath the sapling’s first fragile leaves, whispering to it: *"You were here before I was."* The sapling did not grow taller that night, but it held its ground.

Years later, when the courtyard was replanted, the sapling had grown into a small, gnarled tree—a silent witness to the seasons. The gardener, now elderly, told the story to a stranger passing by, who took the acorn as a reminder: *"Sometimes, what seems insignificant at first becomes the thing that holds us together."* The stranger planted it in their garden, where it took root and grew, though neither knew the other’s name.
