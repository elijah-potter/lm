In the quiet hum of a café, where the scent of coffee lingers like an old memory, I found myself staring at the same wall for the third time. The paint had peeled in places, revealing the faded remnants of a once-grand mural—now just a patchwork of dust and forgotten dreams. Outside, the city’s pulse was a distant rhythm, a symphony of sirens and footsteps, but here, in this corner, time had stopped.

I reached out, my fingers brushing the edge of the half-empty espresso cup. The warmth seeped through my palm, a ghostly echo of the energy I’d just drained. What if, I wondered, the world could be like this—still, but without the noise? The absence of meaning isn’t always worse than its presence; sometimes, it’s just different.

The waitress appeared, her eyes sharp with the kind of exhaustion only city dwellers know. "Another?" she asked, but her voice was a question, not an order. I hesitated. What would I choose? The quiet or the chaos? The story or the silence? The choice was already written in the way my hands trembled—not from fear, but from something deeper, something that had been waiting for its turn to speak.
