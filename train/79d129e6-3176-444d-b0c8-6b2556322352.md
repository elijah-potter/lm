In the hollowed heart of an ancient library, where the air hummed with the whispers of forgotten tomes, Dr. Elias Vey stood before a mirror cracked with time. The reflection was not his own—it was a stranger’s, clad in tattered robes, fingers tracing the edge of a journal half-buried in dust. The pages within were a labyrinth of equations and half-remembered sketches, each one a fragment of a greater truth.

He turned to the window, where the city below pulsed like a living organism. Neon signs flickered like dying stars, casting shadows that danced across the cobblestones. Outside, a child ran barefoot, chasing after a stray pigeon, oblivious to the world’s weight. Elias exhaled slowly, his breath fogging the glass. The journal clutched in his hands was warm, as if the past had left its mark there.

A knock at the door shattered the moment. His assistant, Mira, stood framed in the doorway, her expression unreadable. "The package arrived," she said, holding up a small, unmarked box. Elias’ pulse quickened. He had ordered it months ago, but the delivery had been delayed by customs and bureaucracy. Inside, wrapped in layers of tissue paper, lay a single photograph: a black-and-white portrait of a woman with eyes like deep oceans, standing against a stormy horizon. Her name was never written on the back.

Mira pressed a key into his palm. "The server’s down. The last transmission was encrypted."

Elias’ fingers trembled as he typed the code into the terminal. The screen flared to life, revealing a holographic projection: a shifting constellation of data streams, pulsing with energy. Among them, a single image emerged—floating like a ghost between stars—a woman’s face, half-lit by an unseen source, her silhouette etched against a void. The image blurred, then stabilized.

The last line of the transmission appeared in his vision:

**"She was never gone. She was waiting."**

The screen went dark.
