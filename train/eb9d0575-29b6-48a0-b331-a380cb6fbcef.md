In dimly lit corridors of a crumbling library, fingers tracing over worn spines, the seeker’s breath fogs the air. A whisper of static hums from an old radio in the corner, its tune a half-remembered lullaby. The object they’re after isn’t carved in stone or etched in gold—it’s a ghost of a thought, a fleeting echo of a name scribbled on a napkin years ago. The walls, once lined with books, now host only dust motes that drift like lost letters. A half-empty coffee cup, still warm, sits atop a cracked table where a half-closed journal lies open, its final entry: *"If I could just hear it again..."*. The seeker’s shadow, long and jagged, stretches across the floor, blurred by the flickering fluorescent light. Somewhere in the maze of shelves, a single book—its cover faded to match the despair—remains unchecked, as if deliberately left behind.
