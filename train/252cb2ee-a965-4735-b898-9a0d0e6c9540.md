The hollowed-out core of an abandoned train station, its arched ceilings cracked with age and weathered by years of forgotten footsteps. The floor, once polished steel, now bears the patina of time—scuffed by carts, cracked by planks shifted by drifters, embedded with the remnants of lost schedules and half-remembered stories. A single, rusted lamppost stands sentinel, its bulb long gone, casting flickering shadows of the past onto the walls that once buzzed with the hum of steam engines and the clatter of wheels. The air smells of damp earth and old iron, a ghostly echo of the bustle that was here once—trains arriving and departing, passengers arriving and departing, life moving in and out like a tide. Yet beneath the layers of decay, the bones of the structure hold fast, unyielding in their endurance. The doors, once grand, now swing weakly on their rusted hinges, but they never fully close. It is a place where the temporary feels permanent, where absence lingers like a half-remembered melody.
