An abandoned cityscape where skeletal buildings loom over cracked pavement, their once-grand facades now marred by rust and boarded-up windows. The air hums with the ghostly echoes of laughter and distant machinery, a perpetual dusk that never quite settles. Streets stretch endlessly, lined with overgrown gardens and rusted streetlights, their bulbs long since failed. A broken bridge, half-collapsed, spans a dried-up riverbed where the water once rushed, now reduced to a trickle. The place feels alive only in fragments—an occasional flicker of light from a broken neon sign, the distant murmur of a radio playing static, the scent of damp earth and old oil. It’s a liminal space, neither fully abandoned nor thriving, a place where the past refuses to fade and the future lingers uncertain.
