The dimly lit forest path ahead split into two, one winding steeply upward through dense underbrush while the other stretched broad and level across the open meadow. The traveler paused, clutching their satchel, as the sun dipped lower, casting long shadows. The first path whispered of effort, the second of time and distance. They considered the map—*2.3 miles versus 3.7*—but the map had no voice, no weight. The weight of the choice was in the pulse of their own breath.

They turned left. The first path’s ascent was steep, but the air thinned and the valley below shimmered with distant villages. Each step was a promise: the climb was not for the faint, but the reward was worth the sweat. By noon, the trail had rewarded them with a view: a crimson lake, its surface like molten glass, and the silhouette of a village nestled at its edge, its roofs glowing gold in the fading light.

They had chosen the longer path.
