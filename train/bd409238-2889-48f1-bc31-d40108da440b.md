A half-finished library, shelves stacked with books left open mid-read—pages crumpled, ink still wet on the page—where the last reader paused just long enough to sign their name. The air smells like old parchment and damp wood, the only sound a faint hum from the ceiling fan, its blades whispering like lost words. The windows are open, letting in a light that’s neither day nor night, a golden haze that flickers like a dying candle. The only thing moving is the dust motes swirling in the dim light, tracing patterns no one is watching. A half-empty coffee mug sits on a desk, steam condensing into a ghostly cloud. The silence is thick enough to eat, but it’s not empty—it’s full of stories that haven’t been told yet.
