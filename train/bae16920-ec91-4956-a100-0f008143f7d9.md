Decisions that prioritize individual interests over collective harmony often trigger intra-group conflict, exemplified by the 2016 U.S. presidential election, where divisive rhetoric and policy clashes amplified ideological splits, fostering long-term polarization. Similarly, corporate mergers where leadership values short-term profit over sustainability can fragment employee trust, leading to workplace dissent.

In open-source communities, disagreements over project direction (e.g., Linux kernel debates) result in forks, while political movements where factionalism overruns unity (e.g., Brexit factions) erode cohesion. The core tension: decisions that prioritize efficiency or innovation over consensus inevitably fracture groups, as seen in failed coalition governments or corporate buyouts where dissent becomes systemic resistance.
