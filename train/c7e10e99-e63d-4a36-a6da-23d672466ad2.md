The person began with a simple daily ritual: a 10-minute morning meditation to center themselves. Over time, the habit expanded to include journaling, stretching, and a calming tea ritual, eventually becoming a daily routine. Initially, it brought clarity and reduced stress, but as years passed, the habit became rigid—an obligation rather than a choice.

Stress from work and personal life compounded, turning meditation into a performance: perfect poses, mindless repetition of affirmations, and avoiding discomfort. The journal became a checklist of "must-dos," and the ritual evolved into a coping mechanism for emotional exhaustion. Friends noticed the withdrawal, the late nights, and the detachment from hobbies they once loved. The habit had become a cage, trapping the person in a cycle of guilt and self-loathing when they failed to meet its demands.

The irony? The original intention was to find peace, but the habit had twisted into something that demanded more than the person could give. Now, they question whether they’re free or just another slave to the routine.
