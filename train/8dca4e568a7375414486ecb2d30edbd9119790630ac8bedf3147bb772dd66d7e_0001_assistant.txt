The air was thick with humidity and the sweet scent of petrichor. The dark green leaves of the plants around him were larger than he was used to, and for a moment, he felt dwarfed in comparison. His shoes sunk into the wet soil, and he stumbled as he walked, pushing the leaves aside so he could better see. The canopy of trees above him was so densely packed; only muted light fell down, casting everything in inky, murky colors.
When he had decided to go hunting for El Dorado, Sal was aware that he’d have to trudge through the jungles, but knowing something and experiencing something were two completely different things. He knew he would find El Dorado; now, he just had to experience it.
He pushed back another heavy monstera leaf and stopped short. In front of him, without any warning at all, was a sheer drop from a cliff. And below that cliff, sprawled across the wide valley of the jungle, was a golden city. THE golden city: El Dorado.
Sal’s heart caught in his throat and all he could do was stare, and the longer he did, the more he picked up. The gold – it wasn’t actual gold, no, it was golden bricks. Whole houses and structures and curling roads and streets, all of them were made of the same shining golden bricks, and nestled as it was amidst the lush, dark greenery, it glittered all the more.
