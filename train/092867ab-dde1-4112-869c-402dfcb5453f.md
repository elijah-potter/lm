A lone figure stands at the threshold of a dimly lit apartment door, the hallway’s fluorescent glow casting long shadows. Their coat is unzipped, sleeves rolled up, fingers tapping an absent rhythm on the wood. The fridge hums softly behind them, the door slightly ajar. A half-eaten sandwich sits on the countertop, a half-drunk cup of coffee cooling near the sink. The TV’s static crackles in the living room, but their gaze lingers on the door, a pause too long to ignore. The sound of a car engine revving outside the window grows louder, closer. They hesitate—then turn, shoulders slumped, the door swings shut behind them without a word. The hallway grows quiet.
