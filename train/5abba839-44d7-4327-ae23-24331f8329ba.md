Waiting in the dim glow of a flickering streetlamp, the door of the newsstand creaked open. A gust of cold wind carried the scent of rain and damp pavement. Inside, a single newspaper lay open on the counter, its headline bold and urgent:

**"BREAKING: Massive Fire at Old Mill—12 Missing"**

The old woman behind the counter, her hands trembling slightly, flipped the paper over. The back page, a small classified ad, read:

**"Wanted: Volunteers for Search Team. Call 987-6543. Bring sturdy shoes."**

She handed the paper to the young man standing in the doorway, his coat damp, his breath misting in the cold. The headline was already fading under the weight of the words beneath it:

**"Crying for Help: Local Child Found in River. Parents Urge Search Efforts."**

The young man scanned the ad, then looked up at the woman, her face lined with exhaustion. She nodded once, her voice barely audible over the distant wail of sirens.

*"You’re the only one who’ll get this first."*

The young man took the paper, the weight of it heavy in his hands. Outside, the wind howled, but inside, the newsstand hummed with a quiet, urgent pulse—like the heartbeat of a city waiting for answers.
