The neon flickered weakly outside the abandoned theater, casting jagged shadows across the cracked floorboards. Inside, the last of the flickering fluorescent bulbs sputtered before dying entirely, plunging the space into near-total darkness. A single bulb in the projection booth above the screen still glowed dimly, its light barely piercing the dust-covered curtains.

Inside, a handful of people huddled near the back row, their faces illuminated only by the pale glow of a single cellphone screen. The murmur of whispered conversations grew louder as someone clutched a half-empty bottle of whiskey, its amber liquid sloshing against the glass. The air smelled of stale beer, sweat, and something metallic—fear or desperation, hard to tell.

The projectionist, a gaunt man with a face lined by deep circles, adjusted the last remaining film reel, his fingers trembling slightly. The screen behind him was blank, the only sound the distant groan of the old projector, its gears grinding to a halt. A few scattered pieces of debris lay across the floor, remnants of a once-grand theater now reduced to a ghostly relic.

A sharp crack echoed from the balcony above. Someone screamed. The group froze, the tension thick enough to cut. The projectionist’s voice, barely audible over the pounding of his heart, cut through the silence:

*"Stay down."*

From the darkness beyond the stage, the distant sound of gunfire erupted. The power flickered again, briefly illuminating a shadowy figure moving toward the door. The group’s screams turned to terrified silence as the last of the light vanished.
