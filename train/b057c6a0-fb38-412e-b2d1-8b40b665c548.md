The old lighthouse keeper’s lantern flickered weakly against the storm-wracked horizon. A ship’s distress beacon had cut through the rain earlier, its signal repeating in Morse code: **DASH-DOT-DASH-DOT-DASH-DASH-DASH**. The keeper’s hands trembled as he traced the letters on the cracked board:

**SOS**

The waves crashed higher, but the radio’s antenna still crackled. Outside, a gull screamed as it dove toward the sea. Inside, the keeper’s only weapon was the lantern, its beam painting jagged shadows on the walls.

Then, the radio’s dial spun wildly. A voice—distorted but unmistakable—cut through the static:

*"This is the Coast Guard. We are on our way. Stay put."*

Silence. The lantern’s glow dimmed.
