A **chronopoleis**—a city where time flows in reverse at the center, gradually resetting outward in concentric waves.

At its heart, the **Eternal Hourglass** (a single, infinite sand flow) marks the central moment, where clocks operate backward. The streets curve like petals, with buildings shifting their positions daily. Pedestrians walk in loops, revisiting the same landmarks in reverse. The **Clockwork Fountain** drips water in reverse, and the **Midnight Bazaar** only opens at noon, trading goods from future eras.

Outlying districts follow a staggered inversion: the outer rings experience time delays proportional to distance from the core. A traveler from the outer ring might arrive at the city’s central hourglass *after* it has already passed its peak. The city’s laws enforce "retroactive compliance," where laws enacted yesterday are still in effect, and future events must be *foreseen* retroactively.

Time is measured by **anti-clockwise pendulums**, where the swing period doubles every hour, and **echo-calipers** that measure delays by listening to sound waves. The only "normal" time is in the outer margins, where the city’s temporal distortion is minimal—enough that a traveler might mistake it for a conventional timekeeping system.

Visitors are told: *"Time is a river. Here, the current runs backward."*
