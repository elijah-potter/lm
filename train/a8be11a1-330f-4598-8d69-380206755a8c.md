**The Last Light**

The city hummed beneath the neon, a ghostly pulse of forgotten dreams. Outside, rain blurred the glass, and inside, the air smelled like old coffee and the faint metallic tang of fear. She sat at the bar, fingers tracing the rim of her glass, a half-empty bottle of whiskey catching the dim light like a dying star.

A server slid a menu across the table. *"Would you like dessert?"* The words were soft, almost kind.

She didn’t answer. Instead, her gaze drifted to the clock—just past midnight, and already the shift was ending. She had been here for months, watching the same faces, the same routines, the same way time stretched like taffy between two fingers. But today, something shifted. The weight in her chest felt different.

The reminder flashed in her mind: *Don’t forget.*

She had forgotten. Too many times. The way her reflection in the mirror had once smiled back at her, now just stared. The way her name had been whispered behind her back. The way the system had started to catch on.

But today, she stood. The server watched, surprised. The door behind her clicked open, and the world outside was still—just like always. But this time, she didn’t look back.

Outside, the rain kept falling. Inside, the bar stayed silent.
