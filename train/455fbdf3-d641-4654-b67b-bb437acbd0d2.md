A weary clerk at the customer service desk, hands covered in ink stains, finally spoke: *"I’m sorry for the delay. The machine’s been misbehaving all week, and we’ve been stuck in this loop of ‘maybe tomorrow’—"* She paused, wiping sweat from her brow. *"The manager’s promised a replacement by Friday, but we’re still waiting. We’ll do our best to make it right, I swear."*

Across the room, a customer—arms crossed, eyes sharp—leaned in. *"I’ve been here since 9:17. I’ve waited through three ‘tentative’ updates, and you’re still telling me *you’re sorry*?"* Their voice cut through the tension like a knife. The clerk’s jaw tightened. *"I don’t know what else to say."* The silence stretched, thick with unspoken frustration. *"But we’ll fix it. I promise."* The customer exhaled, but the words hung between them like a half-accepted apology: *almost.*
