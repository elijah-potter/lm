In the historic district of **Veldora**, residents were jolted by the unexpected discovery of a 1,200-year-old artifact beneath the ruins of the ancient **Tower of the Celestial Wind**. Archaeologists from the local university confirmed the find—a fractured stone tablet bearing an unknown script—during routine excavations near the city’s old aqueduct. The tablet, partially cracked, shows symbols resembling a mix of runes and geometric patterns, suggesting it may be a fragment of an earlier lost language. Mayor Elias Voss declared the discovery a potential breakthrough, urging the city to apply for UNESCO protection for the excavation site to preserve it as a cultural monument.

Meanwhile, neighboring **Blackthorn**, a port city notorious for its smuggling trade, has been placed under temporary lockdown after authorities seized a high-value shipment of "stabilized ancient relics" believed to be looted from the same region. Rumors in the docks suggest these items could be fragments of the same artifact, though no confirmation has been made. Police are investigating potential ties between the find and the city’s long-standing black-market trade in relics. The Blackthorn Chamber of Commerce has already issued a statement condemning the closure, calling it an overreach by "fascist municipal officials." No arrests have been made yet.

Local historians speculate the artifact could be tied to the city’s legend of the **"Silent Watcher"**, a cryptid said to lurk beneath Veldora’s oldest churches. Some believe the symbols might encode warnings about the Watcher’s return, while skeptics dismiss it as a hoax—though the university’s lead archaeologist, Dr. Liora Chen, insists the discovery warrants serious study. The city’s cryptid division has been placed on standby to investigate "unusual disturbances" in the area overnight, though no sightings have been reported.

Veldora’s stock market saw a 4% dip after the news, with investors speculating on potential tourism revenue from the find. The city’s tourism board has already begun preparing promotional materials, though security teams are reinforcing patrols near the excavation site to prevent unauthorized access. The discovery has also reignited debates about the city’s relationship with its past—should Veldora open its doors to scholars and tourists, or keep the secrets of its ruins buried? The mayor’s office has not yet commented on these broader implications.
