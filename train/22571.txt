What Is the History of the American Pitbull Terrier XL?
The American Pitbull Terrier XL is an off-shoot of the original American Pitbull Terrier. A descendant of “Old Family Red Nose,” these dogs originated in the 1800s and were used as farm dogs and working dogs. As time passed, they grew in popularity thanks to their strength, loyalty, agility and intelligence.
The first American Pitbull Terriers were bred in England during the 19th century by crossing terriers with bulldogs. This combination created a strong dog full of energy and enthusiasm for work – a combination that was attractive to many farmers looking for help with vermin control or tending livestock. Historians believe this combination was so effective that some early fanciers began breeding for size as well as for other characteristics.
In 1988, the United Kennel Club (UCK) accepted the breed which led to increased recognition both in North America and abroad. By 2000, the breed had become quite popular both due to its imposing physical appearance and its reputation for being loyal companions – especially in households with small children.
However, despite this newfound fame came controversy surrounding its potential safety record. This fuelled much debate about whether the breed should be classed as ‘dangerous’ or not; ultimately leading to restrictions on ownership across several countries including New Zealand since 2003 and Australia since 2012 – making it illegal to possess a Pitbull without approval from your local council or court authority depending on where you live.
Though not all breeds are considered inherently dangerous – various traits such as guarding behaviour & aggression towards strangers could easily result in injury if not managed properly through training & socialisation measures so caution is always recommended when coming into contact with any canine! Many people fearing this stigma have sought out specific lines such as ‘American PitBull Terrier XL’ who are noted for their larger bone structure & easier trainability compared to other types – allowing even those who feel intimidated by smaller standard sized variants an alternative option should circumstances permit it!
These days, modern type American PitBull Terriers XL are commonly seen participating successfully in agility competitions alongside their smaller relatives proving them just as capable when it comes down to performing tasks assigned at events – all whilst still retaining their signature powerful build & inspiring character which made them famous over two centuries ago!

FAQs About the American Pitbull Terrier XL
What is the American Pitbull Terrier XL?
The American Pitbull Terrier XL is a mixed breed dog obtained by crossing an American Pit Bull and an Old English Bulldog. This new type of pit bull was introduced in order to provide a larger version of the typical APBT that still had many of the favorable characteristics desired from the old English Bulldogs. The size, strength and loyalty of this new breed made them instantly appealing to pit bull lovers.
Are there any health or behavior concerns with this breed?
American Pitbull Terrier XL puppies can be prone to hip dysplasia, just like many other large breeds – thus it’s important to purchase only with certified responsible breeders who have tested their dogs for healthy hips and elbows. These pups may also inherit some behavioral issues from their parent breeds if not socialized early on, as both Old English Bulldogs and American Pit Bulls can be quite stubborn when it comes to commands and obedience training. It’s always best to research potential breeder options carefully before making a decision, and opt for those who are dedicated to producing physically healthy specimens that have been properly socialized.
What kind of lifestyle should I expect from my APBT XL pup?
American Pit Bull Terrier XL pups are very intelligent dogs, so will benefit greatly from positive training methods such as reward-based obedience courses or agility courses. They love attention from people, as well as games such as fetch, agility activities or swimming (given proper supervision). They are loyal companions but due to their tenacity need consistent structure around mealtimes and potty breaks – practice patience when teaching basic instincts such as ‘sit’ or ‘stay’ too! Because they are known for being slightly dominant by nature, they need someone capable of teaching them how appropriate boundaries look like while providing them with sufficient physical and mental enrichment – excercise is key!

Top 5 Facts About the American Pitbull Terrier XL
The American Pitbull Terrier XL is no average-sized pooch. This muscular, compact breed has developed a reputation for being fierce and intimidating, but that’s far from the truth! Here are five top facts about this strong and loyal companion:
1. History: The American Pit bull Terriers (APBT) were bred in America from the late 1800s and were developed from crosses between old English Terriers, Bulldogs and other terrier breeds. They were originally used for dog fighting before they became popular family companions.
2. Size & Appearance: The standard APBT stands 18 to 22 inches in height at the withers and weighs between 30 to 45 pounds, while XL Pitbulls range in size from 23 to 30 inches tall, weighing up to 90 pounds or more depending on gender. These dogs have a stout yet athletic build with broad heads and powerful jaws making them one of the strongest canine breeds in existence! Their reasonably short coats come in a wide variety of colors including brindle, black & white, red & white and fawn & white plus many others.
3. Temperament: Contrarily to popular belief pit bulls are not inherently aggressive – they can actually become quite friendly when given ample socialization early on in life! In addition to being loyal companions, their strong work ethic makes them great at a number of dog sports such as agility courses and weight pulling events not just obedience classes.
4. Health Info: While APBT XLs generally live long healthy lives there are certain health matters that owners should be aware of such as hip dysplasia, luxating patella (floating kneecap), demodectic mange or allergies which all can occur if proper care is not taken with diet exercise routines etc.. Overall though these animals have few genetic disorders overall compared to other purebred lines due their rigorous breeding practices throughout history!
5. Trainability: Lastly like any good pup the APBT stands out when it comes time for training – they’re eager learners who respond positively with consistency; however if training isn’t done correctly then they can become prone to reactivity strains so its important patience is maintained throughout each session while recognizing negative behavior quickly without too much punishment.*
*punitive measures should always be avoided when working with an APBT as harsh methods could lead towards unwanted aggression issues down the line; make sure only positive reinforcement methods are implemented for optimal results during any training session involving this breed type!
