The board room was dimly lit, the air thick with tension. Data from the last quarter’s sales dashboard flickered on the screen, but critical variables—customer sentiment scores, competitor pricing volatility—were missing. Executive vice president Mark hesitantly pushed a button, triggering the AI-driven risk assessment tool.

*"Likelihood of success: 62% (confidence interval: 55–69%)."*

His gut told him to wait. The CEO’s voice cut through the static: *"Move forward. The board’s patience is wearing thin."* Mark’s fingers hovered over the approval key. He checked the backup report—only 27% of the team had signed off, but the CEO’s deadline loomed. The call was made.

Three months later, the numbers arrived: a 12% revenue bump, but the margin crunched by 3%. The unaccounted factors—supply chain disruptions, a sudden competitor price war—had tilted the odds. The board’s "62%" had been a one-sided bet, and the losses had been steep.

Now, the next report would need sharper data—or a sharper pivot.
