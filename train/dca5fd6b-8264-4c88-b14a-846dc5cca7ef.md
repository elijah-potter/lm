A dimly lit alleyway at dusk, the air thick with the scent of damp pavement and distant exhaust fumes. A lone figure—clad in a worn leather jacket—stands against a flickering neon sign, its letters dissolving into static. Their hands rest on a rusted metal box, its lid slightly ajar, revealing nothing. The streetlamp casts long shadows, and the only movement is the slow, rhythmic hum of a distant traffic light. A silence so heavy it presses against the bones. No one knows why they’re here, only that they’ve waited long enough that the world has moved on without them. The box feels heavy, the wait feels endless.
