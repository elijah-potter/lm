The last day of summer vacation, and twelve-year-old Mia Carter was staring at the ceiling, her notebook open beside her, pen hovering over a blank page. The heat outside felt like a living thing, pressing down on the windows like a thousand tiny hands.

"Mia?" her dad called from downstairs. "You still working on that essay?"

She didn’t look up. "Yeah, Dad. Just got stuck."

Outside, the neighborhood was alive with kids—some playing soccer, others running back from the pool, splashing water everywhere. But Mia was stuck on the same sentence: *"The night sky above the old mill was a canvas of stars that seemed to glow brighter than ever."*

That night, her dad told her about the old local legend—the *Mill Ghost*, a girl who vanished one summer evening when the mill’s doors had been sealed shut by a mysterious storm. The story said she’d been working late, helping fix the machinery, and that the next morning, the mill was empty, the doors locked, and the girl nowhere to be found.

Mia’s eyes widened. She’d always loved stories about ghosts, but this one felt different. It wasn’t just a tale—it was something that might *actually* happen.

That afternoon, she found an old key in her grandma’s attic. The label was faded: *"For when the storm comes."* She didn’t know what it meant, but curiosity made her grip it tighter.

The next evening, as Mia sat on the rooftop of the old mill (the one she’d always avoided because of the stories), she saw the storm brewing. Dark clouds rolled in, thunder rumbled like a warning, and the wind howled through the trees. The mill’s windows rattled violently.

Mia’s heart pounded. She pulled out the key and turned it in the lock. The mechanism clicked—no sound, no resistance. The door swung open.

Inside, the air was thick with dust and the faint scent of old wood and something metallic. The mill was dimly lit by a single, flickering bulb hanging from the ceiling. Shadows stretched long and wavy against the walls.

And then she saw *them*.

A group of kids, all dressed in tattered work clothes from the 1970s, stood frozen in the middle of the room. Their faces were blank, eyes wide, hands gripping tools they hadn’t used in decades. One of them—a girl with dark hair tied back—turned slowly, her eyes locking onto Mia. Her lips moved, but no sound came out.

Mia’s breath hitched. The girl reached out, and suddenly, the air around them *shimmered*. The kids dissolved into dust, their forms flickering like a bad TV signal. The girl stood there alone, her expression unreadable, before vanishing completely.

The door slammed shut behind her. Mia stumbled back, the key slipping from her fingers. The storm outside raged on, lightning illuminating the mill’s empty interior. She grabbed the key, shoving it into her pocket, and ran for the stairs.

At home, she told her parents everything. Her dad didn’t believe her—until he saw the key in her hand. He didn’t know what it meant, but he didn’t question it anymore.

That night, Mia dreamed of the girl with dark hair. She woke up sweating, her notebook still open, the same sentence staring back at her: *"The night sky above the old mill was a canvas of stars that seemed to glow brighter than ever."*

The next morning, the mill’s doors were sealed shut again. The key was gone.

And the legend of the Mill Ghost? It was just another story. Or maybe… it wasn’t.
