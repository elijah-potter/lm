Although few people decide to go into teaching to make their fortune, you might be surprised to read how little some newly qualified teachers make. The National Education Association calls for a $40,000 starting salary for all pre-K-12 teachers, and while some new teachers are beginning to start on $40,000-plus salaries, many are still lagging behind.
Graduates majoring in elementary teacher education started at a median salary of $35,800 in February 2012, according to the National Association of Colleges and Employers' Spring 2012 Salary Survey. Pre-elementary teacher education majors had to make do with a starting salary of just $32,400 at this time, while physical education teaching/coaching majors and special education majors took home $40,100 and $42,200 in their first year, respectively.
Specific academic/vocational teacher education majors working in educational services started at median salaries of $40,400 in February 2012, according to the NACE. Special education majors who managed to land a job in educational services could expect to earn a median salary of $45,900 at the same time. Elementary teacher education majors working in educational services had starting salaries of $38,500.
Pre-elementary teacher education majors working in health care and social assistance started their careers earning a median wage of just $23,700 in February 2012, NACE data reveals. Specific academic/vocational teacher education majors working in health care and social assistance would have had good reason to feel a little jealous of their classmates who pursued a career in educational services in February 2012. Specific academic/vocational teacher education majors working in health care and social assistance could expect to earn just $27,400 in their first full year of work.
The salary news does get better for more-experienced teachers. The overall median salary for kindergarten teachers was $49,520 in May 2011, data from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics shows. Secondary school teachers and post-secondary education teachers earned median salaries of $54,270 and $65,050. Post-secondary education teachers working in junior colleges were the best rewarded among their peers in May 2011, taking home a median salary of $67,080 before tax and other deductions.
