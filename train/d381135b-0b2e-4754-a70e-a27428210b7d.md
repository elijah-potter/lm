The hum of the crowded subway was a symphony of whispers and clacking heels, each sound a foreign tongue. She clutched her phone like a shield, her fingers tracing the screen too fast, the glow casting jagged shadows across her face. The others—those with their hands tucked into pockets, heads nodding along with some unseen rhythm—seemed to belong to a different world. She had been late, again, her usual spot by the window now crowded with strangers, their faces blurred by the dim glow of the digital billboards. A child’s laughter cut through the noise, sharp and unexpected, and she flinched. *Where do they come from?* The subway felt like a sieve, letting everyone else in while leaving her out, a ghost in the machine. She wanted to disappear into the crowd, but the way her reflection stared back at her from the window—eyes wide, mouth a thin line—told her she was still there, just not in the right place. The train slowed, the doors hissed open, and for a moment, she wondered if she’d ever find a place where she didn’t feel like an outsider.
