The clock on the wall chimed three times as I stepped into the café’s dim glow, the scent of espresso thick in the air. My fingers traced the rim of my cup, but the coffee inside was already cold—another borrowed moment. I had been here before, years ago, when I was younger, my name still a question mark in this place, my life a story half-told. Now, the same booth, the same barista’s hands moving over the espresso machine, the way the morning light filtered through the stained glass windows—all of it felt like a ghostly echo of someone else’s past. The wait felt longer than it should have. The conversation across the table was as familiar as the ache in my shoulders. The borrowed moment wasn’t in the time, but in the way the world here had been rewritten for me, spun into something I could hold and call my own. I leaned forward, but my voice sounded like it belonged to someone else, a version of me that had already lived this moment once before. The table was my only anchor, and even that felt like it belonged to someone else.
