A cavernous ship’s cargo bay, its walls lined with rusted bulkheads and abandoned life-support modules, humming faintly with residual energy. The air smells of ozone and old oil, thick with static electricity crackling between corroded wiring. The only movement is a slow, lazy drift of dust motes spiraling in the dim, flickering glow of broken maintenance lights. The silence is suffocating, broken only by the occasional *clank* of a loose rivet or distant *whirr* of a malfunctioning pump. Mirrors, cracked and smeared with grime, reflect distorted images of forgotten faces—crew members who never returned, their presence lingering like ghosts in the empty corridors. The weight of unseen bodies presses down, a physical force that makes every step feel like walking through quicksand. The space isn’t empty—it’s *crowded* with the ghosts of those who were here, and the weight of what they left behind.
