The fluorescent lights buzzed overhead, casting an eerie glow on the empty conference table. She sat alone, her fingers drumming against the edge of the notebook she’d brought, the pages filled with scribbled notes and half-finished ideas. The others had already begun their presentations, their voices overlapping in a cacophony of clichés and polished jargon. *Data-driven insights*, *innovative approaches*—words that sounded like they belonged in a corporate brochure, not the kind of raw, messy creativity she was working on.

She had come here to share something different. Something that felt fragile, something that didn’t quite fit into the neat boxes of their discussions. The presentation was titled *"The Unseen Threads"*—a metaphor for the invisible connections she’d observed in her research, ones that didn’t align with the conventional narratives they were all fixating on. Her stomach twisted as she watched the others nod along, their expressions polite but distant, like they were listening for the wrong things.

*"What’s the bigger picture here?"* she thought, the question echoing in her head like a warning. The moment she opened her mouth, the words spilled out—too personal, too unfiltered. A few heads turned. One person smirked. *"The human element is often the most overlooked."* The words hung in the air, unanswered, unclaimed. The room felt suddenly too large, too cold. She had expected applause. Instead, she got a silence so heavy it felt like it could crush her.

Outside the window, rain began to fall, a steady rhythm that seemed to match the pulse of her own disorientation. She reached for her phone, fingers trembling as she scrolled through the messages she’d sent earlier, all half-hearted attempts to explain herself. But what was there to say? The words had already been lost in translation. The moment had been stolen by the silence, by the weight of the unspoken. She closed her notebook with a quiet *clack*, the sound swallowed by the hum of the office.

The rain stopped. The lights flickered. And for the first time in years, she felt like she was the one who didn’t belong—not in this room, not in this moment, not in any of the spaces she’d carved out for herself. She leaned back in her chair, her throat tight, and wondered if she’d ever find a place where her voice didn’t feel like a foreign tongue.
