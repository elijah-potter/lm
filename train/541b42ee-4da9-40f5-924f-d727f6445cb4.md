**"The Last Light"**

The city hummed under a sky too bright for the season—early autumn, and the air smelled of damp earth and stale coffee. Eli sat on the train’s cold metal bench, his fingers tracing the cracked edge of his phone screen. The notification flickered: *"Reminder: Call Mom before she flies to Boston. She’s already booked the flight."*

He hesitated. The last time he’d called, she’d scolded him for not coming home sooner. *"You’re twenty-four, not a teenager,"* she’d said, voice tight. *"Stay safe. I’ll miss you."*

He exhaled. The reminder was still there, untouched, like a ghost in the corner of his screen. His thumb hovered over the call icon. What if she’d changed her number? What if she was already on the plane?

The train lurching forward pulled him from the thought. Outside, the neon signs blurred into shapes—*Starbucks, Uber Eats, the hospital down the street*. His phone buzzed again. Another reminder: *"Meeting at 3 PM. Bring documents."*

He sighed. The city was alive around him, indifferent. His own life, though, felt like a half-finished sketch—lines that needed sharpening, a corner that needed cutting. The reminders were just that: reminders. But the real question was whether he’d remember to *act* on them.

The train doors hissed open. He stepped off, into the quiet before the rush. Somewhere, a siren wailed. Somewhere, a mother waited. Somewhere, a future was waiting to be shaped.
