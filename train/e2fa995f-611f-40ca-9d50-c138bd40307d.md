A flickering neon sign casts a warm glow over a cramped, shared workspace in a repurposed shipping container. The walls, lined with cardboard boxes and temporary plywood partitions, bear the remnants of past tenants—scrawled notes, half-finished projects, and a mix of discarded materials. The air smells of stale coffee, burnt circuit boards, and the faint metallic tang of humidity.

Outside, the pavement is lined with discarded furniture and makeshift art installations, some still under construction, others abandoned after a weekend of experimentation. A chain-link fence, barely visible through the overgrown vines, marks the boundary of a zone that shifts daily. The ground beneath is a patchwork of tarps, cardboard, and the occasional concrete slab that hasn’t been removed yet.

The hum of a portable generator mixes with the distant wail of sirens, the occasional laugh of strangers passing through, or the murmur of a crowd pushing against the rusted gate. A single, cracked mirror reflects a different version of the same scene—another day in a place that never quite stays the same.
