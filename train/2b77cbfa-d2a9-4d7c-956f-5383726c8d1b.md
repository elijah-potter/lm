The scent of damp earth and rain-soaked pavement lingers, mingling with the faint musk of old wood and rusted metal. The cobblestones underfoot feel softer than before, worn smooth by decades of footsteps. The streetlamps flicker with a dim, nostalgic glow, their light casting long shadows that stretch toward the crumbling archway of the bridge—once grand, now cracked but still standing. A familiar café’s windows shimmer with condensation, its faded sign barely legible, but the scent of fresh coffee and cinnamon still drifts out, a ghost of the past.

Across the square, the park’s benches are crowded with people, their laughter and chatter blending with the hum of the city. The bench where I used to sit now has a new occupant, but the view remains the same—across the river, the bridge’s reflection dances in the water, a silent reminder of the time that has passed. The scent of blooming jasmine from the nearby garden, mixed with the distant sound of a child’s laughter, fills the air.
