A quiet suburban street, lined with flickering streetlights casting long shadows on cracked pavement. A lone figure walks past a half-empty diner, humming an old tune, their coat sleeves rolled up. Outside the window, a clock ticks past midnight, but the time is frozen on a broken digital display at 23:59. A child’s toy car rolls slowly down the sidewalk, spinning a single, defunct wheel. The neighbor’s garden gnome stands motionless, watching the world with one eye—its other missing, replaced by a polished silver teardrop. The air smells faintly of rain that never arrives, and the birds in the trees above chirp in perfect, unnatural harmony.
