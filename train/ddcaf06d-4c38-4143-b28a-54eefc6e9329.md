In the hollow of the old oak, where moths curled in damp silk and the wind carried whispers of the sea, a child knelt.

The star above her was a question mark, a single tear in the sky. She reached, fingers trembling, and plucked it. It shattered like glass, but she caught the shards—each one a memory of her mother’s laughter, the salt taste of her first stolen kiss, the way her father’s voice softened when he spoke of storms.

She pressed the pieces into her palm and pressed her palm to the oak’s bark, whispering, *"If you listen, tell me what I’m made of."*

The tree groaned, and from its fissures, ink trickled—black as the midnight before dawn. The child traced the words, which formed in her mind’s eye:

*"You are the ghost of a name I never knew,*
*a storm that left no trace but the wind’s cry.*
*You are the weight of a world I can’t hold,*
*a question left unasked, a love untold."*

She sat back, hands empty now, and the last shard of her star fell into the earth, dissolving into the soil. The oak’s leaves rustled, as if laughing, and the child smiled. The world, for once, was quiet.
