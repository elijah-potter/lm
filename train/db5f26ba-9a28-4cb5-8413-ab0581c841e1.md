The lawns are meticulously trimmed, but the grass near the gate is always patchy, as if something had been dug up recently. A parked car’s taillight flickers intermittently, casting eerie reflections in the cracked pavement. A mailbox labeled *"HOUSE NO. 42"* sits empty, yet the postmark on its last envelope shows a date from three years ago. A neighbor’s dog barks at nothing, its ears twitching toward the gate, while its collar—worn smooth by years of wear—has a single, fresh tear. The streetlights hum a low, rhythmic pulse, like a heartbeat, and the air smells faintly of ozone and something metallic, like old blood.
