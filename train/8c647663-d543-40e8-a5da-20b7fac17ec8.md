Lost in the labyrinth of shifting paths, the first step led to a village where the river’s bend had vanished overnight—now flowing through an archway of living stone. The map in my pocket was stitched from the wings of a moth, its ink bleeding into the wind. The compass spun wildly, its needle dissolving into the sky, revealing only a constellation of doors that pulsed with the breath of forgotten gods. A handprint on the wall—one I recognized—guided me through a door that wasn’t there yesterday. The sky was a map of stars I’d never seen before, each one a promise of a place that didn’t exist in the atlas. The last thing I heard was the echo of my own voice whispering, *"There’s always another turn,"*—then the world dissolved into static.
