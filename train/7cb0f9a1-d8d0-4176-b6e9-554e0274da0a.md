The air in the dimly lit room was thick with silence, the only sound the faint hum of an old fluorescent bulb flickering overhead. A single, worn armchair sat against the wall, its fabric stained with time and neglect. On the chair’s armrest, a half-empty coffee cup glinted with condensation, its contents long gone cold. The person inside had stopped moving, their posture rigid—back straight, fingers curled into a loose fist, knuckles white.

Outside, a distant train whistle cut through the quiet, though it wasn’t the noise that had drawn them here. They had arrived an hour ago, but the station had felt empty, the usual rush of commuters replaced by a quiet stillness. A sign above the doors read **"Next train: 07:34 (to Central Station)"**, but the time was irrelevant. The waiting was the thing itself—endless, unmarked by clocks or hands.

Across the platform, a woman in a raincoat adjusted her scarf, her breath visible in the cold. She had been there longer than the stranger, but she had gone to bed at midnight, assuming it would be over by dawn. The stranger had no such assumptions. They had woken to this—this waiting, this nothing. The train’s whistle was a distant promise, but the promise itself was empty.

The clock on the wall, a cheap, digital thing, ticked steadily, its numbers shifting without meaning. Somewhere, a car engine roared to life. The stranger watched it, but their gaze didn’t linger. They had been waiting for something that no one could name—not a person, not a job, not even a reason. Just the weight of time itself, unbroken.
