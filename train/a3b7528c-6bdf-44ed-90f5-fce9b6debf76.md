**The Last Light**

The clock struck twelve, and the neon glow of the alley pulsed like a dying heartbeat. Inside the dimly lit café, a single candle flickered on the table, casting long shadows across the faces of the patrons. The air smelled of stale coffee and something metallic—old blood, perhaps, or the faint tang of regret.

Across from her, Elias leaned forward, his fingers tracing the rim of his whiskey glass. "You always were a good listener," he murmured, though the words were half-hearted. His gaze flickered to the window, where the city lights blurred into streaks of color—red, gold, the ghost of a forgotten past.

Then, the door creaked open. A gust of cold wind swept in, carrying with it the scent of rain. No one moved. The candle’s flame dipped lower.

Outside, a streetlamp flickered to life, its beam cutting through the twilight like a blade. A single, solitary drop of rain fell from the lamppost—just one, slow, deliberate. It splashed against the pavement, and for a heartbeat, the world held its breath.

The last detail was the way Elias’s shadow stretched long and thin against the wall, just as the rain began to fall.
