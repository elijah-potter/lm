The sharp crack of a chair scraping against concrete echoed through the dimly lit diner as Jake slammed his coffee cup onto the table, spilling hot liquid across the worn wood. Across from him, his wife, Claire, stood with her arms crossed, her jaw set in a stubborn line.

"You’re taking this job without even asking!" she snapped, her voice rising.

Jake’s face flushed. "I *had* to. The rent’s due, and we’ve got a kid to feed."

Claire’s eyes narrowed. "And you think *I* don’t know that? You’ve been stressing over this for weeks!"

A heavy silence stretched between them. Outside, the rain tapped against the window, but neither noticed. The tension coiled tighter, the air thick with unspoken words.

Then—

"Fine," Jake muttered, rubbing his temples. "But we’re *not* arguing about it again tomorrow."

Claire exhaled sharply, her shoulders relaxing just a fraction. The bell above the door chimed as the waitress entered, refilling their coffee. Neither looked away from each other.
