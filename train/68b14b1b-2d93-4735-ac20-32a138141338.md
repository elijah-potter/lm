The Grand Clock of Eldermere stood at the heart of the city’s decaying cathedral, its face etched with blackened brass gears that hummed with a sound like distant thunder. Every hour, its pendulum swung, marking not time but a cycle of forgotten warnings.

On the morning of its 737th hour, a thief named Veyra slipped past the guards, her gloves damp with rain. She had stolen the clock’s heart—a single brass wheel, the core that kept time itself. The city’s people had long since stopped listening to its chimes; they only remembered the stories of those who dared to touch its mechanism.

As Veyra carried the wheel to her hideout beneath the docks, the clock’s hands began to move backward, rewriting the seconds as if she were altering fate itself. By the time she reached her lair, the hourglass beside her had spilled its sand into the shape of her name—*Veyra*—as if the clock had read her mind.

The next morning, the city woke to find the streets unmarked by time. The clock’s hands had frozen, but the gears inside had begun to spin again, this time in reverse. The sun rose early, and shadows stretched long before dawn. By the time the old guard returned, the city’s citizens were already gathering in the plaza, whispering of a clock that had swallowed time and spit out a thief’s curse.

Now, every hour, the clock ticks backward, and those who listen too closely find their own clocks running in reverse. The city’s inhabitants have learned to ignore it—or fear it. The thief, Veyra, now walks with her hands reversed, her shadow moving in reverse motion. Some say the clock will stop forever. Others say it will keep going, forever undoing what was done.
