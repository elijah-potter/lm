A forgotten festival called **"Veles' Lanterns"** celebrated the return of winter solstice in an ancient agricultural village. Originally, it honored the god **Veles** (or a local spirit of fertility and earth), whose role was to ensure the soil remained fertile for next year’s harvest. Villagers lit bonfires and lanterns to guide the spirit’s journey through the underworld, believing it would prevent drought and ensure plentiful crops.

Over time, the meaning faded, replaced by stories of **"Light of the Wild"**—a communal vigil where families shared food, sang, and traded stories while watching the stars. The festival became a mix of pagan tradition and folklore, later absorbed into regional customs with only vague references to "ancient spirits" or "the first harvest’s blessing."

Now, remnants of its original purpose are lost, though some elders whisper that the lanterns once carried offerings to unseen watchers beneath the earth.
