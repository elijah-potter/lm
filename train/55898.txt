Prospect School fifth graders recently had the opportunity to learn Civil War history through a unique hands-on experience. Teachers had arranged for a visit from the "Civil War Traveling Trunk." The "trunk" is a container of items that is shipped from school to school and includes reproduction artifacts, books, music, pictures, and other items that help students understand not only the war itself, but also the era that citizens were living in at the time. Prospect teachers divided students into small groups to rotate through stations where they could explore products like an 1800's toothbrush, playing cards, maps, a fife, posters and political cartoons, and confederate currency." Using iPads, students recorded their responses to teacher prompts, which encouraged students to consider the purpose of each item and what life was like for U.S. citizens during the war. Teacher Bukurije Akiti notes, "We were very fortunate to receive the trunk because it allowed the students to travel back in time and immerse themselves in the content." After the activity, students had the opportunity to reflect on the trunk experience. "I enjoyed it because it was hands-on and we got to learn things that we couldn't learn by just reading online or in a textbook," said fifth grader Annie Beargie. She went on to note that she was surprised to read in an etiquette book from the era that women weren't allowed to let their dresses drag on the floor.
