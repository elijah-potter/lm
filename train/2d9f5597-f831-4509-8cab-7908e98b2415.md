**Quantum Dot Dispersion Field (QDDF) Lab, MIT**
A semi-transparent 20-foot cube of **superfluid helium-4** at near absolute zero, embedded with nanoscale **quantum dots** (CdSe/CdS) in a photonic crystal lattice. Laser pulses (100 fs, 800 nm) excite electron-hole pairs, causing **spontaneous coherent photon emission** via Förster resonance energy transfer (FRET). Observed phenomena:

1. **Non-classical light propagation**: Wavepackets propagate as **quasiparticles** (excitons) in a **nonlinear Schrödinger equation** regime, exhibiting **ballistic coherence** despite thermal fluctuations.
2. **Quantum Hall effect for light**: In a magnetic field, **Bloch bands** form, enabling **topological photonics**—deflection without scattering via edge states.
3. **Entangled photon pairs**: Via **spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC)**, pairs emit into **correlated polarization states** (Bell-state analysis confirms violation of CHSH inequality).
4. **Temporal solitons**: High-power pulses (10^13 W/cm²) generate **dark solitons** in the exciton-polariton continuum, self-trapping via **nonlinear refraction** (χ^(3) ≈ 10^5 cm³/s).
5. **Metamaterial cloaking**: A **negative-index metamaterial** (plasmonic nanoparticles in a dielectric matrix) bends light around a **focusing lens**, creating a **Huygens-inverse cloak** for optical beams.

**Key metrics for measurement**:
- **Coherence length (L_c)**: > 10 µm at 4 K (vs. ~λ for classical light).
- **Photon emission rate (R)**: 10^12 s⁻¹ per dot (vs. ~10^18 for spontaneous emission).
- **Nonlinear index (n₂)**: 10⁻⁴ cm/J (vs. 10⁻⁴⁵ cm/J for vacuum).
- **Quantum efficiency (η)**: 80% for FRET, 99% for SPDC.

**Downstream applications**: Quantum internet, high-speed optical computing, ultra-low-loss waveguides.
