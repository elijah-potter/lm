**The Whisper of Static**

The rain fell in silence, a slow, deliberate pulse against the windows. Inside, the hum of the old television filled the air like a ghost’s breath, half-listening to the news reports about the city’s forgotten corners. The curtains were drawn tight, but the light still seeped through cracks like a confession.

I had come here to disappear. Not just vanish into the crowd, but to slip into the cracks of existence itself—where the stories didn’t care about names or dates. The café smelled of stale coffee and something metallic, like old blood on a rusted blade. The barista didn’t look up as I slid into the booth, his fingers tracing the rim of a mug with the same precision as a surgeon’s scalpel.

*"Another one?"* he asked, voice smooth as polished glass.

I didn’t answer. The weight of the past pressed against my ribs, a leaden shadow that couldn’t be shaken. The city’s heartbeat was a rhythm I’d learned to ignore.

**The Hollow**

The elevator doors hissed shut with a sound like a dying animal. Below, the streets were alive—laughter, the clatter of shoes on pavement, the distant wail of a siren. But up here, in this sterile glass box, the air was thick with the weight of things unsaid.

I turned to her. She was different now. Her hands trembled, not from fear, but from something sharper—exhaustion. The mask she wore was too tight, the smile too wide, like a clown pretending to be kind.

*"You know what I did,"* she whispered.

I should have lied. I should have walked away. But the past had a way of clawing its way out when no one was looking.

*"I remember,"* I said, my voice rough. *"But you’re the one who’s still running."*

The elevator doors opened with a groan. The world outside was louder, brighter, *alive*—but inside my head, the silence was drowning me.
