A neon-lit alleyway in a city that pulses with shifting skylines. Raindrops blur the glass of flickering storefront signs, reflecting distorted images of passing crowds. Streetlights flicker intermittently, casting long shadows that dissolve into the haze of exhaust and distant sirens. A broken bench, its metal rusted through, sits unoccupied under a plastic sheet draped over it, half-eaten sandwich wrappers scattered beneath. The pavement is littered with discarded fast-food packaging and half-finished sketches from street artists. A few discarded clothes and bags of trash hint at a temporary settlement, perhaps a nomadic community or a displaced group. The air smells like ozone and old paint, the scent of something that has been here long enough to feel forgotten, yet still feels alive with the energy of fleeting moments.
