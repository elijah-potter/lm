**Narrator:** *"Once upon a time, in a small village nestled between rolling hills and a misty river, there lived a storyteller named Elias. One evening, as the sun dipped below the trees, Elias gathered the villagers around the crackling fire, his fingers tracing the edges of a worn leather-bound book. He flipped it open and began to speak—"

Elias’s voice was low, rich with the weight of many tales. *"Long ago, in a kingdom where the stars themselves whispered secrets to mortals, there was a princess named Liora. She was known for her kindness, her eyes as clear as the first light of dawn, but she had a secret: beneath her heart, a small, unmarked stone hung—a relic of her mother’s faith, said to hold the power of forgotten dreams. One day, while exploring the ancient library, Liora found a locked chest beneath the shelves. Inside, she discovered a map drawn in crimson ink, leading to a place called the Hollow Between Worlds. The villagers whispered that it was a gateway—one that allowed travelers to step into stories they’d never imagined."*

The villagers leaned forward as Elias paused, his breath visible in the cold night air. *"The map led to a village of storytellers, where each tale spun a thread that could weave reality itself. But there was a catch: only those who could hear the stories as if they were already true could pass through. Liora followed the map, her heart pounding with fear and wonder. As she stood at the edge of the Hollow, she heard a voice—soft, like the rustling of leaves—calling her name. A figure emerged from the mist: a woman with eyes like polished silver, her cloak woven from the threads of countless narratives. ‘You seek the power of stories,’ the woman said, ‘but stories are not given—they are earned. The Hollow Between Worlds is a place where the past and future intertwine. To enter, you must tell one of your own.’ Liora hesitated, her hands trembling. ‘I… I don’t know if I can do that,’ she whispered. The woman smiled. ‘Then you must be ready to listen.’"*

*"The Hollow Between Worlds was a swirling vortex of colors and sounds—some familiar, some foreign. Liora found herself in a room where every object told a story. A chair whispered of a king who sat there for a hundred years, a mirror reflected not her own face but a stranger’s, and a clock ticked backward. A child stood before her, no older than ten, with eyes that held the weight of a thousand regrets. ‘You came for the stone,’ the child said, ‘but the Hollow does not give gifts—it demands sacrifices.’ Liora realized the truth: the stone was already hers; she just hadn’t realized it yet. ‘I want to go home,’ she said. The woman nodded. ‘But first, you must choose. Will you keep the power of stories, or let it vanish like smoke?’ Liora closed her eyes. She thought of the villagers back home, of the stories they told each other by the fire, of the way their laughter filled the dark. She reached into her pocket and pulled out a single silver locket—a gift from her mother. Inside was a portrait of her, smiling, holding a book. ‘I will keep the stories,’ she whispered. The Hollow Between Worlds dissolved into light, and Liora stood once more in the village square, her heart full, her eyes alight with something new."*

*"The villagers cheered as Elias’s book fell silent. ‘And so,’ he concluded, ‘the princess who found the power of stories became a keeper of tales herself. From that day on, whenever she spoke, the world listened not just with ears, but with hearts.’"*
