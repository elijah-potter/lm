The first routine was a structured morning regimen after the initial chaos of new independence. It began at dawn, just before the city woke, with a cup of tea steeped in the old cast-iron kettle—its sound a familiar rhythm. The routine followed a rigid order: wake, stretch, check the calendar for the day’s tasks, then breakfast in the kitchen where the morning light spilled through the uncurtained window, painting the walls in a soft golden hue.

By mid-morning, the first task was addressed—an email reply to the office, a reminder of the cycle of productivity. Lunch was simple: leftover grilled chicken from yesterday, wrapped in parchment paper, paired with a side of roasted sweet potatoes. Afterward, a walk through the neighborhood, observing the same streetlamps and the same parked cars, a habit that felt both ordinary and deeply personal.

The evening returned to the routine, winding down with a book under the dimming light, the scent of lavender from the night-blooming jasmine clinging to the air. By bedtime, the mind was quiet, the body relaxed, and the first night of consistency had settled in—no longer a question, but a foundation.
