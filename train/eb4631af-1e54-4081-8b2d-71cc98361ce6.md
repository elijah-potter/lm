Cliffs of jagged black basalt rise from the misty sea, their jagged teeth scraping against the wind. The air hums with the scent of salt and damp earth, where the last remnants of sunlight paint streaks of gold across the jagged surfaces before vanishing into the swirling clouds. Below, the ocean churns like a restless beast, its waves crashing against the base of the cliffs, leaving behind a trail of foam and spray. A lone stone path winds along the edge, carved through the rocks by hands long forgotten. The horizon stretches into the distance, a faint line where the earth meets the sky, but it feels too far, too distant—like the edge of a world that might not exist if you step too far.

A watchtower, half-collapsed, stands sentinel at the precipice, its broken windows staring blankly into the void. Inside, the air is thick with dust, and the floorboards groan underfoot. A single, flickering lantern casts long shadows across the walls, illuminating the faint, half-buried marks of something carved into the stone—words that might mean nothing to you, or perhaps a warning. The wind howls through the gaps in the walls, carrying whispers that seem to repeat the same phrase: *"Do not look too far."*

At the center of the ruins, a small, weathered sign hangs from a rusted hinge, its paint long faded. The inscription reads:
*"Here, the world ends. Or begins."*
