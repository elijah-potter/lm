The air is thick with the scent of damp earth and rusted iron, lingering like a forgotten sigh. Buildings loom like skeletal fingers, their windows dull and lifeless, some cracked or boarded up, as if waiting to collapse. Streets are narrow, overgrown with vines, their surfaces slick with recent rain that hasn’t had time to dry. Shadows stretch long and unnervingly still, pooling where the light barely touches, as if the world itself is holding its breath, too afraid to move. The wind whispers faintly, barely audible, like a breath held too long. The silence is deafening, punctuated only by distant, muffled sounds— distant machinery, the occasional splash of water, or the creak of something heavy shifting. The sky is a bruised, overcast canvas, its edges blurred, as if it’s holding its breath too, waiting for the storm to pass. The horizon is a hazy, indistinct blur, the land itself appearing to ripple slightly, as though suspended in time.
