Bed bugs are a common household pest whose presence can lead to physical and mental distress. These unwanted bugs can leave marks on your sheets and skin that can be annoying and sometimes harmful. To combat these bugs effectively, you must understand why they appear and how to prevent them from infesting your home. Here is everything you need to know about these insects:
What Are Bed Bugs?
Bed bugs are small, reddish-brown parasitic insects that feed on the blood of humans and other warm-blooded animals. They are nocturnal, and their bites are painless but can cause itchy welts on the skin, much like mosquito bites. While they are not known to transmit diseases to humans, bed bugs can be difficult to eliminate after infesting homes.
These insects are known for their ability to hide in small crevices and tight spaces. They can also travel long distances in luggage and clothing, making them difficult to detect and control.
Different Prevention Ways for Bed Bugs
Here are four ways to prevent home infestation:
1. Vacuum Regularly
Vacuuming is an effective and relatively inexpensive way to prevent insect infestation. To get the best results, make sure that you vacuum regularly and thoroughly. Start by vacuuming your floor and all seams, crevices, and folds in your mattress, including along the edges of your box spring. You should use a high-powered vacuum to remove any dirt or dust particles that may be harboring bug eggs or larvae.
Check the common points of entry, such as door frames and window sills, since these are common hiding spots for bed bugs. Empty the contents of your vacuum cleaner bags soon after cleaning.
2. Cover Your Mattress
Mattress covers do not kill existing bed bugs, but they can block access and deter them from using the mattress as a nest. When selecting a protective cover, look for one with densely woven fabric and tightly sealed seams for extra protection. An effective encasement should provide protection against existing insects and create a barrier that prevents new ones from being able to gain access inside by blocking their entry points.
3. Reduce Clutter
One of the best ways to reduce the risk of insect infestation is to keep your home clutter-free. Clutter provides the bugs with hiding spots, making it more difficult for you to identify and treat an infestation if one occurs.
To reduce clutter in your home, regularly clean and organize your living spaces. Clear out unnecessary items, such as old boxes that could be harboring bugs. Do your best to keep clothing, bedding, and other items off the floor as well. Avoid storing items under or next to your bed if you know you have an infestation.
4. Inspect Secondhand Furniture and Clothing
When buying secondhand furniture and clothing, it’s a good practice to thoroughly inspect them for bed bugs. Pay special attention to the seams and tags of the items, as these are common places where insects hide. Items that have been stored in a garage or basement for long periods of time should also be inspected carefully before bringing them into your home.
Choosing the Right Extermination Method
Extermination methods for bed bugs vary depending on the severity of the infestation and the size of the area affected. Prevention strategies like those above are helpful in stopping the spread of bedbugs. This will help limit the infestation and prevent future infestations after extermination.
One effective method for extermination is using chemical treatments such as insecticides or other pesticides. To achieve the best results, these pesticides should be applied by a professional exterminator. These experts know how to safely and effectively eliminate household pests. You may be able to help prepare your home for extermination by isolating your bed and infested items from non-infested areas.
