The flickering fluorescent light in the dimly lit room cast long shadows across the worn armchair where Daniel sat. His fingers traced the rim of the steaming mug, the scent of stale coffee clinging to his breath. The news was supposed to come at 8 PM sharp. His phone buzzed—unexpected text from his sister: *"Just got off call, they’re waiting for you."*

He exhaled slowly, then leaned forward, the chair creaking. The TV’s static hummed as he scrolled through the news alerts, but none of them were his. His phone lit up again—another message: *"They’re in the lobby. Meet me outside."*

Daniel stood abruptly, the chair collapsing behind him. The hallway beyond the door was quiet, the distant hum of the city’s pulse a distant whisper. He stepped out into the cool night air, the scent of rain on pavement mixing with the faint metallic tang of his nervous system. The news vans were parked along the curb, their lights blinking like dying stars.

He approached the cluster of reporters, his breath visible in the chill. One of them—tall, in a dark coat—raised a hand. *"Daniel?"* He nodded, relief flooding him like a sudden wave. The reporter handed him a mic, the sound of the crowd thinning as the crowd shifted. The first words were muffled, then clearer: *"Ladies and gentlemen, we have a major development."*

The crowd erupted. He held up his hand, the mic pressed against his lips. The words spilled out in a rush, but only fragments stuck: *"breaking news," "confirmed," "collapsed."* The reporter’s voice cut through the chaos, but Daniel barely heard it. His phone buzzed again—another message: *"They’re on their way. Don’t wait."*
