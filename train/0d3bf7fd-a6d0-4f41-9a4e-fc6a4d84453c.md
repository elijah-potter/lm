The empty coat rack creaked under the light rain, its usual collection of winter jackets and scarves reduced to a single, damp wool sweater hanging limply. Clara stepped closer, her fingers tracing the frayed collar of the sweater she had worn last winter—until it vanished over a month ago. The hall clock chimed the quarter hour, and she frowned at the second hand spinning toward six. The kitchen cupboard had been searched, every drawer, every shelf. The lost sweater was no ordinary item; it was a gift from her grandmother, embroidered with the family crest.

She turned to the living room, where the armchair’s cushion had been shifted aside, revealing a half-eaten chocolate cake cooling on the coffee table. The note was tucked under the plate: *"I’m fine. See you soon."* Her phone buzzed with a text from her coworker: *"You know you’re not supposed to eat that whole cake, right?"* She ignored it, the ache in her chest sharper than the cold seeping through the floorboards. Outside, the wind howled like a ghost calling her name.
