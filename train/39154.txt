By growing your own spinach (Spinacia oleracea) and harvesting its seeds, you can inexpensively provide your family with the green each summer and fall. Fresh spinach leaves from the garden enhance salads, sandwiches and other culinary dishes. This cool-season vegetable is grows quickly and easily from seed with proper care. It is best to grow only one variety of spinach to keep the resulting seed true to its parent plants. Spinach is hardy in U.S. Department of Agriculture plant hardiness zones 3 through 10.
Allow growing spinach plants to go to seed. Cut the seed stalks from the plant with a knife when they are brown and completely dry. Shake the seed heads over top a large bowl to dislodge the seeds. Separate the seeds from the chaff and other plant debris. Place the seeds into a small paper bag. Fold the top of the paper bag down three or four times, and then place the paper bag into a glass jar. Screw the lid tightly onto the glass jar. Store the jar in a dark, cool and dry room until planting time.
Plant spinach seeds four to six weeks prior to the last frost date in spring or four to six weeks prior to the first frost date in fall when daytime temperatures reach 40 degrees Fahrenheit. Choose an area that receives full sunlight and contains fast-draining, loamy soil. Spread a 2- to 4-inch layer of compost over the planting site using a rake. Mix the compost into the top 12 inches of soil with a rototiller or garden fork. Rake the soil surface smooth.
Dig a 1/2- to 3/4-inch-deep trench using a trowel. Space multiple trenches 12 to 18 inches apart. Sow the spinach seeds along the bottom of the trench, spacing each seed 1 inch apart. Backfill the trench with soil, tamping it down gently over the seeds. Water the planted seeds using a garden hose.
Water the spinach seeds when the top 1/4- to 1/2-inch layer of soil starts to dry. Keep the soil evenly moist but never soggy. Apply the water slowly to avoid washing away the soil covering the seeds. Watch for emerging sprouts 10 days after planting.
Reduce the frequency of watering once all spinach seeds have germinated and produced at least four true leaves. Then, water the spinach when the top 2 to 3 inches of soil becomes dry, moistening the soil to a depth of 6 to 8 inches. Apply the water during morning hours to allow the foliage time to dry before nightfall.
Thin the spinach seedlings to 2 inches apart once they reach 1 to 2 inches in height. Pull any weeds growing around or in the rows of plants. Spread a 1-inch layer of mulch around the spinach plants to slow moisture loss from the soil and prevent weed growth.
Fertilize the spinach plants when they reach 2 inches in height. Apply ammonium sulfate a rate of 2 tablespoons per linear foot of row. Dig a 2- to 3-inch-deep trench along the side of the spinach row, spacing the trench 3 inches away from the plants. Spread the fertilizer evenly along the bottom of the trench. Backfill the trench with soil. Water the area to a depth of 6 inches to activate the fertilizer. Repeat applications every two weeks only when the spinach leaves are light green.
Set snail traps around the spinach rows to protect the leafy greens from these chewing pests. Wash dust and grime off the plants' foliage periodically to avoid attracting harmful insects. Spray neem oil or insecticidal soap onto spinach leaves infested with mites or aphids.
Things You Will Need
- Large bowl
- Paper bag
- Glass jar
- Rototiller or garden fork
- Garden hose
- Ammonium sulfate
- Snail traps
- Neem oil or insecticidal soap
- Plant new rows of spinach every two weeks in spring or fall to produce a longer supply of fresh leafy greens.
- Collect new seed every two years, because spinach seeds typically remain viable for only two growing seasons.
