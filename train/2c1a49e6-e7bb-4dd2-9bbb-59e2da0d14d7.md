I remember the way the light spilled through the curtains of that old house,
how my mother’s voice hummed like a lullaby while she stirred the pot on the stove,
the scent of cinnamon and something sweet she called *"honey cake"* that still lingers in my memory.

I remember the way the river’s reflection stretched long and silver across the pond,
how the willows bent like old men in the breeze, their branches whispering secrets
to the children who splashed in the shallows before the summer faded.

I remember the taste of the first apple I ever bit, still warm from the tree’s embrace,
the way the sun kissed the horizon at dusk, painting the sky in shades of gold and crimson,
the way my father’s hands, rough and warm, guided mine as I learned to ride a bike.

I remember the way the stars seemed to dance just for me that night in the backyard,
how the fireflies blinked like tiny, flickering lanterns against the dark,
how the world felt small and full of wonder, and everything was possible.

I remember the weight of my own heart beating fast when I told her I loved her,
how the words tasted like salt on my tongue, how the room seemed to hold its breath,
how the world seemed to tilt just a little, just for that moment.

I remember the way the wind carried the scent of rain and earth,
how the past was a map I traced with my fingers, how the future was a door I couldn’t yet open,
how the present was the only thing I had left to hold onto.

I remember the quiet between the questions, the unspoken things that lingered
like echoes in a room we never fully left.
I think sometimes the past isn’t just what happened—it’s what stays.
