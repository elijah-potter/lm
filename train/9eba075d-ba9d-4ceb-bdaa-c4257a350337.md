Silent Market Square, a dimly lit plaza in an ancient city.
Rows of wooden stalls stretch endlessly, each offering artisanal goods—silk robes folded neatly, hand-carved jade figurines, and lanterns with intricate designs. The air hums with the soft chatter of merchants and patrons, but conversation is forbidden. Instead, the crowd engages in silent trades, passing objects or money without words. Some sit cross-legged on the cobblestones, sketching or playing instruments, while others watch in silent contemplation. The only sounds are the rustle of fabrics, the occasional clink of coins, and the distant murmur of a calliope playing a slow, melancholic tune. The atmosphere is one of quiet intensity, where every glance and gesture carries meaning without speech.
