Waiting can be a quiet rebellion against haste, a deliberate act of resistance in a world that glorifies speed. It’s often not the loudest choice—no grand gestures, no dramatic declarations—but the quiet endurance of patience. In moments like these, one might sit in a café, fingers tracing the rim of an empty coffee cup, watching the steam curl into the air like a slow, deliberate breath.

Waiting is the space between *could have* and *will be*. It’s the pause after a breath before the next one begins, the hesitation before a decision is made. Somewhere between the moment you decide to wait and the moment you decide to act, the world often rearranges itself. The right person might arrive. The right opportunity might present itself. Or simply, the right time might pass—one that feels less like waiting and more like preparation.

A person who waits doesn’t always know why. They might be waiting for someone to realize they’re there. Waiting for a letter to arrive, a phone call to ring, a word to be said. Sometimes it’s not about the thing itself but the space it creates—a quiet room where the noise of the world can fade. Waiting can be a form of courage, even when no one is watching. It’s the act of saying, *"I’ll be here."* And sometimes, that’s enough.
