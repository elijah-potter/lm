## A Counseling Session

**Setting:** A comfortable, softly lit therapy office. Sarah, the client, sits on a plush armchair facing Dr. Eleanor Vance, the therapist.

**Sarah:** (Hesitantly) It feels… overwhelming. Just everything. Work, family, trying to maintain some semblance of a social life. It’s just a constant pull, and I feel like I'm failing at all of it.

**Dr. Vance:** (Nodding gently, maintaining eye contact) That sounds incredibly draining, Sarah. It takes a great deal of strength to acknowledge feeling overwhelmed, and I appreciate you sharing that with me. Can you tell me a little more about what aspects feel particularly challenging right now?

**Sarah:** Well, at work, there’s this new project with a really tight deadline. I want to do a good job, I really do, but I'm constantly worried about making mistakes. And then I come home, and my mother’s been calling more frequently, needing help with things. I feel guilty saying no, but it’s just adding to the pressure.

**Dr. Vance:** It sounds like you're carrying a significant burden, both professionally and personally. The desire to perform well at work and the sense of obligation towards your mother are understandable, but they seem to be contributing to this feeling of being overwhelmed. Let's unpack that a bit. When you say you’re worried about making mistakes at work, what specifically are you concerned about?

**Sarah:** (Sighs) Just… disappointing my boss. And my colleagues. I want to be seen as competent, reliable. I fear that if I slip up, it will reflect poorly on me. It’s a silly feeling, I know.

**Dr. Vance:** It’s not a silly feeling, Sarah. It's a common feeling, especially when facing high expectations. It sounds like you’re tying your sense of worth to external validation – your boss’s and colleagues’ opinions. Let’s explore where that might stem from. Has this been a pattern throughout your life?

**Sarah:** (Pauses, considering) I suppose so. My father always had very high standards. It was never explicitly stated, but there was always this unspoken expectation to excel.

**Dr. Vance:** That’s insightful. It’s understandable that you might internalize those expectations and apply them to your own performance. It's important to recognize that your father’s expectations are *his*, and they don’t necessarily define your worth or dictate your actions. Let’s talk about strategies for separating your sense of self from these external pressures. We can begin by identifying situations where you feel particularly susceptible to these expectations and then explore ways to challenge those thoughts.

**Sarah:** That sounds… helpful. I just feel so stuck. Like I’m always trying to please everyone else.

**Dr. Vance:** It's a courageous step to acknowledge that, Sarah. It's a common tendency, particularly for those who prioritize the needs of others. We're going to work on establishing boundaries and prioritizing your own well-being. This isn't about dismissing the needs of your family or colleagues, but about finding a balance that allows you to function effectively without sacrificing your own emotional health. Let's start by examining one specific instance this week where you felt pressured to say yes when you wanted to say no. Can you describe that situation for me?



**Sarah:** (Takes a deep breath) Yes, I can. It was…
