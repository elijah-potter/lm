**Character:** Elias Carter
**Setting:** A dimly lit, cluttered study on the third floor of his family estate, autumn leaves crunching underfoot outside the cracked window.

Elias sat hunched over his desk, fingers tracing the rim of an empty whiskey glass. The fire in the hearth had died hours ago, leaving only embers glowing weakly. His reflection in the glass was sharp—sharp eyes, a jaw clenched so tightly his knuckles had turned white. He hadn’t slept since last night.

Across the room, the old grandfather clock struck ten. The sound was a cruel reminder. His father had found him here last evening, his voice low, measured. *"You’ve been missing too long."* Elias had stood, stiff, and said nothing. Just stared at the floor.

*"You’re running,"* his father had said. The words had been a blade. Elias had turned away before the truth could unravel. He still remembered the taste of betrayal.

Outside, the wind howled through the trees, rattling the windows. Elias exhaled sharply, the breath curling into the cold air. His hands shook—not from fear, but from something worse. The weight of the lie he’d told himself for years.

He grabbed the half-finished letter on his desk, the one he’d been working on since morning. His father had given him this task: *"Write your thoughts. Let me see what’s really on your mind."* But Elias had never trusted himself to be honest with himself. He’d filled the page with empty words—flattery, half-truths, excuses.

Now, he crumpled the letter in his fist.

*"I don’t know what I want anymore."*

The clock struck eleven.
