**BREAKING:** Mayor’s Office Announces Free Solar Panel Installation Program for Low-Income Residents of Eldermere

Eldermere, the city known for its historic copper mines and vibrant arts district, is stepping up its commitment to sustainability with a groundbreaking initiative. Effective immediately, all households in the city earning below 80% of the median income will receive a complimentary solar panel system, including installation, battery storage, and a 10-year warranty. The program, launched in collaboration with local energy cooperative GreenHaven and renewable tech firm SolarNova, aims to reduce Eldermere’s carbon footprint by 25% within five years.

The announcement was made at a press conference held at the city’s new Community Energy Hub, where Mayor Elara Voss highlighted the economic benefits: "For families who’ve struggled with rising energy costs, this isn’t just an upgrade—it’s a lifeline. We’re also creating 120 new jobs in solar installation and maintenance while keeping utility bills down for decades."

Critics argue the program risks overstretching city budgets, but city planners emphasize that funding will come from a mix of federal green subsidies, local taxes on solar panel manufacturers, and a one-time levy on Eldermere’s tourism sector. "We’ve modeled this after successful programs in Portland and Austin," said Energy Commissioner Daniel Whitmore. "The data shows these investments pay off in long-term savings and energy independence."

Residents are encouraged to apply by submitting proof of income and a completed application form at the city’s website. First come, first served for installations—expected to begin within two weeks. The program will pilot in Eldermere’s central district first, expanding to all neighborhoods by spring 2025.
