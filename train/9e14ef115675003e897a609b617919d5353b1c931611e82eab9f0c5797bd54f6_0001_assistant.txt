*Summary*
When Randle Patrick McMurphy (Jack Nicholson) gets transferred from a prison farm to a mental institution for a psychiatric evaluation, he is hoping it will be a less restrictive environment. But the shrewish Nurse Ratched (Louise Fletcher) runs the psychiatric ward with an iron fist, keeping her patients subdued through abuse, medication, and sessions of electro-shock therapy. A battle of wills between the rebellious McMurphy and the inflexible Ratched soon affects the behavior of all the ward's patients.

*Review portion* 
This movie does an excellent job of accurately portraying life in a mental institution. You can see that some of the rules were more lenient, even well over 40 years ago. The patients were allowed to smoke in the buildings, (would be disallowed now but many patients aren't allowed to smoke at all while in a mental facility) they were allowed to be outside but some locations do not allow for that. The story revolves around McMurphy (as well as Nurse Ratched) and so does most of the symbolism and the points that the movie is trying to make. McMurphy is transferred from a prison facility at his discretion because he chooses the mental institution and thinks it's an easier way to serve the sentence that he has left. He is quick to realize that the rules and environment aren't as cushy as he believed, while also building genuine camaraderie with the patients. His special bond with Chief is felt but comes to an apex when Chief reveals to him that he can talk, one of the highlights of the film. McMurphy also catches on to Nurse Ratched's intentions to cause chaos and discord early on and tries to make her life hell because he realizes what she is doing. She also intentionally tries to hide it and play coy when people realize she is doing it. Says things like "I don't know what you're talking about?" when confronted.
