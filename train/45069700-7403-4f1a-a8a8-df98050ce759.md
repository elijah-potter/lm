In the crumbling ruins of an abandoned library, beneath the scent of damp parchment and aged dust, a scholar named Elias stumbled upon a sealed envelope wedged between two dust-encrusted tomes. The paper was brittle, its edges frayed, but the handwriting was clear: *"The key to the lost vault lies in the heart of the old clock. Time is not what it once was."* The clock in question was a towering, ornate mechanism, its gears rusted but still ticking softly, marking time as if by habit alone. Elias followed the instructions, finding a hidden compartment behind the clock’s face. Inside, a small, silver key glinted under the flickering candlelight—its surface etched with a single, ancient symbol: a serpent coiled around a hourglass.

Elias carried the key to the vault beneath the library’s grand reading hall. The door groaned open at his touch, revealing a chamber lined with shelves that had never been filled. At the center stood a pedestal holding a chest of black glass, its contents obscured by centuries of mist. Inside, a single, glowing orb pulsed faintly, its light refracting through prismatic fragments that matched the symbols on the key. Elias hesitated—some said the lost vault held more than gold, but also memories. The orb’s light drew him forward, and as he placed the key into the pedestal’s center, the chest’s seal shattered. The orb burst into a cascade of stars, and for a single, perfect moment, the scholar felt something shift inside him—not just the weight of discovery, but the whisper of something older than libraries. Then, silence.
