A crumbling Victorian mansion’s grand staircase echoes with the sound of a child’s laughter—soft, forgotten, now muffled by time. Dust motes swirl in the afternoon light as the protagonist, mid-30s, adjusts their glasses to read a faded ledger, their fingers brushing a worn photograph of a young woman with the same eyes as the one now standing in the doorway: a woman who was once their mother, now just a ghost in the past.

She speaks in a voice that isn’t theirs—sharp with the edge of a memory not yet theirs: *"You were supposed to be here."* The air hums with static, and for a second, the walls breathe as if holding their breath. The photograph in their hands glows faintly, edges curling like smoke. The ledger’s ink blurs into a name they’ve never heard: *Eleanor Whitmore, 1889*. The past is a weight in their chest, pressing down as the present shatters like glass.
