The "promise" was a handshake between two strangers at a crowded market—one with a silver ring, the other with a basket of stolen fruits. He swore by the sunrise that he’d return by dusk with gold for the woman’s lost child. But the sun never set as planned; the merchant had sold the child to a moneylender before dawn. By evening, the basket was empty, the gold missing, and the woman’s grief had turned to rage. The man, now a fugitive, whispered to the wind that he’d return when the moon was full, but the moon never brought him back—only the moneylender’s debt, the child’s name on a ledger, and the thief’s chains. The "promise" was a hollow echo.
