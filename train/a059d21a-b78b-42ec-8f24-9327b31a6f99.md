The half-forgotten melody hummed through the cracks of time,
a fragment of a song left to fester in the back of the mind,
like a half-remembered dream that resurfaces only in fragments—
a single chord, a face blurred by years, a name whispered
in a language now half-swallowed by the tongue.

The past is a library where books are only checked out in
the margins, where pages are dog-eared and the spine
has frayed into something that might as well be new. Some
memories are like old photographs: clear in the edges,
but the center is a ghost, a stain of time that never quite
dries. The world outside feels sharper, more defined,
while the self remembers only the edges—
the way the light caught your hair at 17,
the sound of your mother’s voice when you were six,
the way the city smelled after rain, before it was
anything but a blur.

What was once a map has become a sketch, a guide
so faint it could be any map at all. The compass
still points north, but the needle doesn’t move.
You walk along the same path, but the house you
once knew is now a ghost town, its windows dark
and its doors slightly ajar—just enough to hear
the faint, distant echo of laughter that never quite
reaches the present. The past is a question mark
stamped on the forehead of existence, a half-remembered
name scribbled in the margins of a book no one reads.
