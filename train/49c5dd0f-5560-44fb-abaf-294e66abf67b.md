The **one-time exception rule** in process management mandates that any deviation from standard procedures must be documented, justified, and approved by a designated authority before execution. This ensures accountability, auditability, and consistency across operations.

Exceptions are logged in a designated **Temporary Exception Log (TEL)**, with metadata including:
- **Timestamp** (UTC)
- **Responsible Agent** (name/ID)
- **Reason Code** (e.g., `EMERGENCY`, `SPECIALCIRCUMSTANCE`)
- **Approval Status** (PENDING, APPROVED, REJECTED)
- **Associated Policy Reference** (e.g., `P-42-07-04`)

Once an exception is approved, it is **deprecated in the TEL** after **30 days** of no further use, unless reinstated by a new approval. This prevents stale exceptions from cluttering records.

**Key Enforcement:**
- **Retention Policy:** Exceptions remain archived for **5 years** post-deprecation.
- **Automated Alerts:** If an exception is reused without approval, the system flags it as **inconsistent with prior approvals**.

Example:
*"Purchased bulk server hardware (12 units) for Q3 ramp-up. Justified under `P-42-07-04` (Emergency Capacity). Approved by IT-DEPT-007 on 2024-05-15. TEL entry ID: `EX-2024-05-1234`."*

Failure to follow this rule results in **audit flags** and **process review**.
