**The Last Transmission**

We didn’t mean for this to happen.

The hum of the reactor core was the only sound in the control room when the first warning beep cut through the static. Engineer Vasquez’s fingers trembled as he rerouted the coolant—too late. The alarms screamed in a language of fire and rust. Outside, the blast wave flattened the emergency bunker like a crushed leaf. The air smelled of ozone and something worse: the sweet, metallic tang of something that had been alive.

Dr. Liao’s voice, thin and broken, crackled over the comms. *"We have a containment breach. The containment breach."* The words dissolved into a hiss of escaping steam. The blast shield had failed at Sector 7. The containment field was a sieve.

They didn’t have time to mourn. The extraction protocols were already in motion. The evacuation team moved like ghosts through the radiation-choked corridors, their gear humming with the weight of lead and hope. Behind them, the reactor core screamed—a sound that wasn’t a sound, a vibration in the bones of the building. The city’s skyline, a city of glass and steel, was melting into dust.

They never made it to the surface.

What was left of them was found in the skeletal remains of the old subway tunnels, half-buried in the ash. Their faces were gone, but their clothes still held the scent of ozone and something older: the scent of the thing that had been waiting for them all along.

The thing that had been *hungry*.
