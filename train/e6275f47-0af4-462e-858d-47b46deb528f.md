The train arrived twenty minutes past the scheduled departure time, causing a single lone figure at the platform to scramble for their bag. Their phone buzzed repeatedly—emails, missed calls, and a text from their boss: *"Where the hell are you?"*

With a deep breath, they grabbed their purse and sprinted toward the exit, keys rattling in their pocket. The doors opened just as the last passenger stepped out, and they slid inside, barely catching the seat.

Inside the car, they slumped against the back of the seat, head in their hands. The relief was overwhelming—no more awkward glances, no more frantic text messages, no more excuses. The commute home would be a blur of exhaustion and good food.
