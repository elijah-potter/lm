AITA for refusing to let my sister use my inherited vacation home?

Okay, this is a bit complicated, so bear with me. It involves my late grandmother, my sister, and a property that’s become a surprisingly large source of familial tension.

My grandmother, Eleanor, passed away last November (2023) after a long illness. She lived in Charleston, South Carolina, and owned a small, charming cottage near Rainbow Row. She always intended it to be a family heirloom. In her will, she explicitly left the cottage to me, with a clause stating I could choose to share it with my sister, Amelia, if I so desired. The wording was deliberately vague – she wanted me to have the ultimate say.

Amelia and I have always had a… competitive relationship. Nothing overtly malicious, but we’ve frequently compared accomplishments and subtly undermined each other. She’s a lawyer in Atlanta, very successful, and tends to view everything as a transaction. I’m a freelance writer, living in Asheville, North Carolina, and value experiences over material possessions.

For years, Amelia has been subtly hinting about wanting to use the cottage. After Eleanor’s passing, she immediately started pushing the issue. “It’s such a waste for you to have it and not share,” she’s said repeatedly. “Think of the income you could generate renting it out!” I initially resisted, wanting to preserve the cottage as a personal retreat and a tangible link to my grandmother. I did, however, entertain the idea of letting Amelia use it for a week or two each year, thinking it might be a gesture of goodwill.

Then, last week (December 12, 2023), Amelia presented me with a formal proposal. She’s found a property management company in Charleston (Charleston Coast Rentals) and wants to list the cottage on Airbnb. Her plan is to manage the rentals herself, taking a significant cut of the income. She presented a spreadsheet outlining potential revenue, which, admittedly, looked impressive. However, it also included a detailed breakdown of expenses, including repairs, cleaning fees, and insurance – all of which would be my responsibility. She argued that her management fees would offset those costs and that I’m “being selfish” by not allowing her to capitalize on the property's potential.

I explained to Amelia that I value the cottage for its sentimental value, not its financial potential. I want to use it for personal retreats and occasionally host close friends and family. I've expressed concerns about the wear and tear of frequent rentals, the potential for problematic guests, and the disruption to the peaceful atmosphere Eleanor cultivated. I also pointed out that the spreadsheet’s projections were overly optimistic and didn’t account for seasonal fluctuations in tourism.

My mother, who lives in Florida, has weighed in, siding with Amelia. She believes I’m being unreasonable and that I should share the property to “maximize its value.” She’s accused me of hoarding and suggested that Eleanor would have wanted us to share the benefits. My brother, David, who lives in Denver, hasn't taken a side, but has privately told me he understands my perspective.

Amelia has become increasingly insistent, sending me daily emails and calling multiple times a day. She’s even started subtly reminding our mother about my “lack of ambition” and how I’m “wasting” my inheritance. I feel pressured and manipulated. I’m genuinely worried that if I cave, I’ll regret it and the cottage will lose the character that made it so special. I'm also concerned about the potential for conflict with Amelia if the rental business doesn’t go as planned. She tends to blame others when things go wrong.

I’m at a loss. I feel like I'm being ganged up on. I’m trying to honor my grandmother’s wishes while also protecting something that’s deeply meaningful to me. I’m beginning to think I’m being unreasonable, but I also believe I have a right to decide how I use my inheritance. ESH, perhaps, for everyone pushing so hard. NTA for wanting to preserve something sentimental. YTA, Mom, for siding with Amelia.
