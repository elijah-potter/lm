**The Last Light in the Alley**

We didn’t mean for this to happen.

The first time she touched him was a mistake—his hand brushing against hers as he passed by, the way his fingers lingered just a second too long. She should have pulled away, but the warmth of his touch had already seared into her skin. He didn’t know then that she was the one who had stolen his favorite scarf from his locker three days ago, that she had watched him from the bleachers, her breath shallow, her heart pounding like a trapped thing.

By the time she realized he was watching her back, it was too late. His smile was too sharp, his eyes too knowing. He had read her like an open book—every hesitation, every trembling nerve—and he had used it.

The alley behind the diner smelled of rain and old cigarette butts. She had been running since the first drop of sweat broke on her forehead. The streetlights flickered, casting long, jagged shadows that made the pavement feel like a living thing. She clutched the scarf to her chest, its fabric damp with her own tears.

*"You’re going to get yourself killed,"* he said, his voice low, dangerous.

She didn’t answer. She had no words left.

He stepped forward, close enough that she could smell the coffee and the whiskey on his breath. His hand found her wrist, fingers twisting just enough to make her flinch. *"We didn’t mean for this to happen,"* he repeated, softer now, like he was trying to convince himself.

She wanted to scream. She wanted to run. But her legs wouldn’t obey.

Then his mouth was on hers, rough and demanding, and she was already drowning.
