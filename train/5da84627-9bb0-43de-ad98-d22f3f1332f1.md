The **Casimir effect** in a vacuum cavity: quantum vacuum fluctuations cause an effective attraction between two parallel, closely spaced metallic plates, independent of their distance, due to zero-point energy variations in the electromagnetic field.

For **fiber optics at high dispersion**, light exhibits anomalous dispersion where the group velocity decreases with increasing frequency, leading to pulse broadening.

In **quantum dot arrays**, light behaves as a collective excitation called a **plasmon-polariton** when confined in nanostructures, exhibiting hybridized dispersion relations with both light and matter properties.

At the **edge of a 2D material** (e.g., graphene), light can propagate along the boundary without radiating (quasi-bound states), exhibiting **topological edge modes** that guide light without scattering.

In **photonic crystal slabs**, light exhibits **bandgap effects** where certain wavelengths are forbidden, causing strange diffraction patterns and total internal reflection for specific angles.

**Nonlinear optics** in high-intensity fields: light can generate **harmonic generation** (e.g., second harmonic generation) or **four-wave mixing**, producing unexpected frequency components.

**Quantum dots in liquid helium** at cryogenic temperatures: photons trapped in quantum dots can exhibit **quantum interference effects** even in low-density environments, defying classical expectations.

**Bose-Einstein condensates of light** (e.g., in optical cavities): bosonic photons exhibit **coherent superposition** and **Bose statistics**, allowing them to form macroscopic quantum states with strange interference patterns.
