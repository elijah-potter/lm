Public Health: Suspected dengue fever case not dengue
After a bunch of tests, experts have weighed in on the best mosquito repellents. Buzz60's Sean Dowling has more. Buzz60
The Department of Public Health and Social Services got results back for a suspected case of dengue fever which turned out to be negative, said Linda DeNorcey, the agency's director.
Public Health had said there was one suspected case of dengue fever, but officials were waiting for laboratory results for confirmation.
The agency is monitoring dengue fever outbreaks that have been reported in places near Guam, such as Palau, the Philippines and the Marshall Islands.
"While Guam is free of endemic mosquito-borne diseases, it is not uncommon to have imported cases from returning residents or travelers," a release from Public Health stated.
- Palau has a dengue serotype 3 outbreak with 486 cases and one death, the release stated.
- The Philippines has more than 146,000 cases and 622 deaths.
- The Marshall Islands has a reported dengue serotype 3 outbreak, according to Public Health.
People traveling to the Philippines, Palau and the Marshall Islands should be vigilant about taking precautions and preventing mosquito bites, the release stated.
Residents are encouraged to take action to prevent the spread of all mosquito-borne diseases by making sure there isn't any standing water around their homes.
The Aedes aegypti mosquito is the primary transmitter, of dengue viruses. The mosquito isn't found on Guam. However, the Asian tiger mosquito, which can transmit dengue, is here, according to Public Health.
