Wet stone floors slick with morning dew, reflections of dying light pooling in cracks. The scent of damp earth and rotting leaves lingers—fresh from the rain. Rusting iron gates creak open, revealing a courtyard half-buried in ivy, branches clawing at the iron bars like skeletal fingers. The air is thick with the scent of damp wood and old paint, the hum of distant traffic muffled by layers of forgotten time.

A lone figure stands at the threshold, shoulders hunched against the weight of what comes next. The cobblestones beneath them are uneven, worn smooth by decades of footsteps—yours. Beyond the gate, the world stretches out: a city that once hummed with life, now a shadow of its former self. The buildings loom, their windows dark, their facades peeling like old skin. The last remnants of the old life—clocks stopped, clocks never started, clocks that just don’t work anymore. The last gasp of a world that never truly died.

The wind carries whispers of the past: laughter, screams, the distant clatter of a train that’s been silent for years. Your breath fogs in the cold air. This is it. The end. The final time you see it. The final time you hear it. The final time you *believe* it’s not the last.
