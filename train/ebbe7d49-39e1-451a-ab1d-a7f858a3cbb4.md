You were supposed to be at the station by 9 AM, not wandering through the abandoned cathedral at midnight. The church’s stained-glass windows had long since crumbled into dust, their colors bleeding into the cobblestone floors. The air smelled of damp stone and something older—something like old blood. The last time you’d been here, the rain had stopped, and the sky had been that sickly green hue before the storm hit.

A whisper slithered from the shadows behind the altar. You spun, heart hammering, but there was only the echo of your own breath. The words repeated themselves, sharp and final:

*"You shouldn’t be here."*

The floor beneath your boots gave way underfoot. The ground wasn’t stone anymore—it was skin, stretched taut over something that moved beneath you. A voice, muffled and guttural, called from the dark:

*"Let’s see if you’re brave enough to stay."*

The walls closed in.
