The warehouse’s fluorescent lights flickered weakly against the dust motes swirling in the stale air. Old boxes, half-buried under layers of rust, lined the floor like silent sentinels. Elias crouched beside a wooden crate labeled *"Samples, 2007"*—its seal broken, its contents scattered inside. His fingers traced the faded print as he pulled out a small, ornate key. His breath hitched. The lock on the chest behind him groaned open.

Inside, a single photograph remained: a child’s face, blurred but unmistakably familiar. The caption read *"For Liora, who forgot me."* Elias’ pulse quickened. The key, the photo—they were fragments of a story he hadn’t told in years. The chest’s lid slammed shut. The moment the lock clicked, the warehouse’s alarms blared, cutting off his escape. He had no choice.
