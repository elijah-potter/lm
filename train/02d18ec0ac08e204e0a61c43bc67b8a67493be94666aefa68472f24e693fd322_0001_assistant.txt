The 1971 Ford Pinto is usually considered one of the worst cars of all time, but that isn't the case. Ford came up with the concept to compete with Toyotas and Datsuns. They didn't waste any time getting it on the road. Production time is usually 43 months, but Ford had it on the market in just 25 months; the shortest production time in history. The Pinto was a very good car. It was inexpensive and cost less than $3000 which was less than other sub-compact cars at the time. It also got great gas mileage at 34 miles per gallon. When the car was introduced, it was advertised as the "Little Carefree Car." Car and Driver magazine said it had the feel of a sports car and gave it positive reviews. The car was famous for bursting into flames when hit from behind, but Ford crunched numbers and figured out it was less expensive to pay victims than it would be to fix the vehicles that were on the road. Years later a UCLA law professor wrote a paper that said that the Pinto was no more dangerous than similar cars at that time. With the speed that it was brought to market, the low cost, and the excellent gas mileage, any problems that the Pinto had were outweighed by these factors.
