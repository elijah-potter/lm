- Lift the horse's foot - Make sure to raise the horse's hoof in a way that is comfortable and natural, and make sure the horse is aware of your presence beforehand, so it is not startled. Put your hand on the horse's leg, and run your hand down until you reach the tendon above the ankle. When the horse feels this, it will shift its weight to the other three legs. Once you have the foot raised, try resting it between your knees, with the sole facing up toward you.

- Remove the existing shoe - Use a clinch cutter to straighten the nails holding the old shoe on, then use a pair of metal pull-offs to remove the shoe from the hoof. This can also be done with a flat screwdriver. Wiggle the tool in a rocking motion until it is loose enough to pull off by hand.

- Clean the hoof - Make sure to clean away any dirt and debris to prevent it from being trapped between the hoof and the new shoe. Use a hoof pick to remove dirt or manure, using downward strokes from heel to toe. Follow up with a wire brush to remove any remaining debris. Be careful of the fleshy area here because it is very sensitive.

- Remove the excess hoof - Use a hoof knife to remove the excess flaky part of the bottom of the hoof. The outer layer will be hard and dark, and once cut, the hoof underneath will be softer and whiter, similar to a human's toenail. Make sure not to cut too deep, and not to cut yourself.

- Trim excess hoof wall - The hoof wall is the edge of the hoof. It should only extend about 3 inches from the hairline of the hoof. If it is longer than this, trim it back and make sure to cut it straight and level so it will sit flush with the ground when standing.

- Use a rasp for leveling - This is the last step before applying a new shoe. Use a rasp and gently file any uneven areas so that the hoof is smooth and level. Use a knife to cut away a slight bevel away from the hoof wall to prevent the sole of the shoe from coming in contact with the soft part of the foot.
