The old wooden door creaked as I pushed it open, the weight of the evening pressing down on me like a silent, unspoken question. The hallway stretched before me, its walls lined with flickering shadows and half-remembered stories. My hands trembled—not from cold, but from the doubt that had settled into my bones like rust.

I stepped forward, my foot catching on something unseen. The floor groaned. I swallowed hard, the taste of my own hesitation lingering in my mouth. What if I made the wrong choice? What if this was all a mistake? The words felt like they were being spoken by someone else, distant and echoing in my mind.

The first room I entered was a mess of half-finished projects and half-remembered dreams. A stack of crumpled papers, a half-painted canvas, a half-empty mug of something dark and bitter. My fingers brushed the edge of the paint, and for a moment, I wondered if I was even capable of finishing anything anymore.

A noise from the kitchen made me freeze. My mother’s voice, sharp and tired, called from the back of the house. "Dinner’s ready." I exhaled slowly, the words catching in my throat. *She’ll know.* She’d see the way my hands shook, the way my eyes darted to the floor when I looked at her. She’d know I wasn’t sure anymore.

I forced myself to move, the weight of the dishes in my arms heavier than I expected. The kitchen was small, cluttered with the remnants of meals long gone. My mother was already seated at the table, her back to me. She didn’t turn when I set the plates down, but I could feel the tension in her shoulders, the way her fingers tightened around her teacup.

"Did you get everything?" she asked, her voice barely above a whisper.

I nodded, my jaw set tight. "Yeah."

She turned slowly, and for a second, I thought I saw something in her eyes—pity, maybe, or something older, something that had been there for years. "You look like you’re going to be sick," she said, and I wanted to argue, to tell her how I *was* going to be sick, but my voice failed me.

I sat down, my chair scraping against the floor. The silence between us stretched, thick and suffocating. I reached for the fork, my fingers numb. What was I doing here? What was I supposed to do next?

The answer didn’t come. And that was the worst part.
