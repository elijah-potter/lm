The storm rolled in without warning, its thunderous echoes shaking the old house. Rain lashed against the windows in sheets, but the narrator stood firm by the door, arms crossed, watching the downpour as if it were a performance they were missing. The wind howled through the cracks in the ceiling, but they did not flinch—only the faintest creak of the floorboards beneath their boots betrayed the house’s creeping fear.

Inside, the lantern flickered, casting long shadows that danced like restless spirits. The narrator’s fingers traced the edge of a half-empty bottle of whiskey, the amber liquid reflecting the dim light like a lie. *"Another round,"* they murmured, though no one was there to hear. The walls seemed to whisper secrets, but the narrator pressed their lips to the glass, determined to block them out.

Outside, the rain turned to hail, each pebble a silent accusation. The narrator exhaled slowly, the breath curling into the cold air. *"No,"* they whispered. *"Not yet."* The lantern burned lower, the flame flickering into something unrecognizable—was it fear? Or was it the promise of something darker waiting in the dark?
