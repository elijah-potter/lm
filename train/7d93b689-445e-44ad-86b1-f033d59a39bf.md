The café’s hum was a steady murmur of unnoticed conversations—some whispered, some loud, all lost in the rhythm of coffee cups and the clink of cutlery. She watched a woman in a fluorescent blazer, her fingers tracing the rim of her latte, eyes half-lidded. The others would have seen her hand trembling slightly, the way she shifted her weight, the faint exhale of relief when the waiter approached. They missed the tension beneath the casual posture, the quiet battle of exhaustion she fought not to show.

Across the room, a child’s laughter—too loud, too bright—echoed off the walls. The adults around her glanced up, momentarily distracted, but no one paused to look. She followed the sound, tracing it like a ghostly trail, until it faded into the corner where a toddler stood, arms wrapped around a stuffed rabbit, too young to understand why grown-ups ignored the sound of their own joy.

The train’s engine groaned, its breath labored, as it pulled into the station. The passengers moved in unison, their bodies folding into the seats like empty shells, unaware of the way the air around them shimmered—thick with the scent of travel, stale coffee, and something metallic, like the first drop of rain after months of drought. She watched a man’s fingers tap restlessly on the armrest, his knuckles white. The others would have missed the way his shoulders tensed when his phone buzzed, the abrupt stillness that followed the notification.

She noticed the cracks in the pavement beneath her feet—the ones that widened with every step, invisible to those who hurried past. She noticed the way light bends around shadows, how the edges of everything dissolve into something softer, something unseen. The world was a tapestry of things half-glimpsed, half-remembered, and she was the only one who saw the threads.
