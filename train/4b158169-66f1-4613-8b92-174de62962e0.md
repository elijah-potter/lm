The moon hung low over the cracked asphalt of an old highway, its silver glow reflecting in the puddles of melted tar. Inside a rusted Ford pickup, old man Elias adjusted his wire-rimmed glasses as he drove, his fingers tracing the worn leather of his worn-out gloves. He had been driving this route for years, always stopping at the same spot near the abandoned gas station at midnight.

Elias had once been a postal worker, but after his wife’s passing, he’d found solace in the quiet rhythm of the road. Tonight, however, he wasn’t just driving—he was *listening*.

The wind carried a faint whisper through the open window, and Elias leaned closer. "You know, the mail hasn’t been delivered since the storm," he muttered, though no one was there to hear him.

A single envelope lay on the gas station’s rusted counter, its wax seal broken. Inside, a letter in his wife’s handwriting: *"The key beneath the pebble near the old well. It’s not gold, but it’s silver. Trust the silence when the river runs dry."*

Elias pocketed the letter. He had no key, no well, and no river nearby—but the words had stuck in his mind like a bad dream. He pulled out his pocketknife, sharpened it with the edge of his boot, and began digging where the letter said.

Beneath a smooth river rock, he found it: a silver locket, cold and heavy in his palm. Inside was a photograph of his wife, smiling, her eyes reflecting the same quiet hope that had kept him going for years. But when he opened the back, he saw something else—a tiny, folded note: *"The first letter is the last."*

The locket was heavier than he remembered. As he turned it over, the words on the inside began to glow faintly in the moonlight:

**"E L I A S   H A S   D R I V E N   T O   T H E   R I V E R   Y O U R   S I L E N C E   W A S   S O   Q U I E T   Y O U   W O L D   N O T   H E A R   T H E   W I N D   T H A T   S E E M S   N O I S E."**
