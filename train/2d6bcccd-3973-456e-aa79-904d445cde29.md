**The Quiet Hour**

The city hummed outside the window—a distant roar of engines and laughter, a thousand breathless voices lost in the static. I traced the edge of my cup with my thumb, the heat lingering like a ghost. Outside, the sky had turned the color of bruised overripe fruit, its edges bleeding into the dusk. I didn’t know what I’d been chasing—time, maybe, or the illusion of control—but here, in this quiet corner of the room, the weight of it all had finally settled.

A breeze slipped through the curtains, carrying the scent of rain and something older, something earthy. I exhaled slowly, letting the breath out like a slow unraveling. The world had been a storm for too long, and now it was just… still. The hum of the city faded into the background, replaced by the soft murmur of my own thoughts, unhurried and unbroken.

The lamp flickered, casting long shadows across the floor. I closed my eyes and let them drift, not to sleep, but to rest. The return wasn’t to peace—peace was a myth, a word too soft for the weight of it all—but to this: the quiet space between the chaos, the pause before the next storm. And for now, that was enough.
