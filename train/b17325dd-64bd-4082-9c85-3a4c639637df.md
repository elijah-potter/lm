In the quiet hum of a midnight train, where the stars stretched like silver threads across the blackened sky, Clara found herself clutching a half-empty whiskey glass. The bartender had called it "The Last Call"—a name that felt prophetic. Outside, the city slept, but the train’s engine groaned like a dying beast, its rhythmic pulse a warning.

She had come to this place for answers, but the answers had come to her instead. The letters in her hands—stamped with a date older than her own mother’s birth—had been waiting in the attic since she was a girl. Each one contained a name, a date, a whisper of a truth she’d been told she’d never uncover. Today, the final envelope was open.

The paper within was crumpled, ink smudged with time. A single name was written in her mother’s looping script: *Elias Veyne*. Below it, a single sentence, unreadable until she held it closer—*"He was never supposed to leave."*

A cold prickle ran down Clara’s spine. She had spent years chasing shadows, chasing ghosts of a past that had tried to erase her. But now, standing in the flickering glow of the train’s headlights, she realized the truth was simpler than she’d imagined. She had spent her life running from what she didn’t understand, but the question staring her in the face was this: *What would you do if the past you thought you escaped was the key you’ve been searching for all along?*
