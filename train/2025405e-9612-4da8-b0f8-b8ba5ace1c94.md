The dim glow of a flickering oil lamp cast long shadows across the walls of the cramped attic. Elias sat hunched over his half-finished sketches, the charcoal dust clinging to his fingers. His breath came in shallow gasps, each one a battle against the weight of his own mind. The letter in his hand, sealed with wax, trembled—not from cold, but from the fear that clawed at his ribs.

*"You cannot stay here. They will find you. They will never stop."* The words of his older sister, Mira, echoed in his skull, raw and unfiltered. But the voice in the dark whispered, *"What if I’m right? What if you are the one they want?"*

His hands trembled as he clenched the letter. The ink had faded, but the inkling of betrayal remained—his own doubt, the gnawing fear that he had not done enough to protect them. The attic door creaked open. He froze, heart hammering. The figure in the hallway was familiar, yet distant—his father’s voice, sharp with exasperation. *"Elias? Come down. The supplies are ready."*

Elias swallowed hard. The conflict inside him was a storm. One side urged him to flee, to disappear before they discovered his guilt. The other, weaker side, held onto the last shreds of hope—*maybe he could fix this. Maybe he could prove he was more than the son they feared.* He stood slowly, the weight of every regret pressing down on him like a physical force. The lamp snuffed out. In the dark, he chose silence.
