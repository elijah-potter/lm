---
**Lena** sat at the kitchen table, her fingers tracing the rim of her coffee cup. Outside, the rain drummed against the windows, a steady rhythm that matched the quiet frustration in her chest.

*"He was just late again,"* she muttered to herself, staring at the clock. The alarm had gone off at 6:05 AM, but Daniel’s phone was still on silent, buried in his bag. His excuses were always the same: *"Traffic,"* *"The subway was packed,"* *"I forgot my keys."* But this time, the delay stretched into forty minutes.

She grabbed her keys, the cold metal clinking against her palm. *"Fine,"* she said, more to herself than to him. *"But not again."*

---

**Daniel** checked his phone again, the screen glowing a sickly blue. He’d been running late, but the subway had been a nightmare—crowded, unpredictable. The train had arrived fifteen minutes late, and he’d barely made it to the station before the next one pulled in. His phone buzzed; a text from Lena: *"Where are you?"*

He sighed, rubbing his temples. *"I’m on my way,"* he typed, then hit send. The message disappeared into the void of her inbox.

Outside, the city was alive, but it was also cruel. The rain had turned the streets into slick mirrors, and the traffic was worse than usual. Daniel’s commute was already stretching. He checked the time again—6:30 AM—and sent another text: *"I’ll be there in 15 minutes."*

---

**Lena** was already halfway down the block when her phone buzzed. A new message from Daniel: *"Sorry, traffic’s worse than I thought."*

She scoffed, but before she could respond, her phone buzzed again. *"Lena, I’m on my way."*

She didn’t reply. Instead, she turned the corner, her heels clicking against the pavement. The rain had slowed her, but she wasn’t waiting. Not again.
