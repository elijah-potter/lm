The air hummed with the weight of someone else’s presence—each breath a ghostly echo, each step a mirror of another’s shadow. The coffee cup sat half-empty, the steam curling like a question mark against the morning light, neither mine nor anyone else’s. The keyboard beneath my fingers had been used before, its keys worn smooth by fingers that had pressed the same letters in the same way. The phone in my pocket buzzed with an unread notification, a notification I’d never opened, never read. Outside, the city pulsed with its own rhythm, but here, in this moment, the world had slowed, and I was the one who felt the absence.

I reached for my phone again, fingers trembling as if they were trying to hold something that wasn’t mine. The screen glowed—an email, a message, a reminder—all of it waiting, all of it already *known*. The clock ticked, but it wasn’t ticking for me. It was ticking for someone else’s schedule, someone else’s exhaustion, someone else’s quiet desperation. I sat there, the chair digging into my back, the weight of the borrowed moment pressing down like a debt I couldn’t repay. The coffee cup grew colder. The light dimmed. And somewhere, in the static between my thoughts and the world, I heard the sound of someone else’s sigh.
