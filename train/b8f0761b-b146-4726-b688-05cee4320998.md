The golden hour blurred into twilight’s ghost, and the weight of the past—like smoke—faded into the wind.

A single word, carved into the air: *"Maybe."* It flickered, then dissolved. The hand holding the pen trembled, but the stroke pressed on, the ink bleeding into the page before it could retreat. The mirror’s reflection didn’t stay still. It was a ghost of what was, what could’ve been, what *was*—all pressed into this moment, this moment that would not hold. The light, once sharp, now dripped like melted wax.

I looked up. The sky, once vast and unbroken, had split open. The stars, those distant witnesses, blinked out one by one, swallowed by the dark’s slow hunger. The clarity wasn’t gone—it was a wound, a scar of light that would never quite mend, only remember. The pen dropped. The page, now stained with the last traces of what was said, was left to dry. The moment was gone.
