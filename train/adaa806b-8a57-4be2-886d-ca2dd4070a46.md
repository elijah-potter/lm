The last time I saw the old warehouse, its rusted steel doors stood open like skeletal fingers against the setting sun, their once-glossy paint peeling into brittle flakes. The scent of damp wood and something faintly metallic clung to the air—rust, maybe, or old paint. Inside, the flickering fluorescent lights cast long, jagged shadows across the cracked linoleum, where a single cobweb danced lazily in the draft from the broken window high above. The walls bore the faint impressions of years of graffiti, faded but still legible: *"Never forget"* and *"This place was ours."* The floor, once polished concrete, now had deep grooves where heavy machinery had once rolled. A single, half-buried fire extinguisher stood by the door, its label long faded. The silence was thick, heavy with the weight of what had been and what remained.

I walked the perimeter, my boots crunching on the gravel that had been a parking lot once. The overgrown garden at the back was a tangled mess of weeds, where a rusted metal gate lay propped against a tree, its lock broken. Beyond, the skeletal remains of what had once been a fence—now just a pile of bent wire and splintered wood—marked the boundary of this place. The wind howled through the broken windows, carrying with it the last echoes of the life that had lived here. I stepped back, letting the door close behind me. The warehouse was gone. The last time I saw it, it was still standing.
