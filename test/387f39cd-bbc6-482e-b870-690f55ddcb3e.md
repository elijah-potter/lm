## Explain Like I'm Five: Why the Sky is Blue

Okay, imagine you have a big box of crayons, right? And all the crayons are different colors: red, yellow, blue, green, purple, and so on. Sunlight is kind of like that big box of crayons, but instead of crayons, it's made of *all* the colors mixed together! You can’t see them separately when the sunlight is coming to Earth.

Now, when that sunlight comes down to Earth, it bumps into tiny bits in the air, like teeny-tiny dust particles and air molecules.  It’s like when you roll a ball down a bumpy hill – it bounces around a little.

Blue and purple colors in sunlight are a little bit like bouncy balls. They bounce around *a lot* when they hit those tiny bits in the air. Red and yellow colors are more like bowling balls – they mostly go straight through.

Because the blue and purple colors are bouncing around so much, they spread out all over the sky.  That's why when you look up, you see blue! It's not that the sky *is* blue, it's that the blue light is bouncing around everywhere and reaching your eyes.

Sometimes, when the sun is setting, you see orange and red colors. That's because the sunlight has to travel through *more* air to reach you.  All the blue light has already bounced away, so you only see the colors that don’t bounce as much, like orange and red. 

So, the sky isn't actually painted blue. It’s just the way the sunlight bounces! It's a big, colorful game of bouncing light.
