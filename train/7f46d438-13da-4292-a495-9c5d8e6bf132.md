An unspoken village near a river where the old oak stands as its silent sentinel. The rules are whispered through shared glances—no one speaks of the unpaid debts carved into the bark, or how the youngest child’s laughter is the only sound during the *quiet hours* after the sun dips below the ridge. The elders nod at the man who never leaves his stool by the well, even though he never drinks. The children learn to listen for the *click* in the wind, the signal that means the elders will share their stories. The elders don’t need a signpost; they read the soil’s color, the way the shadows stretch differently at certain times of the year. The river’s current changes direction subtly, and those who follow it too closely find their reflections missing. The rules aren’t written, but they are the way the world *turns*—not with a clock, but with a heartbeat.
