A damp autumn evening. Rain splattered against the grimy windows of an old bookshop. The scent of aged paper and aged wood clung to the air. Inside, the shop’s single light flickered, casting long shadows across the worn shelves.

Lena knelt in the cluttered corner, her fingers brushing through a pile of crumpled receipts and half-empty coffee cups. The space was a maze of forgotten things—old magazines, half-eaten sandwich wrappers, a discarded scarf, and the faintest echo of a forgotten conversation.

Then, she felt it.

A smooth, cold metal object, half-buried beneath a rusted keychain. She pulled it free—a small, tarnished locket. Its surface was etched with faint, intricate designs. She turned it over, tracing the worn edges with her thumb.

The locket was heavy, too heavy for something so small. It was old, maybe even antique. Her breath hitched as she realized she’d been searching for it for weeks—ever since she’d moved back to this town.

The locket felt like a secret, like a promise left behind. She closed her fingers around it, the weight of its history settling into her palm.
