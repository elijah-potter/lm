The last train to Black Hollow Station pulled into the station at midnight, its headlights cutting through the mist like a blade. The conductor, a man with a face lined with too many years, announced the departure to the last passenger—a woman in a faded coat, clutching a single, yellowed photograph of a child in a field of wildflowers.

She didn’t look up from her hands. "I’m sorry," she whispered.

The train groaned to life, the doors hissing shut behind her. The conductor nodded, then vanished into the night. The stationmaster, an old man with a cane, stood beside her, holding a lantern. "You’ve waited too long," he said quietly. "The door’s always closed by then."

She turned to him, her breath fogging the lantern’s glass. "I know."

He exhaled sharply. "You’re not the first."

The lantern flickered. The stationmaster’s face blurred for a second—then sharpened into something else, something ancient. His eyes were too bright, his mouth too wide. "You’re the last."

The photograph slipped from her fingers. It shattered on the platform, petals of paper scattering like snow.

The stationmaster’s voice dropped to a whisper. "The hinge is here."

The train’s engine roared, but the doors remained closed. The conductor stood at the window, staring into the dark. The stationmaster’s cane fell to the ground.

A sound—like a door creaking from the other side.

Then, silence.
