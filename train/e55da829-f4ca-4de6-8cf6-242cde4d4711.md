**A Tale of Neon and Shadows**
The neon flickered like dying stars as the city hummed beneath the rain-slicked pavement. The old clock tower, once a beacon, now stood as a silent sentinel, its hands frozen mid-second. A stranger in a sleeveless coat—his coat—hovered near the neon sign of *The Last Whisk*, its words barely legible against the glow.

*"Drink up,"* the stranger said, his voice a ghost of static. *"Before the lights go out for good."*

The café’s last customer lingered, clutching a half-empty glass. The bartender, a woman with ink-stained fingers, wiped her hands on her apron and whispered, *"They say the city’s memory isn’t what it used to be. But the ghosts… they remember everything."*

Outside, the neon pulsed—*The Last Whisk*—then died. The stranger’s coat vanished in the dark. The bartender poured the last drops into the empty glass and watched the streetlights blink off one by one. Somewhere, a clock struck midnight.
