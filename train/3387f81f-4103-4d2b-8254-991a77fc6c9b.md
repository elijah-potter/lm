**The Library at Dusk**

The air in the old library smelled of dust and old paper—each breath a whisper of forgotten stories. I sat by the cracked window, fingers tracing the spine of a book that had seen better days, its cover worn smooth by countless hands. The rain outside hammered against the glass, turning the world into a soft, misty blur. Outside, the city pulsed with life, but here, in this sanctuary of silence, time felt still.

A sudden hum—like the distant chime of a bell—cut through the quiet. I turned, expecting a visitor, but the shelves remained empty. Then I saw it: a single, crumpled note tucked under the last shelf. My pulse quickened. The paper was yellowed, the ink faded, but the words were still legible:

*"You’re the first to find me."*

I exhaled sharply, gripping the edge of the table. The weight of the moment pressed down, heavy as a secret I wasn’t supposed to know.

The next afternoon, I stood in the same room, this time alone. The note was gone. The library was silent. But in my pocket, something cold and smooth lay against my palm—a key, small and unremarkable, its metal dull. I stared at it, then at the empty shelves. The rain had stopped. The city was alive again.

I left the library without a word.

**The Garden Key**

The key fit perfectly into the rusted lock of the old greenhouse. Inside, the air was thick with the scent of damp earth and something else—something metallic, like old blood. The walls were lined with vines that pulsed faintly, their leaves shifting like living things. At the center stood a tree, its branches twisted into a shape no one recognized. It didn’t bear fruit. It didn’t bloom.

A voice, soft as wind through leaves, spoke from the darkness behind me.

*"You’ve been waiting for this."*

I turned. The voice belonged to the tree itself—its bark split open, revealing something like a mouth, teeth made of glass. The leaves shivered. The vines coiled around my ankles, pulling me closer.

*"You’re not alone,"* the tree whispered. *"We’ve been waiting for you."*

The key in my hand felt heavy, like it was holding something I wasn’t ready to let go. The greenhouse was filling with light. The moment stretched, thick with possibility.

Then—*a sound.*

A child’s laughter, sharp and bright, cut through the silence. I stumbled back, gasping. The greenhouse door slammed shut behind me. The key slipped from my fingers.

Outside, the city was loud. Too loud.

I ran.
