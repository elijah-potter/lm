**The Old Clockmaker’s Secret**

Beneath the dusty shelves of the abandoned clock shop, where time had long since stopped its ticking, I found what no one else did—until the morning of my discovery. The grandfather clock, its gears rusted but still turning, was the last to speak. Its chime had been silenced, but its face still bore the scars of a hand that had once been too bold. The pendulum swung weakly, marking the hours with a whisper, as if the past itself was afraid to be heard.

I traced the missing teeth to the back of the mechanism, where a single, jagged edge had been filed away—something no apprentice would attempt, not even one as skilled as me. The key wasn’t lost. It was *hidden*. Not in the usual places—the hollowed-out oak of the mantel, the crevice beneath the brass knocker—but beneath the floorboards, where the old floor had once been nailed down tight, as if to keep something out.

I pried them up with a crowbar, careful not to disturb the dust, and found it: a small, silver key, its surface worn smooth by decades of use. But it wasn’t just any key. It opened doors no lock should have been meant to open, doors that led to places no one remembered, places where time didn’t exist, or worse—where it *didn’t matter*.

I kept it. For now.

But the shop itself was changing. The walls, once smooth with age, now showed the faintest cracks, like the skin of something waking. The clock’s hands had stopped at twelve, but the time before was different. And when I turned the key in the lock of the back door, the hinges didn’t creak. They *sang*.

Outside, the wind howled—not as it had before, but with a voice that wasn’t wind at all. And somewhere, a child’s laughter echoed through the empty streets, too bright, too *alive*.

I should have known.

I should have *seen*.

But I didn’t.

Because some secrets aren’t meant to be kept. Some doors aren’t meant to be opened. And some things, once found, don’t stay hidden for long.
