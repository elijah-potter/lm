The gardener noticed the single thistle sprouting between the cracked sidewalk stones near the fence. It was the only one in the entire row, defying the meticulous rows of daisies and roses that lined the path. Every day, she bent down to pull it by hand, whispering to it, *"You’re not supposed to be here."* The neighbors called it a nuisance, but she kept returning, convinced it had a message—perhaps from the soil itself, a stubborn seed refusing to be buried.

One morning, the thistle bloomed. Its purple thorns glistened with dew, and for the first time, it stood tall. That evening, the gardener watered it carefully, not out of habit, but because she saw something new in its persistence. The next day, the neighbors stopped complaining. They watched from the porch as the thistle swayed in the wind, its roots burrowing deeper, its petals unfurling like a promise. By summer’s end, it had grown into a small, wild bush, a silent testament to what grows when ignored.

The act of nurturing the thistle became the garden’s secret: small, unexpected things could thrive when given just a little care.
