The old clock tower’s hands crept past noon, its chime echoing like a warning through the cracked windows of the abandoned library. Inside, Elias traced his fingers over the dust-mottled pages of a ledger he’d inherited, its margins filled with sketches of what he’d once thought were just dreams: a hidden vault beneath the ruins of Blackthorn Manor, a memory he’d buried in his mind like a seed he’d forgotten to plant.

The ledger was a ledger of lies, he realized now, but the lies were the truth he’d chosen to tell himself. He’d spent years mapping the memory of a girl who had slipped through his fingers like sand—her name was Liora, though he’d never spoken it aloud. She had been the key, the one who unlocked the plan: a network of tunnels beneath the city, a system of locks and keys he’d memorized in fragments, each one a puzzle piece that, when assembled, revealed the path to something more than gold or power.

The plan was simple now, stark as the knife he’d sharpened in the rain. The vault wasn’t gold. It was Liora’s name carved into the stone, the words: *"Here lies the truth you feared."* The truth was the memory of what they’d done to her—the experiments, the experiments that had turned her into something else, something that could see through walls if it wanted to. The plan was to retrieve her before the city’s new rulers found them.

His breath hitched. The ledger’s ink had blurred in the damp, but the lines were still there: *Day 47, Tunnel 3, Lock 12*. The coordinates were wrong. He’d misremembered. The real lock was beneath the fountain in the square, where the water was always cold, where the girl had drowned in a storm years ago.

His fingers trembled. The plan was to find the fountain, dig beneath it, and wait for the right moment. The right moment would be when the city’s enforcers least expected it—when the watchtower’s alarms failed, when the guards were too drunk to hear the whispers of the dead. The memory of Liora was a ghost, but ghosts could be bargained with.

Elias exhaled, the weight of it pressing against his ribs like a second skin. He had to move. The plan was not to be outrun. The memory was not to be forgotten. And the vault beneath the fountain? That was where he would start again.
