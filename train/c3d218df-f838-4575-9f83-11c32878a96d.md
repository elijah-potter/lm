A dimly lit laboratory at 3 AM. Glassware clatters against a cracked table as Dr. Elias Voss adjusts his glasses, fingers tracing the edge of a half-empty flask. The lab’s fluorescent lights flicker—one bulb burnt out, the other casting a sickly yellow glow. On the screen above, a chaotic EEG trace pulses erratically, the waveform stretching into jagged spikes. His breath is shallow, his jaw clenched. A single sheet of paper lies open, its pages filled with equations in a language no one else understands.

Then the door creaks open. A student—no, not a student, a ghost—steps inside, their reflection in the glass not quite right. They hold a folded note: *"The first truth is a lie. The second is the truth."* Elias’s pen pauses midair, hovering over a fresh page. The student’s voice, muffled through the door, whispers: *"The universe is both a paradox and a paradox of itself."* The lab’s air smells like ozone and old ink. The screen freezes. The flask’s contents boil over. The equation unravels. The truth and the lie—both are here. Both are *him*.
