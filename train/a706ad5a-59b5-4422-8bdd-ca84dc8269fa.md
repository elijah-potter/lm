You shouldn’t be here.

The door creaked shut behind you, the last thing you remembered was the café’s humming coffee machine, the scent of stale pastries lingering in the air. Now, the hallway stretched before you, dimly lit by flickering fluorescent tubes, the walls lined with peeling posters of long-forgotten bands. A single streetlamp glowed weakly, casting long shadows across the cracked floor.

Your feet moved forward on instinct, the familiar ache in your knees reminding you why you shouldn’t have taken this step. The air was thick with the scent of damp stone and something metallic—old blood?—from somewhere deep in the building.

A whisper slithered from the darkness ahead.

You turned, fingers twitching toward your pockets. No phone. No keys. Just a rusted knife in your boot, its edge dull after years of being stored away. The whisper came again, closer now, a voice you didn’t recognize, but the sound of it made your skin crawl.

Then the door at the end of the hall swung open, revealing a figure in a tattered trench coat, their face obscured by a scarred mask. The air smelled of gunpowder and something sweet, like burnt sugar.

*"You shouldn’t be here,"* the figure said, voice smooth as oil.

You didn’t answer. You didn’t have to. The knife in your boot felt heavy, but you kept your hands in plain sight. The building’s walls seemed to breathe around you, the weight of centuries pressing down. Somewhere, a clock ticked. Too slow. Too loud.

*"They’re looking for you,"* the stranger said, stepping forward, the mask glinting under the lamplight. *"And you’re the last one left who remembers."*

The door behind you groaned. The hallway stretched, but it wasn’t the same one anymore. Shadows moved. Something watched you.

You shouldn’t be here.

But you were. And the knife in your boot didn’t feel like a weapon anymore. It felt like a promise.
