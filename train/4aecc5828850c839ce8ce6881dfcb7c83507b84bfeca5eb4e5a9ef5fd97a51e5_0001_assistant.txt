You feel the sucking sensation of the dark mud around each foot; you cannot move or lift either boot. You’re bogged down. The sweat trickles down your face into your eyes, and you feel the flutters of anxiety. Your heart is racing. How long have you been here? Is anyone looking for you?

You’ll have to do this yourself. “Maybe I can lift my boot by pulling with my hand”, you think. Anything is better than standing here, slowly sinking into the foul-smelling mud. You reach under your left boot and try to lift it with your hand. It doesn’t work free, and by lifting one leg, you lose your balance. You flail and begin to fall over backward.

 You land on your back with a splat, and wet mud enters your ear.  Having mud on your feet was bad, but on your head sets you to a new level of panic. You feel your body slowly descending into the mud. It slowly rises, up your face, to your cheeks. 

You try to push yourself up, but your hands and arms are pinned by the goo. “Help”, you scream, “Help, I’m stuck here! Anyone?” There’s no answer but the wind in the trees, and the patter of rain on your face. You’re alone, being slowly sucked down into this sticky, muddy death-trap. The mud is up to your eyes, and in your mouth. “Harrlp” you gargle, as your mouth is filled with mud and you’re drawn under.
