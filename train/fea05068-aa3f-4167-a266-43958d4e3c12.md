In the quiet village of Eldermere, the baker’s oven had malfunctioned for three days straight. The villagers, accustomed to daily bread, grew restless. Old Man Harkin, the town’s watchman, noticed the first morning the oven’s dial was stuck at 475°F.

He tried to fix it with a wrench from the blacksmith’s shop, but the wrench snapped under the weight. The next day, he borrowed the blacksmith’s hammer, but the strike cracked the oven’s ceramic lining. By the third day, the oven had become a warren of cracks, and the bread turned out denser than stone.

That evening, as the villagers gathered at the tavern, the baker’s wife, Elspeth, decided to use the oven’s last functional flame to bake a single loaf for the family. Instead of bread, she crafted a strange, spongy pastry that tasted of burnt sugar and rust. It was the first time in Eldermere’s history that someone had accidentally invented a new flavor.

The next morning, the villagers whispered about the "Rust-Bread." Soon, it became a secret delicacy. The blacksmith, who had seen the oven’s ruin, noticed the spongy texture and thought, *"What if we could use this?"* He experimented, adding old nails and charred twigs to the dough, turning the Rust-Bread into a spiced, crackling treat.

By the end of the week, the tavern’s menu had changed. The Rust-Bread became a legend—some said it cured scrofula, others claimed it made one’s hair grow. The blacksmith’s shop became a hub for those seeking its power, and the baker’s wife, now called the "Oven’s Alchemist," sold it by the bucketful.

Years later, when the villagers spoke of the first mistake that changed everything, they called it the day the Rust-Bread was born.
