Lost in the shifting sands of an ever-turning dune, the compass in my pocket spun uselessly. The first destination—a sunlit village at the foot of a mountain—faded as the wind carried whispers of a hidden temple in the clouds. I followed, only to find the temple’s path redirected to a labyrinth of ancient ruins where time itself seemed to warp, merging past and present. The ruins whispered of a future where rivers ran backward, and the sky was a kaleidoscope of forgotten constellations.

A second compass appeared, pointing to a frozen lake where the ice cracked open to reveal a shipwrecked vessel, its hull carved with names of cities that no longer existed. The crew, now ghosts, urged me toward the horizon, which dissolved into a city of floating islands, each a new destination, each a memory erased before I could grasp it.

The journey ended not at a place, but in the endless loop of the wind itself, carrying me toward something I couldn’t name—only the certainty that every destination was a starting point for another, and that the only constant was the endless chase.
