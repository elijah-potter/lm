The neon glow of the alley pulsed like a dying heartbeat as I ducked behind the rusted dumpster, my breath fogging the cold air. A pair of scuffed boots clattered against the pavement, then a low, strained voice—*"You’re gonna pay for this!"*—echoed through the silence. The second figure, a broad-shouldered guy in a faded hoodie, lunged toward the one in a torn leather jacket, fists swinging. I didn’t move fast enough. The leather jacket was gone in a spray of blood and glass, the guy’s arm snapped back, his scream cut short.

A squad car’s siren wailed. The first officer, a young guy with a badge still glinting, rushed over, his face pale. "Get out of here!" he hissed, shoving me toward the nearest dumpster. The second officer, a grizzled veteran, knelt beside the body, his radio crackling. The crowd thinned as cops began rounding up witnesses. I stayed low, my fingers twitching toward my phone. The news would call this a "domestic altercation," but my stomach twisted. The guy in the jacket had been alone, and now he was just another name in the system.

The cop’s radio buzzed again. "Sir, you need to come with us." I swallowed hard. No one asked for my name.
