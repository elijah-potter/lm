The air smells faintly of damp earth and pine, mingling with the scent of rain-soaked pavement. The sidewalk is littered with discarded wrappers and crumpled newspapers, yet there’s a quiet charm in the way the streetlights flicker against the overcast sky. The bench by the old fountain—its worn wooden slats still bearing the marks of decades of use—has seen more than one rainy afternoon.

Across the plaza, the clock tower’s hands tick away, their rhythm steady as the world around it. The café’s windows are dark, but the hum of a distant coffee machine and the occasional laugh from inside hint at life just out of sight. A tree, its branches heavy with autumn leaves, casts long shadows across the cobblestones. The way the light catches the edges of the pavement, the way the wind shifts through the park’s ruins, it’s all here—waiting.
