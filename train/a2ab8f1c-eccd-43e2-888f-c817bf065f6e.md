Whispers of wind through rusted iron gates of an abandoned churchyard, their creaks forming a chorus of forgotten prayers. Raindrops patter against stained glass, their colors blending into a shifting, translucent language. Beneath a crumbling stone archway, moss grows in spirals like fingers tracing the walls. Inside a hollowed-out tree stump, a moth’s wings catch the dim light filtering through, its frantic dance a slow, deliberate pulse—every flicker a question. The air hums with static, the kind that makes the skin prickle. Shadows stretch unnaturally, their edges dissolving into something alive, watching. The earth beneath your feet trembles—just slightly—like it’s holding its breath.
