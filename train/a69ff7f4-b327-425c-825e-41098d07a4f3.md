The garden was a quiet place, though not quite deserted. The roses—those stubborn, overgrown things—drooped under the relentless weight of summer, their petals curling like old letters never sent. The bees buzzed around the dandelion at the edge of the lawn, not out of curiosity, but because they had no choice. The rain had stopped, but the puddles lingered like half-finished promises. The neighbor’s cat, usually a shadow at dusk, was nowhere to be seen today. Perhaps it had simply given up on the hunt.
