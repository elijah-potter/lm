The abandoned train station’s platform, half-collapsed under a canopy of ivy, hums with the ghostly echo of a distant train whistle. The air is thick with the scent of damp concrete and old, rotting wood. A single flickering bulb casts long shadows across the cracked floor, where a few discarded suitcases and rusted tools lie forgotten. The bench beneath you, once sturdy, now sags with age, its back broken, its arms half-collapsed. A rusted sign, half-eroded by time, reads: *"Stop Here."* The silence is not absolute—there’s the occasional rustle of leaves, the distant hum of a passing car, the occasional creak of wind through the broken windows. It’s a place where time, like the station itself, has slowed to a crawl.
