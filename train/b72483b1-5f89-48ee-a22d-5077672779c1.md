The walls of the dimly lit office were damp, condensation glistening on the chipped paint near the flickering fluorescent light. A half-empty coffee cup sat on the desk, its surface cracked, the remaining liquid now a dark, thick sludge. A single tear stained the bottom right corner of the open laptop screen, where a half-finished spreadsheet still flickered with the remnants of data—rows of numbers crossed out in red ink, some scribbled over with hastily written notes in pencil.

The desk was a mess: a half-eaten sandwich wrapper, a crumpled receipt, a half-open book with the pages turned to a scene of a handwritten map and a single bloodstain near the center. The floorboards groaned underfoot, a loose panel near the door creaking slightly as someone—perhaps the person who left—had just stepped out. The air smelled of stale air conditioning, burnt coffee, and something metallic beneath it, like old blood. A single candle, unlit, stood on a small table near the window, its wick unburned, its wax cool. The curtains were drawn, but the blinds were half-raised, allowing just enough light to show that outside, a storm raged, thunder rumbling in the distance.
