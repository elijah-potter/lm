I was watching an inspirational video about a group of sixth-graders growing their own food, having it sold in their school cafeteria, and actually making money. That's interesting on its own; however, it's not the end of the story.
I got to thinking about the concept of growing your own food. The reality is that it's nice to have others do things like making our clothes, growing our food and building our furniture. But there is something appealing about growing the food you eat. It's nice to know exactly how it was grown and that there were no pesticides used, and it's rewarding to see the fruits of your labor on your own dining table.
You'd be amazed by how many different kinds of vegetables you can grow in even a relatively small backyard.
Here are a few tips and suggestions to get you started with creating your own garden farm.
According to Craig Jenkins-Sutton, owner of Topiarius Urban Garden and Floral Design (www.topiarius.com), there are four steps to creating an urban vegetable garden:
-âChoose the site: Make sure the spot you choose gets at least six hours of sunlight each day. Preferably the location will also have easy access to water and be protected from strong wind.
-âAssess and build the soil: Testing the soil to understand what it needs is an important step. Many garden centers have test kits you can purchase for this purpose. Use the results to guide you as you make the appropriate adjustments. For many urban gardeners, the soil may contain contaminants. If this is the case it may make sense to build raised beds and bring in organic soil.
-âIdentify sources for seeds or plants: This is becoming much less difficult as many seed companies are offering so many options. Purchasing starter plants is still a little more difficult. Try asking at local farmers markets and garden centers.
-âResearch and locate organic gardening products for both pest control and fertilizer: Please understand that many of these products will have application warnings and can still be harmful.
Here are a few smart places to start buying seeds for your garden:
