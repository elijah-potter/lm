Two detectives stood outside the diner at closing time, both staring at the same empty booth where the last customer had left. Detective Reynolds adjusted his glasses and leaned against the brick wall, arms crossed.

**"She said he called her ‘sweetheart’ before he left,"** Reynolds muttered, tapping the worn tabletop. **"But she was arguing with him—slammed her bag down, said he was ‘stealing from her.’"**

Detective Carter crossed his arms, squinting at the alley behind them. **"I heard the same thing, but the guy was laughing. Said she was just mad because he was late. ‘You’re always late, sweetheart,’ he called out—like he was teasing her."**

A gust of wind rattled the window. Carter’s jaw tightened. **"She’s got a point. He was in a rush—had to pick up his kid at the hospital. But the way she *reacted*—like he’d betrayed her. That’s not late, that’s abandonment."**

Reynolds exhaled sharply. **"Abandonment or jealousy? Both could be true. Or neither. The body is cold, the crime scene’s empty. All we have is her words—and his smirk."**
