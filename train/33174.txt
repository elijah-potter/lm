The American flag is a strong sign of American identity and national pride. Also known as Old Glory, the U.S. flag has a vibrant history and also has gone through numerous adjustments from the very first official flag of 1777.
Today the U.S. flag consists of thirteen straight stripes, seven red rotating with 6 white stripes. The stripes represent the original 13 colonies; the stars represent the 50 states of the nation. The shades of the flag are symbolic as well: red represents hardiness and also valor, white symbolizes pureness and virtue as well as blue stands for vigilance, determination and justice. The reason the Continental Congress originally picked the red, white, and also blue shades was not made clear in the resolution embracing the flag. Chroniclers think it was most likely a color selection based on the British Union Jack, which had formerly flown over the colonies.
Over time, some have actually connected a little various meanings to the three colors, for example, the shade red representing the bloodshed spilled to maintain our liberty, but the significance of the original definition has been rather regular from 1782.
Just how did the American flag turn into what it is today?
It is a lot more than the 3 colors or a “decor”. Consider the locations around the world that the American flag has actually flown, think of the improvement it has gone through throughout the years on American land. It is absolutely humbling to think of all that was offered and also compromised to make sure that the American flag can fly freely across this nation.
The flag that began with just 13 stars expanded to 50 with the addition of states to the Union. The variety of stars on the flag slowly increased to its existing number today wherein a brand-new star would be added to the blue area on the 4th of July after the day of each new state’s admission.
The number of rotating horizontal red and also white stripes has actually remained at thirteen except from 1795 to 1818 when fifteen stripes appeared on the flag to show the admission of Kentucky and Vermont to the Union. In 1818, it was agreed that adding a stripe to the flag for every new state would not take place as it will make the flag look crowded and it would make the flag unwieldy. It was decided then that the flag would return to including just thirteen stripes to stand for the original colonies.
The American flag is an icon not just of hardiness, valiance, pureness, virtue, vigilance, perseverance as well as justice; it is a sign of freedom. Liberty that has actually been fought so hard for over the decades. Liberty that has cost this nation and the households within so much, but it is still a sign to those wanting they had the liberty that the country has.
Folding up the American flag.
Typical flag etiquette prescribes that before an American flag is stored or presented, its handlers ought to twice fold it in half lengthwise; after that (from the other end opposite the blue field) make a triangular fold, remaining to fold it in a triangular patter til the other end is reached. This makes a triangular “pillow” of the flag with only heaven starred area revealing outside, and also it takes thirteen folds to produce: two lengthwise folds as well as eleven triangular ones.
The flag isn’t folded up in this manner since each of the folds has a special symbolic significance; the flag is folded this way since it provides a dignified ceremonial touch that distinguishes folding a flag from folding a common object such as a blanket, and since it results a visually pleasing, easy-to-handle form. This thirteen-fold procedure was a typical technique long before the creation of a ritualistic assignation of “suggesting” to every of the steps.
An elaborate flag folding ceremony incorporating these meanings has from then on been devised for special events such as Memorial Day and Veterans Day. These associations are “real” in the sense that they indicate something to individuals who take part in the event, but they are not the reason why a flag is folded up in the traditional thirteen-step way.
