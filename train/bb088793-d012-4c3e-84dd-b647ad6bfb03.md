The shifting sands of Tidehaven, where erosion carves ever-new paths along the jagged shoreline. Time’s relentless tide reshapes the cliffs, burying old ruins beneath new dunes. The ruins of a forgotten lighthouse stand half-submerged, its glass panes cracked by storms that rearrange the pebbles into new constellations. The harbor’s moored ships drift like driftwood, their hulls worn smooth by the ceaseless wave action. Even the riverbank’s vegetation blooms, wilts, and returns in cycles no one can predict. A solitary watchtower, half-collapsed, still holds a clock face that doesn’t tell time—just the next moment. The air hums with the scent of salt and decay, where memory and absence merge seamlessly.
