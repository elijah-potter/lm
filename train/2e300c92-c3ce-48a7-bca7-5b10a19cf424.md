The morning sun spilled through cracked windows, painting the room in gold. She sat at the kitchen table, coffee cup half-empty, fingers tracing the rim. *"What if I was wrong?"* The thought had seeped in like smoke—small, insistent, creeping from the edges of her mind.

Then—something shifted. The weight of the question dissolved into a whisper. The phone buzzed; a text from him: *"You okay?"* The answer was a lie she’d been telling herself for years. But in that moment, the lie was just noise, and the truth, fragile as morning dew, clung to her like a ghost.

She exhaled, the breath shallow. The clarity wasn’t there anymore—not like it had been. It had been a spark, a half-remembered flame that flickered out too soon. The room blurred. The words she’d meant to write dissolved into the air like ink on water.
