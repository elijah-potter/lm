A silent violation of protocol often occurs when norms are unspoken yet taken for granted. For instance, the unspoken rule that "no one should ask for help" persists despite its clear utility. This is a rule broken not by defiance, but by habit—each individual assumes others will step in when needed, leaving gaps that only reveal themselves in crises. Another example is the tacit agreement that "someone else will clean up after themselves," fostering an environment where responsibility is deferred until it becomes impossible to ignore. These quiet breaches erode trust incrementally, invisible to those who benefit from the system’s laxity. The damage isn’t in the act itself, but in the collective decision to let it happen.
