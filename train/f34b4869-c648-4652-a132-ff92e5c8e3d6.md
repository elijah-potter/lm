A sunrise too pale, its warmth barely piercing the gray of the morning. The city hums with quiet machines, each moving at its own pace, like a clock stuck in slow motion. Coffee is brewed too fast—steeped too long, bitter and bitter still—while the toast burns unevenly, its edges crisp, its center still soft.

The commute feels like a sprint in reverse. Traffic moves in slow motion, a parade of headlights and taillights, the endless repetition of stoplights blinking like a heartbeat too fast. The air smells like exhaust and stale coffee, a mix of nostalgia and resignation.

Work begins before the mind is truly awake. Emails pile up like unread chapters, deadlines stretch like taut wires. Meetings blur into one endless loop, where ideas dissolve into noise. The lunch break feels like a betrayal—ten minutes of silence too precious to waste, but also too brief to truly disappear. The evening stretches, a thin line between work and home, the kitchen still buzzing with the hum of a half-finished dinner.

The homecoming is a relief, but the house feels like a cage. Chores wait, unspoken demands in the laundry pile or the dishes still warm from breakfast. The TV flickers on, offering distraction, but the screen is just another screen, another layer of noise.

Bedtime arrives, and the night is endless. The clock ticks away, a relentless countdown. Sleep eludes, thoughts whirl like a storm in a jar. The day feels like a marathon—too fast to finish, too slow to forget.

The next morning starts the same way, a cycle of exhaustion and repetition. The gray persists, and so does the sense that time itself is running out.
