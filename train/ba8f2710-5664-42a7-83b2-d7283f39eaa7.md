The 1960s **Space Needle** in Seattle was rejected by early developers due to skepticism about its "unnatural" appearance and fear of it symbolizing "corporate greed." Critics argued it looked like a "giant egg" or "monstrous mushroom" and that its modernist design was "too futuristic." Some local business owners worried it would overshadow historic landmarks. After persistent lobbying by the city and engineers, the design was refined and accepted, becoming an iconic symbol of Seattle’s skyline.
