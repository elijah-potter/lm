The room was dimly lit by the flickering glow of a single lamp. Outside, a steady rain pattered against the windows, the sound muffled by the curtains. **Maria** sat at the wooden desk, her fingers tracing the edge of the open laptop screen. The screen was blank except for a single, glowing notification icon at the top.

She exhaled sharply, her breath fogging the glass. The phone beside her buzzed—another notification from the hospital. She ignored it, pressing her palm against the glass, the cold surface sharp under her skin.

Her phone buzzed again. A text from her brother, *Dad’s in surgery. They’re saying—waiting for results.* The words were barely legible in the dim light.

She opened the laptop, the screen suddenly alive with a blue progress bar. **Results in 10 minutes.** The countdown ticked down to zero. Her heart pounded, the noise of the rain outside growing louder as it drowned out her own breathing.

A single message appeared in the center of the screen:

**"Dad is stable. He’s awake. They say it’s a miracle."**

Maria’s hands trembled. She didn’t cry. She just stared at the screen, her vision blurring, until the words blurred into something else—something she couldn’t quite name.

Outside, the rain stopped. The world fell silent.
