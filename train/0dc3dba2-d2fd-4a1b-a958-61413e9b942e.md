## Project Chimera: Rulebook

**A Cooperative Game for 2-4 Players**

**Designer’s Note:** *Project Chimera aims to simulate the challenges of scientific crisis response, emphasizing the importance of collaboration, risk assessment, and ethical considerations. Players will confront difficult choices with limited information, mirroring the pressures faced by real-world researchers. The game is designed to foster discussion about the social responsibility of scientific advancement and the potential consequences of unforeseen outcomes. It is not intended to be a purely scientific simulation, but rather a framework for exploring these themes in a compelling and engaging way.*

---

### 1. Components

*   **Crisis Board:** A central board depicting a world map with designated research locations and escalating threat levels.
*   **Researcher Cards (12):** Each card represents a researcher with unique skills (Analysis, Logistics, Containment, Diplomacy) and starting resources.
*   **Threat Cards (60):** These cards drive the crisis, introducing new challenges, escalating existing threats, and triggering events. Divided into three tiers of increasing severity.
*   **Resource Tokens:** Representing Funding, Personnel, and Equipment.
*   **Containment Markers:** Used to suppress threat levels at research locations.
*   **Event Tokens:** Representing unexpected complications and opportunities.
*   **Research Tokens:** Track progress towards critical breakthroughs.
*   **Dice (6):** Six-sided dice used for skill checks and event resolution.
*   **First Player Marker:** Indicates the current active player.

---

### 2. Setup

1.  Place the Crisis Board in the center of the playing area.
2.  Shuffle the Threat Cards and place them face down in three separate piles, designated Tier 1, Tier 2, and Tier 3.
3.  Each player chooses a Researcher Card and takes the corresponding starting resources indicated on the card.
4.  Place three Containment Markers on the designated starting locations on the Crisis Board as indicated.
5.  Place the First Player Marker with the designated starting player.
6.  Draw the top three Tier 1 Threat Cards and place them face up beside their respective piles. These represent the initial threats.

---

### 3. Turn Structure

Each player's turn follows these phases in order:

1.  **Threat Phase:** Draw one Threat Card from the highest available Tier (Tier 1 initially). Resolve the card’s effects immediately. These effects may include increasing threat levels at locations, triggering events, or introducing new challenges.
2.  **Action Phase:** You may perform two actions from the following options. Actions can be the same or different.
    *   **Move:** Move your Researcher to an adjacent location on the Crisis Board.
    *   **Research:** Attempt to gain a Research Token at your current location. Roll two dice. The target number depends on the location’s threat level and your Researcher's Analysis skill. Success advances the location’s research track. Failure may trigger a negative event.
    *   **Contain:** Attempt to reduce a threat level at your current location. Roll two dice. The target number depends on the location’s threat level and your Researcher’s Containment skill. Success places a Containment Marker. Failure may increase the threat level.
    *   **Gather Resources:** Gain one resource of your choice (Funding, Personnel, or Equipment).
    *   **Diplomacy:** Spend one Personnel resource to attempt to mitigate the effects of a specific Threat Card. Roll one die. Success reduces the card’s severity. Failure has no effect.
3.  **End Phase:** Pass the First Player Marker to the player on your left.

---

### 4. Win and Loss Conditions

**Winning:** The players win the game if they collectively achieve a designated number of Research Tokens (dependent on player count - see Difficulty Scaling) before the Loss Condition is met.

**Losing:** The players lose the game if any of the following conditions are met:

*   **Global Threat Level:** The Global Threat Level marker reaches its maximum value. This marker increases as Threat Cards are resolved.
*   **Containment Failure:** A location’s threat level reaches its maximum value without sufficient Containment Markers. This triggers a cascading failure, potentially increasing the Global Threat Level.
*   **Resource Depletion:** The players collectively exhaust all available Funding resources.

---

### 5. Difficulty Scaling

The game's difficulty scales with the number of players, primarily affecting the number of Research Tokens required for victory and the initial threat level.

*   **2 Players:** 6 Research Tokens required. Start with two Tier 1 Threat Cards in play.
*   **3 Players:** 8 Research Tokens required. Start with two Tier 1 Threat Cards and one Tier 2 Threat Card in play.
*   **4 Players:** 10 Research Tokens required. Start with three Tier 1 Threat Cards and one Tier 2 Threat Card in play.

---

### 6. Advanced Variants & Trade-Off Mechanics

These variants introduce greater complexity and force difficult choices under uncertainty.

1.  **Ethical Dilemma Cards:** Introduce a separate deck of Ethical Dilemma Cards. At the start of each player's turn, draw one and resolve its effects. These cards present players with choices that have both positive and negative consequences, forcing them to weigh potential benefits against ethical considerations. *Example: “Accelerate Research: You can immediately gain one Research Token at a location, but increase the Global Threat Level by one.”*
2.  **Limited Communication:** Restrict communication between players during the Action Phase. Players can only verbally communicate a single word or phrase per turn. This forces players to rely on non-verbal cues and shared understanding, increasing the tension and challenge.
3.  **Resource Allocation Uncertainty:** At the start of each round (after all players have taken a turn), a single die is rolled. The result determines how resources are distributed among players. A low roll might mean all players receive minimal resources, while a high roll could signify a windfall. Players must adapt their strategies based on this unpredictable distribution. This mechanic simulates the inherent instability of funding and support for research projects.
4.  **Cascading Containment Failure (Optional):** When a location’s threat level reaches its maximum value, instead of immediately triggering a cascading failure, players must make a collective decision: attempt a desperate, high-risk Containment attempt (requiring all players to contribute resources and rolling a single, difficult die) or accept the consequences and trigger the cascading failure. This forces players to assess the potential rewards against the risk of exacerbating the crisis.
