Sarah was a fairly ordinary 32-year-old from Vermont. But she hid a secret that she kept to her grave. She swore to herself never to tell anyone. After all, she didn't want to end up as a science experiment in a lab. She could see up to a minute into the future and it was always accurate. Depending on the circumstance, she could change the outcome more than not.

Sarah's first big breakthrough with her gift was when she sensed that her grandmother was about to fall. She didn't know what was going to happen, but at 86, a broken femur or other critical bone would end her life in a hurry. Sarah opened her car door and charged onto the scene as fast as she could. Her grandmother was navigating up her mobile home steps and was on the first step. Sarah ran to catch her as her grandmother's foot slipped on the third step. Sarah lunged forward, saving her grandmother from an otherwise potentially fatal accident. 

Knowing this gift, Sarah became deeply involved in the stock market and enjoyed gambling. The #1 rule she told herself was to never take more than would be reasonable and never be suspicious. She wanted to use her gift to help others. She'd go to the roulette wheel and play her odds. She'd drop chips on the 2, 4, and 9, knowing they weren't going to gain anything. But the 14 was going to be the winner. In an hour, she'd come in with $100 and walk away with $1,800. The dealers shrugged it off as a lucky evening. But a $1,700 profit in an hour was nothing to scoff at. To diversify, she also experimented with some trading with stocks and options. It was scary how much money she could make. She severely limited her earnings to keep under the radar from the SEC.

By the time she was 50, she had earned $19 million in profits, mostly from gambling and trading. She gave most of it back anonymously to her family, friends, community, and those in need. She hoped to be an inspiration for others to give back and change the world.
