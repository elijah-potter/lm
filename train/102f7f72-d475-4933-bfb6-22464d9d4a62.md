A dense fog clings to the outskirts of a crumbling industrial district, where the last neon signs flicker weakly against the overcast sky. New construction lines the riverbank, skeletal steel frames jutting into the air like skeletal fingers. Workers in hard hats move in deliberate, rhythmic patterns, their boots echoing on concrete as they align the first sections of the elevated walkway. The air hums with the distant sound of machinery, the scent of damp earth and freshly poured concrete mixing with exhaust fumes.

At the center of the construction zone, a prototype energy grid hums quietly, its panels arranged in a spiral, pulsing faintly with light. Nearby, a community garden sprouts between the foundations of abandoned warehouses, where children and elders gather to plant seeds. The routine here is a mix of precision and chaos—everyone has a role, but the pace is fluid, adapting as the city breathes new life into its bones. The old ways are fading, but the new rhythm is still learning how to hold its shape.
