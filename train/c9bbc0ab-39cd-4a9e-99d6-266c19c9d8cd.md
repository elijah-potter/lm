The cycle begins at dawn with a faint, unchanging alarm, yet the first light shifts imperceptibly each morning—just enough to make the coffee brew differently. The same streets, same buildings, same faces, but the air hums with a subtle, ever-changing current. A neighbor’s dog barks at the same mail carrier, but the carrier’s gait is slightly faster, or slower, or wears the same hat differently today.

The same breakfast—eggs, toast, black coffee—is prepared with the same motions, yet the toast burns a hair darker, the eggs a whisper richer, or the coffee’s first sip tastes faintly of yesterday’s rain. The morning news plays the same headlines, but the announcer’s tone varies, and the newsroom’s shadows move as if they’ve shifted overnight.

By midday, the same lunch crowd gathers at the same diner, but the waitress’s apron has a new stain, a new smile, or a new way of setting the table. The same arguments unfold with the same words, yet the room feels colder or warmer, the air thicker or lighter.

Evenings mirror the day, but the stars align differently. The same book is read, the same song played, the same bed made—yet the rhythm of life feels like it’s always been slightly off. Tomorrow repeats, yet the small, unnoticed differences accumulate until the day feels like a ghost of yesterday’s ghost.
