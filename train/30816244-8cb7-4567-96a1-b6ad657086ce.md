A morning where the alarm’s bleak chirp stretched too long,
sunrise blurred by a haze of caffeine’s slow ascent.
The commute—crowded, humming, a heartbeat of steel and noise—
slowed by the weight of the day’s endless, invisible stretch.

Lunch was a sandwich eaten too fast, the crumbs still clinging to time,
while the clock’s hands marched forward like a metronome counting down
to the next meeting, the next errand, the next thing that would
fill the empty spaces between now and tomorrow.

Evening arrived too soon, a tired sigh after the day’s relentless march.
The bed felt like a stranger’s couch, and sleep was just another
promise broken by the clock’s relentless, unyielding march.

A day that stretched too thin, too fast, too much—
time a commodity, life a transaction,
and the hours, like shadows, slipped through fingers too quick to hold.
