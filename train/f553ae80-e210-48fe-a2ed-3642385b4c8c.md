**The Last Transmission**

We didn’t mean for this to happen.

The server room was silent except for the hum of old hardware—flickering monitors, dust motes dancing in the fluorescent glow. The air smelled like ozone and something metallic, like the edge of a forgotten war. Outside, the city pulsed with life, but inside, we were just three of them, staring at the screen.

*Error: Critical failure in primary cooling loop.*

A man in a rumpled lab coat grabbed the edge of his desk. His fingers trembled. “We were just testing the system again—”

The door burst open. A woman in a high-collared coat, her face pale under the emergency lights, stepped inside. “You’re the ones who triggered it,” she said, voice low. “We have to stop it.”

The screen flashed red. A warning appeared: **DO NOT SWITCH OFF POWER.**

The man’s eyes widened. “You don’t understand—”

The lights went out. In the darkness, only the faint glow of static remained. Then, a voice—distorted, like static itself—whispered from the speakers:

*“It’s already here.”*
