The clock struck seven, but she was still halfway across the campus, her heels clicking sharply against the pavement. Behind her, the early-bird line at the coffee shop stretched like a snake, and the last seat in the lecture hall was already occupied. Her phone buzzed—another reminder: *Late again?*

She exhaled sharply through her nose, gripping her bag tighter. The relief was immediate, like a weight lifting off her shoulders. She’d made it, just. The professor’s voice drifted from the auditorium—*“Class, please stay for the presentation…”*—but she barely heard it. The guilt was there, always lurking, but today, it felt like a distant memory. She’d been caught up in the moment, the way time seemed to slow when you were running out of options.

A sharp *ding* cut through the noise—her phone, a new message: *Where are you?*

She ignored it. The walk home would be easier now.
