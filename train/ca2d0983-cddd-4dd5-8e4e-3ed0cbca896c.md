The rain had turned the alley into a mirror, reflecting the flickering neon glow of distant streetlights. Elias adjusted his gloves, the fabric damp against his fingers. The letter in his pocket—*not* his own name on it—had been waiting for him since last week. The postmark read *New Orleans*, and the ink had smeared with age, though not enough to hide the handwriting: *Come alone. Meet at the docks at midnight.*

He exhaled slowly, the breath curling like smoke in the cold air. His reflection in the puddles was blurred, but his eyes were sharp—too sharp. The city hummed around him, a thousand voices, a thousand lies. He had come for answers, not just this one.

The door of the abandoned bookstore creaked open behind him, revealing the dim interior. Dust motes danced in the dim light, and the scent of old paper and something metallic lingered. He stepped inside, the weight of the letter pressing against his chest. Somewhere, a clock ticked. The city waited.

The docks were silent except for the creak of the rusted pier. Elias stood at the edge, the tide lapping at his boots. The boat was already there—small, weathered, its hull slick with salt. A single lantern burned on its deck, casting long shadows. He didn’t look back.

The motor roared to life, and the boat cut through the water, vanishing into the blackness. The city’s lights blinked out behind him. He didn’t turn around. The rain kept falling, but it was no longer his problem.
