The morning light, a delicate curtain of gold, filtered through the half-open blinds like a patient breath. The coffee, steeping in the old mug, had settled into a quiet amber, neither too strong nor too weak—just right for a man who had chosen to rise before the sun.

Across the room, the cat watched from the windowsill, tail curled like a question mark, as if to say: *I see you’re here, but do you see me?*

The newspaper’s headline—*"Local Businesses Recover"*—was printed in bold, though the ink had faded over time, as if the words themselves had been worn down by the passage of small, unremarkable days. The phone rang once. It was the delivery guy. Another package. Another quiet promise of something more than what was already there.
