The morning sun spilled golden streaks through the half-drawn blinds of my apartment, casting elongated shadows across the floor. I sat at my desk, fingers tracing the edge of my coffee cup, the steam curling upward like a forgotten ghost. Outside, the city hummed—a symphony of distant horns and the occasional siren—but inside, it was quiet, too quiet, the silence thick with something heavier than air.

I had spent the night hunched over my laptop, the glow of the screen bleeding into my eyes, my mind a tangled mess of half-formed thoughts. But now, as the light touched my face, something shifted. The weight of the unknown pressed against my chest, and suddenly, the answer wasn’t just there—it was *visible*, a flicker in the corner of my vision, like a candle in the dark. For a single, perfect moment, the pieces clicked into place: the way the words had been arranged, the way the question had hung between us, the way the silence had felt like an invitation.

Then—

The phone buzzed. A notification from a project I’d abandoned months ago, an email from a colleague asking about something that didn’t matter anymore. The clarity dissolved like smoke in the breeze. The answer, once sharp and clear, now felt like a memory, something I’d once known but had let slip through my fingers. The city outside kept its rhythm, indifferent. The moment of clarity had been a dream, and now the world went on as it always did—loud, relentless, and impossible to hold.

The coffee was cold. The screen was still. And somewhere, out there, the answer waited, just out of reach again.
