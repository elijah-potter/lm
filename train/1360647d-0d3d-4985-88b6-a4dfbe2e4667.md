The rain fell in quiet, unhurried sheets outside the window, each drop a whisper against the glass. Inside, the air smelled like old paper and stale coffee, the kind of smell that lingered after a morning spent in a half-empty library. I had been here for three weeks, curled up in this corner with a half-finished novel, my fingers tracing the spine of a book I’d read twenty times already.

The first time I noticed it, it was just a small thing—a loose thread on the sleeve of my favorite sweater, the kind of detail that didn’t matter until it did. Then came the way the light caught the rim of my coffee cup at exactly eleven o’clock every morning, how my reflection in the window always looked slightly younger than I was. But the truth I’d been holding for months was simpler than any of those things.

I had a key.

Not just any key. One that fit into a lock I’d never used before I moved here. It was in the bottom drawer of my dresser, beside the half-empty bottle of whiskey and the half-finished sketchbook. The lock was on a box I’d never opened, one I’d found in the attic after my parents’ divorce, the one with the faded label: *"For when you’re ready."* I’d stared at that key for hours, running it between my fingers, wondering what it had been meant to open. What if it had been a door? A secret room? A letter I’d never read?

I had to stop thinking about it. The truth was, I didn’t even know what it was for anymore. It was just a key, like the others, like the one that unlocked the door to my childhood bedroom, the one that held my old toys and the half-buried photo of my grandparents. But this one was different. It was the kind of thing you keep for years, not because you need it, but because you’re afraid to lose it. Because you’re afraid that if you did, you’d have to admit something.

So I didn’t open the box. I didn’t look inside. And I never told anyone.

But the rain kept falling, and the novel kept gathering dust, and somewhere in the dark, the key waited, still.
