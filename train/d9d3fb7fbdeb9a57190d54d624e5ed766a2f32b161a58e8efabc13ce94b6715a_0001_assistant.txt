The UK will soon begin restoration projects in two "Celtic rainforests" through native planting and natural restoration. The first is Creg y Cowin on the Isle of Man, which will have 28 hectares, or 70 acres, planted by hand with native species and another 8 hectares, or 20 acres, left for natural regeneration. The second is Gwynedd in North Wales, where 40 hectares, or 112 acres, will undergo a combination of native planting and natural regeneration. The project aims to provide high-quality habitats for native species, improve flood prevention and water quality, and allow locals and tourists alike to appreciate the rare temperate rainforest biome. These restoration projects were funded by a £30 million donation from Aviva, a UK insurance company hoping to sequester carbon in the rainforests.
