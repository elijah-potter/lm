A suffocating mist clings to the skeletal remains of a crumbling cathedral, its stained-glass windows shattered into jagged fragments that refract the last embers of a dying sunset. The air is thick with the scent of damp earth and something metallic—rust, perhaps, or the faint, lingering perfume of the old incense that never burned fully. Trees, their branches twisted into grotesque, half-buried shapes, press against the skeletal frames of a forgotten park, their leaves blackened and brittle. A frozen lake mirrors the oppressive sky, its surface so still it seems to have absorbed the world’s breath entirely. The silence is a pulse, a slow, deliberate exhalation, each breath taken by the land itself, waiting for something—or nothing—to wake.
