FRB 20190520B is a fast radio burst (FRB) that emits radio waves persistently, indicating it may be a young source and has been selected for long-term study. The selection of the FRB was motivated by its similarity to the well-studied FRB 20121102A, which also exists in a highly magnetized plasma environment. Researchers from West Virginia University observed FRB 20190520B for 17 months using telescopes in Australia and the US. The FRB's Faraday rotation showed high variability, suggesting a turbulent magnetic environment at the source. The team hypothesized that the observed variations were caused by a wind from a massive companion star, which was reinforced by a similar system in the Milky Way.
