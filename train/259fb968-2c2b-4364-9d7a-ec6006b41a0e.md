**The Last Light**
The hallway stretched into darkness, walls lined with faded photos of a younger woman—her hands folded in prayer, a child’s laughter echoing from a childhood home. A single bulb flickered, casting long shadows of a man kneeling beside a crumpled newspaper. The date was scrawled in ink: *"Your father’s words, never forgotten."*

His voice was rough, but his eyes burned with grief when he whispered:
*"I promised you… I’d always be here."* The air smelled of rain and old wood, the scent of a life half-lived.

Outside, the wind howled through the broken windowpanes. The girl—now in her thirties—held a photo of them, both standing on a porch in the rain, her father’s hand in hers. *"You were a boy then,"* she murmured, *"but you were my everything."* The camera had missed the tears, the way his fingers trembled when he signed the document. The paper was wet with his signature, but the ink had run—like time, like the promise itself.
