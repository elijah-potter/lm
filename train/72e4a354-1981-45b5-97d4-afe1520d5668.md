The ruins of Eldermere’s last cathedral, half-buried beneath the moss-stained stones of what was once the River’s Edge. The spires lean at impossible angles, their golden mosaics cracked by time, yet the stained glass still shimmers faintly—glowing blue when the mist rolls in from the Blackwood. Locals whisper that the river’s waters here whisper back in fragments of forgotten hymns, the last echoes of a choir that sang for a thousand years.

The old inn, the *Whispering Hound*, stands now as a shell of its former self—its sign carved by desperate hands, its walls lined with names scratched into the wood: *Lirien*, *Cassia*, *the boy who vanished in the fog*. The tavern’s hearth still crackles, but the fire burns low, and the air smells of damp earth and something older—something that was once a scent of wine and laughter.

The bridge over the river is a story. It was said to have been built by the same architect who designed the cathedral, its arches adorned with runes that glow when the moon is high. Now, it’s a crumbling archway, its stones slick with algae, the only place where travelers dare to cross. Some say the bridge is still there, beneath the water, waiting for those who remember how to find it.

The village of Duskhaven was once a thriving market town, its streets paved with cobblestones that still bear the imprints of hooves and cartwheels. Now, only the church bell tower stands, its bell silent, its clock face frozen at 3:17—the last time anyone was seen alive here. The market square is overgrown with ivy, and the old well’s water runs black, but if you listen closely on still nights, you can hear the faint sound of laughter, like a chorus of voices just beyond hearing.
