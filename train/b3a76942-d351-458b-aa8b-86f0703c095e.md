The pen slipped from my fingers as the first draft of the story unraveled beneath my hands. I leaned back in the chair, the scent of old paper and coffee lingering in the air like a ghost from a past I couldn’t quite grasp.

*"Should I have started with the storm?"* The doubt coiled tighter than the thread of my own voice. The words on the page felt like a lie—too neat, too polished. The characters moved as if they’d been sculpted from clay, but I knew clay doesn’t hold its shape unless you’re willing to break it again and again.

Outside, the rain tapped against the window, slow at first, then relentless. It was as if the world itself was asking questions. What if they hated it? What if it was just me, the one who couldn’t decide? The doubt wasn’t mine alone. It was the weight of every decision I’d ever made—every time I’d chosen one path over another, even when I didn’t know which was right.

I closed my eyes, trying to listen to something beyond the noise. But the only thing I heard was my own breath, uneven, like it was struggling to stay steady.

The story needed more. More hesitation. More fear. More of the thing that made me human—not just the words on the page, but the space between them, where the real work happened.

I reached for the eraser, but the page was already gone. Just the silence.
