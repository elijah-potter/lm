In a dimly lit village square, an elderly man, wrapped in a frayed cloak, paused before a fork in the winding road. The path ahead forked: one led steeply uphill, the other a broader trail downhill. He had traveled this same road for decades, always taking the shorter route. Yet today, his cane trembled slightly, and his eyes flickered with uncertainty.

He chose the longer path. The incline was relentless, his boots sinking slightly into the loose stones, but he pressed on. The villagers watched from their windows, some murmuring in disbelief, others in envy. The air grew thicker, carrying the scent of damp earth and wildflowers. At the crest of the hill, he found a small clearing where a single, ancient oak stood guard over a hidden spring. The water was clear, and beneath its surface, the village’s oldest well pulsed with life. This was not the path he had known—it was the one that had waited for him.
