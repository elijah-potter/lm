One winter’s evening in a small village nestled between mist-shrouded mountains, a lantern flickered in the chilled air as a lone traveler paused at an old stone bridge. The river below churned with ice, its surface reflecting the last golden light of a setting sun. The traveler’s hands trembled as they unfurled a crumpled letter, its edges yellowed with age. The words inside were a promise—*"Meet me at the crossroads by midnight, or forever lose this chance."* The wind carried the scent of pine and distant smoke, and for the first time in years, the traveler’s heart raced with something like hope. That moment, between the lantern’s glow and the frozen river, was the weight of a choice that would echo through the seasons. It could not be repeated—because time, like the river’s ice, had its own rules.
