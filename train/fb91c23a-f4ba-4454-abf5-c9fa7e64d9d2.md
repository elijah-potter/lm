The first light of dawn crept through the cracks in the curtains, painting the room in soft, golden streaks. Elias sat at his desk, fingers tracing the edges of the half-finished manuscript, the ink still damp on the paper. Outside, the city slept, a quiet hum of distant traffic and the occasional chirp of birds that had found their way into the rooftop gardens.

He had written for hours, lost in the rhythm of the words, the way they flowed like water through a river. But now, the page was bare except for a few scattered lines that didn’t quite fit. The story needed more—the weight of betrayal, the ache of longing, the sharp edges of truth.

A knock at the door made him jump. His mother stood there, her face lined with exhaustion but her eyes bright with something unspoken. She didn’t say anything, just stepped inside, the scent of coffee and something sweet—maybe cinnamon—washing over him. She poured him a cup, her hands steady, and sat beside him.

“You’re writing again,” she said, voice low.

He didn’t answer. He couldn’t. The words were still tangled in his mind, raw and alive, but the story needed more than that. It needed the sky.

She reached out, her fingers brushing his arm. “What does the sky look like when you’re writing?”

The question hit him like a slap. He exhaled sharply, the air thick with the weight of the unsaid. “It’s always different,” he muttered. “Sometimes it’s a vast, endless blue, so deep it feels like it could swallow you whole. Other times, it’s like a storm has passed, leaving behind a pale, bruised gray—dark and heavy, as if the world is holding its breath.”

She nodded, her expression unreadable. “Like your stories.”

He swallowed. “I don’t know.”

Outside, the sun rose higher, casting long shadows across the streets below. The city awoke, its lights flickering to life like dying embers rekindled. Above them, the sky stretched endlessly, a canvas of shifting hues—purples bleeding into oranges, streaked with the first hints of dawn’s pale light. It was vast and untamed, a reminder of the world beyond the walls of his apartment, beyond the words he struggled to put on the page.

The manuscript lay unfinished, but the sky was always there, waiting.
