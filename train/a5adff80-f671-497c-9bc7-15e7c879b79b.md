The rain fell in silence, each drop a whisper against the cracked pavement, carrying secrets no one dared to unearth. Inside the dimly lit café, the scent of stale coffee mingled with the faint metallic tang of old memories. Elias sipped his black coffee, fingers tracing the rim of his mug, the weight of unspoken words pressing against his chest.

She was the only one who saw the truth—how the world had become a stage for performances, where kindness was just another act, and love was a fleeting shadow in the light of convenience. He had spent years building walls, each one a brick of regret, each one a promise left unkept. But tonight, the weight of those walls felt like a thousand knives digging into his soul.

He reached across the table, his hand trembling as he brushed his thumb against her knuckles. Her breath hitched, and for a moment, the air between them crackled with something raw and dangerous. Then she looked away, her voice barely a whisper when she spoke.

"You’ve been hiding something."

Elias exhaled sharply, the breath carrying the taste of defeat. The admission was easier than he thought—until he saw the way she stared at him, as if the truth had already been written in the lines of his face. He had spent so long running, so long pretending, that the moment he finally let himself fall, the ground beneath him crumbled.

He deserved it.
