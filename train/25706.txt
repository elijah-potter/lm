Eastern Lake County planners hoping to balance drainage
Local planners want to make sure eastern Lake County isn't literally left up the creek.
Planners and environmental watchdogs want to prevent any aridity drying up wineries, nurseries and residential lawns and even to halt any flooding of the same grounds mainly in a Madison-Perry area with multiple creeks.
That's why planners and environmental watchdog groups seek a $100,000 state grant for a "Balanced Growth Plan" to survey current quality, land use, zoning, soil conditions, infrastructure and stormwater management, county Planning Commission Director Jason W. Boyd said.
"Over the long term, we have better success rate working as a group as opposed to one community trying to handle a drainage or soil problem. Hopefully, our data will wind up on the community bookshelf when the plan comes out," he said.
As outlined by Boyd, the study would focus on the "Eastern Lake County Coastal Tributaries Watershed" in a creek-filled area vulnerable to "poor drainage" off Lake Erie, the Grand River and Arcola Creek in Madison Township.
Most of the tributaries are Ice Age remnants noted in scientist G.W. White's "Glacial Geology of Northeast Ohio" and are "unnamed or significantly named watercourses."
Among places watched by the Ohio Department of Natural Resources and U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, six named places are:
* McKinley Creek in Perry Township.
* Red Mill Creek and Red Creek in Perry Village and in Perry and Madison townships.
* Church Creek in Madison Township and North Perry Village. Continued...
In this stormwater management area, the six districts each might get a "rain garden in a public place" to salute and stress their heritage.
Boyd's office, the county Geographical Information System Department, county Soil and Water Conservation District and county Stormwater Department would mainly study the idea of balanced growth.
Lake County's "plain area" was a small Midwest part of the 1930s' "Great Plains" of severe drought and sandstorms. The SWC District was established in 1946 as part of a major national post-World War II corrective plains effort.
As written, with the White geology study cited, Boyd's proposed plan states:
* "These streams and the numerous unnamed tributaries are draining 30 square miles of land" in the Madison-Perry area, Fairport Harbor and Painesville Township.
* "The lake plain is a small flat area comprised of silt and fine sand with small sand and gravel beach ridges. With the exception of the beach ridge, drainage is generally poor due to the fine grained soils."
Perry Village is still trying to control its Red Creek, which flows into the Grand River, and Red Mill Creek, which flows into Lake Erie.
The ODNR said the Perry creeks sometimes rise 4 feet higher in potentially dangerous flooding in areas flowing around Main Street and railroad tracks.
* About 51 percent of the eastern county watershed is development, 66 percent of which is residential and the remainder either agricultural or vacant, the Boyd proposal noted.
For that area, damages can be caused by two things, the U.S. EPA says. Continued...
One is "hydro-modification." It changes the volume, speed or timing of high and low flows in a water body and damages properties.
The other effect is "nonpoint pollution," which comes from almost untraceable multiple sources from anywhere.
The EPA wrote: "A major cause (of such damages) is development, which changes vegetation and covers land with roofs, sidewalks, streets and parking lots. Rainwater, unable to soak into soil, rushes with flash-flood-like intensity to streams."
Boyd wants his group's study to focus on such potential damages.
"Hydro-modification in agricultural and urban areas would be the greatest threat," Boyd wrote. "Additional threats would include nonpoint source pollution from stormwater inputs and/or pesticide/fertilizer inputs from agricultural areas."
The six communities in the study have yet to collectively participate in a "holistic plan" based on watershed boundaries, the county planning director said.
"The study area is certain to face developmental pressures in coming years as the county's population shift continues its west to east movement," Boyd wrote.
Almost all central and western Lake County towns already have a balanced growth plan for such areas as Newell Creek in Mentor, Kellogg Creek in Concord Township, and Ward and Corporation creeks in Eastlake.
Most fix-up and preventive costs were paid with state and federal money or with some funds from county government.
The eastern Lake County groups' update would "provide local/regional decision makers an additional tool to address the complex issues often accompanied by land development," Boyd wrote. Continued...
