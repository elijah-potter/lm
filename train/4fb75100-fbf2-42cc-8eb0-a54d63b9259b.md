The words *‘she was laughing’* appear in the search bar, typed without thought—yet the screen fills with faces of a woman you’ve never met, but whose name you’ve known since childhood. A news article on a broken marriage, a photo of her at a wedding you never attended. The algorithm, cold and precise, matches the fragments of a memory you’ve suppressed for decades.

The email arrives: *"We found your missing sister. She’s in rehab."* The screen glitches, then stabilizes—her name, her age, her last known location. The system whispers in your ear: *‘This is who you were searching for all along.’* The moment is split second: doubt, then certainty. The phone rings—it’s her, her voice crackling through static, familiar but distorted. The screen fades to black, replaced by the sound of her laughter, the same sound that haunted your dreams for years. *"You found me,"* she says, *"I just didn’t know how."* The room spins. The keyboard glows. You type: *"I was looking for you."* The page refreshes. The moment is gone.
