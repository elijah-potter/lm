The rain fell in silence, each drop a whispered secret against the windowpane. I sat in the dim glow of the lamp, fingers tracing the edge of my coffee cup, the steam curling like a ghost. Outside, the city pulsed—voices, distant laughter, the hum of a thousand unseen lives—but none of them knew.

I had always thought the truth was too heavy to carry, like a stone too large to lift. But today, I realized it was just another door, one I’d been afraid to open.

The truth? That I had once stolen an apple from the bakery stand. Not for hunger, but for the thrill of it—because I wanted to. Not because I needed it. Just… because.

The first time, I was twelve. The second, sixteen. The third, I stopped. Not because I felt guilty, but because the weight of it had become my own. I had turned into a thief of stories, not apples, but lies I told to escape the ones I kept. The truth, I learned, wasn’t a burden—it was a key. And I had been afraid to use it.
