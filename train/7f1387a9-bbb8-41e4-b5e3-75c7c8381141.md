The **key distinction** between **atomic force microscopy (AFM)** and **scanning tunneling microscopy (STM)** lies in their **operating principles** and **sample requirements**:

1. **Mechanism**:
   - **STM**: Uses a sharp tip to probe electrons tunneling between the tip and a conducting sample. Requires **conductive surfaces** (e.g., metals, doped semiconductors).
   - **AFM**: Detects **force interactions** (van der Waals, electrostatic, etc.) via a cantilever-tipped probe. Works on **all materials** (insulators, semiconductors, polymers).

2. **Resolution**:
   - STM: **Atomic resolution** (surface-level), but limited to **conductive samples**.
   - AFM: **Atomic resolution** in non-conductive samples, but may suffer from **topographical artifacts** in conductive materials.

3. **Applications**:
   - STM excels in **surface chemistry** of metals/electronics.
   - AFM covers **biomolecules, polymers, and insulators** (e.g., DNA, graphene).

4. **Sample Preparation**:
   - STM: Must be **clean, flat, and conductive**.
   - AFM: Tolerates **thicker samples** and non-conductive substrates (e.g., glass, mica).
