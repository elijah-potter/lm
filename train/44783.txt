The First Cent Ever Made: A Comprehensive Account
We all use cents everyday without affording much reflection to their origin. However, did you know that the first cent ever made dates back to over 1,200 years ago? In this write-up, we will survey the account of cents, their fabrication process, and their transformation over time.
1. What Is a Cent?
Prior to examining the history of cents, let us first define what a cent is. A cent is a coin worth one penny in the United States and several other nations. In the US, cents are presently made of copper-coated zinc and are 0.75 inches in dimension.
2. The First Cent Ever Made
The first cent was made in England during the eighth century. Originally, it was a silver coin called the “sterling cent” and weighed one pennyweight, which is equal to approximately 1.5 grams. The term “cent” comes from the Old English term “penning,” which denotes “money.”
3. The Metamorphosis of the Cent
Since the first cent was made, it has undergone several changes. In the 16th century, copper cents were introduced, and in the 17th century, they were made bigger in dimension. In the 19th century, cents were made of bronze and were smaller in size. It was also during this epoch that a portrait of Queen Victoria was added to the front of the cent.
4. The Fabrication Process of Cents
Today, cents are made using a mix of copper and zinc. The blanks are punched out of a strip of metal, and ridges are appended to the peripheries to forestall tampering. Afterward, the blanks are sent to a high-speed punch press, where the cent’s design is stamped onto them. Lastly, the cents are scrutinized and packaged for distribution.
5. Dispute Surrounding the Cent
Despite its extensive history, the cent has faced controversy in recent times. Some individuals argue that the cent should be eradicated altogether since it costs more to produce than its actual value. Others contend that the cent should be made of a less expensive metal, like steel, to diminish production costs.
