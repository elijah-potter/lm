In the dim glow of neon, a lone figure—**Elias**—stepped off a subway platform, his breath fogging the cold air. His phone buzzed: *"You’re late. The last train leaves in 30 seconds."* The line blurred into static. He reached for his coat, fingers brushing the pocket where he’d hidden a crumpled ticket for **The Last Light Opera**, a show he’d never seen.

A hand clamped onto his shoulder. *"You’re not going in there alone."* The voice was rough, familiar. **Lena**, his ex, stood there, her sleeves rolled up, her eyes sharp with something like relief. "You were supposed to meet me here—" she began, but Elias cut her off, his voice tight. "I’m not leaving until I see it."

The doors hissed open. Inside, the theater hummed with anticipation. The stage was a void, save for a single spotlight. At its center, a figure—**The Last Light**, a woman in a tattered cape—stood, her back to the audience. She turned. The crowd gasped. The lights dimmed. Then, with a whisper, she sang: *"A minute too late, a minute too soon—this is the only time we ever had."*

Elias’s pulse roared. He grabbed Lena’s wrist, pulling her toward the stage. "Come on," he breathed. "We have to see it."

The curtain rose. The Last Light’s voice washed over them, weaving through the air like smoke. When it ended, the auditorium was silent. The stage was empty. Elias exhaled, watching Lena’s fingers twitch at her lapel—a ticket stub, torn and damp. The Last Light had given it to her. As the final note faded, the theater doors creaked open. The audience filed out, but not before Elias noticed the clock on the wall: **59:59**.

Outside, the neon sign flickered. *"The Last Light Opera"* vanished. The streetlights blinked on. Elias took one last step back, then vanished into the crowd. Lena’s phone buzzed again: *"You missed it. The last train leaves now."* She pocketed her ticket. The train pulled away, leaving only the echo of the Last Light’s voice in the empty air.
