A cavernous cathedral of rusted iron and mildewed stone, its vaulted ceilings sagging like the ribs of a drowning man. The air is thick with the scent of damp earth and iron, thick enough to be almost tangible. Windows, once arched and clear, are now shattered, their glass reduced to jagged shards that catch the dim, eerie light filtering in through the gaps. The floor is a mosaic of cracked tiles and exposed roots, some as old as the structure itself. The walls, lined with what appear to be half-buried objects—rusted relics, skeletal remains, or perhaps the last remnants of human or mechanical life—seem to pulse with an invisible force, their edges slightly blurred, as if the space itself is holding its breath, waiting. The silence is so profound that it feels almost physical, pressing against the skin like a living thing. In the distance, the faint, rhythmic drip of water echoes—too slow, too deliberate, like a heartbeat stuttering. The air moves in slow, heavy currents, as if the space is fighting to escape its own suffocating embrace.
