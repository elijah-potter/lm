The flickering neon glow of a broken streetlamp cast jagged shadows across the cracked asphalt. Rain tapped against the rusted metal roof of a flickering diner, its fluorescent lights dimming as the power flickered. Inside, the air smelled of stale coffee and the acrid tang of kerosene lanterns.

Lena clutched a mug of warm, black coffee as she leaned against the bar, her fingers tracing the worn edge of the diner’s counter. Across the room, Jake wiped down a grill with a rag soaked in gasoline-soaked rag, his movements precise despite the chaos.

A distant wail of sirens cut through the night. Outside, the wind howled through the broken windows, rattling the glass. A flicker of movement caught Lena’s eye—someone hurried past the door, their coat damp, their breath visible in the cold.

"Another one?" Jake muttered, not looking up.

Lena hesitated, then stepped onto the cracked linoleum. The door creaked open, revealing the stormy street outside. A group of teens stood near the flickering gas station sign, huddled under a stolen hoodie, their laughter muffled. One of them—a girl with a bandaged knuckle—pushed past them, stumbling into the diner.

"Biscuits?" Jake called, setting down a tray. "Still warm."

The girl blinked at the steam rising from the plates. "Just... one," she said, plopping into a stool.

Outside, the wind howled again. The power flickered. The diner’s clock—its hands frozen at 11:58—ticked slowly, counting down.
