In the quiet village of Eldermere, where the river whispered secrets to the stone bridges, old Elias kept his ledger in a chest beneath his bed. The ledger’s pages were filled with numbers, dates, and names—all meticulously crossed out or rewritten in secret ink.

One evening, as the moon painted the sky in silver, Elias traced a pattern on his arm with ink: **"A silent storm gathers where the old oak stands, and the river sings its last song."**

The next morning, his daughter, Liora, found a small silver key hidden in the oak tree’s roots. When she inserted it into the chest, the lock turned—revealing a single sheet: **"The code is in the numbers of the drowned: 37, 12, 49, 28."**

That night, the villagers heard the river growl, its waters rising unnaturally. The old oak groaned as roots split the earth. The drowned souls of Eldermere whispered in the wind.

**Hidden Message:**
**37** (Years since the Great Flood) **12** (Days the river rose) **49** (Cursed years) **28** (The age of the village’s first drowned)
