The silence lingers like a half-remembered dream,
a space where the past and future bleed together,
softening edges of time. Every sound—even the hum of the air—
carries the weight of what isn’t there, a hollow echo of absence
that’s also presence, a shadow stretched thin across the daylight.
The walls hold the shape of their absence, the cracks in the floor
where something once walked. The air smells faintly of what was,
a scent that clings to the back of the throat like a ghost.
You reach for a name, but the fingers slip—
the name is there, just out of reach, as if it’s still learning
how to be absent. The clock ticks backward,
and the present dissolves into a blur of memory and waiting.
You sit in the quiet, but the quiet is not empty—it’s a vast, trembling
space where everything is half-remembered, half-forgotten.
The absence feels like a presence because it’s the only thing that
remains constant: the absence of what was there.
