You may have noticed that some people are stronger than other people. Why is that? Well to start, the things in our body that make us strong are muscles, and the muscles that are attached to our bones are called "skeletal muscles." For these skeletal muscles to move, your brain sends them messages, like a phone. The receiving part of the muscles for these messages is called actin and myosin proteins. When these proteins receive the message from the brain, they tell each other about the message. When they communicate with each other, this causes something called a "power stroke," which allows muscle movement.

Your muscles are made up of tiny building blocks called "muscle cells." To get stronger, these muscle cells have to grow in size and be able to receive more messages from the brain. When you exercise and rest, your muscle cells have to grow in size to get used to this exercise. The process of muscle cells growing in size is called "hypertrophy." Since your body wants to get stronger when you exercise, it makes sense that more and more parts of muscles receive messages from your brain to move, as a "strength in numbers" idea. Since more power strokes are being made, you naturally become stronger. This process is called "synchronous activation."

Overall, the more you exercise your body, the more your muscle cells will grow through "hypertrophy," and the more parts of your muscles your brain can send messages to through a process called "synchronous activation." Both of these processes make you stronger!
