The first time rain touched the cracked pavement between the cracks in the sidewalk, turning them into tiny rivers that gleamed under the streetlamp’s flicker. The scent of damp earth and old pavement—something between copper and ozone—filled the air as I sat on the curb, watching the city’s pulse slow to a whisper. The wind carried a sound like distant thunder, but it wasn’t rain yet. It was just the city holding its breath, waiting.

Then—it came. The first drop landed like a coin on a ledge, heavy with the weight of something unsaid. My fingers traced the edge of the empty coffee cup on the bench, where the last sip had been. The city’s rhythm shifted. The sound of cars passing became a murmur, the clink of a street vendor’s cart a distant drumbeat. I remembered the way the light bent at the corner, how the glow of the neon sign across the street had stretched into a halo around the streetlamps.

A woman walked past, her coat whispering against the pavement. She didn’t look at me. I didn’t look away. The rain started in earnest then, a steady, rhythmic rhythm, each drop a promise of something deeper than weather. The sidewalk became a mirror, reflecting the neon signs and the blurred figures of strangers moving in and out of focus. I closed my eyes and listened—the city, the rain, the weight of time pressing down like a silent hand.

Then, just as suddenly as it had begun, the rain stopped. The city exhaled. The streetlamp’s glow turned to embers, the pavement damp and cool beneath my fingers. The woman was gone, her coat now a blur of motion. I sat there for a long moment, the weight of the moment pressing against my ribs like a question I couldn’t answer.

That was the first time rain touched me, and I didn’t know what to do with it. It wasn’t just the water on the pavement; it was the way the city had stopped for a second, the way the world had paused just long enough to let something else in. The moment couldn’t be repeated, not exactly. Not when the rain had turned to silence, not when the city had swallowed the pause whole. But I carried it with me, the way a memory lingers even when you try to forget it.
