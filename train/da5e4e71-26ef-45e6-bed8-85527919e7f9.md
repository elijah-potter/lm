Decision-making under uncertainty often involves trade-offs between risk and reward while managing incomplete data. For instance, a healthcare provider might diagnose a patient based on symptoms and lab results, even if a definitive test (e.g., genetic sequencing) is unavailable. The decision prioritizes action over certainty, balancing potential harm (misdiagnosis) against delayed care.

Example: In AI-driven logistics, routing algorithms select paths using historical traffic data and real-time sensor inputs, assuming patterns persist despite unseen disruptions. The trade-off lies in optimizing efficiency vs. robustness against unforeseen events.

Uncertainty forces reliance on heuristics, assumptions, or probabilistic models to bridge gaps in knowledge. Outcomes are probabilistic, requiring adaptive feedback loops for refinement.
