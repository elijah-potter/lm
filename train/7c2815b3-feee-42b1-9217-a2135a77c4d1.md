A vast, dimly lit chamber hung with ancient, cracked tapestries depicting forgotten battles. The air was thick with the scent of damp earth and iron, barely stirred by a single flickering lantern casting long shadows across the walls. The only sound was the slow, rhythmic ticking of a distant clock, muffled by layers of stone. At the center stood a lone figure—**Kael**, his coat draped over a cracked wooden chair, his hands resting on the armrests, fingers twitching as if caught between thought and motion. His gaze drifted toward the far corner, where a half-burned journal lay open, its pages filled with fragmented words in an unfamiliar script. The silence was so absolute it felt like a physical presence, pressing against Kael’s bones, heavier than any wind. Outside, the city’s distant hum faded into nothing. Kael exhaled, then reached into his coat pocket and pulled out a single, charred matchstick. He lit it, watching the flame dance in the dark before he dropped it into the fire pit beside him. The matchstick burned out in silence.
