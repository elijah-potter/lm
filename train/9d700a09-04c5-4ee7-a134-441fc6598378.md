The jagged cliffs of Veythar’s Obsidian Spire rise where the old world’s bones meet the void. Below, the black water of the Abyssal Trench churns with whispers of the drowned, their voices lost in the mist that clings to the stone like liquid shadow. At the base of the spire, the Obsidian Maw—a cavern mouth—gulps in the storm-wracked sky, its depths said to be the last resting place of a god who never died. The air here is thick with static, the wind carries the scent of iron and something older, something that doesn’t belong to this world. The edges of perception blur: sometimes, figures move in the mist that shouldn’t exist, and the cliffs themselves seem to breathe, shifting slightly when no one looks. At the very tip of the spire, the Obsidian Throne—carved from the same stone as the spire itself—stands empty, its surface etched with runes that pulse faintly, as if waiting. This is where the old world ends, and the unknown begins.
