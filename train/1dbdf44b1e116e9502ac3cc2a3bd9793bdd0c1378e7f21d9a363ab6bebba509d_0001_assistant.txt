Birth

At this swell of earth there are spikes of life.
Muddy green and olive swathes of color
bound to grit and dirt, subtle and clean,
apparently ready to guard their young charge.
Strips of lighter lime stand at attention.

A hawk poses in majesty, model for an unknown portrait,
at ease atop a weathered branch,
as tendrils of grass sway gently.
Brown and yellow button eyes spark in silent solidarity.
The copse breathes daintily, quietly, waiting.

A metal glider swoops near, gaudy crimson wings
slashing against nature’s sweet palette.
The interruption pushes a trio of gray-clad
squirrels to heady anger and they bark upward.
Nylon wings slip out of view, the errant aircraft flees.

Tiny movement hushes the wood, stillness washes 
away intrusion. Hearty trees, lords and ladies, fragile
peasant blades of tender grass lean toward 
the center, the beginning of something new,
at the slightest breeze of an innocent sigh.

Scarred wood violated with deep gashed letters
professing love even as the trees bled, hold vigil.
Blackened tendrils of heather, ravaged by careless
red hot fire, stretch out among the waiting,
a reminder of the complicated enemy, sometimes friend.

The sun sends a searching ray down into the group.
Branches part to help, creating an arrowed path
until warmth reaches new life, caresses, kisses,
cradles the seed until the mahogany shell cracks
outward, tiny oak sprout reaching toward the sky.
