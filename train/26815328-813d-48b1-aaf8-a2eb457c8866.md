In a quiet town nestled between rolling hills, **Mira** boarded a train for the first time in years. The familiar hum of the engine, the rhythmic sway of the seats, and the distant chatter of passengers all felt like a return to something familiar. She had spent the last decade buried in work, neglecting her own curiosity, until a friend’s words—*"You’re missing out on life if you don’t travel"*—sent her packing.

The train took her to **Valencia**, a city where the scent of orange blossoms mingled with the sea breeze. She wandered past markets where vendors sold handmade ceramics, climbed the steps of a Gothic cathedral, and sat by the harbor watching fishermen mend nets. For the first time in years, she stopped to *look*—at the colors, the sounds, the way the sunlight danced on the waves.

The trip changed her in small, unremarkable ways. She started asking questions—*"Why does this city look like this?"*, *"What’s the story behind this bridge?"*—instead of just rushing ahead. She learned to savor coffee at a café where the waitress remembered her order. She laughed at a street performer’s joke and cried at a play about lost love.

One evening, she sat on a balcony overlooking the Mediterranean, watching the sunset paint the sky. A stranger handed her a handwritten note: *"You’re not the same now."* She didn’t know the writer, but the words stayed with her. That night, she made a decision: she would keep traveling—not just for the destinations, but for the quiet moments that made her question everything she’d ever taken for granted.

By the time the train pulled into her station, she didn’t feel like a stranger in her own life anymore. She felt *alive*.
