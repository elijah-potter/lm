The library doors creaked shut behind me, the hum of fluorescent lights now a distant murmur. My coat felt too warm, my breath misting in the damp October air. The book I held—*The Last Light of Empathy*—was heavy with secrets. The library’s grand chandelier, its crystal fracturing under the weight of decades, cast long shadows on the spines of forgotten tomes.

I turned the final page. The ink had blurred. Somewhere, a clock struck midnight. The city’s neon glow pulsed like a dying heartbeat against the skyline.

I stepped into the alley. The air smelled of rain and old copper. My phone buzzed—an unread message from my sister. I pocketed the book. The city would swallow it whole, just like everything else.
