**Mayor’s Press Briefing on New "Eco-Haven" Initiative: City Council Approves Land Use Zoning for Sustainable Development**

*Today, Mayor Elias Carter announced the first phase of the city’s ambitious "Eco-Haven" initiative, a 120-acre redevelopment plan in the former industrial district along the Blackwater River.* The project, set to break ground next month, will prioritize mixed-use green spaces, renewable energy infrastructure, and affordable housing with solar-paneled roofs. "This isn’t just urban renewal—it’s a blueprint for climate resilience," Carter stated, citing a 20% reduction in carbon footprint projected over the next decade.

Councilwoman Priya Kapoor highlighted the inclusion of adaptive reuse for historic warehouses, repurposed as cultural hubs, while opposition from nearby residents over noise and traffic concerns led to a compromise: staggered construction phases with temporary noise barriers. The city will also fund a $500,000 community advisory board to monitor progress.

Critics argue the zoning changes could displace long-time residents, but Mayor Carter countered that displacement will be "mitigated through a first-right-to-buy program and a $3 million relocation fund for low-income families." The council’s approval follows a unanimous vote, with dissenting votes from one member citing "unforeseen economic risks."

Next steps include environmental impact studies and public hearings before a final ordinance is drafted. The city anticipates a $200 million investment from a mix of federal grants, private partnerships, and local taxes. "This isn’t charity," Carter emphasized. "It’s smart growth—where every dollar builds for the future."
