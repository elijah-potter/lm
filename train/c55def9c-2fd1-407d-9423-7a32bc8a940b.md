A dimly lit chamber, its walls lined with ancient, cracked stone. A lone figure stands before a table draped in black cloth, fingers tracing the edge of a half-finished manuscript. The air is thick with the scent of aged parchment and damp earth. The room falls silent—no sound of breathing, no rustle of fabric, no whisper of a passing draft. The only movement is the slow, deliberate tilt of a quill over an empty page. A single candle flickers weakly, casting long shadows that stretch like skeletal fingers across the floor. The figure pauses, exhaling a breath that dissolves into the quiet. The silence stretches on, unbroken, as if time itself has frozen mid-beat. Outside, the world continues, oblivious. Inside, nothing does.
