A sudden decision cut through the quiet morning. The door opened with a sigh, revealing a jacket draped over the back of a chair, the scent of stale coffee lingering in the air. A half-empty mug sat on the kitchen counter, its surface cracked at the rim. The person—mid-thirties, eyes tired but sharp—reached for the jacket, pulling it on without hesitation.

Outside, rain tapped against the windows in steady, rhythmic drops. The air was cool, the kind that made skin prickle. They hesitated for only a second before stepping out. The umbrella in their other hand was a relic, left behind from the previous day’s rush. No longer needed.

The sidewalk was slick beneath their shoes, the pavement glistening. A bus passed, its doors swinging open and closed, the sound of distant laughter carrying on the damp breeze. They didn’t wait. The decision had been made—no time for second-guessing. The world, for better or worse, had already begun.
