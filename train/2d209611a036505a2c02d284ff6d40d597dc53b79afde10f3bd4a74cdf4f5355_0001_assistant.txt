Check this out! Astronomers have detected a massive plume of water vapor, just spurting out into space! The culprit? None other than Enceladus, the icy moon of Saturn!

The moon (a whopping 504km-wide or 313 miles) is known for big geysers already, but this one is particularly huge!

That water stream spans roughly 9,600km, and just to put that into perspective, it's about the same distance that it takes to fly from the UK to Japan, talk about long!

Scientists are incredibly interested in Enceladus because of the salty ocean located under the surface. Why, this water source could hold enough conditions to support life!

NASA's Cassini mission (which took place between 2004-2017) gathered a plethora of engrossing evidence of the necessary chemistry by flying many times through the geysers and getting samples of the water, and though no biology was detected, it's still a possibility!

The new and impressive super-plume was caught by the James Webb Space Telescope. The older observations had managed to track vapor emissions extending for hundreds, and I do mean hundreds of kilometers! But this geyser? Oh, this geyser is on a different scale entirely!
