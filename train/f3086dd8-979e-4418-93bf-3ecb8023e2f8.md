In the quiet corner of the library, a single candle flickered against the cold stone walls. Its flame danced like a restless ghost, casting jagged shadows on the aged shelves. The scent of aged paper and dust clung to the air, a whisper of forgotten stories. Inside the small, leather-bound book, a story was unfolding—one that began with a single candle and ended where it started.

A child, no older than six, sat cross-legged on the floor, their fingers tracing the edges of the book’s cover. The candle’s glow was their only light, a beacon in the dark. "Once upon a time," they murmured, "there was a candle..."

The story unfolded like a ripple in water, spreading outward from that single flame. A village stood by the river, where fishermen caught fish that sang when moonlight touched them. The girl who collected these songs, Lira, would later find the same riverbank where the first candle was lit.

Years passed. Lira became a storyteller, her tales woven from the songs of the river’s fish. One evening, as she sat by the same riverbank, she lit a candle—small, like the first one—to tell a story of its own. The candle burned until it was ash, its light gone, but the river remembered.

Then, one night, a child came to the same spot. They lit a candle, just as the first child had done, and whispered, "Once upon a time..." The river’s fish sang, and the story began anew. The flame circled back, completing its loop.
