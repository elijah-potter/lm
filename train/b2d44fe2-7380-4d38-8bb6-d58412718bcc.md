A vast, geometric void—endlessly mirrored in reflective, frosted surfaces of polished obsidian or glass that stretch into the horizon. The air hums with static, a low-pitched hiss like distant traffic, though no vessels pass. The weight of unseen observers presses against every surface, their presence etched into the fabric of the space itself—every crevice, every shadow, every whisper of movement captured in the air currents. The walls breathe, shifting slightly with unseen pressure, their surfaces rippling like liquid metal under unseen hands. The silence is not absence, but a palpable tension, each moment a pause between the next unseen step. Echoes linger too long, as if the walls themselves are holding their breath, waiting. The sense of being watched lingers even when no one is present, as if the space remembers every gesture, every breath, every silent plea left behind.
