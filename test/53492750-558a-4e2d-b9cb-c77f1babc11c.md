**STARTOWN, EARLY MORNING – October 12th**

The skies above Starstown are shrouded in the first light of dawn, a city of neon-lit skyscrapers and rusted bridges stretching into the mist. Locals have already begun their morning routines, the hum of traffic from the newly renovated highway filling the air. Authorities report no major disruptions, though reports of a faint, eerie glow near the old observatory site persist near the downtown core.

Earlier today, residents near the abandoned science lab on the outskirts of town reported seeing what they described as "floating orbs" drifting across the sky at dusk. Witnesses claimed they were round, iridescent, and emitted a soft, pulsating light—unlike any known celestial phenomenon. The city’s astronomer, Dr. Elias Voss, confirmed no known satellites or meteor showers align with the timing, though he cautioned against premature speculation.

Meanwhile, the Starstown Police Department has issued a general advisory urging residents to avoid the observatory grounds after dark. Local shops in the downtown district report increased foot traffic, with many customers asking about the "new glow" and its possible implications. A small protest gathered outside the city hall yesterday, with signs reading *"Keep Our Lights On"* and *"What’s Out There?"* Police say the crowd was peaceful but non-compliant with curfew orders in the area.

Elderly resident Mrs. Elena Hart, who lives near the lab, told reporters her granddaughter mentioned seeing the orbs last night while jogging. "She said they moved fast, like they weren’t anchored to anything," she said, her voice trembling. Hart declined to press charges, citing fear and disbelief, though she has filed a report with the department.

Government officials have stated that a full investigation will begin tomorrow, with a joint task force from the city and the regional science bureau expected to deploy drones and thermal cameras to the observatory site. The city council has also ordered a review of the abandoned lab’s security systems, though no arrests have been made regarding its previous neglect.

Locals are divided on the situation—some see it as a strange but harmless cosmic occurrence, while others whisper of old legends about the observatory’s former director, Dr. Alistair Graves, who vanished years ago after studying "unseen phenomena." Graves’ research files, some still sealed in glass jars, remain in storage, though they were last accessed by the lab’s last staff in 2019.

As for now, Starstown remains in a state of quiet unease. The glow lingers, and the questions grow. What will tomorrow bring?
