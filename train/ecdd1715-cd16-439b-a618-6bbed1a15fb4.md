The autumn wind carried whispers of the old city’s secrets, rustling through the empty streets where the neon signs flickered like dying stars. I sat in the café’s dim glow, sipping black coffee that tasted of regret and the faintest hint of something bitter—bitter as the truth I’d chosen to leave unspoken.

Across the room, the woman at the table by the window did not notice the way her fingers traced the rim of her glass, the way she paused before answering my question. She’d told me she’d seen him last near the docks, just before dawn, but her voice had trembled when she mentioned the boat. *"He was already gone by the time I reached the edge of the pier."* The way she said *"gone"* made me think of something else entirely—something she’d buried beneath the weight of her own silence.

I ordered another coffee, let the waitress ignore my request for another, and watched the city breathe around me. The key detail was there, just out of reach, like a shadow slipping through the cracks of memory. I’d chosen to look away, not because I didn’t care, but because some truths were better left to fester in the dark. The world needed its stories incomplete.
