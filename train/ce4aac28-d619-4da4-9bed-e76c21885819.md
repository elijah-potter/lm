The café’s dim glow cast long shadows across the wooden tables, where a lone figure sat with a half-empty coffee cup, the steam curling into the quiet morning air. Outside, the city hummed—a distant clatter of traffic, the occasional laugh from a passing group. She traced the rim of her mug with a trembling finger, memories flickering like fireflies in her mind.

*"You were always the kind of girl who could make even the dullest moments feel alive,"* her mother had said, years ago. The words hung between them, unspoken, unhealed. She closed her eyes, the weight of them pressing down like the weight of the past.

The bell above the door jingled. A stranger—tall, with sharp cheekbones—stepped inside, their coat catching the light. She watched them as they took a seat across from her, their gaze lingering too long, too curious. She exhaled slowly, fingers tightening around the cup.

*"I don’t do this anymore,"* she whispered, but her voice cracked. The stranger’s smile didn’t waver. They reached across the table, their fingers brushing hers—just for a second—before pulling back, leaving her breathless.

She stood, the chair scraping against the floor. The stranger didn’t follow. Just watched, then left through the door, the world outside swallowing them whole.

She walked toward the exit, her heels clicking against the pavement, the city’s rhythm now just another backdrop to her retreat. The door slammed behind her, and for the first time in a long time, she let herself disappear.
