The quiet breaking of rules often occurs when expectations are subtly adjusted without explicit notice. An example could be a workplace where an unspoken norm, such as mandatory overtime, starts being flexibly interpreted by managers—initially allowing occasional exceptions, then gradually becoming the norm without formal policy updates. Employees may comply because they assume it’s understood, but the rule shifts unobserved until it becomes the standard. This erosion of boundaries happens when compliance is treated as optional or when enforcement becomes inconsistent, leading to gradual normalization of the deviation. The quiet nature arises from the lack of confrontation or documentation, making it difficult to trace back to its origin.
