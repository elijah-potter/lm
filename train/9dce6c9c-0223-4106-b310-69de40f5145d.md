The air hummed with an eerie silence, thick as cobwebs. A single candle flickered in the corner, casting jagged shadows that stretched like frozen rivers across the room. The clock on the wall, its hands frozen mid-tick, marked twelve thirty-five—though the moment felt like a lifetime. My breath caught in my throat as the world seemed to breathe too, expanding and contracting in slow, deliberate pulses.

My fingers trembled as I reached for the glass of water on the table, the surface rippling like a pond after a storm. The water splashed—not once, but a dozen times, each droplet lingering before falling, as if time itself had paused to watch. The door creaked open, and the draft inside was a whisper, not a gust. A figure stood in the doorway, their silhouette blurred, the edges of their clothing dissolving into mist.

*"You’re not alone,"* they said, their voice a thread of sound, stretched and fragile. The walls seemed to pulse with the same slow heartbeat. The world dissolved into a kaleidoscope of light and shadow, each second a heartbeat, each breath a whisper. I stumbled back, my vision swimming, as the minutes stretched into hours—*or was it years?*—and the air grew colder, heavier, as if the world itself had slowed to observe its own breath.
