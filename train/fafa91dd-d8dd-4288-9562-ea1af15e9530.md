A promise was left unfulfilled in the quiet town of Hollow Creek, where every promise held weight. The elder, a grizzled man with eyes like storm clouds, had vowed to protect the village’s sacred oak from the encroaching timber company. He bound himself to a promise carved into the tree’s bark—*"No axe shall split this bark until the last child’s laughter fades."* When the villagers discovered the violation, they demanded justice. The company’s lawyer, slick and smug, argued the promise was "unenforceable" due to "oral tradition." The elder’s descendants, now disillusioned, whispered that the oath had been misinterpreted—*"Not that the tree was sacred, but that the *time* of its fall had been set."* The oak fell anyway, its bark split open, revealing a hidden script in runes: *"The land remembers. The heart does not."* The company’s contracts were voided, but the village’s trust was broken. Some say the elder’s descendants still sit beneath the oak, counting the days until the next promise is broken.
