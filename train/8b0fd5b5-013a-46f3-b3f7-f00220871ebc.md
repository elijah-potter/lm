Smooth obsidian cliffs glisten under a slow, amber sky, their dark surface reflecting fractured light like a thousand shattered mirrors. Beneath, damp moss clings to weathered stone, its velvety texture absorbing moisture, leaving faint, damp footprints. Warm, soft wool blankets the air, caught in slow, lazy currents from distant breezes that whisper through ancient, gnarled roots. Fine, golden sand drifts in slow spirals around cracked, sun-bleached pottery shards. A cool, moist cave floor—pebbled and uneven—softens under bare feet, interspersed with smooth river stones and the occasional slick, polished obsidian. The scent of damp earth and pine lingers, thick and earthy, with an occasional whisper of salt from the last tide.
