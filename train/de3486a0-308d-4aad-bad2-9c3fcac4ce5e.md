In a dimly lit library, the air thick with the scent of aged paper and old books, an elderly woman sat hunched over a cracked leather-bound journal. Her fingers traced the faded ink where she had scrawled a promise—*"I’ll find you"*—in trembling script beneath the date of her mother’s funeral. The last time she’d seen her mother alive, she’d whispered it to her daughter, then left without looking back. The promise was buried in memories like this one: a child’s small hand holding hers as they raced through a field of wildflowers, the way her mother had laughed at the sky turning gold at dusk. Now, the words stood as a silent vow, a thread in a tapestry of unspoken regrets and quiet hope.
