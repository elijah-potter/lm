A Good Beginning is Half The Ending
Before you start writing, you need to understand your purposes and the result you want to reach. To cut a long story short:
- Understand why you need the paper. It is easy when you have got a clear assignment with a paper type, length, etc., indicated. If you haven’t, check which format is the most suitable for your situation. Consult someone, or search the Internet for associations.
- A science paper is usually a piece of academic writing intended for publishing in a scientific journal. In most cases, it is required for some publications needed to obtain a degree. It would be a good idea to get this journal and guess what kind of papers it accepts.
- Learn your audience before you start working. It will help define what language you’ll use, the overall quality and clearness of the forthcoming paper. Also, you can meet the anticipation from the article and make people love to read it.
Make It Clear for a Reader
Here are some recommendations concerning your language choice:
- You will have to use professional language with terms, acronyms, and abbreviations. Each acronym or abbreviation has to be explained in the text after the first usage. Remember, it must be clear for every reader, regardless of their background.
- Avoid jargon, obscene lexicology, bookish words, etc.
- Try to avoid unnecessary shortenings, as you’ll, don’t, LMFAO, etc.
- Try to avoid complicated sentences. The simpler they are, the easier you make it for a reader.
- Do not use passive voice. The language of science usually avoids it.
- Do not use plain text. You’ll need to prepare as much illustrative material as possible. Giant columns of the text will distract a reader by dissolving their attention. Lists, columns, tables, and diagrams will make it more interesting to read.
To understand your future style better, read through some analogical papers. Try to find some of the standard fields and intentions.
Structure and Formatting
If you know the journal that will publish your paper, try to find the publishing guide. There, you will learn everything about the font size, type, margins, length of the text, etc. If you do not have such information, take the same article as a template.
Are there any templates? On the Internet, you will find the rules of writing and everything about formatting and citing styles. A few words about citing: many journals set a limit for the number of works cited. Choose only essential information to cite and avoid so-called “common knowledge” citations.
The style guide will also help you with the structure of the paper. Here, the requirements might differ: some require materials to be placed at the end of the article. Others require the results and discussion to be separated.
In general, the structure of the scientific paper looks like this:
- Introduction (the main task here is to set the scientific question answered by an article).
- Methods (here, you need to explain how you study the issue).
- Results (an objective layout of the findings of the research).
- Discussion (here you interpret the results and analyze them to state your arguments).
Note that you do not have to add any additional pages, like the title page, list of references, abstracts, etc.
Writing Your Scientific Paper Step By Step
Here is a brief list of your steps in writing a scientific paper:
- Start with the methods section. Describe what kind of research you’ve conducted and the materials you’ve used. Explain how you choose the field and the objects for the experiments. Mention all the necessary conditions.
- Continue with the results part. Here, you need to explain the findings you’ve got after the experiments. Be transparent and honest. Do not forget about unexpected and irrelevant results. Here, you should illustrate your material.
- The discussion section comes next. Here, you interpret the results and understand how they support the answer to the paper’s central question. Do not give statements you cannot prove. Also, do not forget to mention the other articles that might have opposite results. Compare them with your paper.
- When the body is ready, start writing an introduction. Here you must sound out the questions to solve and review the literature sources used during writing.
- The last part of writing, but the first in the paper, is abstract. It is a summary of your article that comes before it. Here, you write down the main point, give a short description of the experiment, and explain what you’ve found.
When the text is written, make sure to prepare all the illustrative materials. Do not forget to proofread and edit your paper. As soon as you are sure that the journal’s writing guide is complete, you can submit your paper.
