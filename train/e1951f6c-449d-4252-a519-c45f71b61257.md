**The Last Shift at the Foundry**

Rain hammered the metal roof of the foundry like a thousand hungry hands, each strike a whisper of the past. Inside, the air smelled of ozone and something older—rust, maybe, or the slow decay of the old man who had once held the anvil. His name was Elias, and today was his last day.

The forge’s fire flickered low, casting long shadows across the walls where the last batch of pistons had once burned. The machine had been running for six months now, and the gears had begun to groan like an old man’s joints. Elias had seen this before. He’d fixed it once, years ago, when the foreman had laughed and called him a "waste of space." But then the machine had given up, and the foreman had been forced to admit the job was done.

Now, the foreman, a man named Hargrove, stood at the counter, arms crossed, watching Elias work. "You’re slow today," he said. "You look like you’re thinking about something."

Elias wiped his brow with the back of his sleeve. "Just thinking about the ones who come here. The ones who don’t walk away."

Hargrove scoffed. "They always walk away. The ones who can’t make it don’t come back."

Elias didn’t answer. He was already at the forge, shaping a new hammerhead from the scrap that Hargrove had told him to discard. The metal was cold. Too cold.

The foreman cleared his throat. "You’re not supposed to be working on the old ones."

"Neither are you," Elias said quietly.

Hargrove’s face darkened. "This isn’t about politics."

"It’s about the truth," Elias said. "The truth is, the machines here don’t just make things. They make memories. And memories aren’t just things to throw away."

The forge’s fire flared. The metal hissed. Elias didn’t look up.
