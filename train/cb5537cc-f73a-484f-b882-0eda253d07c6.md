The absence hums—a quiet vibration in the corners of thought, a breath you half-remember when you close your eyes. It’s the way the kitchen still smells like the coffee you drank this morning, but the mug sits empty, the warmth now a ghost. The silence between sentences in a conversation where one person’s voice is missing, yet you still hear it in the pauses, the unspoken.

It’s the shape of a name you used to say aloud, now a word you press against your tongue like a memory that won’t stick. The way the floor feels different underfoot—no longer solid, but layered with the weight of things left unsaid, the steps you didn’t take. The absence is in the way shadows stretch longer than they should, in the way time moves differently when you’re missing, as if the clock itself is holding its breath.

It’s the absence of the person you thought you’d always have, the one who filled the gaps of your days, the one who made the absence itself feel less like absence and more like… a space that needs to be filled, but isn’t sure how. The absence is in the way you reach for them, half-expecting them to appear from the air, to step into the room and say something, anything, to bridge the gap that’s grown too wide.

It’s the absence of the version of yourself you knew, the one who didn’t pause to wonder what was missing. Now, you pause. And the pause is the absence. And the absence is the thing you’re trying to name, but the words won’t come.
