The air hums thick with the scent of damp earth and rusted iron, trapped between towering, skeletal buildings draped in a perpetual twilight. Windows are half-buried in the soil, their glass cracked like old bones, while the streets below are a muddy quagmire, reflecting the eerie glow of flickering streetlamps that never quite reach the pavement. The sky is a bruised, smudged canvas—no horizon, only a vast, unyielding void where the sun never quite rises or sets. Wind, if there is any, moves in lazy, labored waves, barely stirring the cobblestones. Shadows stretch unnaturally long, clinging to the walls like forgotten fingers. The entire city seems to exhale slowly, then hold its breath, waiting.
