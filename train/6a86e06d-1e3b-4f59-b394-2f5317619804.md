A sunrise crackles through the window like a whisper of time before it’s even dawn. The clock ticks like a metronome set to an impossible speed, each second stretching into a marathon. The morning’s coffee brews too fast, steam curling into the air like a promise left unfulfilled. The morning routine—shower, skincare, the familiar hum of the fridge—feels like sprinting a marathon without a finish line.

The day unfolds in a blur: emails to answer, messages to reply, decisions to make faster than they can be digested. Meetings blur into one another, coffee stains the sleeves of shirts that should have been pressed yesterday. The afternoon slips away in a haze of tasks, obligations, and the relentless pull of evening.

The sun sets too soon, casting shadows that feel like the end of everything. Dinner is a rush—food that tastes like it’s been sitting too long, a meal eaten in silence because the mind is already wandering to what’s next. The night arrives too fast, and the bed feels like a stranger’s space, a temporary refuge in a day that never ends.

A day like this is a loop—endless, repetitive, and yet never quite enough. The cycle repeats, and the clock still ticks.
