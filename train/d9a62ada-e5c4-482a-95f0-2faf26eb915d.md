A weathered wooden pier juts from a tranquil harbor, its planks groaning under the weight of salt and time. The stone foundations, cracked with age, cradle the remnants of old fishing nets and rusted buoys. The sea, ever restless, laps against the base like a patient guardian. Above, the sky is a canvas of fleeting clouds—one moment blue, the next a bruise of storm.

The pier is not built to last. Its supports are too slender, its railing too weak against the tide’s relentless pull. Yet here, the wind carries the scent of driftwood and old fish, and the waves whisper secrets that only the tide understands. This place holds permanence in its decay—where the sea erodes the wood but never the stories etched into its bones. The pier may crumble, but the stories of those who’ve stood here, the fishermen who’ve lost their nets, the lovers who’ve whispered promises to the horizon—these remain. They are the unbroken threads in the fabric of what feels temporary but is not.
