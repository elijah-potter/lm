The village elder’s fire burned low as the children gathered around, their laughter echoing softly against the damp autumn wind. Outside, the river whispered secrets through the willows, its surface rippling like a restless spirit. I sat on the edge of the mat, my fingers tracing the worn leather of my father’s old knife—the one that had once carved his name into the bark of the great oak.

*"The old man knew,"* the eldest boy said, his voice sharp with excitement. *"He said the storm would come tonight, and if you listen close, you’ll hear the bones of the drowned girl singing in the reeds."*

The others gasped, their eyes wide with belief. But I only stared at the fire, its flames dancing like trapped light. I had heard the stories before—the stories of the drowned ones, how they rose again, not as ghosts, but as something worse, something that took their place and left their bodies hollow.

I knew the truth, of course. I knew the girl’s name: **Elspeth**. Knew how she drowned, how her last breath was stolen by the current, how she was never found. But I also knew the village’s rule: no one spoke of her. No one mentioned the night she vanished, or how the fish in the river stopped swimming for three days afterward. The elders said it was the storm, the cold, the way the wind howled like a thing that had already been dead.

I didn’t say any of that. I didn’t tell them the way the villagers had whispered behind closed doors, how they had built a shrine to her in the woods, how they had left offerings of silver coins and dried herbs at the water’s edge. I didn’t tell them about the way the reeds had begun to whisper back, not with words, but with something older, something that slithered through the air like smoke.

Instead, I leaned forward and said, *"Perhaps the storm is the girl herself,"* and watched as the children’s eyes glowed like embers in the dark. Because that was the truth, wasn’t it? The storm was the girl, and the river was her grave. And if I listened too closely, I might hear her singing from the depths.
