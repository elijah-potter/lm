The classroom hummed with the same scripted pauses—teacher’s voice rising, students nodding in unison. A chalk mark on the blackboard mimicked the same worn-out rhythm: *question, hesitation, answer, repeat*. The chalk clattered against the board with the precision of a metronome, each stroke a measured beat. The bell rang, not by chance but by the same preordained signal, and the door swung open with the familiar *click* of a lock turning. The hallway stretched out, a corridor of faces already rehearsed in the same hollow smiles. Even the air smelled the same: stale coffee, the faint scent of antiseptic, the faintest trace of fear. It was a performance, and I was the last to realize it was a script.
