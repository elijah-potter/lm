The clock struck midnight in the quiet town of Hollow Creek, where the river whispered secrets only the old trees understood. Elias sat on the bridge, his fingers tracing the edge of the notebook he’d kept since childhood—each page a story half-finished, each one a door left ajar.

*"This is where it all began,"* he murmured, turning to the last page, where the ink had blurred into a half-smudged sketch of a child in a raincoat, running toward a black door with no handle. The rain had started again, a slow, relentless tap against the windows.

His mother had found the notebook one winter’s evening, her eyes widening as she flipped through the pages. *"You’ve been writing stories for years,"* she’d said, voice trembling. *"But why do you keep writing about doors?"* Elias had shrugged, then looked at her. The first time she’d said *"I don’t want you to open any doors"* was the first time he’d understood she didn’t mean *doors*.

The notebook was gone now, lost in the attic after his father’s sudden illness. But the child—his younger self—had been right there in the room, pressing his palm against the cold wood of the door, his breath misting in the damp air.

---

The first thing Elias did when he woke was find the door. It wasn’t the one in his memory; this one was smaller, carved into the wall of his grandmother’s old kitchen, the kind that only opened from the inside. He pressed his ear against it, listening. The house was quiet, the kind of quiet that hummed with expectation.

The child was still there. Not a memory, but a presence—a handprint in the dust on the floor, a half-chewed apple core where he’d sat. Elias followed the trail, his shoes silent on the linoleum, until he reached the back door, where the child had been waiting.

*"You came back,"* the child said, voice like wind through the trees.

Elias swallowed. *"I was looking for you."*

The child stepped aside. The door behind them wasn’t a door at all—it was the riverbank, the same one that had run through Hollow Creek for as long as anyone could remember. But this time, the water was still, like glass, reflecting not the sky but a city that had never existed.

*"Now you see,"* the child said, *"what happens next."*
