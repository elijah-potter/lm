The garden shed stood as a quiet sentinel between the overgrown lawn and the rusted fence, its once-smooth wood now split with age and the slow embrace of moss. Inside, a single lamp flickered weakly against the damp, its bulb dimmed by decades of neglect. The shelves held remnants of forgotten things: half-eaten tins of beans, a child’s drawing of a smiling sun, and the faint scent of old paint and damp wool. Outside, the wind carried the sound of distant thunder, muffled by the same stillness that had settled over everything since the last family moved away. The key, long rusted in the lock, was the only thing that still felt new.
