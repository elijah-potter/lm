We didn’t mean for this to happen.

The first time I saw her, it was in the fluorescent glow of an empty store on a Tuesday night, the kind of place that stays open late for the wrong reasons—late-night snack runs, forgotten keys, the occasional drunken confession. She was leaning against the counter, arms crossed, one hip pressed against the shelf of half-empty bottles. A cigarette dangling from her fingers, her breath fogging the glass like a mirage.

I should have walked away.

But the way she looked at me—just for a second—like I was the only thing she hadn’t already decided was worthless—made me stay.

She was laughing at something I didn’t hear, her voice sharp and dry. *"You’re gonna have to explain this to me, new guy."*

I didn’t know what to say.

She didn’t wait for an answer.

By the third time, she was calling me in the mornings, asking if I’d slept at all. *"You’re not a ghost,"* she’d say, like it was the only thing keeping us from falling apart. *"You’re just tired."*

I told her I wasn’t tired. That I wasn’t running.

But the nights started bleeding into each other. The way she touched my arm when we walked past the same coffee shop, how she’d press her forehead to mine when we were both too quiet. The way her voice changed—soft, then sharp, then soft again—like she was learning to sound like someone else.

She said I was reckless. That I didn’t know how to stop. That I was already too late.

I told her she was wrong.

But then she said it again, and this time, I believed her.

And the world kept turning.
