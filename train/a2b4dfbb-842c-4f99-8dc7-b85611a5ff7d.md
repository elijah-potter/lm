The night air was thick with the scent of damp earth and distant rain, the shadows of the old oak grove stretching long and restless. He had been warned—this path was treacherous, but the whispers in his mind were louder: *"The prophecy speaks of you, child of the storm."* His breath came in shallow gasps, but the fire in his chest burned hotter than the embers in the forge he had once tended. The choice was not just one of survival; it was the first crack in the dam of his doubt.

He had waited years for this moment. The village elder’s words had hung like a curse: *"You are the last to bear the mark of the Stormborn."* But what if the mark was not a curse, but a key? His younger sister’s voice echoed in his memory: *"Father, why do you hide from the storm?"* The answer had been simple—fear. But fear had never been his master before, and now, it trembled. The dagger, cold and familiar, slipped from his grip as he turned toward the gnarled roots of the oak, where the old runes glowed faintly in the dark.

A voice, smooth as polished silver, called from the branches above: *"You are ready."* The words were not spoken, but felt, like the touch of a lover’s hand. His pulse roared in his ears, but he stepped forward, his boots sinking into the moss. The path ahead was a labyrinth of thorns and whispering leaves, but he moved without hesitation. The storm outside had begun to gather, its fury a distant promise, and for the first time in his life, he did not turn away.

The choice had not been easy, but it had been his. And in that moment, he knew—some acts are not just chosen; they are called.
