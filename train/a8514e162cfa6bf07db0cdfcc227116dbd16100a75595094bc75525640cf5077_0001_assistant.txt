German chemist Friedrich Ernst Dorn discovered radon in 1900 while studying radium. In 1904, Scottish chemist Sir William Ramsey won the Nobel Prize for chemistry for his further study of radon. Ramsey was able to isolate radon and calculate its density so it could be included in the periodic table and, in doing so, discovered that radon is the heaviest gas known to man. Radon is radioactive and undetectable to humans. It is typically harmless when breathed outside but causes problems when allowed to build up indoors.
