Silent laughter echoed through the dimly lit bar, the hum of the neon sign casting long shadows across the faces of the patrons. The bartender, a man whose hands were steady despite the way his fingers trembled slightly, placed a glass of whiskey in front of a man who sat alone at the far end of the bar. The man did not look up.

A woman stood beside him, her voice a whisper that carried only to his ear. *"You knew."*

The man did not answer, but the way his gaze shifted—just slightly—to the corner of the room where the door was—told her everything. He had seen something. Something that should not have been seen. The weight of it settled between them, unspoken but undeniable.

She reached out, brushing her fingers against his wrist where his pulse thumped against her skin. *"You understand."*

He exhaled, slow and controlled. *"It was never meant to be."*

Outside, the city slept. The bar’s door creaked shut behind them as the woman leaned in, her lips just inches from his ear. *"Then tell me what you saw."*

His eyes darkened. *"A door. A door that shouldn’t exist."*
