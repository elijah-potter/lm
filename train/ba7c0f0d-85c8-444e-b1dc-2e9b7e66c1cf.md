A dimly lit apartment hums with the quiet clatter of a coffee machine. The scent of bitter brew lingers in the air, mixing with the faint metallic tang of something old—rust, perhaps, or blood. The protagonist, arms crossed over a steaming mug, watches the news on a cracked screen, their reflection in the glass distorted by static. A news anchor intones about a recent discovery: a forgotten relative’s diary, found in a dusty attic, detailing a family secret that predates their current lives.

Their phone buzzes—a text from an unknown number: *"You remember that night?"* The message is old, typed in a handwriting that doesn’t match theirs. The protagonist’s fingers hover over the reply button, but before they can type, the screen flickers—images from a childhood home flicker in reverse, a door creaking open, figures standing in the dark. The coffee machine suddenly overflows, the liquid turning black and thick, like old blood.

Outside, a child’s laughter echoes down the hall—same voice, same cadence. The protagonist turns, sees their own reflection in the hallway mirror, but the person staring back isn’t them. The reflection smiles, then vanishes, leaving only a trail of dust. The protagonist grabs the phone, swipes it away. The news flickers back to life. The screen reads: *"Local police investigating suspicious activity in the attic."*
