**The Last Letter**

She found it in the pocket of her father’s old coat—the one she’d worn to school every Tuesday. The envelope was yellowed, its edges frayed at the corners, the postmark from New York, dated three years ago. Her fingers trembled as she lifted it, the wax seal cracked like brittle glass.

Inside, only a single sentence, written in looping script:
*"You were never supposed to see this."*

Her mother had died in the fire. The house had burned to the ground, and though she’d buried the rest of the papers, this one had slipped through her fingers—until now.

The letter was addressed to her, but the handwriting was familiar. The same precise, deliberate strokes that had once filled her mother’s notebooks with poetry and half-formed dreams. The same voice, quiet but insistent, as if speaking to a child.

She read it again, the words bleeding into her skin. *"The accident was an accident. You were never in danger."* The rest was a list—names, dates, numbers she didn’t recognize. Her breath hitched. *The others.* The ones who had vanished after the fire. The ones who had never been found.

The narrator didn’t ask why she kept it. She already knew. Some truths were not meant to be told. Some scars were meant to stay buried.

The ink on the page felt warm against her palm. She tucked it back into her coat, the way she’d been taught to hide things. But the fire had taught her nothing. Nothing but that the world was a cage, and some doors were locked from the inside.
