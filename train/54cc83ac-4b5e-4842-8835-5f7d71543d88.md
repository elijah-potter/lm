The fluorescent hum of a 24-hour laundromat crackled with static as a single, rusted dryer spun lazily, its motor groaning like a dying whale. A lone figure—clad in a hoodie the color of bruised overripe fruit—stole a moment to watch a stray sock drift in mid-air, suspended in the vortex of the machine’s exhaust vent. Their fingers traced the faded logo of a defunct coffee chain embroidered on the hem of their jeans, now faded to a ghostly, translucent blue. Outside, the neon glow of a broken sign flickered: **"OPEN"** in jagged, corroded letters, its last bulb a smoldering ember. The air smelled of ozone and stale detergent, and for a fleeting second, the scent of something older—earth, maybe—lingered in the corners of the room, as if the laundromat itself had exhaled a sigh. A child’s toy, a plastic dinosaur, lay abandoned on the floor, its mouth stretched in an expression neither happy nor surprised. The dryer’s motor cut out. Silence. Then, the sound of a refrigerator door opening—too far away to be human.
