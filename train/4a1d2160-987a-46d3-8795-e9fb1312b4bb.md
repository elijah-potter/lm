The winding forest path, though narrower, stretched ahead with towering oaks and whispering ferns, their leaves rustling secrets older than the last trail taken. The shorter route led through the cracked asphalt of the abandoned highway, its faded yellow lines marking a path of quick decisions, but this one—this one was the one with the weight of the stars.

Her boots crunched over fallen pinecones, each step slower now, as if time itself had thickened. The air smelled of damp earth and something wild, unchecked. She wasn’t sure if she was running from something or toward it, but the forest seemed to exhale, to lean in, as if it knew the choice had been made. The lantern flickered, casting long shadows across the moss, and in those shadows, the answers she’d been avoiding seemed to blur into one another—some soft, some sharp, all tangled.

Ahead, the path twisted around a fallen tree, its roots sprawling like ancient hands. The air grew thicker, the scent of pine and damp soil mingling with the distant call of a hawk. She didn’t look back. The choice had been made. The longer path was not just a route—it was a promise.
