**Subject: Resignation – [Your Name]**

Dear [Boss's Name],

Please accept this letter as formal notification that I am resigning from my position as [Your Job Title] at [Company Name], effective [Your Last Day of Employment]. I have given this decision considerable thought and believe it is the appropriate course of action for my professional growth at this time.

My time at [Company Name] has afforded me valuable experiences and opportunities for which I am genuinely grateful. I particularly appreciate [mention a specific positive experience or opportunity – e.g., the chance to work on the X project, the mentorship provided by Y, the collaborative team environment].  However, I have reached a point where the nature of my work and the direction of the team are no longer aligned with my long-term career aspirations.

**(Choose ONE of the following three options for detailing your reasons. Select the one that best suits your comfort level and the relationship you have with your boss.  Option 1 is the most detailed, Option 3 is the most general.)**

**Option 1: Detailed Explanation**

>Specifically, I have become increasingly concerned with the persistent prioritization of short-term feature releases over the establishment of robust, scalable architectural foundations. The current development practices, which frequently involve rapid prototyping and deferred technical debt resolution, are, in my professional judgment, unsustainable and create significant risks to the long-term stability and maintainability of our core systems. While I understand the pressures to deliver quickly, I feel that my expertise in building resilient and well-architected solutions is not being fully utilized, and I am unable to contribute in a way that aligns with my commitment to engineering best practices.

**Option 2: Moderate Detail**

>Over the past several months, I have observed a growing divergence between the strategic direction of the team and my own professional goals. The emphasis on immediate feature delivery has, in my view, overshadowed the importance of building a solid technical foundation for future growth. While I respect the need for agility, I believe a more balanced approach is necessary to ensure the long-term health of our projects.

**Option 3: General Explanation**

>I have found that the current work environment and the direction of the team are not conducive to my continued professional development. I am seeking a role that offers greater alignment with my long-term career goals and allows me to utilize my skills in a more impactful way.

I am committed to ensuring a smooth transition during my departure. I am happy to assist in training my replacement, documenting my work, and completing any outstanding tasks before my last day.  Please let me know how I can best facilitate this process.

I wish you and [Company Name] continued success in the future.  I value the relationships I have built here and will cherish the experiences I have gained.

Sincerely,

[Your Signature]

**Notes and Considerations for Customization:**

*   **Legal Review:** This is a sample letter. It’s always a good idea to have any resignation letter reviewed by a legal professional, especially if you have concerns about potential legal ramifications.
*   **Company Policy:**  Review your company's official resignation policy.  Ensure your notice period aligns with the stated requirements.
*   **Specificity:** The level of detail provided regarding your reasons is crucial.  Too little detail might seem dismissive; too much detail could be unnecessarily negative. Tailor the explanation to your comfort level and the existing relationship with your boss.
*   **Tone:** The tone is professional and respectful. Avoid accusatory or overly emotional language.
*   **Positive Framing:**  Even when explaining reasons for leaving, try to frame them in a way that emphasizes your desire for growth and opportunity, rather than solely focusing on the negatives of the current situation.
*   **Offer of Assistance:**  Offering to assist with the transition is a gesture of professionalism and goodwill.
*   **Delivery:** Consider how you will deliver the letter. A face-to-face conversation followed by a written letter is often the most professional approach.
