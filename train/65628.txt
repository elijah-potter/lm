Undesirable insects, diseases and fungal pathogens can infest the peach tree (Prunus persica), leaving you scrambling to treat the problem. While you can use chemical pesticides, they're not necessarily the best option because they may contain harsh toxins that prove harmful to people, pets and wildlife. Instead, use organic pest control methods to successfully and safely treat the peach tree.
Peach trees need proper watering to prevent problems and ensure the tree is healthy. Too much or too little water will result in a water-stressed tree that is more susceptible to attacks from diseases and pests. The University of California Statewide Integrated Pest Management Program recommends watering the peach tree deeply every two to three weeks and providing supplement watering during periods of drought. Sanitation is another important step in reducing problems in peach tree. Regularly prune diseased wood from the peach tree to prevent new infections from occurring. Keep the area near the fruit tree free of debris, stumps, brush piles, weeds and leaf litter. These areas provide the perfect hiding, breeding and living environment for diseases and damaging insects. The peach tree also needs fertilizer to help keep it healthy and vigorous. Apply a 10-10-10 fertilizer once a year at a rate of about 1/4 pound of fertilizer for newly planted trees and 5 pounds for peach trees 10 years old or older.
Several pesticides are considered organic and are not toxic to people, animals and most beneficial insects, and are safe to use on peach trees up to the day of harvest. These pesticides include plant-based chemicals -- such as jojoba oil and neem oil -- and insecticidal soap. Jojoba and neem oils aren’t toxic to humans or mammals and won’t leave a chemical residue behind. These plant-based pesticides control both insects and fungal pathogens, including mites, aphids, earwigs, weevils, scale insects and whiteflies, as well as rusts, mildews, blights, leaf curl and leaf spots. Insecticidal soaps contain long-chain fatty acids and work by interfering with the fungal pathogen’s cell membranes or by smothering undesirable insects.
Biological control consists of using biological means -- such as insects, pathogens and parasites -- to organically control pests on peach trees. Not all insects are considered pests, and several species can act as natural pest control, preying on the undesirable insects that damage fruit trees. Lacewings, predatory wasps, ladybugs and predatory flies are common beneficial insects that feed on aphids, mites, scales, mealybugs and whiteflies. Other options for biological control include attracting birds to feed on caterpillars and using bacterium insecticides -- such as Bacillus thuringiensis -- to target peach tree pests, including leafrollers, borers, cutworms, fruit worms and caterpillars.
Use chemical as a last resort after you've tried cultural care, natural pesticides and predatory insects. Horticultural oil is generally considered nontoxic and -- when applied as a dormant oil -- won’t kill beneficial insects but can still control overwintering pests. Take care when treating peach trees with any pesticides -- organic or not. Never apply the pesticide when the temperatures are above 85 degrees Fahrenheit. Doing so could result in leaf burn and cause more harm than good.
