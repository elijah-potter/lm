The rain pattered against the crumbling stone archway as the lone figure stepped off the winding path, their boots sinking slightly into the mud. The streetlamps flickered erratically, casting long, jagged shadows over the crumbling pavement. The neon sign of a closed bakery flickered weakly, its glass face cracked. A distant siren wavered—too close, too familiar. The backpack on their shoulders felt heavier, stuffed with maps that had been torn, the edges frayed and unrecognizable. A half-eaten sandwich in their pocket tasted stale. The wind carried the scent of damp earth and something else—burnt oil, maybe, or old cigarettes. The phone in their pocket buzzed; the last call was to their cousin’s number. The screen read: *"You’re 50 miles from the city. Do you know where you are?"* No answer. Just the sound of their own breathing, shallow and rapid. The street sign above was half-buried in the weeds, the letters blurred by time and rain. The GPS in their pocket had died hours ago. The last exit sign—now a pile of rusted metal—was the only thing that still pointed north. The person turned slowly, staring at the ground, the weight of the unknown pressing down like a physical force. The city’s glow was a distant, fading promise.
