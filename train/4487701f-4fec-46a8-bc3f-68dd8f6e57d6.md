The morning light spilled through the cracks in the curtains like scattered gold, softening the edges of the room. His fingers traced the worn spine of the book—*The Alchemy of Loss*—still open at the passage about the "moment when the veil between knowing and forgetting thins." He had read it years ago, but now the words stung like a memory that refused to stay still. The coffee in his mug had turned lukewarm, the steam curling upward into a ghost of its former warmth.

Outside, the city hummed—a distant, rhythmic pulse. The train would arrive in forty minutes, but the train station was a thousand miles away. Instead, he stood in the kitchen, the kitchen he had shared with her, the one she had scrubbed until the grout gleamed, the one she had called home. The walls were still damp from the shower she had taken before leaving. The sink was clean. The floor was polished. The mirror reflected not just his face, but the ghost of her reflection too—smiling, laughing, alive.

He exhaled, slow and deliberate. The clarity had come like a wave, sharp and unmistakable: *She was gone. Not dead. Not gone forever. Just gone.* The grief had been a weight he had carried for years, a stone he had rolled with each step, each breath. But now, for the first time in years, the stone felt lighter. Or maybe it was just the weight of the moment itself—this moment of knowing, of *seeing* it, of understanding that the grief wasn’t a storm that would pass, but a quiet, persistent current.

He picked up the book again, turning the page. The passage had faded, but the ink remained, stubborn as memory. *"Clarity is the shadow before the fall."* The words made his stomach twist. He had thought he was ready. He had thought he could handle this. But clarity was just the first step—what came after was the part that always unraveled.

Outside, the city’s pulse grew louder. The train would arrive. He would leave. And somewhere, in another city, another life, she would still be waiting, still believing the moment of clarity would last forever.
