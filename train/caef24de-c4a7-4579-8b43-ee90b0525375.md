The storm front had been brewing for days, its dark clouds swallowing the last embers of the sun’s descent. Inside the cramped office, Elias adjusted his tie with deliberate precision, fingers tracing the edge of the coffee cup before him. The plan—*Operation: Horizon*—had been meticulously laid out on the whiteboard, its steps marked with blue tape like a map of a warzone. But now, as the first raindrops tapped against the window, the ink on the paper blurred.

"Elias," his assistant murmured, stepping into the dim light, "the courier just left. The shipment’s delayed—again."

He didn’t look up. "Another delay?" His voice was a razor’s edge.

She exhaled, frustration clinging to her like smoke. "They say the bridge’s still under reconstruction. The road’s flooded."

A pause. Then, softer: "We were supposed to be in Marseille by sundown."

The air in the room thickened. The plan—*unwritten, but there*—had always relied on a smooth passage through the Pyrenees. Now, the mountains loomed like silent sentinels, their peaks swallowed by the storm.

Elias stood abruptly, knocking over his coffee. The cup shattered on the floor, silent and final. He didn’t clean it up.

"Cancel it," he said. "Call it off."

The words hung between them, heavy with the weight of what was lost. The board’s blue tape scraped against the wall as the light flickered out, leaving only the hum of the city outside, indifferent. The plan had died with the first raindrop.
