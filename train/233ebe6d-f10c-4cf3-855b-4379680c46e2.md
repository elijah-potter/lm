The neon glow of the city pulsed like a heartbeat, casting jagged shadows across the wet pavement. A lone figure—clad in an old leather coat—stood motionless, fingers tracing the edge of a rusted pocket watch. The air hummed with an unnatural stillness, as if the world itself had frozen to observe the observer.

Then, the sky split open.

A crack of light, silver and sharp, split the horizon like a blade. Below, a man in a tattered overcoat stumbled into view, his steps unsteady, his breath shallow. He carried a satchel, its contents shifting in the current of his movement—something glinted inside: coins, a half-smoked cigar, a single, bloodstained letter.

Time had begun to unravel.

The figure watched as the man’s shadow stretched unnaturally long, elongating into a thing of shifting, ghostly proportions. The city’s traffic—cars, pedestrians, even the distant hum of a subway—paused. The air thickened, pressing against the skin like liquid glass.

The pocket watch’s hands froze mid-motion. The second hand had stopped.

The man reached into his coat and pulled out a revolver. The trigger clicked. The shot echoed—not in sound, but as a physical sensation, a wave of force that slithered outward, warping reality itself. The world dissolved into layers: the man’s reflection flickered like a dying candle, his movements stuttering into something mechanical.

Then, the city sighed back to life.

The man collapsed, the revolver clattering to the ground. The pocket watch’s hands resumed their slow march, its hands trembling. The figure watched, silent, as the past and future blurred into a single, aching present. Time was no longer a river but a mirror, and he was the glass.

The man’s breath came in ragged gasps. His coat had vanished, replaced by a plain white robe. His fingers curled around the letter, its edges singed, its ink faded but legible: *"You were never here. You are never here."*

The figure stepped forward, the watch clutched tightly in their grip. The air shimmered, and for a heartbeat, they were both the man and the observer, watching themselves from the outside.

Then—

The moment shattered.

The man’s body dissolved into dust. The figure’s reflection remained, but the world resumed its steady rhythm. The city breathed. The watch ticked.

The figure exhaled, the coat slipping from their shoulders like smoke. They picked up the revolver, the letter, and the shattered pocket watch. The city’s pulse returned to normal.

No one would ever know they had seen it.
