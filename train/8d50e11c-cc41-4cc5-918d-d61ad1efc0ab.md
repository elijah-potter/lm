The threshold of a half-submerged old church porch, its weathered wooden door cracked open, rainwater pooling in the cracked floorboards. The scent of damp earth and aged wood mingles with the faint, musky aroma of candle wax from a long-departed altar. Inside, the air hums with residual silence, punctuated by the occasional whisper of wind through broken stained glass. The threshold itself is a jagged line between the world of the living and the lingering remnants of one that was, a place where the past and present bleed into each other.
