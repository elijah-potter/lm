The abandoned lighthouse at Blackthorn Cove, a crumbling relic jutting from jagged cliffs, pulses with faint, rhythmic light—once steady, now flickering like a dying breath. The wind carries whispers of the sea, shifting voices from the past: sailors’ warnings, children’s laughter, the distant hum of an old radio that still broadcasts static. The air smells of salt, damp wood, and something metallic—like copper and old blood. When the tide recedes, the tide pools reflect not mirrors but faces: one is a fisherman’s, another a drowned child’s. The sand beneath your feet shifts under your weight, rearranging itself into shapes that dissolve before you can name them. The stones here hum when you press them; the waves answer in a language no human ear remembers. At night, the lighthouse itself seems to lean toward you, its lantern burning low, as if waiting for someone to step inside and finally answer.
