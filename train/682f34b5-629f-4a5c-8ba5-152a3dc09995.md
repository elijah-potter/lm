The storm’s first warning was a crackling in the air, like distant thunder before the rain. The old clock tower’s pendulum swung erratically as the wind howled through the hollows of the city’s skeletal buildings. The sign on the door of the abandoned bakery flickered in the dim glow of dying streetlamps—*Do Not Enter*—but the sign was already torn away, leaving only a smear of rusted metal and the faint scent of stale bread.

Inside, the air smelled of damp stone and something older—something that had been here for decades, waiting. The floorboards groaned underfoot, each creak a whisper of what came next. The warning was in the way the walls seemed to breathe, in the way the shadows moved when no one was looking. Somewhere deep in the cellar, a child’s laughter echoed, sharp and distant, like a ghost’s memory.

The first step was always the hardest. The second was the one that never came.
