What is Pavacol-D used for?
- Suppressing a dry, irritating and persistent cough, where you're not coughing up phlegm.
How does Pavacol-D work?
Pavacol-D linctus contains the active ingredient pholcodine (5mg per 5ml), which is a type of medicine called a cough suppressant. It works by blocking the cough reflex.
Coughing is a reflex response to irritation of the airways. It's normally useful for clearing mucus, dust and other particles from the throat and lungs. However, sometimes, coughing can become overstimulated even when there is no mucus to clear from the lungs or throat. In this situation coughing serves no purpose and can become painful and frustrating.
Pholcodine reduces the nerve signals that are sent from a part of the brain called the coughing centre to the muscles that produce coughing. This reduces the urge to cough and can be used to block the cough reflex in situations where the cough serves no purpose.
How do I take Pavacol-D?
- Adults and adolescents over 12 years of age should take one or two 5ml spoonfuls as required., this can be increased to three 5ml spoonfuls if needed. Do not take more than 60ml (12x5ml doses) in 24 hours.
- Children aged 6 to 12 years should be given one 5ml spoonful three to four times a day as required. Do not give more than four doses in 24 hours.
- Don't use this medicine for longer than five days in children without getting medical advice from a doctor. If your child's symptoms get worse while using this medicine you should consult your doctor.
- Do not exceed the recommended dose.
What should I know before taking Pavacol-D?
- Pholcodine may cause drowsiness and dizziness. It may be an offence to drive while you are taking this medicine. You should not drive if you think it affects your ability to drive safely, for example if it makes you feel sleepy, dizzy, or unable to concentrate or make decisions.
- You should avoid drinking alcohol with this cough medicine.
Who should get medical advice before using Pavacol-D?
- People with any breathing problems, history of asthma or lung disease.
- People with kidney problems.
- Women who are pregnant or breastfeeding.
- People with any of the following symptoms, as the cough should be investigated further:
- coughing up phlegm that is green, rusty brown, yellow, blood-stained or foul smelling
- shortness of breath, wheezing or difficulty breathing
- chest pain
- pain and swelling in the calf
- sudden weight loss
- recurrent night-time cough
- or a long-term persistent cough.
Who shouldn't take Pavacol-D?
- Children under 6 years of age.
- People with severe breathing problems, such as people having an asthma attack, and people with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) or chronic bronchitis.
- People with a condition called bronchiectasis, in which there is persistent widening and excessive mucus in the airways, as a result of lung disease, eg infection, inflammation, tumours or cystic fibrosis.
- People with a condition called bronchiolitis, which is inflammation of the smallest air passages in the lungs (bronchioles).
- People with liver disease.
- People who have taken a monoamine-oxidase inhibitor antidepressant (MAOI) in the last 14 days.
Can I take Pavacol-D if I'm pregnant or breastfeeding?
- If you are pregnant or breastfeeding you shouldn't take Pavacol-D without getting advice from your doctor first.
- The safety of pholcodine for use during pregnancy has not been established. It should only be used during pregnancy if the benefits to the mother outweigh any potential risks to the developing baby.
- Pholcodine passes into breast milk in small amounts. It should only be used by women who are breastfeeding if the expected benefit to the mother outweighs any potential risk to the nursing infant.
What are the possible side effects of Pavacol-D?
Medicines and their possible side effects can affect individual people in different ways. The following are some of the side effects that may be associated with pholcodine. Just because a side effect is stated here does not mean that all people taking Pavacol-D will experience that or any side effect.
- Feeling excited.
- Nausea and vomiting.
- Allergic reactions such as a rash or itching. Stop using the cough syrup and get medical advice if you think you've had an allergic reaction to it.
Read the leaflet that comes with the medicine or talk to your doctor or pharmacist if you want any more information about the possible side effects of Pavacol-D linctus. If you think you have experienced a side effect, you can report this using the yellow card website?
Can I take Pavacol-D with other medicines?
If you're already taking any other medicines, including herbal medicines and those bought without a prescription, you should always check with your pharmacist before taking Pavacol-D linctus as well, to make sure that the combination is safe.
However, you shouldn't use it with other cough or cold and flu remedies. Different products may contain the same or similar active ingredients and using more than one at the same time could lead to you exceeding the recommended dose. In particular don't use it with medicines containing cough expectorants like guaifenesin, or with other medicines that suppress a cough, such as dextromethorphan, pholcodine or codeine.
You shouldn't take Pavacol-D if you have taken a monoamine oxidase inhibitor antidepressant (MAOI), such as phenelzine, tranylcypromine, isocarboxazid or moclobemide, in the last 14 days.
If you find Pavacol-D makes you feel sleepy this may be made worse if you take it with other medicines that can cause drowsiness, such as the following:
- benzodiazepines, eg temazepam, diazepam
- sedating antihistamines, eg chlorphenamine, diphenhydramine, promethazine, triprolidine (these are often found in other non-prescription cough and cold or hayfever remedies)
- sleeping tablets, eg zopiclone
- strong opioid painkillers, eg codeine, co-codamol, dihydrocodeine, morphine, tramadol
- tricyclic antidepressants, eg amitriptyline.
