In the heart of World War II, a momentous event was about to unfold. As the clock struck the early hours of June 6, 1944, the world held its breath. The largest sea-bound invasion ever known to mankind was quietly launching. This was D-Day, the fateful operation set to invade Normandy. Led by the Western Allies - primarily the United States, Britain, and Canada - an astounding number of over 156,000 troops began their treacherous journey across the English Channel. This was a mission not just of courage, but of necessity. As the month of June drew to a close, the Allies had achieved the improbable. Normandy was secured, marking a pivotal shift in the tides of World War II. The D-Day invasion, a daring and successful operation, had indeed turned the course of the war.
