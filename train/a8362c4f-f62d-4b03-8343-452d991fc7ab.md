The hollowed-out core of a crumbling cathedral, its stained-glass windows shattered into jagged fragments that refract light into shifting, distorted patterns. Clock faces hang in perfect alignment but tick backward, their hands frozen at varying points—some at midnight, others at 3:17 AM, others at a future epoch no one remembers. The air hums with an eerie, static-like resonance, as if time itself is a distorted signal. In the distance, a single, slow-moving shadow crosses the stained glass—it appears to be walking backward down a spiral staircase, its reflection in the glass following it, yet always slightly ahead, as if caught in a loop of delayed perception.

The ground beneath shifts underfoot, revealing hidden passages where time appears to have no anchor. A child’s laughter echoes from a room where the walls are made of layered, translucent sheets—each layer a different era, yet the child’s movements blur between them. The scent of old books and damp stone lingers, mixed with the faint metallic tang of something not quite rust. Occasionally, a clockwork mechanism in the ceiling stutters to life, its gears grinding against each other as it tries to realign itself, forcing time to slow to a crawl. The last thing you remember is the sound of a bell tolling—then nothing.
