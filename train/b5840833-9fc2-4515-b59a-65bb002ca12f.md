The rain hammered against the rusted metal roof of the abandoned lighthouse, its beam sweeping erratically through the storm. Inside, Elias sat at the old desk, fingers tracing the edge of the half-finished promise note. The paper smelled of aged parchment and pine—his father’s scent still clinging to the ink.

His father had promised to bring his daughter home. Not just *home*—home to the old house on the cliffs, where the wind howled through the broken windows, but home to *himself*. To the boy who had been too afraid to ask for more than scraps of bread and stories at the docks.

Now, the note lay on the desk, its words barely legible under the dim lamp. *"I’ll take her back. I’ll keep her safe."* The ink had blurred with time, but the intent was clear.

Outside, the waves crashed against the foundation, shaking the floorboards. Elias exhaled slowly. He had spent a year hiding in the hold of a fishing trawler, listening to the old sailor’s tales of storms and forgotten promises. He had stolen enough to buy the ticket back to shore.

But the lighthouse keeper’s daughter, Mara, was different. She didn’t just need a roof over her head. She needed something else—something he couldn’t give her in a cramped cabin, something that cost more than his last coin.

The door creaked open. Mara stood in the doorway, her dark hair damp, her eyes wide with the same fear that had chased her from the docks. She clutched a worn leather satchel, the kind sailors used.

"Elias," she whispered. "You came."

He reached for her hand, his thumb tracing the calloused knuckles. "I’m sorry," he said. "I didn’t know how to say it."

The storm outside raged, but inside, the air was thick with the weight of what came next. The promise wasn’t just about returning. It was about proving he could keep it.
