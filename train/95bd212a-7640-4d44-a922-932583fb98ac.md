An endless void stretches before me, the horizon a distant, shimmering line that refuses to recede. Walls of cold, unyielding darkness stretch infinitely in every direction, their depth measured only in the absence of light. Shadows of forgotten stars drift lazily, forming shapes that dissolve like mist. The silence is not empty—it hums with the weight of silence itself, pressing down like a suffocating breath. Every step feels like a leap into the unknown; the ground beneath me, if there is one, is either a thin, unstable layer of dust or nothing at all. The air, if there is air, is stale and thick, thickened by the weight of timelessness. The sky, if there is a sky, is a bruised, bruised purple, bleeding into the black like a wound that never heals. The stars above are not distant points of light but vast, pulsating orbs, their glow dimmed by the distance—or perhaps by the fact that they are not meant to be seen. The horizon bends, curves, and twists into something unnatural, a fractal of its own creation. I am not alone here, but neither are the others. There are echoes, whispers of voices that were never spoken, and the faint, rhythmic *thump* of something vast and ancient moving just beyond the edges of my perception. The walls here are not just barriers—they are living things, breathing in the slow, steady rhythm of the universe, indifferent to my existence. The space itself is a wound, a gaping hole in reality that refuses to be closed. It demands to be filled, to be understood, to be *known*—but the knowledge is already too vast, already drowning me in its own weight.
