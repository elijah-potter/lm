The weight of decisions pressed like a heavy stone between my ribs, each option a potential trap or salvation. The path ahead blurred into a spectrum of possibilities—some whispered of glory, others of ruin. The doubt was not in my ability, but in the silence of the universe itself, as if the choice demanded more than my will, more than logic, and instead called for faith in the unknown.

A fork in the road, but not one I could see—only the faintest echo of footsteps behind me, the scent of distant smoke, the way my reflection in a cracked mirror seemed to waver between two faces. The test was not in the doing, but in the standing. To move forward was to accept the risk of failure; to hesitate was to invite the ghosts of what could have been. The air hummed with the tension of a question no one dared ask: *Are you brave enough to choose, or do you prefer the comfort of indecision?* The answer was carved into the choice itself, and the choice was the test.
