AITA for refusing to let my sister use my inherited vacation home?

Okay, this is a bit complicated, and I’m genuinely unsure how to feel about it. My grandmother, Eleanor, passed away last November (2023) after a long illness. She left her property, a small, charming cottage in Cape Cod, Massachusetts, directly to me in her will. I was surprised, as my sister, Clara, always assumed she’s who Eleanor wanted to inherit it. They were very close for years, and Clara visited Eleanor weekly for over a decade. I, on the other hand, live across the country in Seattle, Washington, and saw my grandmother maybe twice a year.

I’m incredibly grateful for the inheritance. The cottage is in excellent condition, and I'm planning to use it as a source of income by renting it out on platforms like Airbnb. I’m also looking forward to using it for my own vacations, of course. I’ve already had a realtor assess the property and have a business plan drafted.

The issue arose last week, on December 2nd. Clara called me, genuinely excited, and asked if she could use the cottage for two weeks over Christmas, from December 22nd to January 5th. She explained that she and her husband, Mark, were desperate for a break and that a trip to Cape Cod would be "exactly what they needed." She emphasized how much it would mean to her, especially given their history with my grandmother. I initially hesitated. I'm still figuring out the logistics of managing the property remotely, and having a guest during my initial setup period felt… complicated.

I explained that I was planning to begin renting it out shortly and that her requested dates might conflict. She became quite upset, accusing me of being selfish and implying that I was deliberately trying to deny her something that rightfully belonged to her—because of their long-standing relationship with Grandma. She said I was "throwing away" a precious connection to our family history.

I tried to explain that the property was *mine* now, and that I had plans for it. I offered to consider it for a future visit, but firmly stated that her requested dates were not available. This seemed to escalate her frustration. Mark joined the call, and he chimed in, saying I was being unreasonable and that my grandmother would be "disappointed" in my behavior. He even suggested that I should just *give* the cottage to Clara if I didn’t want it. 

I felt incredibly pressured and frankly, a little bullied. I ended the call, feeling drained and guilty. My mother, Patricia, called me later that evening. She sided with Clara, saying I should be more generous and understanding of Clara’s feelings. She admitted that Clara's relationship with Grandma was deeper than mine ever was, and that I was being insensitive. She implied I was being greedy, prioritizing financial gain over family sentiment.

I’m now feeling conflicted. Part of me feels guilty for denying Clara what she wants, especially given their history. But another part of me feels entitled to do what I want with property that is legally mine. I’m also annoyed at the pressure and the implication that I'm somehow a bad person for having financial plans. I've tried to communicate my position calmly, but it feels like nothing I say is being heard. My sister and mother seem to believe I'm deliberately trying to hurt them.

I’m truly unsure how to proceed. Should I cave and let Clara use the cottage, even though it disrupts my plans and makes managing it more difficult?



AITA?
