At first glance, the scientist dismissed the farmer’s claim of seeing double vision—a mere optical illusion caused by the old spectacles. But during the next trial, the farmer pointed out that the farmer’s glasses were the same brand, yet only one of their lenses was cracked. The cracked lens distorted the light, making objects appear to shift, confirming the farmer’s earlier observations.

Later, when the farmer’s wife brought a pair of identical, unmarked glasses from a different store, she saw the same effect—double vision where the cracked lens was placed. The farmer’s glasses, it turned out, were not the issue. The problem was the farmer’s eyesight—he had been misdiagnosed for years, his vision incorrectly attributed to faulty optics.

The real truth: the farmer’s eyes were misaligned, and the cracked lens in the wrong glasses merely highlighted the deeper issue. The misunderstanding started with assuming the farmer’s glasses caused the vision problem, but in reality, the glasses had no fault—only his eyes. The cracked lens merely provided the context for the misdiagnosis.
