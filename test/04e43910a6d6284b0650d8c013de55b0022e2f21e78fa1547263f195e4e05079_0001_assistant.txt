1. Lionfish 

These fish have venomous spines along their backs for protection. Lionfish are slow swimmers, so they need envenomation as protection against larger, more predatory sea creatures. 

For humans, lionfish stings are rarely fatal unless there’s an extreme allergic reaction to the venom. However, the sting is still tremendously painful and causes diarrhea, extreme nausea, and headaches. 

2. Sea Snakes 

One of the most venomous aquatic animals in the world, sea snakes are often reluctant to bite unless severely threatened. However, their one drop of venom is potent enough to kill an adult human. 

3. Cone Snails 

Cone snails use a venomous harpoon for both protection and prey. They are extremely slow movers, so envenomation is their only hope for caching meals of marine worms, small fish, and mollusks. 

These snails have bright shells with intricate, unique patterns, which can be a detriment to humans. One dose of harpoon venom has the potential to internally damage, or even kill, hundreds of people. 

4. Stonefish 

The stonefish looks like a rock, which means swimmers in the Indian and Pacific Oceans often step on these fish by mistake. The fish is covered with venomous spikes that shove venom into whatever it comes into contact with. Their stings are excruciating and can be fatal to humans. 

5. Flower Urchin 

One of the most dangerous urchins on the planet, flower urchins are covered in venomous stingers. One touch can lead to paralysis of muscles, lips, and eyes, but the pain diminishes quickly, typically in 15 minutes or less. However, an extreme allergic reaction can result in hospitalization or death for humans.
