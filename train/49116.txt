The volume of reporting on rising food prices, famine, and instability can get overwhelming (and 2011's been one of the worst years for all three in recent memory), but Oxfam--a large global organization that fights hunger and poverty--unveiled a new project last night to help people parse what's going on with the world's food.
The "Food Pressure Points Map" lays out which countries are vulnerable to different kinds of food crises, and what kind of impact food problems have there. But more importantly, it gives a solid reason why these countries are so painfully affected by food instability. This is all a part of Oxfam's GROW campaign to "fix the broken food system," and the "Act Now" link in the map helps point towards solutions for the worst of the food problems.
According to Oxfam, the number of people without enough to eat could soon top one billion.
