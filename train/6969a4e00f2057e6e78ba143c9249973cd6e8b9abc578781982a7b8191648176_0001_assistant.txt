A Guide to Mental Health and Suicide Prevention

Mental health is a real issue today. Yet, people aren’t completely aware of the underlying implications. Unfortunately, misinformation and stigma around the issue often lead to suicides. We want to offer informative advice on how to prevent suicide.

1. You CAN Make a Difference
People often overlook this. You CAN make a huge difference and do helpful things to prevent suicide. Learn about it and inform others about the warning signs. You can use available resources so people who are at risk receive support and help. 

2. Explore All Your Treatment Options 
Therapy, peer support groups and many other treatments can help improve your mental health. Discuss these treatments with your doctor and find the right treatment plan. And we can’t say this enough – don't be afraid to ask for help. You don't have to face this alone. 

3. Recognize Warning Signs and Respond to Them 
Suicide tendencies almost always have warning signs like a lack of interest in activities, withdrawal from social connections, sudden mood shifts, impulsivity, and hopelessness. Try to identify and intervene before a crisis arises. Be an active listener and help connect them with professional help. 

4. Speaking Up Can Save Lives 
Talking about suicide will not trigger someone to become suicidal. Open and honest conversations about suicide create a supportive environment and give hope to people who’re on the edge and struggling with their mental health.

In the end, being considerate and inclusive goes a long way when it comes to suicide prevention. People often need external support to realize there’s more to life than they think. If you come across someone who has suicidal tendencies, you should talk to the right people about it.
