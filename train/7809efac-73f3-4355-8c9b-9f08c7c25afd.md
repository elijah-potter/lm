## Reclaiming Resilience: Transforming Oakhaven Ravine into a Climate-Resilient Community Oasis

**Executive Summary (For Municipal Funders)**

The City of Meadowbrook faces increasing challenges from intensified rainfall events and rising summer temperatures. Oakhaven Ravine, a currently underutilized and ecologically degraded public space bordering the Elmwood and Briarwood neighborhoods, presents a unique opportunity to address these concerns while simultaneously fostering community connection and enhancing quality of life. This proposal outlines a comprehensive plan to transform Oakhaven Ravine into a climate-resilient community asset, incorporating stormwater management infrastructure, native habitat restoration, accessible pathways, and a dedicated community garden. The project, spearheaded by the Meadowbrook Environmental Action Committee (MEAC) in partnership with the City Parks Department and local volunteer organizations, will demonstrably improve flood mitigation, reduce the urban heat island effect, enhance biodiversity, and create a welcoming space for recreation and education for all Meadowbrook residents.  We request $150,000 in municipal funding, matched by $75,000 in volunteer labor, donated materials, and in-kind services, for a total project value of $225,000. This investment will yield significant and lasting benefits for the entire Meadowbrook community.

**Project Plan: Phases, Partners, and Deliverables**

The “Reclaiming Resilience” project will unfold across four distinct phases, each with assigned responsibilities and measurable deliverables.

**Phase 1: Assessment & Design (Months 1-3) – Responsible Partner: MEAC, City Landscape Architect – Deliverables:** Site assessment report detailing existing conditions and vulnerabilities; Preliminary design plans incorporating green infrastructure solutions (rain gardens, bioswales, permeable paving); Community feedback report incorporating input from public forums and surveys; Finalized design plans approved by City Planning and Engineering.

**Phase 2: Infrastructure Development (Months 4-8) – Responsible Partner: City Parks Department, MEAC Volunteer Crew, Local Contractor (bidding process) – Deliverables:** Removal of invasive plant species and debris; Installation of stormwater conveyance system (bioswales and rain gardens); Construction of accessible pathways compliant with ADA standards; Permeable paving installation for designated areas; Erosion control measures implemented.

**Phase 3: Habitat Restoration & Community Garden Establishment (Months 9-12) – Responsible Partner: MEAC Native Plant Committee, Briarwood Horticultural Society, Community Volunteers – Deliverables:** Planting of native trees, shrubs, and wildflowers; Establishment of a community garden with raised beds and accessible irrigation; Installation of interpretive signage detailing ecological benefits and native species; Soil remediation and fertilization.

**Phase 4: Educational Programming & Ongoing Maintenance (Months 13-18) – Responsible Partner: Meadowbrook Environmental Education Center, MEAC Stewardship Team, City Parks Department – Deliverables:** Development and implementation of educational workshops on stormwater management, native plants, and sustainable gardening; Creation of a volunteer stewardship program for ongoing maintenance; Establishment of a maintenance schedule with assigned responsibilities; Community training on garden upkeep and ecological preservation.

**Budget & Financial Transparency**

The total project cost is estimated at $225,000. We are requesting $150,000 in municipal funding, which will be leveraged by $75,000 in matching funds secured through volunteer labor, in-kind donations, and material contributions.

    *  Permeable Paving: $40,000
    *  Construction Materials (gravel, mulch, lumber): $25,000
    *  Contractor Labor (bioswale construction, pathway grading): $30,000
    *  Signage and Interpretive Panels: $10,000
    *  Irrigation System Components: $10,000

* **Matching Funds: $75,000**
    *  Volunteer Labor (estimated value): $35,000 (calculated at $20/hour for 1750 hours)
    *  Donated Materials (lumber, gravel, mulch – secured commitments): $25,000

A contingency fund of 5% ($11,250) is included within the municipal funding request to address unforeseen expenses, such as material price fluctuations or unexpected site conditions.  Detailed invoices and receipts will be meticulously tracked and provided to the City throughout the project’s duration, ensuring full financial accountability.

**Community Engagement, Equity Impacts, and Risk Mitigation**

Community engagement has been central to the project’s conception.  Public forums were held in both Elmwood and Briarwood to solicit input on design preferences and desired amenities.  A dedicated online platform facilitated ongoing communication and feedback.  The design incorporates accessible pathways and raised garden beds to ensure inclusivity for individuals with disabilities.  The community garden plots will be allocated through a lottery system, prioritizing low-income residents.

Potential risks have been carefully assessed.  Heavy rainfall during construction could delay progress; this risk will be mitigated by implementing robust erosion control measures and scheduling work during drier periods.  Vandalism is a concern; security patrols and community involvement will help deter such activity.  The presence of potentially contaminated soil will be addressed through a preliminary site assessment and appropriate remediation protocols.

**Post-Grant Sustainability**

Long-term sustainability is paramount.  The Meadowbrook Environmental Education Center will provide ongoing educational programming, fostering community stewardship.  The MEAC Stewardship Team will organize regular volunteer workdays for maintenance and upkeep.  The City Parks Department will incorporate the ravine into its routine maintenance schedule.  A dedicated endowment fund will be established to support ongoing expenses, such as irrigation and soil amendments.
