**The Quiet Before the Storm**

The city hummed beneath my feet, a steady pulse of neon and steel, while I sipped my coffee black, the warmth of the cup a fragile rebellion against the cold. The morning air carried the scent of rain and distant engines, and for a moment, I allowed myself to believe I was still alive—still *somewhere*.

A hand clapped my shoulder. "You look like you’ve seen a ghost."

I turned, fingers tightening around the mug. "Or maybe I’ve seen a future I don’t want to remember."

Outside, the crowd thinned, the usual chaos melting into something quieter. A woman in a raincoat leaned against the lamppost, her breath fogging the glass. She smiled, but it didn’t reach her eyes. I should have said something—anything—but the words stuck like glue.

Then the announcement came over the speakers: *"All passengers, please report to the terminal by 23:00."*

The crowd erupted. Not with fear, but with the kind of reckless energy that comes from knowing you’re running out of time. I watched them move, swift and unbroken, as if they’d been doing this for years.

I followed.

---

**The Weight of the World**

The terminal smelled like stale cigarettes and regret. The air thickened with the sound of boots on concrete, the hum of engines, the distant wail of a siren.

I was the last one in line.

The security guard’s eyes flickered over me. "You look like you’re trying to outrun something."

I exhaled. "Or trying to run *into* it."

The line moved. The guard’s voice dropped. "You know, some people say this place isn’t just a gate. It’s a mirror."

I turned away, fingers digging into my pockets. "I’ve seen enough mirrors."

The doors hissed open.

The last train was pulling out.
