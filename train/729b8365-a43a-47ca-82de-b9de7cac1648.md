Two colleagues in a project meeting argued over a minor change to a specification document. One team member insisted on stricter data validation rules, citing past incidents where incomplete data caused delays. The other argued that the changes were unnecessary overhead, believing the current process was adequate.

The disagreement escalated when one team member’s suggestion to add a new dependency (a third-party API) was dismissed as "unnecessary complexity." Their colleague countered that the API had already been tested and was reliable.

Over time, frustration grew as small issues piled up. Deadlines slipped, and morale dipped. The team split into two factions: those who supported stricter controls and those who wanted to maintain efficiency. Conflicts became more frequent, and the original project goals were pushed back multiple times.

Eventually, the project manager was forced to intervene, forcing a compromise—but tensions remained. The unresolved friction led to a breakdown in collaboration, and the team’s productivity suffered. The larger issue now included distrust, missed deadlines, and a strained working relationship that threatened the project’s success.
