The firelight flickered across the cracked leather of the armchair, casting long shadows on the worn carpet. Elias adjusted his coat, fingers tracing the silver locket at his throat. His voice was rough with age, each word a careful weight.

*"You know, when I was a boy, there was a tale told in the tavern by an old sailor—one who swore he’d sailed these waters himself. He called it ‘The Ghost of Blackthorn.’ He said a ship, the *Marina’s Revenge*, vanished near these cliffs centuries ago. Crew vanished, not drowned, not lost at sea. They just… vanished. The locals say the mist curls wrong here at night, and if you listen closely, you can hear them laughing in the wind. No one ever found them, no bones, no wreckage. Just… gone. Like they were never there at all."*

Elias exhaled, the smoke curling into the silence. *"The man who told me that story was a drunk, but he was right about one thing: the cliffs remember. And sometimes, when the tide’s low, you can see the *Marina’s Revenge* resting on the rocks—just a ghost ship, waiting for its crew to finish what they started."*
