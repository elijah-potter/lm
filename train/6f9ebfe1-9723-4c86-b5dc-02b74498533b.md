**The decision to act** was the moment the old man hesitated before the cliff’s edge, his breath shallow but resolve firm. He had watched the storm rage for hours, the wind howling like a dying beast, while the others inside the shelter whispered of survival or escape. The rope, frayed but sturdy, dangled between his fingers—no longer a tool, but a test of will.

He had failed before. The last time, his fear had frozen his limbs, and the wind had carried his name away like smoke. But this time, the storm was his ally. The lightning split the sky, casting jagged shadows across the rocks, and in that split second, he saw the path—not as death, but as the only way forward. The others would stay. He would climb.

The first step was weightless, then heavy with purpose. His hands scraped against the stone, blood trickling down his arm as he pressed on. The wind screamed, but he did not turn back. Each pull was a choice: to endure or to end. When his feet touched the ledge below, he looked back—not at the shelter, but at the horizon, where the storm’s fury met the earth.

The act was not in the leap itself, but in the moment he chose to leap. The storm was not the enemy; it was the crucible. And he had chosen to rise.
