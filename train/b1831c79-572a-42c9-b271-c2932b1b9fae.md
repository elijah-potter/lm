A newly forming routine unfolds in the abandoned subway tunnel beneath the city’s east end. The concrete walls, once slick with condensation, now bear faint, parallel lines of rust, the remnants of an old maintenance crew’s forgotten work. A single flickering bulb casts a sickly glow over the damp floor, where discarded crates and rusted tools litter the space. The air smells of damp earth and stale paint, thick with the scent of the city’s decay.

A figure moves along the tunnel’s curved ceiling, their silhouette cast in shadow. They carry a worn notebook, its pages filled with handwritten sketches and notes. The routine isn’t just a place—it’s a rhythm: the hum of the city’s unseen systems, the occasional distant clatter of a passing train, the faint echo of footsteps on the same path they tread. This is where ideas take root, where the old crumbles to make way for the new. The tunnel’s hollow echoes their progress, a silent metronome marking the passage of time.
