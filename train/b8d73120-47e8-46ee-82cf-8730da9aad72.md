Crumbling stone pavement, rough and slick underfoot, interspersed with moss-clad slabs that absorb the dampness like a sponge. The air hums with the scent of damp earth and rusted metal—old hinges, half-buried iron grates, and the faint, sweet tang of mildewed fabric from forgotten belongings. Warmth seeps through uneven planks, revealing layers of hardwood beneath, polished by centuries of bare feet. Cobblestones shift beneath fingers, some smooth from centuries of passing, others jagged with hidden cracks. Nearby, a weathered wooden door—its paint peeling in patches—drips water onto the floor. The air feels thick with the weight of forgotten history, the hum of distant machinery, and the quiet, creaking resonance of time pressing against the bones of the place.
