A decision feels like a leap when the path ahead is uncharted, the risks are invisible, and every choice feels both exhilarating and terrifying. The mind hesitates, as if suspended between gravity and flight—one wrong step and you could plummet, one right and you could soar. The fear isn’t just of failure; it’s the fear of the *unknown*, the quiet panic that this choice might define you in a way the safe choices never could.

Yet, the leap isn’t just about daring. It’s also about the quiet certainty that staying still means surrendering to the weight of all the "what ifs" you’ll never know. The leap demands faith—not in the outcome, but in the courage to trust that the world will still turn even if you don’t land perfectly. Sometimes, the hardest part isn’t the fall; it’s the moment you realize you’ve already taken the jump and must simply trust that the landing will come.
