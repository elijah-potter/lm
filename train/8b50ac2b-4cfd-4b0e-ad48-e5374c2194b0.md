**Setting:** *A dimly lit attic, dust motes swirling in the afternoon sun filtering through a cracked skylight. Old leather-bound books, faded photographs, and a half-empty teapot sit on a wooden table. The air smells of aged parchment and something faintly metallic.*

**Characters:** *Lena, 38, a former librarian with sharp eyes and a habit of collecting odd trinkets. She’s pulling a chair closer to the edge of the table when her phone buzzes—a notification from an old friend.*

---

Lena’s fingers pause over the screen. The name glitches: *"Darius."* She swipes past the message—*"Found it. Same as yours."* Her breath catches. The phone slips from her grip.

The teapot clatters. She turns slowly, half-expecting the attic to echo with laughter or the distant *clang* of a bell, but it’s silent. Then, from the corner, something moves. A small, ornate box, half-buried under a pile of yellowed papers, jolts to life.

Her pulse quickens. The box is smooth, etched with symbols that aren’t Latin or Cyrillic. The lid isn’t nailed shut—just left open. Inside, a single pendant glints in the light, its surface etched with the same symbols, though now she recognizes them: the *same* as the one in her pocket.

The attic door creaks. A draft.

She grabs the box, her heart hammering. The pendant feels warm. The symbols on it—*they’re pulsing.*

Outside, the wind howls. The skylight rattles.
