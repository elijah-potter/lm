A flickering fluorescent light casts long shadows across the cracked linoleum of a dimly lit apartment hallway. The walls, once pristine, bear faded peeling paint where the moisture from the old pipe beneath has seeped upward. On the countertop beside the sink, a single, half-eaten sandwich sits alongside a half-empty cup of tea, the steam long condensed into dust.

Beside it rests a small, smooth stone—no larger than a quarter—engraved with what appears to be a spiral, though the carving is too shallow to determine if it’s natural or deliberate. The stone’s surface glints faintly under the light, reflecting an image of the hallway’s ceiling, which is missing a single tile and shows a hidden, circular opening.

The doorknob of the apartment door twists slightly when no one is present, as if resisting being closed. Inside, a single chair’s legs creak when no one sits. The fridge hums softly, its doors slightly ajar, revealing a single, half-rotten apple inside. The refrigerator’s freezer compartment is empty except for a small, unlabelled can of beans that has never been opened. The can is slightly dented, and when gently tapped, it emits a faint, resonant *ting*.
