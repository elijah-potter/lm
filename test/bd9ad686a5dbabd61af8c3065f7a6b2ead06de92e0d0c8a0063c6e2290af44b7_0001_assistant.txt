I like to run. I never used to like it when I was younger, but as I get older I find myself enjoying running immensely. Running becomes a great form of moving meditation that also serves as a fantastic workout. But I wasn't born with the ability to run, I had to learn how to run. Yes, running is pretty simple, but efficiency in running is something that requires a lot of practice. Here's what I have noticed in the years since I started running that has helped me to improve:

1. Start low, and work your way up - I started running based on time, rather than distance. My beginner's routine was a mix of running and walking, made up of 60- to 90-second runs interspersed with 60-second rest periods over 7 or 8 cycles. As I ran longer minutes, I was gaining more distance, and after some time I was able to switch to distance running where I would run based on how far I wanted to go.
2. Low and slow is the key - running slowly and ensuring I got my running form correct were keys to my improvements in running. Low-impact running, not going all-out trying to break records or push my body unnecessarily, helped me to build on my weekly mileage.
3. Relax and breathe - relaxing your body during a run is important to conserving energy over long distances. Also, learning to breathe while running (such as cadenced breathing or breathing with a certain tempo) helps to ensure you're getting enough air into your lungs while still being able to work your body.
4. Finding the zone - when I run, I am sometimes able to reach a specific "zone," where I am running and not expending much energy, yet I am still getting a workout. It feels like being on autopilot, where my body can act accordingly while my mind can meditate on things like my surroundings, the music I'm listening to, or whatever I want to think about that day. This zone takes time to reach because it requires training the body to reach a certain level of fitness before you can be comfortable with running multiple miles and not being tired. But once you've reached it, it will feel like you're gliding effortlessly.
