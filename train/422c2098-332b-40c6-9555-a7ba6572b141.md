A line of jagged stone curves from the Arctic tundra’s frozen hump toward a volcanic crater’s rim, where geothermal vents hisse steam like a sleeping dragon. The horizon is a spiral of black sand, where dunes twist into swirling galaxies of dunes. The wind here does not blow—it *sings*, a whisper of friction between grains that coalesce into shapes before dissolving into mist. At the center of this spiral lies a lake of obsidian glass, reflecting nothing but the endless black of the void beyond, its edges dissolving into the mist like smoke. The edges here are not walls but rivers: one of molten rock that feeds the crater’s heart, another of salt that crystallizes into diamonds when the wind shifts. The sky above is a membrane of smoke, stretched taut over a chasm where the land splits open to reveal a second spiral of darkness below. The edges of this place are not boundaries but bridges—between the frozen and the burning, the seen and unseen, the past and the future. The air tastes of ozone and iron, and the ground hums with the slow pulse of the world’s heartbeat.
