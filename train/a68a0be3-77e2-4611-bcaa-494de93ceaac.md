A hollow vow hung between us—words carved in dust that never quite caught the wind.
*"I’ll be there for you."* The voice cracked like a branch in winter, but the hands that shook it were already cold, already pulling away.
The "I" in *"I’ll"* was a ghost, and the "you" was a shadow stretching toward the horizon, where the sky split into two halves of the same unbroken promise.

She sat on the edge of the bed, knees pulled to her chest, watching the rain blur the window like it was trying to erase the years between them. *"What if I don’t feel it anymore?"* she whispered. The answer was in the way her own breath turned to mist, the same way the promise had.

The therapist’s words were a blade: *"You don’t have to keep it if it’s not yours."* But what if it wasn’t yours at all? What if it was a debt, a debt written in ink that couldn’t be undone? The pen had been dry for so long, the paper yellowed with time, the ink long since dissolved into something else—something that didn’t need to be kept.

The phone rang. A number she didn’t recognize. She picked up the receiver, and the voice on the other end was smooth, patient, like a clock winding down. *"I hear you’re struggling with your promise. That’s okay. Some promises are just meant to be shared, not kept."* It wasn’t a question. It was a confession.

She hung up. The silence between them was thicker than any answer. The promise had dissolved like sugar in water, leaving behind only the taste of something sweet that had never been real.
