The hum of fluorescent tubes in the sterile glow of the hospital ward echoed like distant thunder, a sound so ordinary it felt alien. Outside, the city’s pulse—taxis and sirens—murmured against the glass, but here, in this sterile bubble of white, time slowed to a crawl, each breath a measured act of survival. They called it a ward, but the walls were more like the pages of a book left open too long, each section a story half-written, a life suspended between the margins.
