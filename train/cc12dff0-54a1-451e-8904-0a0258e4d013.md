The clock struck midnight in the old attic, its chimes dissolving into the scent of damp wood and old books. Outside, the city slept, its neon veins pulsing weakly through the fog. Inside, Clara sat cross-legged on the floor, her fingers tracing the spine of a worn leather-bound tome. The book was empty—except for the single, glowing line that pulsed like a heartbeat at its center:

*"The door is always open."*

She reached for it. The moment her palm brushed its surface, the air shimmered. The room dissolved into a swirling vortex of colors, and when it settled, Clara found herself standing in the same attic—but the clock had struck noon. A door at the far end of the space was ajar, revealing a staircase descending into darkness. A voice, warm and familiar, whispered from the void below:

*"Come down."*

She hesitated. The weight of her own name—*Clara*—echoed in the silence, as if it had been waiting for her all along. The book in her hands was no longer empty. Its pages unfolded, revealing a story that had already begun, and the line at the center glowed brighter:

*"The past is a door."*

She took the first step.
