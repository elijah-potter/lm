**The Last Light**

The city slept under a veil of frost, its neon dreams shattered by the first light of dawn. Across the alley, a broken clock’s hands spun wildly, frozen midway between midnight and morning, as if caught in a time loop no one dared break. The air smelled of cold steel and old books, the kind that had seen centuries before the world remembered to turn pages.

She found the door ajar through the rusted gate, its hinges groaning like a dying breath. Inside, the walls pulsed with the faint glow of an unseen camera, its lens capturing not just the scene but the soul of those who passed. The floor beneath her feet was a grid of black squares, each one a window to another world—some still, some flickering with colors she didn’t recognize.

A mirror hung over the fireplace, but instead of her reflection, it showed a stranger: a child in a tattered coat, holding a small camera in their trembling hands. The image blurred, then sharpened—just for a second—into a face she knew too well.

Outside, the first birdsong pierced the silence. The camera clicked once, capturing the moment the city woke up. The image on the screen was a single frame: a hand reaching into the dark, fingers brushing against the cold glass of a window, before dissolving into static.
