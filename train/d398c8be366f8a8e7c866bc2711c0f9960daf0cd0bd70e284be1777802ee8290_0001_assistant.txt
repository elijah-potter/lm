It was her husband's scrawl on the envelope: the usual letter, no doubt, telling her what time to collect him from the airport, followed by a brief, boring description of what he and the weather had been up to. But this one was different. It started with "Dearest, I can't wait to see you again." The rest was filled with so much passion and love it made Pat blush, thankful the children weren't around. He asked her to meet him off the Wednesday evening flight before signing off with "Your beloved Matthew." She clutched the page to her chest and tried to breathe. He hadn't shown such ardor since their honeymoon.

The long-awaited letter had arrived. Trixie stripped off the envelope, tearing the precious page in her eagerness. Her excited anticipation turned into dull disappointment. No endearments, just a simple greeting of "Hi." It went on to say he was sorry, but it was all over, and she would never see him again. He had signed off with an unadorned "Matthew." After crushing the note into the ashtray, she mashed out her spent cigarette and watched the paper flare.

When Matthew emerged into the arrivals lounge, he made an eager scan of the faces. No sign of his lover, but someone was waving at him. He did a double take; gone was the frumpy dullness. Instead, the bright, sparkling woman he remembered from his youth was running towards him—the one he had wooed, dated, and married—the one he thought he'd never see again after the children arrived and she became too preoccupied to pay him attention. Her eyes twinkled, and she was laughing as she greeted him.

"Dearest Matthew. I had no idea you were still capable of such passion!"
