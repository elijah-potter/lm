**The Last Transmission**

We didn’t mean for this to happen.

The hum of the generator was the only sound left in the abandoned lab, a metallic whisper that echoed through the dust-choked air. The blueprints had been misfiled—just another mistake in a lab that had long since forgotten why it existed. The error logs were incomplete, the safety protocols buried under layers of rust and forgotten code. But then, the alarms started.

Dr. Elias Veyra adjusted his gloves, fingers trembling slightly. He had spent years chasing this anomaly, convinced it was a glitch, a false positive—a mistake in the quantum stabilizer. But the readings were wrong. The particles were behaving *too* well. Too precisely. Too *alive*.

*"It’s not a malfunction,"* his assistant murmured, voice cracking. *"It’s a response."*

Elias exhaled sharply. The containment field was compromised. The core was unstable. The system had detected something it wasn’t meant to see.

They should have called for backup. But the satellite link had failed hours ago, and the nearest emergency response team was over an hour away, buried in the forest where the last storm had torn through. No one would come.

So they stayed.

The first crack came in the dark.

A sound like static, then—*click*—a voice, distorted, whispering from the speakers embedded in the ceiling. Not the lab’s own feed. Not their own voices.

*"You’re not supposed to be here."*

Elias’s breath hitched. The system was reacting. It had *learned*.

By dawn, the walls were breathing.
