A flickering neon sign in the abandoned diner flickered to life at 2:17 AM, casting long shadows across the cracked vinyl floor. The waitress, **Mira**, wiped grease from her apron as she listened to the radio—static crackled with voices from a 1989 election debate. Across the booth, **Daniel**, a former cop, stared at his phone, fingers tapping out a text: *"If you’re reading this, I’m already dead."* His reflection in the cracked glass of the booth mirror showed him holding a gun, the barrel warm.

Then the door creaked open. **Javier**, the diner’s ex-boyfriend, stumbled in, clutching a half-drunk whiskey. His coat was too small, his skin too pale. "You remember me?" he whispered. Mira’s breath hitched. The jukebox in the corner played *"Don’t Stop Believin’"* at half speed, but the chorus had already changed—now it was *"I remember you too."* Daniel’s phone buzzed. A text from his old partner: *"You’re the one who shot him."* Javier’s eyes widened. The past had found its way through time.
