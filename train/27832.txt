Treating ear infections
Page 2 of 2
My baby keeps getting ear infections. Is there anything I can do to prevent them?
Yes, these steps can help:
- Keep your baby away from tobacco smoke, since smoke raises his risk of infection. Make your home a smoke-free environment and keep your baby out of smoky places.
- Breastfeed for at least the first six months. Breast milk provides antibodies that make him less likely to develop ear infections.
- When feeding your baby, hold him with his head higher than the rest of his body. Babies fed while they're lying down are more likely to develop AOM.
- Wash your hands — and your baby's — often. While ear infections aren't contagious, the respiratory infections that can lead to them are. So keep your baby's hands clean and keep him away from people with respiratory infections whenever possible.
- If your baby repeatedly suffers from AOM, especially after bouts with the flu, you may want to consider an annual flu shot. But talk to his doctor: Flu shots are only given to babies over 6 months old, and even then they aren't recommended across the board.
- Make sure your baby's vaccinations are up to date. The pneumococcal vaccine has been shown to help reduce the number of ear infections a child gets.
- Consider ear tubes. This one's controversial, but the surgical route may be recommended if your baby has recurring ear infections. That's because the persistent presence of fluid in the ears makes him more susceptible not just to ear infections but to hearing loss. And hearing problems can impede language development.
The procedure, called a myringotomy, is performed using general anesthesia. An otolaryngologist (ear, nose, and throat surgeon) makes a tiny incision in the eardrum and inserts a small tube into the slit. This tube releases pressure and acts as a vent, letting air in and fluid out so bacteria can't flourish.
