The Indian Administrative Service (IAS) is one of the most important civil services for the Republic of India. The entrance test for IAS also covers other services such as the Indian Police Service, Indian Foreign Service etc. These exams are highly competitive with a very small percentage of the applicants passing the test. This article will help in preparing an aspirant from middle school for IAS.
1Know if you are right for it. Civil service requires a lot of determination, hard work and aptitude, make sure that the aspirant has all the above. Middle school might be too early for an aspirant to choose a suitable career but preparing for civil services can help them in discovering their aptitude.
2Pay attention in school. The syllabus for civil service exams are revised periodically, but the basic structure remains the same with, English, history, civics (polity), geography, science and maths, which are taught from middle school itself. Learning is easier at school since it will be at a pace and level that is suitable for the aspirant.
3Read newspapers. This is the most important step. IAS exams rely heavily on current affairs and government policies and requires the aspirants to be knowledgeable and have opinions regarding them.
4Watch news, current affairs, science and history programs on TV or internet. Most of the topics for IAS exams are too dry for a child, documentaries and videos can make them interesting.
5Read books in English and regional languages. Reading fiction and non-fiction books in English and regional languages will increase their knowledge and language skills.
6Discuss with parents, teachers or friends. Sometimes books and newspapers might be difficult to understand, discussing with your elders will help in understanding issues better and in forming and expressing opinions.
7Get trained in life. If you are a parent, train the child to face real life situations and in public speaking. These will help them in tiding through aptitude questions, interview and eventually in the job.
8Know more about your country. Books, TV and the internet will have its limitations compared to actually seeing and experiencing your country. Use school trips and trips with your parents to observe and understand the diverse cultures, ethnic and social background of the citizens of the nation.
9Respect your nation. IAS is a position of power from where you could do a great deal of good things for your fellow citizens. Learn to respect and love your country and its rich and varied heritage. Learn more about the people who have worked for its freedom and well-being and learn the spirit of unity and integrity from a young age so that it lasts for a lifetime in the service of the nation.Advertisement
- Be a role model for your child. Civil service is in fact service to your nation, if you are the parent of an aspirant make sure that you are a model citizen who treats all your countrymen equally and with respect and follows the law of the land.
- Read and solve puzzle books, these help in the preliminary phase of the exams.
- Rope in an sibling or a friend with whom you share your aptitude, it will make learning easier and fun.
- Don't forget to play games, learn arts or sports, they are not looking for plain bookworms, but all-rounded individuals.
- Not all aspirants pass the civil service exams, don't worry if you are unable to clear them even after your best effort, there are state civil service exams and by the time you are eligible to write the exam you will know what you are good at, choose a career that suits you.
