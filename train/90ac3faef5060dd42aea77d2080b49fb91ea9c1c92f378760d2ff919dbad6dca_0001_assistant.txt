It was cold. It was so, so cold. 

The wind nipped at her skin, her hands shook and her teeth clattered. A blizzard of snow beat against her as she hunched over her horse. The great, black beast snorted heavily, steam rising from its nostrils. Still, she pressed on, her sheep-skin cloaks doing little against the elemental barrage.

She knew the villagers had been right when they warned her against climbing to the castle when they urged her to stay with them until the blizzard had passed. But Nimue had foolishly pressed on, self-assured that her bestial horse could handle the trek far better than any of the other horses the villagers normally used. And now not only was she paying the price, but so was her horse.

She carded her frozen, gloved fingers through his long dark mane and buried her face into his neck. He nickered softly but continued on.

Nimue continued without a clue about where they were or how far away the castle was. She had no idea if she was even going in the right direction.

She had seen the tops of the castle turrets and towers before the storm had started. However, the snow blurred her vision before she could lay her eyes on the gatehouse.

“Just a little further, Arion,” she murmured, her lips frozen, her head pounding with the cold.
