In the hollowed-out core of the old mill, where the gears groaned like forgotten prayers, Elara knelt beside the rusted well. The water beneath her fingertips was cold, too cold, as if memory itself had turned to ice. She reached in—just once—and pulled out nothing but the weight of a question she’d carried for decades: *Why did they stop calling me Elara after the accident?*

The mirror above the well, cracked and stained, reflected not her face, but the faces of all the Elaras who had drowned here before her—each one younger, each one less broken. The last one had laughed, whispering, *"Find the answer, little ghost."* But the answer was always just out of reach, a whisper in the wind, a name she couldn’t remember, the sound of laughter that had been her last.

She pressed her palm to the glass. The water swirled. Then, nothing. Just the silence of the mill, and the faint, guttural hum of something beneath the stones—something that had been waiting for her to ask the right question. The question she’d never dared to voice aloud: *What did they bury beneath the floorboards?*
