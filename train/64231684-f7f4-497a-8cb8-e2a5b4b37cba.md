The weight of expectations pressed like a stone in his pocket, Daniel stood at the edge of the cliff, fingers tightening around the railing. His promise to his sister, a promise carved into the dust of a forgotten summer, had always felt like a debt he owed himself as much as her. "I’ll be back by sundown," he had said, her voice trembling with fear. The words hung between them, a fragile thread stretched taut across the abyss.

He had lied. Or maybe he had simply misunderstood the cost. The promise wasn’t just about returning; it was about something deeper—about the weight of his own hands, the way the wind had howled through the pines like a warning. Now, the sky was the color of bruised flesh, and the only thing he could do was look away. The cliff crumbled beneath him, and the promise he had given dissolved into dust.
