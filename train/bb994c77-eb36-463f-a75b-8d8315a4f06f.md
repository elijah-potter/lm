**The Whispering Walls**

The neon glow of the alley pulsed like a heartbeat, casting long shadows across the pavement where cobblestones hummed with forgotten secrets. Rain dripped in slow, deliberate drops, each one a reminder of how fleeting life could be. Inside the dimly lit café, the scent of stale coffee and cigarette smoke clung to the air, thick and suffocating, like the weight of unspoken things.

Across the room, the bartender wiped down glasses with deliberate strokes, his fingers steady despite the way his knuckles had gone pale. He didn’t look up when the door swung open again, just another face in the crowd of tired, half-asleep souls who came here for more than just a drink. They came for the stories, the quiet comfort of a place where no one judged.

Outside, the city breathed. Cars honked, sirens wailed, and the occasional laugh escaped a passing street musician, sharp and bright against the gloom. But down here, in the corner booth where the light didn’t quite reach, the world had slowed. Time stretched thin, a thread between the past and the future, and the only thing that mattered was the next sip of whiskey and the next lie told in the dark.

---

Then the bell above the door chimed again, louder this time, as if the city itself had shifted gears. The bartender finally glanced up, his eyes sharp with something unreadable. The customer who’d just walked in was different—older, more deliberate, his coat too heavy for the damp air, his hands gripping the handle of a bag that didn’t belong to him.

*"You’re not from around here,"* the bartender said, voice low, almost a warning.

The man didn’t answer. Instead, he slid into the booth across from him, his posture rigid, like a man who’d already made his choice. The air between them crackled, charged with something heavier than tension—something like the first spark before the fire.

*"You’re looking for answers,"* the bartender said, as if reading his mind. *"Or you’re just here to disappear."*

The man’s jaw tightened. *"Both."*

Outside, the rain picked up. The city’s pulse quickened, its rhythm no longer steady but urgent, a heartbeat that couldn’t be ignored. The café’s lights flickered, casting eerie shadows on the walls where the whispers of the past now slithered forward, thicker and louder, as if the dead had finally decided to speak.
