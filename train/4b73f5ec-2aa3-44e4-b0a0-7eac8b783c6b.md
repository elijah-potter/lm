The last light of the setting sun spilled gold across the cracked pavement, turning the shadows into whispered secrets. Behind the iron gate of the abandoned clinic, a child’s laughter—echoed and faint—danced with the wind, a ghost of joy in the hollowed-out silence.

She found the book in the attic: a leather-bound tome with yellowed pages, its spine cracked like old bones. Inside, a map drawn in ink that didn’t fade. The last entry read: *"They’re coming. The river’s their door."* The ink had been added by her own hand, in the same trembling script.

She traced the path with her fingers—past the bridge where the bridgekeeper’s son had drowned, past the church where the bell still tolled at midnight, and toward the docks where the steamers never came. The map ended at the warehouse, its doors sealed with rusted iron. The key was in her pocket, stolen from the guard who’d laughed at her when she asked for work.

The warehouse was empty, save for a single figure hunched over a desk, fingers steepled. She stepped inside, the air thick with the scent of damp wood and something metallic. The man looked up, his face gaunt, his eyes hollow. "You’re late," he said, voice like gravel. "The others won’t wait."

She reached for the key. The door creaked open, revealing a cavernous space filled with crates stacked like broken dreams. At the center, a figure stood—tall, armored, its face a mask of cold precision. The last thing she saw before the world blurred was the river, its surface rippling with something dark and hungry, waiting.
