Boiled peanuts, already a staple in some southern states, are a delicious and nutritious snack food. Served warm, boiled peanuts have the soft consistency of cooked pinto beans. The peanuts are boiled in the shell in a broth made of salt and spices. There are cajun spiced peanuts, plain salty peanuts, and Old Bay flavored peanuts. Boiled peanuts are already so successful in states like Georgia that they can be found in most convenience stores, kept hot in self-serve crockpot-style warmers.

Boiled peanuts retain all the natural flavors and nutrients of other snack peanuts but without the added calories of oil. They can be sold by the cupful, so consumers can fill their cups as much or as little as desired. This saves the retailer considerable costs due to the variation in customer preference. Boiled peanuts arrive at the retailer in safety-sealed cans, requiring very little prep by store staff. These unique snacks attract repeat customers in the way snacks like potato chips, beef jerky, and dry salted nuts have been doing for decades. They have a long shelf life, meaning your store won't lose money in expired inventory.
