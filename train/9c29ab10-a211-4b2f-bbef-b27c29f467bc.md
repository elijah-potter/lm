**The Fireplace**

Ethan adjusted his gloves as the rain hammered the old wooden door, splattering against the wet cobblestones. Inside, the air smelled of damp wool and old leather, thick with the scent of a fire that had burned low since morning.

His host, an elderly woman with silver-streaked hair tied in a bun, sat at the hearth with a half-empty tankard of ale. "You’ve got a tale to tell, haven’t you?" she said, nodding at the worn leather-bound book in his hands. "A story that won’t stay buried."

Ethan exhaled through his nose, the weight of the tale pressing against his chest. He opened the book, the pages whispering as he flipped through them carefully.

**The First Story**

Once, in a kingdom where the rivers ran black with poison and the sky hung low with storms that never lifted, there lived a boy named Kael. He was small, with hands too quick for his age, and a voice that could make even the hounds of the king tremble.

The king, a man named Malrik, had eyes like polished obsidian and a tongue that could cut as easily as his sword. He had promised Kael’s father a trade for his son—a trade that would make the village rich. But Malrik had no intention of keeping his word.

One night, while the family slept, Malrik’s men came. They dragged Kael away, bound his wrists, and tossed him into the dungeon below the castle. The walls were damp, the air thick with the stench of rot and fear. Kael’s father wept, his hands trembling as he watched the guards carry their son away.

Kael sat on the cold stone floor, his breath coming fast. He had never been to a dungeon before, but he knew what it was like to be small and afraid. He had seen the way the rats scuttled at his feet, the way the flickering torchlight made the shadows stretch long and hungry.

Then, one evening, the door creaked open.

A woman stood there, her face hidden behind a long cloak. She was young, with eyes like the sea at dawn, and her voice was soft but firm. "You’re not alone here," she said. "The others remember you. They remember *what you could have been*."

Kael’s heart raced. He had never heard of such things in the stories his mother told. But the woman’s words made him shiver.

"Who are you?" he asked.

"Only a voice in the dark," she replied. "But you have a choice, child. You can stay here, or you can run. And if you run, you might find a way out."

Kael hesitated. The dungeon was a cage, but the woman’s words were a promise. He stood, his legs unsteady, and stepped toward the door.

Outside, the world was different. The air smelled of rain and earth, and the street was alive with the sounds of people—laughter, children shouting, the clatter of hooves. He ran until his feet ached, until his lungs burned, until he could see the end of the city skyline.

There, at the edge of the world, he found the girl who had been waiting for him.

---

**The Second Story**

She was waiting at the edge of the forest, her hair tangled with leaves, her hands clutching a broken bow. "You came," she said, her voice breaking.

Kael didn’t answer. He had run from the dungeon, from the king, from everything that had tried to break him. But now, standing before her, he felt something shift inside him.

"Where do you think you’re going?" she asked, her eyes dark with unshed tears. "You can’t just run forever."

Kael looked at the forest, at the way the trees stretched like fingers toward the sky. He had been a boy once, small and afraid. But he had learned to run. And now, he had to choose.

"Tell me about the stories," he said. "The ones you keep."

She nodded. "There are more than one."

And so began the telling of the stories—the ones that were never meant to be told, the ones that lived in the cracks between the world and the ones who remembered.
