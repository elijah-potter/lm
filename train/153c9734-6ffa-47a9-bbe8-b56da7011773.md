The abandoned subway station beneath the old downtown mall, now repurposed as a communal hub by the city’s homeless collective. The station’s rusted ticket booth stands as a silent sentinel, its doors permanently ajar. The air hums with the low murmur of conversations in multiple languages, though no specific meeting ever follows the gatherings—just the occasional shared meal, whispered gossip, or the quiet hum of someone practicing an instrument in the corner. The walls are covered in graffiti that shifts daily, with new words and symbols added by those who pass through. Some say it’s a safe haven; others whisper about the eerie silence that lingers when no one is present. The only rule is that you’re welcome to stay as long as you like, though no one ever asks why.
