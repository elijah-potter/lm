## A Counseling Session

**Setting:** A comfortable, well-lit therapy office. Soft music plays quietly in the background.

**Characters:**

*   **Dr. Eleanor Vance:** A seasoned therapist, known for her calm demeanor and insightful guidance.
*   **Liam Walker:** A young professional experiencing significant stress and difficulty sleeping.

**(The session begins with Liam seated across from Dr. Vance. He appears visibly tense.)**

**Liam:** Thank you for seeing me, Dr. Vance. I… I’m really struggling.

**Dr. Vance:** It takes courage to reach out, Liam. I appreciate you being here. Could you tell me a little about what's been troubling you?

**Liam:** It's… everything, really. Work has been incredibly demanding. Long hours, high pressure. And I just can't seem to switch off. I’m constantly thinking about deadlines, emails, projects. It’s exhausting.

**Dr. Vance:** That sounds incredibly draining. It’s understandable that you’re feeling overwhelmed. You mentioned deadlines and emails – could you elaborate on how these specific demands contribute to your sense of being overwhelmed?

**Liam:** It’s the constant expectation of immediate responses. I feel like I’m always ‘on,’ always available. Even when I’m supposed to be relaxing, I’m worrying about what I’m missing. And then I lie awake at night replaying conversations, second-guessing decisions.

**Dr. Vance:** So, the pressure to be constantly responsive is impacting your ability to truly disconnect and recharge. It sounds like you're experiencing a significant internal conflict between your desire for rest and the perceived obligation to remain available.  That’s a very common experience in today's work environment.  Tell me, how long have you felt this way?

**Liam:** Probably the last six months, but it's definitely escalated recently. The workload has increased, and I feel like I've been trying to just power through it, but I'm starting to feel… brittle, I guess.

**Dr. Vance:** "Brittle" is a powerful word, Liam. It conveys a sense of fragility and a potential for things to easily break. It sounds like you’re pushing yourself very hard. It's admirable that you're trying to persevere, but it's also crucial to acknowledge the toll it’s taking. What do you typically do to relax or de-stress?

**Liam:** I used to enjoy hiking and reading, but I haven’t had the time or the energy lately. When I do try to do something, I end up thinking about work anyway.

**Dr. Vance:** It sounds like your coping mechanisms, which previously brought you joy, have become entangled with your work-related anxieties. That's a natural consequence of prolonged stress.  We can explore ways to disentangle those activities and rediscover their restorative qualities.  Let's consider some strategies. One approach might be to implement specific boundaries around your work hours, even if it feels challenging initially. What are your thoughts about that?

**Liam:** It’s… difficult. My boss expects immediate responses, and I worry about appearing uncommitted.

**Dr. Vance:** I understand that concern. It’s important to find a balance that respects your professional responsibilities while also prioritizing your well-being. We can explore ways to communicate those boundaries effectively and proactively, perhaps by setting expectations regarding response times or delegating tasks when possible.  It’s about finding a solution that feels both sustainable and respectful of your workplace dynamics.  Let's brainstorm some specific phrases or approaches you could use.  Before we move on, I want to acknowledge your honesty and willingness to discuss these challenges. It’s a significant step.



**(The session continues with Dr. Vance and Liam collaboratively exploring strategies for boundary setting and stress management.)**
