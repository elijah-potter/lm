**Mayor’s Office Press Briefing – The New Haven Gazette – June 12, 2024**

In a move aimed at revitalizing its struggling downtown, Mayor Elias Carter announced tonight that The Riverfront Plaza will reopen as an open-air public space on July 1st, following a $1.8M renovation. The project, funded by a mix of city bonds and a grant from the National Arts Council, includes a new outdoor amphitheater, solar-powered lighting, and a pedestrian-friendly plaza with adaptive seating for visitors. "This is more than just a plaza," Carter said during the press conference, "it’s a symbol of hope for New Haven’s future." The plaza will host free summer concerts, farmers' markets, and community art installations, with plans for a seasonal pop-up café by the end of summer.

Earlier today, the New Haven Transit Authority (NHTA) confirmed a 15% reduction in fares across all routes, effective immediately, to address ongoing economic challenges. The fare hike, which had been expected, was partially offset by a new "Essential Pass" program allowing residents of low-income neighborhoods to ride unlimited for $30/month. NHTA officials noted that the changes come after a 20% decline in ridership over the past year, largely attributed to reduced evening commuter traffic post-pandemic.

Meanwhile, the city’s latest initiative to combat homelessness saw its first major success: the opening of a temporary shelter in the abandoned Mill Creek Warehouse. Operated in partnership with the Red Cross and local nonprofits, the facility provides 50 beds with access to meals, job training, and mental health services. "This is just the beginning," said City Housing Commissioner Dr. Amara Patel, "we’re expanding to permanent housing by next winter." The warehouse’s renovation was completed in under 3 months, thanks to a fast-tracked permit process.

In education, New Haven Public Schools announced a pilot program this week to introduce "digital literacy workshops" in all elementary classrooms. The program, funded by a $750K grant from the EdTech Alliance, will teach students basic coding, cybersecurity basics, and critical thinking skills in a hands-on environment. "We can’t wait for the kids to graduate," said Superintendent Dr. Javier Morales, "these skills will be invaluable in any career." The workshops will begin next month with full implementation by the end of the year.

Finally, the city’s cultural scene continues to thrive, with the New Haven Symphony Orchestra marking its 100th anniversary with a free concert at the newly renovated Carnegie Hall on Saturday, June 15th. The program will feature a world premiere by local composer Daniel Chen, who was awarded the city’s Young Artist Award earlier this year. Tickets are free, but reserved seating is available via the city’s official website.
