The old library’s quiet hum filled the room, the scent of aged paper and polished wood lingering as Clara traced the edges of the worn bookmark. She’d tucked it into the spine of *The Promise* when she was sixteen, after her mother’s last words: *"Find this book and read it before dawn."* The ink on the page still glowed faintly under her finger, though the book itself was brittle with time—its binding cracked, the margins yellowed. Outside, the city’s distant sirens drowned the whispers of the stacks. She had failed to finish it then, but now, the words coiled inside her like a second skin, each one a fragment of the life she’d let slip through her fingers.

Her breath hitched as the last line of the final chapter echoed in her mind: *"Some promises are not broken by time, but by the act of remembering."* The bookmark slipped from her grip. The pages turned, not of her choosing, but as if pulled by an unseen hand. The words blurred, but she read them anyway—*The Promise was never about what she gave, but what she kept.*
