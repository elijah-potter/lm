The late-night train station was eerily quiet except for the distant hum of the clock tower. His breath fogged slightly in the cold air as he adjusted his coat, fingers tapping impatiently against his thighs. The overhead sign flickered: **DEPARTURES – 02:47**. He had arrived at 02:43, but the line was too long, the gates too far.

A voice from behind him—too loud, too familiar—cut through the tension. *"You’re late."* He turned, relief flooding through him. His best friend, Marcus, stood there, arms crossed, smirking. "Took you long enough," Marcus said, tossing him a coffee cup. The steam curled upward, carrying the scent of burnt sugar and exhaustion.

Outside, the city lights blurred into a smudge of neon and lamplight. He exhaled sharply, gripping the coffee. "I was running late," he admitted. "Had to pick up something."

Marcus grinned. "Yeah? What’s that?" He leaned in, voice dropping. "The new apartment? The job offer? The… the *you*?"

A laugh bubbled up, sharp and relieved. He sipped the coffee, the heat seeping into his hands. "You’re a menace."

Marcus laughed, clapping him on the back. "Aren’t we all?"

The train rumbled in the distance, its doors hissing open. He glanced at the clock again—**02:48**. But the world outside had already changed. The relief wasn’t in the moment, but in the knowledge that, for once, he had made it.
