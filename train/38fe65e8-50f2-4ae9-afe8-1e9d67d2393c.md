The sun cast long shadows across the forest floor, where once the path of the hunter was a straight line to his target. Now, from the tree’s perspective, the hunter’s steps were a dance—each footfall a rhythm that hummed through the roots, a song only the earth could hear. The forest had watched him come and go, had seen his fear and his greed, and in that moment, the hunter realized: the forest did not hunt him—he hunted himself. The leaves rustled, the wind carried whispers of old truths, and suddenly, the world was not his to conquer but to listen to. Perspective had shifted not by bending, but by breaking the chain of his own story.
