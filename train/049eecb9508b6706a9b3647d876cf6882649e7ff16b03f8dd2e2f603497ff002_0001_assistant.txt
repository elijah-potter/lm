Seizures are caused by surges of abnormal electrical activity in the brain. The most common cause of seizures is epilepsy, a brain disorder that causes recurring, unprovoked seizures. However, not everyone who experiences a seizure has epilepsy. Non-epileptic seizures can be triggered by several factors including the following: 

1. High fever: When a high fever triggers a seizure, it is called a febrile seizure.
2. Brain infection: Brain infections such as meningitis or encephalitis can cause non-epileptic seizures.
3. Severe general illness
4. Lack of sleep
5. Low sodium levels in the blood: Low sodium levels can be caused by taking medicine that makes the patient urinate.
6. Legal or illegal medication or drugs: Certain medications including medicines to treat pain or depression or illegal substances such as amphetamines or cocaine can trigger non-epileptic seizures.
7. Brain injury: Active brain injuries, including head traumas, can trigger non-epileptic seizures and cause bleeding in the brain or a stroke.
8. Alcohol misuse: Alcohol misuse, including times of extreme intoxication or withdrawal, can trigger non-epileptic seizures.
