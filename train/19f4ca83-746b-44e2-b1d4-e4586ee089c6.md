The first mistake was a misplaced key in the attic’s hidden drawer—one that unlocked an old safe. Inside, a single, yellowed letter from a long-dead grandmother read: *"The clock doesn’t run backward, but the river does."* The protagonist, a librarian with a habit of collecting odd books, took it as a warning.

The mistake that followed was leaving the letter behind at the bookstore the next morning, where it was found by a stranger who recognized the handwriting as belonging to a woman who’d vanished decades earlier. That stranger was a clockmaker who’d heard whispers of a river that flowed backward in a village where time itself seemed to unravel.

The real mistake came when the protagonist, curious, followed the stranger to the village of Veldros, where they discovered the river wasn’t just flowing backward—it was rewinding. The villagers lived in loops of their lives, trapped in repeating cycles of choices, but one key error had been made: the protagonist had accidentally stepped into the wrong loop, becoming the first person to see the river’s true nature.

Now, the protagonist must decide: help the villagers escape their cycles before the loop closes again, or become part of it themselves, rewriting history—once more.
