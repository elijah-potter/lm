The faction split over the contested land deal, with one faction advocating for the immediate acquisition to secure their strategic interests, while the other argued for negotiations, fearing the deal would alienate neighboring clans and lead to future conflict.

Internal debates highlighted tensions: the elder of the negotiating faction called it a "betrayal of trust," while the younger advocate dismissed it as "pragmatic survival."

The decision was delayed until a council meeting, where a third party suggested mediation—only to reveal their own hidden agenda: the acquisition would allow them to expand their own territory.

The group fractured overnight. Some chose to support the deal, seeing it as a temporary compromise. Others walked away, forming a rival faction that still opposes the land acquisition today.

The split has never fully healed.
