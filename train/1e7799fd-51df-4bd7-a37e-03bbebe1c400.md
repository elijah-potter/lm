AITA for refusing to let my sister use my inherited vacation home?

Okay, this is a bit complicated, so bear with me. It involves my sister, Eleanor, my late grandmother, and a house that’s become a surprisingly large source of stress.

My grandmother, Vivian, passed away last November (2023) after a long battle with Alzheimer's. She left a will, and I, along with Eleanor, inherited her beach house in Outer Banks, North Carolina. The house itself is lovely – a three-bedroom cottage right on the sound, with a small dock. It’s been in our family for generations, and my grandmother cherished it. She specifically stipulated in the will that we split the responsibility of the property equally.

For years, I’m embarrassed to admit, I’m not a particularly attentive granddaughter. I was busy with school and then my career. Eleanor, on the other hand, visited my grandmother frequently, especially during the last few years as her health declined. She genuinely seemed to enjoy caring for her. I always assumed that closeness would translate to a shared appreciation for the house and a willingness to share its upkeep. I was wrong.

In early January 2024, shortly after the will was read, Eleanor approached me with a request. She wanted to use the house for a week in June, bringing her husband, Mark, and their two children. I said sure, no problem. I’m generally a very accommodating person. I was planning to use the house myself in July, and thought a June visit would be nice. I even offered to help her find a local property manager to handle things like cleaning and maintenance while they were there. She seemed pleased.

Then, last week (March 2024), Eleanor called, irate. Apparently, she's booked a large group of friends – ten people total – to join them for two weeks in August. She insisted that since she spent so much time with my grandmother, she deserves a longer stay than I do. She said I'm being selfish and that she’s shouldering the emotional burden of the house’s history. She pointed out that I haven't been as involved and that she feels entitled to more time with the property.

I explained that a group of ten is far beyond what’s reasonable for a house that comfortably accommodates six. The noise, the potential for damage, the sheer logistical nightmare… it’s not feasible. I also reminded her that the will specifies equal responsibility, and a ten-person group significantly increases that burden. I suggested a compromise: she could have a week in August, but the group size needed to be limited.

She refused. She accused me of trying to control her and of not appreciating the sacrifices she made caring for our grandmother. Mark chimed in, saying I was being difficult and ruining their vacation plans. He even suggested that maybe she should just sell her share of the house, implying I could buy her out. Honestly, the whole conversation was pretty unpleasant.

I feel caught between honoring my grandmother’s memory and dealing with my sister’s increasingly demanding and entitled behavior. I’d genuinely like to find a solution that works for both of us, but her insistence on a large group feels disrespectful to the house and unfair to me. I’m worried this will damage our relationship, but I'm also unwilling to be bullied into a situation that I know will be a disaster. I'm feeling a lot of frustration, and honestly, a little bit of resentment. I think ESH here – both of us are acting poorly, but her insistence on a massive group feels particularly unreasonable. I’m also leaning toward YTA for Eleanor, but I want to be fair.
