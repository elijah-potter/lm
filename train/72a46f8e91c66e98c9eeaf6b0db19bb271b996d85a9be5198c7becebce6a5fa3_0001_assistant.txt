It doesn't rain in this giant floating metal coffin. What humidity we have is controlled somewhere else by an all-seeing mainframe. It adjusts to what the atmosphere needs. Right now, apparently, the atmosphere needs fog. It's low and covers the ground as I stride through. The wake trails my body like a long cape. Ahead, it diffuses the red and blue lights as I approach. 

The local police are already on the scene. The alley, usually dark, is lit up by hover droids. It's too early in the morning for this kind of light. I haven't had my coffee yet. But murder is murder, no matter what celestial object you find yourself on. That's one thing I've found to always be true. I listen to the medical examiner and first responder list details. I order all the thoughts in my mind, but I'm not giving them the attention they deserve. I can see for one of them, this is their first. They'll get used to it, unfortunately. I have. My mind races with speculation on where to start. 

I turn around and walk back through the fog to do my job. There were only a few hundred people in this tub. Now, there is one less.
