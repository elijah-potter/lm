WHY no Y? Modern humans diverged from Neanderthals some 600,000 years ago – and it seems the sex-determining Y chromosome passed from fathers to sons might be what kept the two species apart.
Fernando Mendez at Stanford University and his colleagues compared the Neanderthal Y chromosome with that of chimps and different modern humans.
They also found mutations in four genes that might have prevented the passage of Y chromosome down the paternal line to hybrid children.
For example, a mutation in KDM5D, a gene that plays a role in cancer suppression, has previously been linked to increased risk of miscarriages as it can prompt an immune response in pregnant mothers (The American Journal of Human Genetics, doi.org/bd43).
“Some of these mutations could have played a role in the loss of Neanderthal Y chromosomes in human populations,” says Mendez.
