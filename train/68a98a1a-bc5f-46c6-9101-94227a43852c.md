A crumbling stone archway stands at the edge of an overgrown courtyard, its mortar cracked and moss-stained. The tiles around its base are faded, half-buried by wildflowers. This place is a relic of a past life, yet the air hums with quiet permanence—like the rustle of leaves whispering secrets of time.

Inside, the walls bear the marks of generations: faded graffiti, child’s chalk drawings, and the faint scent of damp earth and old wood. A single, weathered wooden door hangs ajar, its hinges groaning in the wind. Inside, shelves line the walls, filled with jars of preserved herbs, a leather-bound ledger, and a half-finished sketchbook. The floor is a tapestry of mismatched tiles, some cracked, others embedded with rusted nails from an old workshop.

A narrow staircase spirals up, its steps worn smooth by countless feet. At the top, a small window frames a view of the distant hills, their edges blurred by mist. Here, the weight of history feels like a soft, familiar presence—something that has been here before and will linger, unchanged, long after the rest of the world forgets.

The place feels like a breath held too long, suspended between memory and decay, but the walls remember.
