**The quiet library after sunset**—bookshelves lined with dust motes reflecting golden light through stained glass, but the shelves are rearranged: every title now sits atop its first chapter, the spine facing inward. The air smells faintly of ozone and old paper, but the scent of fresh coffee lingers in the corner, where a roaster has been running nonstop for weeks. The librarian, a woman in a hoodie with a name tag that reads *"Alex"* (not *"Ms. Rivera"*), checks out patrons with a smile but her eyes dart to the ceiling, where a holographic map of the universe flickers above the main desk—visible only to those with the right neural signature. The stacks whisper when you pass them, and the scent of ink is thicker than usual. This is not a library you’ve read about, but the one you *almost* remembered.
