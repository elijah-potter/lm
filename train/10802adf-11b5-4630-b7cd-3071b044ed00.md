**New Haven, Connecticut** – The city council has approved a revised urban renewal plan to transform the abandoned **Old Mill District** into a mixed-use hub by 2025. Under the proposal, historic mill buildings will be repurposed into loft-style apartments, while commercial spaces will house a new **sustainable food hub** and **green market** to support local farmers. The plan includes 30% of new developments dedicated to affordable housing, with incentives for developers who incorporate solar panel integration and rainwater recycling systems. Public workshops held last month received overwhelmingly positive feedback, though some community groups raised concerns over traffic congestion and displacement risks. Construction is expected to begin within six months, with phased openings to minimize disruption. The city’s economic development office notes this initiative aims to revitalize a once-thriving industrial area while addressing housing shortages and food insecurity. The new district will also feature a pedestrian-friendly plaza with art installations and community gardens, designed to foster social engagement. Councilor Maria Reyes emphasized, *“This isn’t just about bricks and mortar—it’s about rebuilding New Haven’s identity.”*
