**First memory of routine:**

The first time I noticed the pattern was when I was **five**, stacking wooden blocks. The sequence was unchanging: **1, 2, 3**—red, blue, green—each time. No variation. The repetition felt like a rhythm, but it wasn’t *calm*, just *inevitable*. My mother watched from the doorway, arms crossed, as if the act itself was a test of will. I didn’t question it then, but I did later, when the same steps became the skeleton of every afternoon—**dinner at 6:00 PM, story at 7:30, bed by 9:00 PM**, no deviations. The system was rigid, but it was also *safe*. The predictability, though, was the first crack in the illusion of control.
