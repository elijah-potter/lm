The weight of the decision pressed against her ribs like a stone. Every fiber of her being had been trained to this moment—her father’s voice in her ear, *"Trust your instincts, Lina."* The clock on the wall ticked, a slow, deliberate heartbeat, while the world outside pulsed with possibility and peril. She exhaled sharply, the breath fogging the glass of the old teacup in her hands.

The path before her was a fork, but not in the way she expected. One branch led to the fortress of the guildmasters, their banners flapping in the wind like promises of gold and power. The other—no, not a branch, but a door, slightly ajar, its hinges groaning with age. The scent of damp earth and iron seeped through the cracks, a whisper of something older than the city’s foundations.

Her fingers trembled as she reached for the doorknob, but her mind hesitated. The guild’s oath was etched into her skin—*"Survive, then serve."* Yet the name scrawled on the door’s frame, *"Veyra’s Rest,"* burned brighter than any promise of honor. The last time she’d come here, Veyra had laughed, her eyes crinkling with the memory of a bet lost over dice and stolen ale. *"You think you’re the first to walk this path?"* she’d murmured, before vanishing into the shadows like smoke.

A voice, distant and familiar, called from the alleyway: *"Lina?"* She turned just in time to see a shadow move—*Riven*, her brother, his face lined with exhaustion. His hand was wrapped in bandages, his voice rough with grief. *"You shouldn’t have come,"* he said, but his eyes burned with something raw and desperate. *"The guild’s got the cartels on their heels. They’re not playing fair."*

She stepped forward, the weight of the choice pressing harder. The door groaned as she pushed it open, revealing a hall that smelled of damp stone and forgotten secrets. A single lantern flickered, casting long shadows that danced like restless spirits. Behind her, the sound of footsteps echoed—her father’s, marching toward the fortress. Time was not on her side. But fate, she knew, was never fair.

The choice was written in the blood of the stars and the dust beneath her boots. She would choose the door.
