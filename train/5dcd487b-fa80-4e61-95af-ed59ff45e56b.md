Returning to the park after months away, the scent of damp earth and blooming jasmine lingers—wildflowers swaying in the breeze where once we sat. The café’s warm glow flickers through the window, the hum of conversation blending with the distant laughter of children playing tag. The old bookstore’s shelves, once crowded, now hold fewer volumes, but the scent of aged paper still clings to the air. A familiar café table sits unoccupied, its worn surface polished by countless hands. The path to the lake is damp from recent rain, reflecting the muted light of an overcast sky. The sound of a fountain trickles softly, its rhythm steady as the city’s heartbeat. The memory of summer evenings here—of shared laughter, stolen kisses, and the way the lake’s surface shimmered like liquid silver—still lingers. The absence made it feel like a ghost town, but now, it breathes again.
