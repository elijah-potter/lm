## Proposal: The Willow Creek Pocket Park – A Climate-Resilient Community Oasis

**Executive Summary:**

The Willow Creek neighborhood, a historically underserved area within the city, suffers from a dearth of accessible green space and disproportionately experiences the effects of urban heat island and stormwater runoff. This proposal outlines a transformative project to revitalize a currently neglected half-acre lot at the corner of Elm Street and Willow Avenue – designated “Lot 42” – into a climate-resilient community park, tentatively named the Willow Creek Pocket Park. This project, spearheaded by the Willow Creek Neighborhood Association (WCNA) in partnership with the City Parks Department and local landscape architects, will create a vital community asset that mitigates environmental hazards, promotes social interaction, and enhances neighborhood pride. The total project cost is $187,500, and we are requesting $125,000 in municipal funding, with the remaining $62,500 secured through community fundraising and in-kind donations. This investment will yield significant returns in terms of improved public health, reduced infrastructure strain, and a strengthened sense of community.

**Project Plan & Timeline:**

The Willow Creek Pocket Park project will be executed in four distinct phases, each with clearly defined milestones, responsible partners, and deliverables.

**Phase 1: Planning & Design (Months 1-3)**

*   **Responsible Partners:** WCNA, Greenleaf Landscape Architecture, City Parks Department Planning Division.
*   **Milestones:**
    *   Community Needs Assessment (Month 1): WCNA will conduct surveys and host two public forums to gather input on park design and desired amenities. Deliverable: Comprehensive report summarizing community preferences.
    *   Site Assessment & Soil Testing (Month 1): Greenleaf Landscape Architecture will conduct a detailed site assessment, including soil testing to determine suitability for native plantings. Deliverable: Site assessment report.
    *   Preliminary Design Development (Month 1-2): Greenleaf Landscape Architecture, in consultation with the City Parks Department, will develop three preliminary design concepts based on community input and site assessment. Deliverable: Three design concepts presented at a public meeting.
    *   Final Design Approval (Month 3): Following community feedback and review by the City Parks Department, a final park design will be approved. Deliverable: Approved final design documents, including planting plans and construction specifications.

**Phase 2: Site Preparation & Infrastructure (Months 3-6)**

*   **Responsible Partners:** City Parks Department Maintenance Crew, Willow Creek Neighborhood Association Volunteer Team, Earthworks Excavation (contractor).
*   **Milestones:**
    *   Site Clearing & Demolition (Month 3-4): Earthworks Excavation will remove existing debris and prepare the site for construction. Deliverable: Site cleared and graded.
    *   Stormwater Management System Installation (Month 4-5): City Parks Department Maintenance Crew will install a subsurface stormwater infiltration system designed by Greenleaf Landscape Architecture. Deliverable: Functional stormwater management system.
    *   Utility Relocation (Month 4-5): Coordination with the City Utilities Department to relocate any necessary underground utilities. Deliverable: Utility relocation completed and documented.

**Phase 3: Planting & Amenity Installation (Months 6-9)**

*   **Responsible Partners:** Greenleaf Landscape Architecture, WCNA Volunteer Team, Riverbend Nursery (contractor).
*   **Milestones:**
    *   Soil Amendment & Planting (Month 6-7): Riverbend Nursery, guided by Greenleaf’s planting plan, will install native trees, shrubs, and pollinator-friendly perennials. Deliverable: Park landscape planted.
    *   Pathway Construction & Benches Installation (Month 7-8): WCNA volunteers, supervised by City Parks Department personnel, will construct accessible pathways and install park benches. Deliverable: Accessible pathways and benches installed.
    *   Play Area Installation (Month 8-9): Installation of a small, nature-inspired play area designed for younger children, using natural materials. Deliverable: Completed play area.

**Phase 4: Grand Opening & Ongoing Maintenance (Month 9 onwards)**

*   **Responsible Partners:** WCNA, City Parks Department, Community Volunteers.
*   **Milestones:**
    *   Grand Opening Celebration (Month 9): A community event to officially open the Willow Creek Pocket Park. Deliverable: Successful community celebration.
    *   Ongoing Maintenance (Ongoing): WCNA, in partnership with the City Parks Department, will implement a volunteer-led maintenance program to ensure the park’s long-term health and upkeep. Deliverable: Consistent park maintenance and upkeep.

**Budget:**

The total project cost is $187,500. The requested municipal funding of $125,000 will be supplemented by $62,500 in secured community fundraising and in-kind donations. A detailed breakdown is as follows:

*   **Design & Planning:** $25,000 (Includes landscape architect fees, community engagement consultant, soil testing)
*   **Site Preparation & Demolition:** $37,500 (Includes contractor fees, debris removal)
*   **Stormwater Management System:** $40,000 (Includes materials and City Parks Department labor)
*   **Planting & Landscaping:** $45,000 (Includes native plants, mulch, and Riverbend Nursery labor)
*   **Pathway & Bench Installation:** $15,000 (Includes materials and volunteer supervision)
*   **Play Area Installation:** $10,000 (Includes natural play elements and installation)
*   **Contingency Fund:** $15,000 (Covers unforeseen costs and potential price fluctuations. Represents 8% of the total project cost.)

The secured community fundraising comprises $35,000 in pledges and $27,500 in in-kind donations of materials and volunteer hours. We have established a dedicated bank account for project funds and will maintain meticulous records of all expenditures.

**Community Engagement & Equity Impacts:**

Community engagement has been central to this project from its inception. The initial needs assessment demonstrated a clear desire for a safe, accessible green space within the Willow Creek neighborhood. The design process actively incorporates community feedback, ensuring that the park reflects the needs and aspirations of local residents. The park’s design prioritizes accessibility for individuals with disabilities and provides a welcoming environment for people of all ages and backgrounds. The selection of native plants supports local biodiversity and provides educational opportunities for children. This project directly addresses the inequity of limited green space in underserved communities and promotes social cohesion.

**Risk Mitigation:**

Potential risks have been identified and mitigation strategies developed. These include: weather delays (contingency time built into the timeline), contractor performance (thorough vetting and contract agreements), and community apathy (ongoing communication and engagement). A contingency fund of $15,000 is included in the budget to address unforeseen expenses. We maintain ongoing communication with all partners and stakeholders to proactively address potential issues.

**Post-Grant Sustainability:**

The long-term sustainability of the Willow Creek Pocket Park is ensured through a combination of factors. The City Parks Department will provide ongoing maintenance support, while the WCNA will lead a volunteer-led park stewardship program. We will actively seek opportunities for ongoing fundraising and grant applications to support park enhancements and programming. Educational signage will promote responsible park usage and environmental stewardship.

**Call to Action:**

The Willow Creek Pocket Park represents a transformative opportunity to revitalize a neglected space, enhance community well-being, and build climate resilience. We respectfully request municipal funding of $125,000 to realize this vital project. Your investment will yield substantial returns in terms of improved public health, environmental sustainability, and a strengthened sense of community pride. We are confident that this project will serve as a model for future community development initiatives.
