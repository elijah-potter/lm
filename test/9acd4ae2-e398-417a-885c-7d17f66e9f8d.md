The last day of summer was supposed to be perfect—sunny, warm, and endless. But for sixteen-year-old Mateo, it felt like a trap. His dad’s job had been cut, his mom was working double shifts at the diner, and his little sister, Clara, was already counting down the days until school started. "We don’t need this," Mateo whispered to himself, staring at the empty backpack he’d packed last week.

The school bus pulled up outside their cramped apartment, the kind of place that made Mateo’s stomach twist every time he walked in. He shoved his books into his bag, adjusted his backpack straps, and climbed on board. The driver nodded at him, but Mateo barely noticed. His mind was already somewhere else—on the old video game he’d saved up for, on the concert tickets he’d been dreaming of since last year.

As the bus rolled, Mateo’s phone buzzed. A text from his best friend, Javier: *"Where are you? I’m at the park. Meet me at 4.50."*

Mateo hesitated. Javier was the only person who knew about his secret: the secret he’d kept since he was twelve. The secret that made his stomach drop every time he thought about it.

He checked the time—3:58. His dad would be home soon, and he couldn’t risk another argument. But Javier was right. They were both kids who needed to escape.

The bus stopped at the park. Mateo stepped out, his shoes crunching on the dry grass. Javier was already there, arms crossed, looking like he’d rather be anywhere else. "You look like hell," Javier said without preamble.

Mateo exhaled. "Same here."

They walked toward the old abandoned house at the edge of the woods, the one they’d been sneaking into since middle school. The air smelled like damp earth and forgotten things. Javier pushed open the rusted door, and they stepped inside.

The place was a mess—half-rotted furniture, broken windows, and shadows that moved when no one was looking. But it was their home. Their secret world.

Mateo’s fingers trembled as he pulled out the old game controller. He remembered the first time they’d played here, how the sound of the game had drowned out the whispers of the house. How it had been the only thing that felt real.

Javier grinned. "You’re not going to tell your dad, are you?"

Mateo shook his head. "Not tonight."

He pressed the button. The game loaded. The world flickered to life—bright, loud, and endless. For a few hours, they forgot about the bus, the bus stop, the world outside the walls of this house.

Clara’s voice cut through the darkness. "Mateo? Where are you?"

Mateo’s blood turned to ice. He dropped the controller. "Shit."

Javier grabbed his arm. "Run."

They bolted through the house, out the back door, into the woods. Clara’s footsteps followed. They didn’t stop until they reached a nearby creek, where Mateo threw himself into the cold water. Javier splashed after him, laughing as Clara’s scream faded into the distance.

When they surfaced, Mateo’s hands were shaking. "What if we get caught?"

Javier wiped water from his face. "Then we’ll be in more trouble than ever. But we’re not. We’re alive."

Mateo nodded, but his mind was racing. What if Clara saw something? What if she figured out the truth? The truth about the game. About the house. About the things that lived in the dark.

He pulled out his phone. A message from his mom: *"Mateo, where are you? We’re worried about you."*

Mateo deleted the message. "We need to go."

They didn’t talk as they walked back to the bus stop. Javier’s shoes were muddy, his shirt damp. Mateo’s hands were cold, but his fingers were numb with fear.

When they reached the bus, Mateo didn’t board. He walked toward the park, toward the house, toward the game. Because that was where he belonged. That was where he was safe.

The door was open. The game was waiting. And for a moment, he forgot about everything else.

But then he heard it—a sound that made his skin crawl.

A whisper.

From the walls.
