- Copper, a trace mineral, is necessary for all living things.
- The liver, muscles, and bones contain copper.
- The adult body has a copper content of between 1.4 and 2.1 mg per kilogram (0.11-0.16% by weight).
- Copper aids in the transport of oxygen, which is essential for life, throughout the body.
- You may feel at ease and in good health if you know that you are providing your body with this necessary mineral.
Interaction with other Minerals
- Minerals like iron and zinc are frequently found together with copper.
These mixtures may aid in enhancing the effectiveness and absorption of copper supplementation.
The liver is where copper is stored after being absorbed in the small intestine.
- It is then transferred to other bodily tissues where it is required for a variety of biochemical procedures.
- Urine and feces contain copper that is excreted.
- For adults, the recommended daily allowance (RDA) for copper ranges from 0.9 to 1.3 mg per day.
- Copper supplements can be found as liquids, tablets, or capsules.
- Before beginning a new supplement regimen if you already take copper supplements, it's important to talk to your doctor.
