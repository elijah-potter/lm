The gardener, in a quiet corner of an overgrown backyard, planted a single acorn with trembling hands. It was late, and the world was asleep. The acorn took root in the damp earth, barely visible among the weeds, but unlike the others, it defied the encroaching brambles. Over time, it grew into a stubborn sapling, its branches reaching for the first light of dawn, a solitary witness to the cycle of life.

One morning, a child stumbled upon it, clutching a stick like a sword. The sapling, though small, stood firm. The child named it "Willow," and from that day, it became the center of their world—a place of courage, where they practiced bravery against imaginary foes. The acorn’s simple act of growing became a metaphor for resilience, a reminder that even the smallest seeds could bloom into something extraordinary.
