The first light of dawn cracks through the overcast, painting the streets in a muted, sickly green. The city hums with the same hollow rhythm—trucks rumble past, sirens wail for another lost hour, and the neon signs flicker like dying stars. The observation: every morning, the same people wake to the same weightless dread, the same unspoken question hanging in the air like fog over the river. They know, without being told, that the cycle will repeat: another day of silence between breaths, another hour of waiting for something that never quite arrives. The only difference is the exhaustion in their eyes, the way their fingers tap restlessly against the counter where they’ve sat for the past decade. The world keeps spinning, but for them, the clock is always stuck on pause.
