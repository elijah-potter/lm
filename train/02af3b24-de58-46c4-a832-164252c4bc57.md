The mist curled over the cracked pavement of an alley, thick and unbroken, as if the city itself had exhaled. The neon glow of a flickering sign—*The Last Laugh*—cast jagged shadows over the peeling wallpaper, where faded letters whispered secrets no one could hear anymore. The air smelled of damp wood and old cigarette butts, dust motes trapped in the stagnant light, like a snapshot of something that had been forgotten.

The floorboards groaned underfoot, each creak a ghostly echo of footsteps that had long since stopped. Behind me, the door swung shut with a finality that made my spine prickle—it wouldn’t open again. The last thing I saw was the reflection of my own face in a shattered window, distorted by the glass, before the world blurred into static.
