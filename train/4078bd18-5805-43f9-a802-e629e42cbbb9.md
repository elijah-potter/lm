The streetlights flickered as the first snow dusted the pavement, coating the asphalt in a quiet, reflective sheen. I stood by the lamppost, arms crossed, watching the crowd near the market square disperse into the cold. The usual hustle—vendors calling out prices, the clatter of hooves—had faded into silence, replaced by the murmur of distant voices carried on the wind.

A man in a worn trench coat stumbled past, clutching a crumpled newspaper. He kept glancing over his shoulder, as if expecting someone to follow. I watched him disappear into the alley behind the bakery, his breath fogging the air. His coat was too big for him, the collar turned up against the chill. No one stopped to help, not even the other bystanders, who had already turned their backs to the alley.

Then, the shouting started.

A woman’s voice, sharp and panicked, cut through the snowfall. *"They’re coming back! They’ll kill us!"* The words were muffled by the falling flakes, but the urgency was clear. The crowd had frozen mid-stride, turning to see what had caused the outburst. A group of men, their faces obscured by masks and hoods, emerged from the shadows of the alley, their movements deliberate, their weapons glinting in the dim light.

The man in the trench coat was gone. In his place stood another figure—taller, broader, his coat unbuttoned just enough to reveal a badge pinned to his chest. He raised his arm, signaling for order. The crowd parted, and the men in the alley stepped back, their weapons lowered. The woman clutched her husband’s arm, her eyes wide with fear. The bystanders exchanged uneasy glances, none of them sure what to make of it.

Then, the police officer turned to me. His voice was calm, but the weight in his words was heavy. *"We need to get everyone inside. The alley’s been cleared, but this isn’t over."* The crowd shuffled forward, some reluctantly, others obeying out of sheer exhaustion. The market square had gone quiet, save for the distant echo of sirens in the distance.

As I watched them go, the snow continued to fall, undisturbed. The world had paused for a moment, and I was left with the weight of what had just happened. The streetlights blinked out one by one, leaving only the faint glow of the last remaining bulb to guide the way home.
