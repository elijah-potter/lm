A dim room hums with the soft glow of an unlit lamp, its flicker casting long, shifting shadows across the walls. The air is thick with the scent of damp earth and old books, the last traces of the morning’s chatter lingering like a whisper in the breeze. Outside, distant traffic fades into a rhythmic, muted drone—car engines slowing, sirens turning to static. Inside, the silence is not empty; it carries the weight of stillness, the quiet breath of a space that has stopped rushing. A cup of tea sits untouched, its surface rippling gently under a single, warm droplet. The hum of a refrigerator, barely audible, pulses like a heartbeat. A child’s laughter from a neighboring room, now muffled and distant, drifts in and out of focus, a memory half-remembered. The world, for a moment, has paused.
