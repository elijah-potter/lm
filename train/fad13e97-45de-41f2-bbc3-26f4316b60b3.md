*The fire crackled low in the hearth, casting long shadows across the wooden walls. Elias leaned forward on the bench, his fingers tracing the grain of the old leather armchair. His voice was rough with the weight of years, the kind of voice that carried stories like a river carries stones.*

---

**"You ever hear the tale of the old man who lived in the hollow tree?"** Elias began, his eyes distant. **"Back in the days before the river ran dry, there was a trapper named Tomas. He’d say he saw the ghost of a woman in the mist, always watching him from the water’s edge. Said she’d whisper, ‘You’ll find what you’re looking for,’ before vanishing like smoke."**

A beat. The wind howled through the crack in the door.

*"One night, Tomas didn’t come back. The next morning, his cabin was gone—only the hollow tree remained, its roots twisted into something that looked like a woman’s face. The villagers called it cursed. But Tomas had been hunting for something: a lost coin, they said. A silver piece he’d buried years ago, a gift from his father. They thought he’d been chasing ghosts."*

Elias exhaled, then smiled faintly. *"You ever wonder what ghosts want? Or if they’re just echoes of what we forgot?"*

The firelight flickered. Outside, a crow cawed.
