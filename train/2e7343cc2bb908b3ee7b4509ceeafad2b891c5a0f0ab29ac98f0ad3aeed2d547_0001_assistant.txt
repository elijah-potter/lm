Vitamin D affects your bones, muscles, joints, and tissues. While we have known for many years that Vitamin D is essential for optimal bone health, several other discoveries have been made about this vitamin. One of the main jobs of Vitamin D is to help your gut absorb calcium and phosphorus from your diet. These minerals help your body build and maintain strong bones. Lower levels of Vitamin D have been linked to stress fractures and other bone issues. 

Vitamin D has many other functions as well. It is a huge part of the delicate symphony that takes place in your body at all times. And if one of those instruments, in this case, your Vitamin D supply is low, it can throw you out of balance. 

Over the years, experts have found out more about the many ways Vitamin D affects the entire musculoskeletal system. Recently conducted research has looked at vitamin D levels in young women between the ages of 16 ad 40 who were treated in the Women's Sports Medicine Center for various issues with their lower extremities. This included injuries to the ligaments, tendons, muscles and cartilage. It was found that around 66% of those women also had low levels of vitamin D in their blood.
