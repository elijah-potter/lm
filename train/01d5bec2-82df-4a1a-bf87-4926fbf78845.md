---
A fleeting glimpse of another’s handprint on my skin—
not mine, yet claimed—
as the light caught the curve of a scar I never knew I had.
The air smelled like coffee and something older,
like the scent of a place that isn’t mine but lingers like dust
in the cracks of the floorboards I walk on.

I was watching them from the corner of my eye,
the way someone watches a ghost they’ve seen before.
My fingers traced the edge of a book
they’d left open—
a story they’d finished, but the words were still there,
worn by their touch, as if the pages had swallowed them whole.

I thought of the way time bends when you’re with someone
who owns the past you can’t name.
How the present feels like a borrowed moment,
stolen from the rhythm of someone else’s life,
and I held onto it like a promise,
not knowing if I’d ever get to keep it.

The clock chimed.
I turned away,
but the echo of their voice lingered—
half-remembered, half-lost—
like the ghost of a song that plays in my head
after the music stops.
