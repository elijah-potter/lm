In the quiet town of Willow Creek, where the river whispered secrets to the old oak trees, there lived a boy named Elias. His eyes, as blue as the summer sky, often wandered toward the abandoned library on the hill, its windows boarded up like a forgotten puzzle. Elias wasn’t like other kids—he spent his free time reading books that didn’t exist in the library’s shelves, books that told tales of worlds he’d never see.

One evening, as Elias sat cross-legged on his porch, a book slid into his lap—a book he’d never seen before. It was titled *"The Map of Lost Stories."* The pages were filled with strange symbols, and the cover shimmered like a mirage. Curious, he opened it and found a map inside. The ink was warm, as if drawn by a living hand. The center of the map was marked with a single word: **"Elias."**

That night, dreams began to twist. He saw a girl with silver hair, her eyes reflecting the same blue as his own. She told him, *"The stories in that book are waiting for you to find them."* Elias woke up trembling, his hands clutching the book. He knew he had to go, but where?

The next day, he packed a single satchel—a compass, a knife, and a map. The compass didn’t point north; it pointed to the center of the map. His first stop was the old library, its doors creaking open as if inviting him in. Inside, the shelves were lined with books he’d never imagined—a book about the first time humans heard music, another about a kingdom where trees spoke. Elias picked one: *"The Song of the Forgotten."*

As he read, the pages started to sing. A melody filled the room, and suddenly, he was standing in a forest where the trees grew from the roots of ancient songs. A woman with silver hair stood before him. *"The stories you seek are not in books,"* she said, *"but in the hearts that remember them."* She pressed a small golden flute into his hands. *"Play what you remember."*

Elias played. The notes he knew from the songs he’d heard in his dreams—flutes, violins, and drums—resonated through the forest. The trees bowed, and the wind carried the music to distant places. A crowd of people, their faces blurred by time, began to sing along, their voices weaving into something beautiful. The woman smiled. *"You’ve found your voice,"* she said. *"Now, go tell the world."*
