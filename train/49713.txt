Michigan became the 28th state (including the District of Columbia) to enact mandatory renewable portfolio standards on Monday, when Governor Jennifer Granholm signed one of three recently passed energy bills (see the other two here and here) into law.
Renewable Portfolio Standards are a cumbersomely named requirement that utilities provide a certain percentage of their electricity from renewable sources by a certain year.
Michigan’s new mandate, which supporters say might well give a boost to the state’s flagging economy, requires 10 percent of the state’s electricity to come from renewable energy by 2015. The state’s two big utilities, Consumers Energy and DTE, are respectively at 5 percent and 1 percent now, according to Skip Pruss, the governor’s advisor on renewable energy.
Not everyone is celebrating the new rules. Randall Swisher, the executive director of the American Wind Energy Association, which typically supports portfolio standards, said that the legislation contained “no meaningful penalties” (penalties vary by type of electricity provider) and the requirements began from too low of a base.
Others have also opposed the legislation out of concern that it will raise electricity bills.
Nonetheless, for a state so closely tied to fossil fuels (think: the auto industry) the measure seems to represent an important first step toward baseline development of renewables. Other parts of the legislation signed today provided for expedited siting for transmission lines (a common sticking point for renewable energy), as well as tax incentives to encourage people to insulate and weatherize their homes.
In addition to the 28 states with Renewable Portfolio Standards, several more states — including Vermont, Utah, Virginia and the Dakotas — have enacted voluntary standards (see the renewable portfolio standards map from the Database of State Incentives for Renewable Energy and Efficiency for details).
Florida is currently considering standards of its own.
