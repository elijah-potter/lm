A dimly lit alleyway, lined with graffiti and flickering streetlamps, hums with the distant sound of a city pulse. A figure stands near the edge, shoulders hunched against the cold, hands tucked into their pockets. A coat, slightly too large, drapes loosely over their frame. Their face is obscured by a hat pulled low, but their breath fogs the air in front of them, visible in the pale glow of a nearby neon sign that reads *"Open Late."*

They wait. The time between the last call and the next is endless, stretching like a river of time. The pavement beneath their feet is cold, the weight of their boots pressing into the cracks. A cigarette burns between their fingers, but they haven’t lit it. Instead, they watch the flicker of a passing car’s taillights, the way the light bends around the curve of a building, the way the air shifts with unseen winds.

No one speaks. No one needs to. The silence is the only sound. The wait is a quiet tension, a half-expectation coiled tight in the chest. They could leave. They could go somewhere else. But they don’t. The city keeps moving around them, indifferent. They are just another shadow in the dark.
