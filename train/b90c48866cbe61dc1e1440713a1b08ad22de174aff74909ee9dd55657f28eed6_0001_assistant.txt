She knew it was gone before she saw it. The unusual lightness of the jar. It should be much heavier. Streaks of grey paper still clung to the adhesive left from the long-removed label. One lone nickel rattled in the jar's bottom, not worth the effort to retrieve it. Gone. The money she was saving for a special day was gone, gone with the man who’d taken it in one last mean-spirited act to hurt her. She thought she should feel something, but she didn’t. Her mind was dangerously quiet. She set the glass jar carefully on the counter and turned. 

The four-year-old sat at the kitchen table. Clutched in her fist was the last half of her peanut butter and jelly sandwich. A glob of sticky red jam had escaped her sandwich. She was pushing it across the table with one small index finger. Her finger stilled, and she looked up and grinned.

That youthful, feral smile dispelled the numbness that was spreading through her chest and filled her with warmth. There was no room for regret to take root. She didn’t think of the man who’d come and gone. The imagined fancy birthday disappeared as she watched her daughter's eyes sparkle. The money was gone. She could replace it. Now is what was important. Today. 

She pulled a chair and sat next to her grinning girl. Her daughter licked the strawberry jam from her finger and laughed. She laughed too.
