The first draft lay sprawled across the desk, ink-stained and half-eaten by a half-drunk coffee. Pages folded at the edges, a single tear of paper where the last sentence had been crossed out. On the keyboard, a half-completed paragraph flickered—*not* the one she wanted.

Her fingers hovered over the cursor. The title—*The Last Letter*—had been changed to *The Last Word*. The first scene, where the protagonist’s father had been a "stoic" man, was now a "man who loved too much," his silence a wound instead of a shield.

A draft note scribbled on the back of the envelope: *"He didn’t just say goodbye. He said ‘I’m sorry’—but the ‘sorry’ was a lie."* The words had been rearranged, a lie folded into the truth. The villain’s name had been shortened from *Vincent Dusk* to *Vincent*, as if the first syllable had been a mistake.

The laptop screen displayed the same draft, but the changes were visible in the line numbers—*inserted this paragraph*, *deleted this line*. She had moved the scene where the protagonist’s grandmother had vanished from the story entirely, replacing it with a flashback where she had *chosen* to leave, a secret buried in the forest.

A new draft note: *"The protagonist didn’t find the truth. She let it find her."* The ending, once hopeful, now carried the weight of a half-remembered dream. The story was a map, not a road, and she had rewritten the coordinates.
