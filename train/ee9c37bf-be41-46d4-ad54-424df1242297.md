**The Last Transmission**

We didn’t mean for this to happen.

The fire alarms had been muted, the emergency lights dimmed—just another Tuesday in the old building, another shift for the team that kept the machines running. But the static hissed louder than the usual hum, and then there was a crack—like the world itself had split open. The screens flickered, then went dark. The alarms wailed, not as warnings, but as screams.

Outside, the sky had turned the wrong color. Not the sickly green of a storm, but something deeper, violet, like ink bleeding into the horizon. The air tasted of ozone and something older, something that had been buried too long. The doors sealed themselves. No one moved.

Only the radio remained. Static, then a voice, distorted but clear:

*"We didn’t mean for this to happen."*

The speaker was a woman—no, not a woman anymore. Her face was a map of scars, her voice a thing that had been stretched too thin. *"They’re coming. Not tomorrow. Not next year. Already here."*

The radio cut to silence. The lights came back on. The building trembled. And somewhere, very far away, a clock struck twelve.
