In the cracked, sun-bleached edges of a forgotten library on the outskirts of Blackthorn, beneath the rusted hinges of a half-buried reading desk, a sealed envelope was discovered. The paper was aged, yellowed with centuries of neglect, the ink faded yet still legible.

*"To whomever finds this note: The lantern in the east wing flickers faintly at midnight. Inside lies the key to a door that was never meant to be opened. The lock is not iron, but something older. —A Traveler"*

The desk’s wood groaned as a figure leaned in, their breath misting in the damp air. The envelope contained no map, no directions—only a single, worn coin and a small, smooth stone marked with strange symbols. The stone glowed faintly when held to the light, pulsing like a heartbeat.

The Traveler’s handwriting, once clear, now blurred with age, hinted at a name: *"Elara."*

A door in the east wing, half-swallowed by ivy, was found ajar. Inside, the air was thick with the scent of ozone and something metallic. A single lantern, its flame steady despite the absence of fuel, stood on a pedestal. The door behind it was unlatched—no lock, no hinges, just a seam where the wood had split, as if the structure itself had wept open.

Beyond lay a hall where the walls pulsed with the faint glow of crystals embedded in the stone. At the far end, a figure sat cross-legged, their back to the door, their fingers tracing a sigil on their palm. They were not human. Their skin was like polished obsidian, their eyes two voids that drank in the light.

*"You are not the first,"* the Traveler whispered from the shadows, *"nor will you be the last."*

The stone in Elara’s palm grew warm. The lantern’s flame flickered to life, casting long shadows that writhed like serpents. The door behind them groaned, and the crystals in the walls flared brighter, casting a glow that seeped into the room like smoke.

*"The choice is yours,"* the Traveler said, *"but remember: some doors should never be opened."* The figure rose, their form shifting—twisting, dissolving into something older, something that had been waiting for centuries. The lantern’s light swallowed them whole.

Elara reached for the key, the coin trembling in her grip. The door behind them stood open, waiting. The lantern’s flame burned brighter, and the crystals in the walls pulsed like a heartbeat.

*"What will you do?"*
