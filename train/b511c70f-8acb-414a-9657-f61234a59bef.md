**The Last Light**

The clock chimed midnight. Outside, the city slept in whispers of neon and distant sirens. I sat at my desk, fingers hovering over the keyboard, the weight of the evening pressing down like a forgotten promise.

*"Tomorrow,"* I told myself, but the word tasted like ash.

My phone buzzed—a reminder I’d forgotten: *"Call your mother before she gets home."* The notification blinked in the dark, unread, as if the universe itself was mocking me for half-hearted effort. I let it slide.

The coffee cup sat cold between my hands. A half-eaten sandwich, crumbs scattered like old regrets. The screen glowed, casting a sickly green light over the chaos of half-finished tasks and unspoken words.

*"Just five more minutes,"* I muttered, but the minutes stretched like rubber, twisting into something heavier than time.

I reached for my phone again—this time, I swiped. The reminder glowed brighter. *"You promised. Remember."*

Outside, the last car passed. The streetlights flickered, one by one, like dying stars.

I closed my eyes. Tomorrow would be another day. Today was just a ghost of what could have been.
