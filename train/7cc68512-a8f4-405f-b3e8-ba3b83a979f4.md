The rain had turned the cobblestones slick, reflecting the dim glow of streetlamps in fractured shards of light. Elias adjusted his coat’s collar, fingers brushing the cold metal of his watch—his father’s last gift, a relic of a life he’d outgrown. Outside the crumbling church, the old man sat on a bench, his hands wrapped around a half-drunk glass of something red, his breath shallow, the way it had been for months.

*"You should’ve stayed,"* the man had said, voice rough as gravel. *"The world’s changed, Elias. But not for you."*

Elias had turned then, his boots kicking up a dust cloud, and walked away, the church’s bell ringing behind him like a final, hollow warning. The city swallowed him whole, indifferent to the weight of what had been said. He didn’t look back.
