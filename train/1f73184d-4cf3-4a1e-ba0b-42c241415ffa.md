The grand chandelier swayed in a deliberate, hypnotic rhythm, its crystals catching the dim, golden light from the dying fireplace. The air smelled of aged wood and something faintly metallic—like old blood. Clara clutched her pocket watch, its brass face now a blur of tiny, looping numbers. Her breath came in shallow, unsteady gasps as the world around her seemed to stretch, every movement a labored, suspended moment.

A sharp *crack* split the silence. A shard of broken glass glittered on the floor, frozen mid-fall. The door creaked open, revealing a figure in tattered robes, their face half-lit by a flickering lantern. They stepped forward, their fingers brushing Clara’s wrist, and the moment stretched—her pulse hammered in her throat, the lantern’s flame flickered, and for a single, endless heartbeat, time seemed to hold its breath.

Then, the figure turned, and the world snapped back into motion. The chandelier’s sway became a rush, the lantern’s flame flared, and Clara stumbled forward, her vision blurring as she stumbled toward the hearth. The pocket watch in her pocket ticked—slowly, painfully, as if the seconds were measured in agonizing inches.
