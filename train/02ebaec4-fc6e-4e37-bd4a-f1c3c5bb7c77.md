The clock struck midnight in the abandoned library, its chandeliers casting long shadows over the forgotten tomes. The last page of the book in my hands was blank, and the ink had faded into the air like mist. Outside, the city slept, unaware of the paradox unfolding within these walls.

I turned the final leaf, and the spine split open, revealing not a conclusion but a spine of its own. The pages before me were not a story, but a series of questions, each one a door I could open or close. The first question whispered: *"What if the end was never the end?"*

I chose to read. The words formed in my mind as if they had always been there, waiting. The first line was simple: *"You are the beginning of everything."* I laughed, the sound echoing through the empty hallways. How could this be? But then, the story shifted again—it was not a story but a loop, a cycle of creation and destruction that had no start or end.

The room began to spin. The books stacked in the corners of the shelves stretched endlessly, their spines whispering in unison: *"Turn back, or go forward, or stay where you are."* I chose to stay, to feel the weight of the words pressing against my skin, the way they had been meant to be read: not as a conclusion, but as a beginning.

The last line of the book I had just read was not a question but a command: *"Do not stop."* So I did. The library doors creaked open, revealing not the city but a forest of new books, each one a potential beginning. The wind carried the scent of ink and possibility, and for the first time, I realized the true magic of endings—they were just the start of something new.

Now, I am standing in a field of daisies, their petals shimmering like stars. A hand reaches out from the ground, and it holds a book. The cover reads: *"The End Was Never the End."* I turn to the first page, and the first word is the same as the last: *"You."*
