Returned to the library’s stone floor, its worn tiles whispering underfoot. The scent of old paper and damp leather lingers—antique coffee mugs crusted with dust sit on the corner table where you once sat, sketching. The overgrown garden outside the arched windows has reclaiming the space, ivy clinging to the brick walls where you used to sit with a book. The library’s quiet hum has shifted to a more organic rhythm: children’s laughter, the occasional hum of a printer, and the distant chatter of a group of elderly patrons who’ve become neighbors. The stacks are slightly rearranged, some shelves now crammed with newer arrivals you’d never have guessed existed before. The bench near the front desk, once yours, is now occupied by a woman who nods at you but doesn’t speak, as if she knows you’d return. The way the afternoon light filters through the stained-glass windows has changed—now golden, not the same harsh winter glow. A new sign for the free book swap circle hangs above the door you used to enter, and the scent of freshly baked bread from the café across the street lingers on the wind, a reminder of how much has stayed the same.
