In the dim glow of a flickering lamp, Elias sat cross-legged on the floor, fingers tracing the grain of the worn wooden table. The hum of the city outside was muffled by thick, heavy curtains. His phone lay abandoned on the floor, its screen dark. He had chosen silence, not because he was angry, but because silence was the only answer that fit the weight of the day.

His breath was shallow, almost a whisper. The room was still, save for the distant sound of his own heartbeat. He closed his eyes, letting the silence seep into his bones. Somewhere, a train whistled, but it was far away, distant, and meaningless. He had chosen to let go of the noise, to let the world become just another quiet space. His hands trembled slightly, but he did not reach for his phone, did not open a message, did not scroll through social media. Instead, he let his mind drift, unguarded and free.

The silence was not empty—it was full of the weight of unspoken words, half-finished thoughts, the echo of the past. He allowed himself to be lost in it, to let the silence be his sanctuary. There was no rush, no need to fill the space. Silence was the only thing that could hold him now. He exhaled slowly, his body relaxing into the quiet, finally letting go of the burden of the world.
