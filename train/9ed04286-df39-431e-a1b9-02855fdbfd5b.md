The neon sign flickered weakly over the boarded-up storefront, casting a dim glow on the cracked pavement. Inside, the hum of the generator filled the air like a low, steady heartbeat. The door groaned open, splintering under the weight of a rusted latch. Inside, the scent of stale coffee and damp wood clung to the air.

A figure in a hooded jacket stepped forward, gripping a flashlight with trembling hands. The beam cut through the darkness, revealing a half-empty café—lamps still glowing in their sockets, though the power flickered behind them. The barista, hunched over a terminal, stared at the screen, fingers twitching, before snapping them back to life with a sharp exhale.

"Another one," a voice muttered, and the figure handed over a mug of steaming coffee. The café’s owner, wiping her hands on her apron, sighed. "The generator’s been running for hours. We’re out of diesel."

A distant siren wailed, the sound of sirens blending with the generator’s growl. The café’s door creaked as the hooded figure turned to leave, their coat damp with condensation. The last light died in a café window, plunging the street into silence.
