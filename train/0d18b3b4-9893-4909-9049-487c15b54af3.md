In the quiet hum of a city that never sleeps, the last train left the station at midnight, its headlight cutting through the smog like a blade through paper. The platform was empty except for a single figure standing at the edge, clutching a half-eaten sandwich wrapped in newspaper, its crust still warm. The figure was young, their face half-lit by the glow of a flickering streetlamp, their eyes reflecting the distant lights of a skyline that seemed to stretch endlessly into the horizon.

The train was gone. The figure exhaled sharply, the stale air of the station mixing with the scent of rain on pavement. They stepped onto the tracks, following the sound of distant footsteps. The footsteps grew louder, closer, and then there they were: a man in a long coat, his face obscured by a hood, his hands clutching a worn leather satchel. The figure froze. They had seen him before, in a crowd, in a mirror, but never so close.

"You remember me," the stranger said, his voice low and rough, like gravel underfoot. The figure nodded, their breath coming in shallow gasps. "I'm not here to hurt you. I'm here to take you back to the beginning."

The stranger reached into his satchel and pulled out a small, rusted key. He pressed it into the figure's palm. "This opens the door. It's where it all started." The figure's fingers trembled as they took the key, the weight of its antiquity heavy in their hand. The stranger turned and walked away, his figure disappearing into the night.

The figure turned back to the tracks, the key trembling in their grip. They had no choice now. They had to open the door. The tracks curved, disappearing into the distance, leading them to a place they didn't know. They stepped onto the platform of a station that seemed to be a mirror image of the one they had just left—except this one was older, the buildings dusted with age, the air thick with the scent of old paper and ink.

A sign above the door read: "Beginning." The figure placed the key in the lock, and with a quiet click, the door swung open. Inside, the walls were lined with shelves of books, their spines worn smooth by time. A single candle burned in the center of the room, casting long shadows that danced across the pages. The figure stepped inside, the key in their hand, the weight of it suddenly familiar.

They found themselves at the start of a story, a tale of a world that had begun long before they were born. The shelves stretched endlessly, each book whispering a part of the beginning, a fragment of history. They reached out, turning the pages, and the words began to form into a new narrative, a tale that was already unfolding, one they would play a part in.

The figure stood in the beginning, the key still in their grip, the story still to be written. The city outside was still there, the train still vanished, and the stranger still walked away, leaving them at the threshold of something new. The beginning had become an end, and the end was the beginning of another tale.
