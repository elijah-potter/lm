**The Last Light**

The city slept under a veil of neon, a thousand eyes blinking in the dark like dying stars. I sat in the back of the old cab, fingers tracing the edge of a half-empty bottle of whiskey, the glass cold against my lips. The streets hummed with the distant murmur of conversation, the clatter of heels on pavement, but I was alone in this moment, lost in the echo of my own thoughts.

Outside, the window was a canvas of flickering streetlights and ghostly reflections—windows, faces, the occasional blur of motion that wasn’t me. I closed my eyes, let the world dissolve into static.

Then—silence.

A door creaked open. The driver stepped out, his coat damp with rain, and I followed, my breath fogging in the cold air. We climbed the stairs to the abandoned theater on the hill, its peeling paint and shattered marquee a silent warning. The air smelled of old dust and something metallic, like rusted metal and forgotten dreams.

The stage was a stage of bones. Chairs, half-buried under layers of dust, sat in a half-circle, their backs broken. A single spotlight flickered to life, casting long shadows across the cracked floor. I stepped forward, my boots crunching on debris.

There was nothing left to take. The set was a relic, the props dusted with age. But then—

A shape moved in the darkness.

Not a figure, but a *mark*. A line of black, smooth, unnatural, pressed against the floorboards. It pulsed faintly, like a heartbeat under the skin of the wood. I reached out, my fingers hovering just above it.

A voice, not quite sound—just a whisper in my mind—*Take it.*

I did.

The moment my fingers touched it, the stage *moved*. The lights dimmed. The shadows stretched, warped, and then—

The world dissolved into color.

Not the color of light, but of *memory*—a riot of reds, blues, and golds, swirling like a storm inside my skull. Faces, not faces, but *layers*, overlapping, melting. A child’s laughter. A lover’s sigh. The taste of blood. The sound of a gunshot. The scent of rain on pavement. And then—

Silence.

I was standing in the middle of a forest, trees pressing in around me like the walls of a tomb. The ground was soft, wet. My hands were empty. But in my palm, resting like a ghost, was a single, perfect black feather.

It shimmered.

And then—

The image faded.
