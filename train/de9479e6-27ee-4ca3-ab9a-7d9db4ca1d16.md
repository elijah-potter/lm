**The Last Light**

The city slept beneath a veil of frost, its neon signs dead like the birds that had fallen from the sky weeks before. In the hollowed-out core of the old library, Elias knelt by the window, watching the horizon blur into something darker than night. He traced the shape of the manuscript in his hands—a spiral of ink on aged parchment—until it dissolved into static.

Outside, the wind howled through the empty streets, carrying whispers of names no one remembered. The manuscript was his ticket. He had spent years piecing together fragments: a map, a prophecy, the names of those who had vanished before him. The library’s grand doors groaned as they swung shut behind him, sealing the city away from the last light.

He unfolded the parchment.

*"The ending is a beginning. The cycle does not break."*

A sound—like the distant chime of a bell—cut through the silence. The manuscript fluttered in his grip. The city’s lights flickered. Then, the air shifted. The door behind him stood open, and beyond it, the sky wasn’t black. It was *blue*, vast and endless, and the wind carried not screams, but the sound of laughter.

Elias looked down. His fingers closed around nothing.

The manuscript lay on the floor, its edges frayed, its pages blank.

He stood. The street beyond the library doors was alive—streets, people, a world he didn’t recognize. The wind carried his name.

He walked into the blue.
