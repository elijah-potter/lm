## Proposal: The Willow Creek Pocket Park – A Climate-Resilient Community Oasis

**Executive Summary:** This proposal requests $75,000 in municipal funding to transform the currently underutilized and degraded lot at the corner of Elm and Willow Creek Road into the Willow Creek Pocket Park. This project addresses critical needs within the Willow Creek neighborhood: a lack of accessible green space, heightened vulnerability to increasingly frequent and severe storm events, and limited opportunities for community gathering and intergenerational connection. The proposed park will incorporate native plantings, stormwater management infrastructure, and accessible design features, creating a climate-resilient community asset that enhances neighborhood wellbeing and provides tangible benefits for years to come. This investment represents a modest sum with a significant return, fostering a healthier, more resilient, and more connected Willow Creek.

**Project Description & Goals:**

The Willow Creek Pocket Park aims to convert a 0.5-acre lot, presently characterized by compacted soil, litter accumulation, and occasional illegal dumping, into a vibrant, functional, and ecologically beneficial public space. The overarching goal is to create a climate-resilient park that serves as a neighborhood gathering place, reduces stormwater runoff, supports local biodiversity, and provides educational opportunities regarding sustainable practices. Specific objectives include: establishing a native plant garden, installing a rain garden to manage stormwater, constructing accessible pathways and seating areas, and creating a small, shaded play area for young children.

**Project Plan & Timeline:**

The project will be implemented across four distinct phases, each with clearly defined responsibilities and deliverables.

* **Phase 1: Planning & Design (Months 1-2) – Responsible Partner: Willow Creek Neighborhood Association (WCNA) & Landscape Architect (contracted).** Deliverables include: Community needs assessment report (WCNA), finalized park design plans (Landscape Architect), securing necessary permits from the City Planning Department.
* **Phase 2: Site Preparation & Infrastructure (Months 3-5) – Responsible Partner: City Parks & Recreation Department & Volunteer Construction Crew (coordinated by WCNA).** Deliverables include: Site clearing and grading, installation of underground drainage pipes, construction of rain garden basin, delivery and placement of topsoil. The City Parks & Recreation Department will provide heavy equipment and technical expertise.
* **Phase 3: Planting & Construction (Months 6-8) – Responsible Partner: Willow Creek Garden Club & Volunteer Planting Crew (coordinated by WCNA).** Deliverables include: Installation of native plant garden, construction of accessible pathways, installation of benches and picnic tables, construction of small play area with natural play elements. The Willow Creek Garden Club will provide native plants and horticultural guidance.
* **Phase 4: Community Launch & Education (Month 9) – Responsible Partner: WCNA & Local Schools.** Deliverables include: Grand opening celebration, installation of interpretive signage detailing park features and ecological benefits, workshops on rain garden maintenance and native plant gardening for community members.

**Budget:**

The total project cost is estimated at $85,000. The requested municipal funding of $75,000 will be matched by $10,000 in in-kind donations and volunteer labor from the WCNA, Willow Creek Garden Club, and local businesses. A detailed breakdown of costs is presented below:

* **Landscape Architect Fees:** $12,000 (Design and Permitting Support)
* **Site Preparation & Infrastructure Materials:** $20,000 (Drainage pipes, gravel, topsoil, erosion control materials)
* **Native Plants & Soil Amendments:** $15,000 (Trees, shrubs, perennials, compost, mulch)
* **Construction Materials (Pathways, Seating):** $18,000 (Recycled materials, accessible paving stones, benches, picnic tables)
* **Play Area Materials:** $8,000 (Natural play elements, safety surfacing)
* **Signage & Educational Materials:** $2,000 (Interpretive signs, brochures, workshop supplies)
* **Contingency Fund:** $10,000 (11.76% of total cost, to address unforeseen expenses such as material price fluctuations or unexpected site conditions)

The in-kind contributions consist primarily of volunteer labor (estimated value: $5,000), plant donations ($3,000), and material discounts from local businesses ($2,000).  The contingency fund will be managed by the WCNA and requires approval from the City Parks & Recreation Department for any expenditure.

**Community Engagement & Equity Impacts:**

Community engagement has been central to this project's development. The WCNA conducted a neighborhood survey and hosted two public meetings to gather input on park design and desired features.  The design reflects these preferences, prioritizing accessibility and intergenerational appeal.  The park's location in a historically underserved neighborhood directly addresses inequities in access to green space.  The inclusion of a small, accessible play area specifically caters to families with young children, and the educational components will promote environmental literacy across all age groups.  The WCNA will ensure ongoing engagement through a "Friends of Willow Creek Park" group, fostering a sense of ownership and responsibility for the park’s upkeep.

**Risk Mitigation:**

Potential risks have been identified and mitigation strategies developed.  Unforeseen site conditions (e.g., buried debris) will be addressed through careful site investigation during Phase 2.  Material price fluctuations will be managed through bulk purchasing and vendor negotiations.  Volunteer recruitment and retention will be supported through ongoing communication and recognition.  Liability insurance will be secured from the City.  The contingency fund provides a financial buffer to address unexpected challenges.

**Post-Grant Sustainability:**

Long-term sustainability is ensured through a multi-faceted approach. The “Friends of Willow Creek Park” group will be responsible for ongoing maintenance, fundraising, and programming. The WCNA will secure ongoing support from the City Parks & Recreation Department for essential maintenance tasks.  The native plant garden requires minimal watering once established, reducing long-term maintenance costs.  Educational signage will promote responsible park usage and discourage vandalism. The City will be requested to include the park in its annual maintenance budget.

**Call to Action:**

The Willow Creek Pocket Park represents a unique opportunity to invest in a vital community asset, enhance neighborhood resilience, and promote equitable access to green space.  We respectfully request your approval of this proposal and the associated funding of $75,000.  This modest investment will yield significant and lasting benefits for the Willow Creek neighborhood and contribute to a healthier, more vibrant city for all.  We are confident that, with your support, the Willow Creek Pocket Park will become a cherished community gathering place for generations to come.
