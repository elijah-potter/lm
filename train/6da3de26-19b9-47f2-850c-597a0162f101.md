The rain tapped against the grimy windows, muffling the city’s distant hum. The old armchair creaked under the weight of a hooded figure wrapped in a patched-up jacket, fingers tracing the rim of a half-empty whiskey glass. The coffee table held a single, unread newspaper folded to a blank page, the headline’s faded ink still legible but meaningless: *"Breaking News: Global Stocks Up 2.3%"*—a statistic that felt like a joke in this quiet room.

Outside, a car horn blared, but the sound dissolved into the noise of the subway below, where a distant train groaned to life, its doors hissing open and shut like a promise kept. The air smelled of damp concrete and something older—old smoke, old fear. The figure exhaled slowly, watching the reflection of the streetlight flicker across the windowsill, then vanish when the light dimmed.

A knock at the door. Not loud. Not desperate. Just… there. The doorknob turned. The door swung open, revealing a figure in a suit, eyes sharp with exhaustion, a clipboard tucked under their arm. They stepped inside, and the moment they crossed the threshold, the room felt different—less still, less empty. The whiskey glass was empty now. The newspaper lay flat, unopened.

The hooded figure stood, their breath misting in the cold air, and for the first time, the question didn’t feel like a puzzle. It was just waiting.
