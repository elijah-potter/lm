In the quiet hum of the city’s clock tower, a stranger sat beneath its shadow, fingers tracing the cracks in the pavement like ancient runes. The first one arrived at dawn—a lost dog, its collar dull with salt and time. The second came at noon, clutching a crumpled banknote, eyes wide with fear. By dusk, the third joined them, whispering in a language none understood. The pattern wasn’t in their arrival, but in the way they *stopped*—not for food, not for rest, but to linger, to *watch* the same corner of the street where the tower’s shadow stretched longest. The fourth arrived the next day, and by the fifth, the pattern was clear: they did not seek. They merely *observed*. The tower’s hands turned slow, deliberate, and each time, the stranger’s posture shifted just so—shoulders back, chin lifted, as if waiting for something unseen. The city moved around them, indifferent, yet the pattern persisted, unbroken, as if the world had been waiting for this moment all along. The narrator watched until the last one left, their footsteps swallowed by the wind, and wondered if the pattern was the question or the answer.
