A dimly lit room hums with the faintest ambient glow from a single, flickering lamp. The walls, lined with aged, dust-covered books, seem to whisper forgotten stories. A lone figure sits hunched over a desk, fingers tracing the edges of a blank sheet of paper. The air is thick with the scent of old parchment and something metallic—like the scent of blood left to dry. The figure doesn’t speak, doesn’t move, doesn’t blink. Outside, the city’s distant machinery stutters like a broken clock. The only sound is the slow, deliberate breathing of the person at the desk, their breath forming a mist in the cold air.

Then—nothing. The silence isn’t broken; it’s the absence of sound itself, the absence of everything but the weight of what was said. A single word, unspoken, lingers like a curse or a blessing, unraveling in the space between them. The page remains untouched. The clock ticks. The world holds its breath.
