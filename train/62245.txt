In your yard and mine, there are plants that can cause harm. Understand the terminology used so that you can estimate the degree of potential damage.
We refer to a common native plant as “poison ivy.” Can it cause bodily injury? Yes, indeed. Normally, will a poison ivy infection result in death? No. The word “poison” refers to a substance that causes harm or injury. “Toxic” is the word that properly refers to death. Thus, contact with this plant can result in an itchy rash.
There may be toxic berries, leaves, stems and roots growing on your property, but they are deadly only if ingested. Exercise caution if you do not recognize a plant, and never, ever put a plant into your mouth.
Do not believe that if birds eat the berries, or if chipmunks or caterpillars eat the leaves, that the plant is not poisonous or toxic to humans. It may be.
You should always be able to identify any plant that you are involved with. Poison ivy has three-parted leaves, often shiny but can be dull. New leaf growth is usually reddish and shiny. It may creep along the ground, develop into a woody shrub or climb the tallest of trees. Leaf edges may be smooth, saw-toothed or lobed. Flowers are greenish-yellow to white, followed by clusters of white berries.
There are pronounced individual degrees of susceptibility that can change from one year to the next. “Leaves of three, let it be.”
