A dimly lit subway car hums with the steady rhythm of ascending. Passengers shift silently, eyes half-closed against the fluorescent glow. A lone newspaper crinkles as a commuter folds it into a tight ball, burying their face in it. Then—

A burst of laughter erupts from the corner, sharp and unguarded. A group of three friends, mid-conversation, lean toward one another, mouths twitching. The woman in the middle, clutching a coffee cup, gasps, hands flying to her throat. The man beside her coughs, doubling over, face red. The laughter spreads, infectious, until the entire car erupts in a cacophony of giggles, shoulders shaking.

The commuter on the opposite bench turns, startled, then bursts out laughing too. The train’s doors hiss open, the world outside a blur of streetlights and distant sirens. The laughter fades like the train’s slow deceleration—just a memory, already dissolving into the evening’s routine.
