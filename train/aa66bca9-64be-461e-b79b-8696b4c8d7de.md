When the pencil first broke free of the sketchpad, it wasn’t just lines on paper—it was a whisper of a dream given form. The first strokes were hesitant, half-hearted, a child’s attempt at a grown-up’s story. But then, the ink ran wild, the page alive with the chaos of ideas that had been stifled in the mind. The "plan" was just a rough draft, a half-finished sketch of what could be, where the characters were still blobs of potential, and the world was a blank canvas.

Then came the revisions. The protagonist’s name was carved into the narrative like a brand—no longer just a placeholder, but a voice, a heartbeat. The plot twisted and turned, a living thing that ate up the original plan like a storm devours a village. The ending wasn’t decided; it was discovered in the chaos, like a hidden door unlocked by the weight of the story itself.

Now, the tale stands as a story—no longer a blueprint, but a tale with its own rhythm, its own purpose. The plan became a story when the writer stopped thinking about *what* to say and started saying it, unfiltered, unedited. The story was born in the act of telling, not in the act of planning.
