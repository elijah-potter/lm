The first light touched the edges of the world like a slow, deliberate breath, painting the horizon in shades of gold that blurred into crimson. The air was thick with the scent of damp earth and something older, something that had been waiting in the dark.

She sat on the moss-stained stones, her fingers tracing the curve of a half-remembered dream. The fire crackled low, its embers dancing like trapped fireflies, casting long shadows that stretched and twisted across the stones. The wind whispered through the trees, a chorus of unseen voices, and the leaves moved in time as if in a forgotten dance.

Her breath came shallow, each exhale carrying the weight of the quiet things she couldn’t say. The world had always been like this—soft, unspoken, full of the things not meant to be spoken aloud.

Dawn crept over the mountains, the sky a canvas of soft lavender and streaked with silver. The first light spilled over the peaks, turning the clouds to lace, and the earth beneath her feet felt warmer, alive with the quiet pulse of creation.
