The evening air was thick with the scent of damp earth and distant rain, a whisper of summer’s last sigh before winter’s grip tightened. The old barn stood sentinel, its wooden planks creaking under the weight of time, its windows like empty eyes watching the world pass by. Inside, the fire had long since burned down to embers, but the heat lingered in the cracks of the floorboards, a stubborn warmth that refused to die.

I sat cross-legged in the corner, my fingers tracing the grain of the wooden table where I’d spilled the last of the whiskey. Outside, the clock on the wall—its hands frozen at three forty-seven—tick-ticked like a heartbeat, slow and deliberate. The wind howled through the broken roof, carrying with it the scent of pine and something older, something buried beneath the soil.

I had come here for peace. Not the kind that comes with answers, but the quiet that lets you breathe. The kind where the past doesn’t chase you, where the future is just another shadow on the wall. But the past, it always finds a way. Always.

A branch snapped outside. Too loud. Too deliberate. I tensed, my breath catching in my throat. The fire had died long ago, but the shadows still moved in the corners, watching. Waiting.

Then—nothing. Just the sound of my own breath, slow and steady, the way it should be. The door creaked open, and for a moment, I thought I heard footsteps. But it was just the wind, playing tricks with the old hinges. The door slammed shut behind me, leaving me alone again.

I exhaled, the smoke curling from my lips like a ghost. The whiskey had warmed me, but it had also sharpened my edges, made me see things clearer. The truth wasn’t here. Not in the fire, not in the wind, not in the hollow of the barn. The truth was in the space between the cracks, the places where the walls didn’t quite meet.

But I didn’t need the truth. I needed to forget. And so, I leaned forward, my hands trembling slightly as I reached for the whiskey bottle again. The last drop was mine. The rest was for the ghosts who still lingered in these walls.
