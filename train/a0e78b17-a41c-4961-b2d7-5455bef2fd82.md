I remember the first time the old radio crackled to life in the attic,
the hum of static and whispers of voices from another world,
each forgotten by time.

I remember the way the moths danced in the flickering lantern light,
their wings like stained glass, casting prismatic shadows on the walls,
while the clock struck midnight, slow and deliberate.

I remember the scent of rain on copper roofs,
the way the rain pattered against the windowpane like a secret,
and the way I listened, heart pounding, until it faded.

I remember the letters I never sent,
the words I couldn’t find words for, the dreams I never dared to keep,
the quiet ache of knowing some things were meant to stay buried.

I remember the silence between the words,
the unspoken truths that linger like ghosts in the hall,
the moments when the world held its breath.

I remember the door at the end of the hall,
the one that wasn’t meant to be opened,
the one that led to what wasn’t meant to be seen.

I remember the way my hands trembled as I reached for the key,
the way the light inside was brighter than anything I had ever known,
and the way the air smelled like fire and ash.

I think of the choices I made with my eyes closed,
the paths I walked into without looking back,
the stories I let die before they could grow.

I think of the voices I heard in the dark,
the whispers that followed me through the years,
the ones that still echo when I close my eyes.
