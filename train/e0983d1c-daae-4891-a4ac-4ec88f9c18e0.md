AITA for insisting my sister return a borrowed heirloom?

Okay, this is a bit complicated, and I’m honestly stressed about it. It involves my grandmother, my sister, and a rather valuable antique brooch.

My grandmother, Eleanor, passed away last October (2023) in Sarasota, Florida. She was a remarkable woman, a collector of beautiful things, and she left behind a significant estate. Among her possessions was a stunning Art Deco brooch, made of platinum and featuring intricate diamond work. It had been in our family for generations, and she’s always told me, her eldest grandchild, that it was intended for me. She made this very clear to me on numerous occasions, particularly around Christmas (December 2021, December 2022). 

My sister, Clara, is three years younger than me. She’s always been a little…scattered, let’s say. She’s a lovely person, but she struggles with responsibility and often relies on others. She was present at the reading of the will in November 2023. The will specifically stated the brooch was to be bequeathed to me. Clara seemed fine with it at the time, offered condolences, and we all moved on.

However, around the first of January 2024, Clara approached me. She mentioned how much she admired the brooch and, almost casually, asked if she could borrow it for a charity gala she was attending in Charleston, South Carolina, on March 15th. I hesitated. I knew she wasn't always the most careful person, and the brooch is irreplaceable. After a lot of back and forth (January 2024), and assurances from her that she would be incredibly careful and insured, I reluctantly agreed. I made her sign a document outlining her responsibility for its care and the insurance coverage she was required to obtain. I thought that was the end of it.

Fast forward to last week (April 17th, 2024). Clara called, sounding flustered. She’s lost the brooch. Apparently, it fell off her dress during a dance at the gala. She claims she searched extensively but couldn't find it. She now expects me to simply accept this and let it go. She argues that it’s “just a thing” and that I’m being unreasonable for being upset. She’s even suggested that, given my tendency to be “overly attached” to sentimental items, she might be doing me a favor by relieving me of the burden.

My mother, who is usually my voice of reason, is now siding with Clara. She says I need to be more understanding and that Clara is already feeling terrible. She thinks I'm being insensitive and that clinging to the brooch is preventing me from moving on. She’s subtly implying that my grandmother would have wanted Clara to have it, regardless of the will. My father, bless his heart, just looks bewildered and tries to mediate.

I’m heartbroken. This brooch represents a tangible connection to my grandmother, and the thought of it being lost forever is devastating. Clara’s dismissive attitude and my mother’s enabling behavior are only compounding my distress. I’m feeling incredibly isolated and frustrated. I feel like I’m the only one who truly understands the significance of this heirloom. I’m starting to wonder if I’m overreacting, but then I remember my grandmother’s explicit wishes and Clara’s nonchalant response. I’m genuinely struggling to navigate this situation. My mother is suggesting I let it go and that Clara has suffered enough. I feel like I’m being pressured to accept something completely unacceptable. I feel like I’m being manipulated. ESH, frankly.

AITA for insisting my sister return the brooch, or at least make a serious effort to locate it, even if it means causing a rift in the family?
