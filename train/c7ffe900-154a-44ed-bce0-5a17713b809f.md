The crumbling stone arches of the bridge sway under the weight of rusted iron chains. The river below mirrors the last light of dusk, its surface broken by the jagged edges of old docks that lean precariously. The scent of salt and damp wood clings to the air, thick with the ghost of laughter and the distant hum of a fishing boat’s motor.

Inside, the tavern’s fire has long died down, its embers smoldering in the hearth. The walls are lined with maps of the world we thought we knew, now faded and brittle. A single, half-empty tankard sits on the counter where a stranger once stood, their handprint still visible in the wax seal. The floorboards groan underfoot, each step sending vibrations through the floorboards like a slow, mourning heartbeat.

Outside, the wind picks up, carrying with it the last whispers of the wind through the empty streets. The distant chime of a clock marks the end of an era, its toll too quiet to hear. The lanterns flicker, their light barely cutting through the gathering gloom. This is where we say goodbye.
