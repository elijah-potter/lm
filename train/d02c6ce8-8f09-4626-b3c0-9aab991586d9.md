In the dim glow of a flickering hallway lamp, **Elias** stood by the cracked glass of the old city newsroom window. The rain hammered against the panes, his breath fogging slightly in the cold air. His fingers tightened around the worn envelope in his pocket, the stamp still damp from the post office. A single name was scrawled on the inside: *"Dr. Lien."*

A radio crackled from the corner, its low hum the only sound beside the distant wail of sirens. He turned to the door—no one moved there, just the creak of hinges under a footfall. The door handle was stiff, but he pushed it open anyway, the wind tugging at his coat.

The hallway stretched endlessly, lined with posters of half-finished buildings, their names faded like the faces of the men who’d once worked here. He stepped inside, the fluorescent light buzzing overhead, casting long shadows. At the far end, a single desk sat empty, its surface slick with condensation. A newspaper lay open, its headline bold: *"BREAKING: Attack on Sector 7—12 confirmed dead."*

Elias exhaled sharply, pressing his palm to his chest. The door behind him slammed shut with a final, decisive *thud*. He turned, walking toward the desk, the paper fluttering to the floor. His phone buzzed in his pocket—an unknown number. He hesitated, then pulled it out, swiping to answer.

*"Elias?"* The voice on the other end was sharp, urgent. *"You’re the only one who’s been here since dawn. Where are you?"* The line went dead. His hands trembled as he pocketed his phone. The news was out. Now, the waiting began.
