I saw a figure standing in the dark alleyway, moving too fast for the flickering streetlight. The shape blurred, but the silhouette was unmistakably human. When I called out, nothing happened—no reflection in the wet pavement, no movement. The air smelled of damp concrete and something metallic, like rust. Then, the streetlight died. Complete blackness. My breath hitched; my pulse raced. I told myself it was just a trick of the light, but the way my vision swam, the way the ground seemed to tilt under me—it felt like I’d seen something that shouldn’t have been there. Now I’m outside, staring at the empty alley, the only sound my ragged breathing.
