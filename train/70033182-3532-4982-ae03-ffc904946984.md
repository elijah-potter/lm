An echoing question is one that returns to or revisits its own premise, often to explore deeper layers of meaning, paradox, or self-reference. For example:

*"Why do you ask why?"* challenges the act of inquiry itself, forcing reflection on the nature of questioning.

Or:

*"If a question is a question, can it truly be answered?"* creates a loop that critiques the boundaries of knowledge.

Echoing questions often:
1. **Invert logic** (e.g., *"Who asks questions to ask questions?"*).
2. **Create tension** between form and content (e.g., *"What is the question of a question?"*).
3. **Invoke paradox** (e.g., *"A paradox is a question that cannot be answered without restating itself"*).

They’re useful in philosophy, game design, or systems where recursive thinking is valued.
