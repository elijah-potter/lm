The morning shift ended without a single complaint about the forgotten coffee order—just a half-empty cup and a half-emptied vending machine tray. The janitor slipped past the surveillance camera at 3:17 AM, wiping down the fluorescent lights with a damp rag until they glowed just warm enough to cast a soft golden hue over the fluorescent signage, making the "CLOSED" letters slightly more legible. The office cat, now a graying tabby with a single white paw, curled up on the empty desk of the intern who’d left their bag of chips in the break room overnight. The intern’s laptop screen, still warm from their last session, reflected the last light of the day like a dying ember. A single, unopened envelope remained on the receptionist’s desk, its return address from a client who’d never show up—only the signature "J. Thompson" and a handwritten note: *"Thank you for the coffee."* No one saw it. No one missed it. The victory was in the quiet, unnoticed: the office running a little smoother, the last light lingering just long enough to remember what it felt like to be home.
