The café’s afternoon light spilled through the grimy windows, turning the neon sign into a slow, trembling heartbeat. I sat in the back corner, my coffee untouched, the steam curling into a ghost of my indecision. Outside, the city hummed—someone’s laughter, the distant wail of a siren, the clink of a fork on a plate. I had come here to finish my novel, to bury myself in the quiet of words that wouldn’t let me go.

But then I saw her. She sat at the counter, her back to the door, a stack of crumpled receipts in her hands. The way her shoulders shook as she exhaled made my chest tighten. She hadn’t aged much since last I’d seen her—just a few more lines, a deeper crease between her brows. But her hands were different now. Calloused, like she’d spent years gripping something that refused to let her go.

I should have left. The novel was my life. But the moment her eyes flicked toward the door, something in me cracked open. The last time I’d seen her, I’d been the one holding the door shut. I’d thought I’d won. But now, watching her, I wondered if I’d just been the one who’d stopped her from walking away.

I grabbed my bag, my fingers trembling. The words I’d been writing for months—*about the girl who loved a boy who didn’t love her back*—suddenly felt like a lie. Not because I’d changed my mind about the story, but because I’d changed my mind about her.

She turned then, her dark hair catching the light like a second skin. The smile she gave me was the same one I’d memorized years ago, but now it felt like a betrayal. I’d spent so long trying to forget her, to make her forget me, but the more I ran, the more I remembered. That the pain wasn’t in the past. It was in the space between us, the one I’d been too afraid to fill.

I should have walked out that night. I should have let her go. But instead, I’d stayed. And now, here I was, standing in this same café, my hands clenched around my coffee, my throat raw with the weight of what I’d chosen to keep.

The novel would wait. The world would turn. But the girl at the counter? She’d get her chance to walk away. And for the first time, I wouldn’t be there to stop her.
