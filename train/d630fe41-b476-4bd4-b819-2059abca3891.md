The contract was signed in ink, yet the terms were never clearly articulated—only vague assurances exchanged in whispered agreements over a firelit table. *"You’ll receive the funds by the end of the month."* The recipient, who had never held such a promise before, assumed it meant a financial settlement. The sender, who had only meant a symbolic gesture, did not foresee the legal implications of the miscommunication.

Letters arrived late, addressed to the wrong party, and the intended beneficiary—now an estranged creditor—sued, claiming breach of contract. The original promise dissolved in legal proceedings, its original meaning lost in translation. A promise, once given, can twist like a river, carving new paths in the minds of those who read it.
