The third Tuesday in January always felt like a curse. The air smelled of damp wool and old books, the kind that clung to the spine of forgotten things. I sat at the kitchen table, coffee cold between my fingers, staring at the calendar pinned above the sink. The same pattern: the *third* Tuesday, the way the light slanted just so through the window, catching the edge of the curtains like a ghostly outline. The first Tuesday had been about the mail arriving, the second Tuesday about the neighbor’s dog barking at the same old tree. But this one? This one was about the *way the coffee tasted*—bitter, like the first sip of a promise you’d forgotten to keep.

Outside, the clock struck eleven, but it didn’t sound right. The seconds dragged like sand through a glass, each one a whisper of something I’d already seen. The pattern wasn’t just in the days—it was in the cracks between them, the ones that let the world bleed into the quiet. I thought about the last three Tuesdays, how the same things had happened, how the same mistakes had been made, how the same shadows had stretched across the floor like old debts. The pattern wasn’t just in the numbers. It was in the way the world remembered me.
