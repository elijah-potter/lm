I remember the first time I saw the stars through a telescope, their twinkling not just in the sky but in the quiet corners of my childhood bedroom. The way light bent through the lens, the cosmos condensed into a single, breathless moment—it was as if time itself paused to let me glimpse what lay beyond.

I remember the weight of a pen in my hand, the ink bleeding across the page as if resisting the shape I wanted it to take. I remember the hum of the radio, static hissing into words that didn’t belong, and the way my father’s voice—so steady, so familiar—would cut through the noise, saying, *"Just keep writing."*

I remember the taste of salt on my tongue after the ocean had swallowed me whole, the way the horizon blurred at the edges of my vision, and the moment I realized the sea wasn’t just water but a promise, endless and untamed. I remember the first time I held a book that wasn’t mine, its pages filled with stories I’d never lived, and the way my fingers curled around it as if it could hold me back from the vast, unreadable world beyond.

I remember the way silence could feel like a physical thing, pressing against the walls of my mind, and the sound of my own breath, a whisper I could barely hear. I remember the fear that coiled in my stomach before a first kiss, the way my pulse hammered like a drum in my ribs, and the moment the other person’s lips touched mine, and the world stopped being a question.

I remember the library at dusk, the scent of paper and old books clashing with the sharp tang of the city outside, and the way the librarian’s smile felt like a secret. I remember the way my hands trembled when I first tried to write, the ink smudged like a child’s, and the way my mother ruffled my hair, saying, *"You’re going to be a writer, you know."*

I remember the first time I lost something—my childhood home, my innocence, the way the world stretched out like a map I couldn’t read. I remember the way my hands shook when I wrote the words *"I don’t know"* for the first time, and the way the paper felt heavy, as if it could absorb the weight of all the things I didn’t dare say.

I remember the way the light caught the cracks in the ceiling, turning them into stars, and the way my heart felt like it might burst from the way it wanted to see everything at once. I remember the first time I cried because I was tired of pretending, and the way the tears came in a flood, raw and honest. I remember the way my voice cracked when I read aloud, the way the words felt like they were being torn from me, and the way the audience—just a few people sitting in a dim room—stilled, as if listening to something sacred.

I remember the way the night sky looked different when I was young, all those constellations I could make up stories for, and the way they faded into something more real, something vast and indifferent. I remember the first time I heard the word *"poetry"* and thought it might mean something else entirely, something wild and untamed. I remember the way my fingers traced the lines of a poem I’d written, the way the words curled around each other like vines, and the way I wanted to keep writing, even when the paper felt too small to hold it all.

I think of the words I’ve written and the words I’ve left unspoken, the ones that sat in my head like stones I couldn’t move. I think of the way the past is a language I sometimes forget how to speak, and the way the present feels like a page I’m still learning to read. I think of the way the future is both a promise and a question mark, and the way I don’t know which one I’m afraid of most.

I think of the light that never goes out, the way the world keeps spinning even when I want to stop, and the way I might not be ready to write the story I’m meant to tell yet. I think of the hands that shaped me, the ones that will shape me next, and the way I don’t know if I’ll ever understand what it means to be a story at all.

I think of the stars, still there, watching. And the way I remember them, and the way I forget them, and the way I wonder if I’ll ever learn to love them again.

I think.
