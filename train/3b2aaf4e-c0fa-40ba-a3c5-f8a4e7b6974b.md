A cold, fluorescent-lit room hummed with the distant drip of pipes, the only sound. The air smelled of stale coffee and something metallic—old blood, maybe, or just the faint tang of despair. A single chair creaked under the weight of an observer, its back facing the wall. The screen in the corner flickered, displaying a single line of text:

**"Silence."**

A pause. The weight of the moment stretched, unbending, like steel. Then, from the mouth of the figure at the center of the room—someone who had sat there for years—came not a word, but the sound of a breath, shallow and broken. The observer exhaled, too, the breath curling into the silence like smoke. The screen blinked out.

Outside, a train whistle wailed into the night. The sound echoed, then faded, swallowed by the void between stations.
