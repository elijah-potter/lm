In the historic district of **Blackthorn Vale**, residents woke to a rare and unsettling phenomenon: the streets, usually lined with cobblestones, were now covered in a thick, blackened sludge. Officials initially dismissed it as a severe storm or industrial spill, but when the sun rose, the sludge began to hiss and bubble, emitting a faint, metallic scent. By mid-morning, the city’s central clock tower, a symbol of Blackthorn’s resilience, had been found slightly tilted, its hands frozen at 3:17 AM.

Local historians speculate this may correlate with the "Silent Night" event from 1892, when the town’s old clock tower mysteriously stopped working for three days. The city council has declared a curfew and activated emergency response teams, though no cause has been confirmed. Authorities are urging residents to avoid the sludge-covered areas, which now extend from the riverfront to the old market square. Meanwhile, whispers of "the Hollow Watch" persist among older residents, describing a spectral figure seen near the tower during the event—one that appears to be *moving* without sound.

The mayor has appealed for quiet, warning that "unexplained disturbances" have been reported near the clock’s foundations. Schools are closed, and all non-essential businesses have suspended operations. The only official statement so far: *"We are investigating."* The city’s old newspaper, *The Blackthorn Echo*, has gone dark overnight, its final print run mysteriously stolen from its presses.
