## Reclaiming Resilience: Transforming Oakhaven Ravine into a Climate-Ready Community Oasis

**Executive Summary**

The City of Meadowbrook faces escalating challenges from increasingly frequent and intense storm events and rising summer temperatures. Oakhaven Ravine, a currently underutilized and often-overlooked public space bordering the Elmwood and Crestwood neighborhoods, presents a unique opportunity to mitigate these impacts while simultaneously fostering community cohesion and enhancing quality of life. This proposal outlines a phased plan to transform Oakhaven Ravine into a climate-resilient community asset through strategic ecological restoration, stormwater management infrastructure, and accessible recreational amenities.  The project, spearheaded by the Friends of Oakhaven Ravine in partnership with the Meadowbrook Parks Department and local environmental consulting firm, TerraFlora Solutions, will demonstrably improve flood resilience, reduce urban heat island effect, enhance biodiversity, and provide a vibrant, accessible green space for all residents.  We request $150,000 in municipal funding, matched by $50,000 in private donations and in-kind labor contributions, to initiate this vital project.  The resulting oasis will serve as a model for sustainable urban development across Meadowbrook.

**Project Plan & Timeline**

The project is divided into three distinct phases, each with specific deliverables and responsible partners.

**Phase 1: Assessment & Design (Months 1-3)**

*   **Responsible Partners:** TerraFlora Solutions (lead), Friends of Oakhaven Ravine, Meadowbrook Parks Department.
*   **Deliverables:** Detailed site assessment report including hydrological analysis, soil testing, and biodiversity survey. Preliminary design plans incorporating native plantings, bioswales, and permeable paving. Community design workshop report summarizing resident feedback.
*   **Milestones:** Completion of baseline data collection (Month 1).  Presentation of preliminary design concepts to the community (Month 2). Finalization of design plans incorporating community feedback (Month 3).

**Phase 2: Restoration & Infrastructure (Months 4-9)**

*   **Responsible Partners:** Meadowbrook Parks Department (lead), TerraFlora Solutions (technical guidance), Volunteer Corps of Meadowbrook (labor support).
*   **Deliverables:** Installation of bioswales and rain gardens to manage stormwater runoff.  Re-establishment of native riparian vegetation.  Construction of a short, accessible walking trail with permeable paving.  Removal of invasive species and debris.
*   **Milestones:** Invasive species removal completed (Month 4).  Bioswale and rain garden construction finalized (Month 6).  Native planting completed (Month 7).  Trail construction completed (Month 8).  Site stabilization and erosion control measures implemented (Month 9).

**Phase 3: Community Engagement & Education (Months 10-12)**

*   **Responsible Partners:** Friends of Oakhaven Ravine (lead), Meadowbrook Recreation Department, local schools.
*   **Deliverables:**  Creation and installation of interpretive signage detailing the project's ecological benefits and stormwater management techniques.  Organization of three community workshops on native plant identification and sustainable gardening practices.  Development of a website and social media presence to promote the ravine and its ecological significance.  Establishment of a volunteer stewardship program for ongoing maintenance.
*   **Milestones:** Signage installation completed (Month 10).  Community workshops completed (Month 11). Website and social media platform launched (Month 11). Volunteer stewardship program recruitment finalized (Month 12).



**Budget**

The total project cost is estimated at $200,000. We request $150,000 in municipal funding, which will be supplemented by $50,000 in private donations and in-kind labor contributions.

*   **TerraFlora Solutions (Consulting & Technical Support):** $65,000 (Includes site assessment, design review, construction oversight, and post-project monitoring)
*   **Native Plants & Soil Amendments:** $30,000 (Purchasing native trees, shrubs, grasses, and soil amendments to promote healthy plant growth and ecosystem resilience.)
*   **Construction Materials (Bioswale liners, permeable paving, gravel):** $40,000 (Covers the cost of materials needed for bioswale construction, trail development, and other infrastructure improvements.)
*   **Tools & Equipment Rental:** $5,000 (Covers the cost of renting specialized equipment for invasive species removal, planting, and trail construction.)
*   **Signage & Interpretive Materials:** $5,000 (Production and installation of interpretive signage to educate the community about the ravine’s ecological significance.)
*   **Community Engagement & Education (Workshop materials, website development):** $5,000 (Supports community workshops and the creation of online resources.)

A contingency fund of $10,000 (5% of the total budget) is included to address unforeseen expenses or cost overruns.  Private donations will be directed towards the purchase of native plants and the development of educational materials.  The Volunteer Corps of Meadowbrook will contribute approximately 500 hours of labor, valued at $10,000, towards invasive species removal and planting activities.



**Community Engagement, Equity Impacts & Post-Grant Sustainability**

Community engagement has been central to the project’s development.  Initial community meetings identified key concerns and priorities, informing the design and implementation plan.  Future engagement will focus on volunteer recruitment and stewardship activities. The project prioritizes equitable access, ensuring the trail is accessible to individuals with disabilities and located near underserved neighborhoods.  The selection of native plants will prioritize species that benefit local pollinators and wildlife, enhancing biodiversity.  Post-grant sustainability will be achieved through the establishment of a dedicated volunteer stewardship program, ongoing partnerships with local organizations, and integration of the ravine into the Meadowbrook Parks Department’s long-term maintenance plan.  The interpretive signage will serve as a permanent reminder of the project's ecological benefits and encourage continued community stewardship.

**Risk Mitigation**

Potential risks include unexpected site conditions, weather delays, and volunteer attrition.  A thorough site assessment will minimize the impact of unexpected conditions.  A flexible project schedule will allow for adjustments due to weather delays.  A robust volunteer recruitment strategy and ongoing recognition program will mitigate the risk of volunteer attrition.  The contingency fund will provide a financial buffer to address unforeseen challenges.

This project will not only mitigate the impacts of climate change but also foster community cohesion and create a vibrant, accessible green space for all.
