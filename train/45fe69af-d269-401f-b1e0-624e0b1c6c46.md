The café’s morning hum was soft, like the whisper of coffee grounds settling into a cup. Across from me, **Ethan** sat with his usual half-smile, the one that never quite reached his eyes, the one that made me wonder what he was hiding.

*"You know, the world’s full of mysteries, and some of them are just… boring."* He repeated it like a mantra, the same words he’d said every Tuesday for the past three months.

I leaned forward, my fingers tracing the rim of my latte. *"What’s the point of mysteries if they’re just boring?"*

Ethan’s smile didn’t waver. *"Because the boring parts are the ones you remember. Like the way the light hits the coffee beans at dawn."* He paused, then repeated, *"The boring parts are the ones you remember."* The café clock ticked. Time stretched like syrup.

Outside, the streetlights flickered to life. Somewhere, a siren wailed. Ethan’s voice softened. *"Some mysteries are worth solving. Others… just remind you why you’re alive."* He took a sip, then repeated, *"The boring parts are the ones you remember."* His eyes met mine, but I didn’t look away. Not this time.
