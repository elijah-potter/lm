A flickering neon glow in the corner of the room pulsed violently before stabilizing into a cold blue hue. Shadows stretched unnaturally long across the floor, their edges sharpening into jagged lines as static hissed across the TV screen. The air smelled like ozone and damp wool, the sudden brightness making everything glow—eyes, skin, the walls like they were covered in a thin, translucent film. The streetlights outside blinked erratically, their light bleeding into the alley, casting everything in eerie, fractured light. A distant car horn honked, its sound swallowed by the sudden silence before the hum of fluorescent tubes returned, steady and warm.
