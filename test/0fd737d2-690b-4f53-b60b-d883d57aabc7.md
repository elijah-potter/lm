The last day of summer had been a blur for twelve-year-old Mia Carter, who spent her mornings chasing squirrels and afternoons playing hide-and-seek with her best friend, Jake. But today, something was different. Her mom had called her into the kitchen, her voice tense.

"Mia, come here," her mother said, setting a small box on the table. Inside was a newspaper clipping—an article about a new school starting in the fall, one that promised "a place where kids like you belong."

Mia’s heart raced. This wasn’t just any school. It was *her* school.

The next morning, Mia stood in the rain as she walked to the bus stop. The bus driver, an older woman with a kind smile, handed her a form. *"Welcome to Riverdale Academy,"* it read. *"You’ve been chosen."*

Inside, the halls were echoing with whispers and laughter. Some kids looked at her like she was a ghost—until she met Leo, a quiet boy with sharp eyes who kept his hands in his pockets. *"You’re new,"* he said, voice low. *"What’s your story?"*

Mia hesitated, then told him about her dad’s job transfers, about never fitting in. Leo’s expression softened. *"We all have stories,"* he said. *"Some just need to be heard."*

That night, as Mia sat in her new classroom, she noticed something strange: the walls were lined with posters of famous scientists, artists, and inventors—people who had changed the world. The teacher, Ms. Rivera, stood at the front, her hands clasped. *"Today,"* she said, *"we start something new."*

The next day, Mia found a hidden notebook in her locker—pages filled with sketches of inventions, theories about space, even a rough draft of a song. The name at the bottom was *Leo.*

That was when Mia realized: Riverdale wasn’t just a school. It was a challenge. And she was ready to take it.
