## A Counseling Session

**Setting:** A warmly lit, comfortable therapy room.

**Characters:**

*   **Dr. Eleanor Vance:** A seasoned therapist, known for her calm demeanor and insightful approach.
*   **Liam Walker:** A young professional experiencing significant stress and difficulty sleeping.

---

**Liam:** (Sitting slightly hunched in his chair, fidgeting with his hands) I just… I don't know where to start, really. Everything feels like a weight.

**Dr. Vance:** (Leaning forward slightly, maintaining gentle eye contact) It takes courage to acknowledge that feeling, Liam. Please, take your time. There’s no rush. Tell me a little about what "a weight" feels like for you.

**Liam:** It’s just… constant. The pressure. I’m always thinking about work, about deadlines, about whether I’m doing enough. Then, when I try to switch off, my mind just races. I’m lying in bed, staring at the ceiling, replaying conversations, worrying about things I can’t control.

**Dr. Vance:** So, you're experiencing a persistent sense of pressure and difficulty disconnecting, even when you’re trying to rest. It sounds incredibly draining. You mentioned replaying conversations and worrying about things outside your control. Can you give me a specific example of a recent situation that triggered this feeling?

**Liam:** Last week, I had a presentation to the board. It went reasonably well, I think. But afterwards, I kept thinking about a question one of the directors asked. I second-guessed my answer for hours. I'm probably overreacting, but it felt like my entire career hung in the balance.

**Dr. Vance:** (Nodding slowly) It’s understandable that a presentation to the board would feel significant. It’s natural to want to perform well and be perceived positively. But it sounds like you’re engaging in a pattern of self-criticism and catastrophizing. You’re taking that one question and interpreting it as a potential indicator of failure.

**Liam:** I suppose so. It’s just… I feel like I *have* to be perfect. There’s so much riding on my performance.

**Dr. Vance:** Where do you think that pressure to be perfect originates? Have you always felt this way, or is it more recent?

**Liam:** (Pauses, considering) I think… I think it started in university. There was a lot of competition, and I felt like I always had to be the best to stand out. My parents always encouraged me to excel, which I appreciate, but it also felt like a lot of pressure.

**Dr. Vance:** So, a combination of external expectations and internal drive has contributed to this feeling of needing to be perfect. It's important to recognize that striving for excellence is admirable, but perfection is an unattainable standard. It’s a trap that can lead to significant stress and anxiety. We can explore some techniques to challenge those thought patterns and develop a more balanced perspective.

**Liam:** That sounds… helpful. I don’t know how to stop the worrying. It’s just always *there*.

**Dr. Vance:** It takes practice, Liam, and it won’t happen overnight. We can work on mindfulness exercises to help you become more aware of your thoughts without judgment. We can also examine the underlying beliefs that contribute to your anxiety and develop strategies to manage them. For example, let's explore the thought, "I have to be perfect." What evidence do you have that supports this belief? And what evidence contradicts it?

**Liam:** (Sighs) I don't know. I guess… I’ve never really questioned it before.

**Dr. Vance:** That’s perfectly alright. That’s precisely what we’re here to do. It’s a process of self-discovery. For next week, I’d like you to keep a brief journal, noting down instances where you experience this pressure to be perfect, and the thoughts that accompany it. We’ll use that as a starting point for our next session. Does that sound manageable?

**Liam:** Yes, that does. Thank you. It’s… it’s good to talk about it.

**Dr. Vance:** You’re very welcome, Liam. Remember, acknowledging the challenge is the first step toward finding a path forward. And I'm here to support you every step of the way.
