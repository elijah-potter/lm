Unspoken rules govern the alleyway behind the bakery, where the scent of fresh bread lingers. The pavement cracks form a natural grid, guiding the path—those who stray miss the hidden bench where the old man sits, his knitting needles weaving through the air like a second language. The lamppost hums a low, rhythmic pulse, marking the boundary where the street ends. A door, slightly ajar, reveals a staircase descending into a dimly lit space where the air carries the weight of old decisions. No signposts, only the way the shadows fall—long where the rules are understood, short where they’re forgotten. The wind carries the sound of a clock striking midnight, but the time isn’t marked; it’s implied by the way the streetlights flicker to life. Here, the rules are in the pauses, the glances exchanged, the unspoken agreements that shape the flow of life.
