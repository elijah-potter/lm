A biologist called Peter Dijkstra has done a study into a colourful fish called the African cichlid fish. This fish lives in Central Africa, but some people have it in their aquariums.

The African cichlid fish keeps their babies in their mouths after they are born. The mother's mouth is like a bird's nest or a fox's den. After the eggs hatch into tiny baby fish, the baby fish are vulnerable and they need a safe place to live, so they don't get attacked by predators. The babies live in their mum's mouths for two weeks. This is called mouthbrooding.

The baby fish swim outside their mother’s mouth, but if there is any danger they go back in and hide. It is a great start for the baby fish. However, it is not good for the mothers. It makes the mother fish very tired. In fact, they can't breathe well with the babies in their mouths. They can't eat because their mouth is full of their babies and eggs. This is why they sometimes eat some of their eggs and babies. They sometimes eat more than three-quarters of their own eggs and baby fish. 

Scientists are very surprised about this.
