In a dimly lit, abandoned factory at midnight, a lone figure—clad in a patched-up leather jacket—stands on a crumbling factory roof. The wind howls through rusted pipes, carrying the scent of old oil and damp concrete. Below, a flickering neon sign flickers weakly: *"BETTER THAN THIS."* The figure grips a rusted wrench, their knuckles white. A child’s laughter echoes from the ground floor, muffled but unmistakable. Inside, a small, unheated room holds a single chair, a half-empty coffee cup, and a scrapbook with faded photos—one of a man in a suit, another of a woman in a white dress, and a third of the same child, smiling.

The man—now older, gaunt—stares at the scrapbook. He was once a promise. A lawyer, a provider, a man who swore he’d protect her. But the factory, the debt, the time stolen into silence—it all took its toll. Now, he watches the child play in the shadows, unaware of the weight of his past. A shadow stretches across the roof—too long, too deliberate. The child stops playing. The figure exhales, the wrench trembling in their grip. The neon sign glitches, then goes dark. Silence. The wind carries the ghost of a promise left unkept.

The child looks up, eyes wide. The figure turns away, but the child is already running. The door behind the figure creaks open.
