In the dim glow of a flickering holographic archive, an elderly woman adjusts her glasses, scanning a projected timeline of fragmented data. The screen flickers with static before stabilizing into a series of abstract nodes, each representing a potential future—some bleak, others flickering with tentative hope.

A group of young people huddles around a communal terminal, their fingers dancing over touchpads as they debate algorithms. A robed figure stands at the edge, arms crossed, their voice echoing through a static-filled channel: *"The systems predict a divergence. Some paths lead to collapse, others to adaptation."* A child’s voice cuts through the murmur: *"What if we don’t choose?"*

The air hums with the weight of uncertainty. A distant siren wails—another city, another war, another cycle of the same. The woman exhales slowly, her fingers tracing a path on a worn map. *"We don’t know yet,"* she murmurs. *"But the past is already written."*
