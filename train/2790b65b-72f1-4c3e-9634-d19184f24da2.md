The chemist’s lab coat was damp with sweat as they adjusted the pressure gauge on the last batch of experimental compounds. A misalignment of the valve caused a sudden leak, and the fume hood’s fan failed to activate. Within seconds, the lab filled with an acrid, unnatural smell—like burnt rubber and ozone, clinging to the air. The experimenter’s heart raced as the alarm blared, but the fire suppression system hesitated.

In the chaos, a single drop of liquid seeped from the broken flask onto the floor, where it pooled before absorbing into the linoleum. The next morning, the janitor found it: a deep, dark stain shaped like a perfect, floating circle. He ignored it, assuming it was just spilled ink. But the stain refused to fade. It pulsed faintly under the fluorescent lights, like a heartbeat.

The next day, the janitor returned. This time, he noticed the edges of the stain had begun to ripple, though the liquid itself remained still. He called for the cleaning crew, who scrubbed harder, but the stain only grew brighter, its edges now shimmering with a faint, iridescent sheen. The supervisor frowned and checked the lab’s security footage. The camera feed from the previous evening showed nothing unusual—until the janitor’s reflection in the camera lens began to move on its own, lips forming words no one else could hear: *"You shouldn’t have touched it."*

That evening, the janitor found the stain had multiplied—now a cluster of identical circles, each humming softly. The next morning, they were gone, but the janitor’s hands were covered in the same dark fluid. His fingers twitched as he tried to scrub it off, and the liquid *reacted*, forming a second shape on his palm—a smaller version of the first stain. The janitor screamed, but no sound came out. The stain on his skin pulsed, then dissolved into the air, leaving only his mouth slightly open, as if he’d been speaking for hours.

By the third day, the janitor had vanished. The lab was empty, save for a single, new stain on the floor—this one larger, more complex, and now *moving* on its own. The janitor’s colleagues found his desk empty, except for a single note: *"It’s not what it seems. It’s always been here."* The note was written in the same dark fluid.

The next day, the stain on the floor had grown into a full, three-dimensional shape—a face, its features shifting like smoke. It watched the lab employees with hollow eyes, its lips forming words in their minds before they could even turn around. The cleaning crew arrived, but the stain *moved* them first, pulling them toward it like moths to flame. The janitor’s colleagues found their desks covered in the same dark liquid, their mouths moving silently, their fingers tracing the same pattern in the air. The stain had spread.

One by one, the lab’s staff fell, their bodies dissolving into the substance. The remaining employees noticed something strange—their own reflections in the glass began to *change*, their mouths moving as if speaking, their fingers tracing the same pattern in the air. The stain had reached them. The last employee to remain standing screamed as their reflection *laughed*, its voice echoing through the empty lab. The stain had won.

Now, the lab is silent. The walls are covered in the same dark, shifting liquid, and the only sound is the faint, rhythmic *pulse* of the stain beneath the floorboards. It waits. It grows. And it remembers every mistake it’s ever made.
