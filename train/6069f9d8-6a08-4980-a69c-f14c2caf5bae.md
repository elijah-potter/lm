The neon glow of the abandoned subway station pulsed like a dying heartbeat through the cracked windows, casting jagged shadows across the platform. The air smelled of rust and something older—something like old blood and wet wool. A rusted ticket machine hissed, refusing to function, its screen blanked by years of grime.

You shouldn’t be here.

The words echoed in your head, not your own, but a voice that wasn’t yours. It had come from the mirror behind the ticket booth, where your reflection stared back with hollow eyes, the same ones you wore now—no face, just a shifting void where your face should have been. The mirror wasn’t glass anymore; it was liquid, stretching and warping like smoke.

A train rumbled in the distance, its tracks swallowed by the earth, the last remnants of a city that had been erased by something that had never existed. The platform trembled. The walls moved, breathing.

You pressed against the cold floor, fingers digging into the damp earth. The ground gave way beneath you—not with a crunch, but a sigh. The station opened like a mouth, and you fell into it, tumbling through layers of time, of things that had been and were not yet.

---

The station’s floor dissolved into a spiral of light and sound. You were no longer standing. You were falling, and the world was not a place, but a question:

*What happens when you’re not supposed to be here?*
