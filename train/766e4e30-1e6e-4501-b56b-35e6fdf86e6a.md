The first time I experienced a routine, it was a Tuesday morning in the year 2004. I was seventeen, sitting in my dorm room in the university’s basement. The fluorescent lights buzzed overhead, casting a sickly yellow glow. I had just finished my first term of classes, and the weight of being a college student settled over me like a second skin.

My alarm clock—an ancient, clattering model from 1996—rang at 6:15 AM. I groaned, rolled over, and groaned again. The room was too quiet, too still. I had spent the last two months drowning in coffee and half-hearted attempts to find rhythm, but this was different. This was the first time I felt like I was being *pushed* rather than *pulling* myself together. The alarm went off again at 6:25, and I threw my pillow at the wall, missing by a hair.

I stumbled downstairs, my sneakers squeaking against the linoleum. The kitchen was empty except for the microwave, which was already running, heating up a bowl of cereal I had prepped the night before. The microwave beeped as it finished, and I grabbed my bowl, adding a splash of milk. I took a bite and realized I was starving, but my stomach had already started growling by the time I finished.

By 7:30 AM, I was at the library, hunched over a desk with a stack of textbooks. The first lecture of the day was on the schedule, and I had to arrive early to find a seat. I had spent last night trying to cram notes into my brain, but my mind kept wandering—imagining what it would be like to skip class, to just sit in the café and people-watch until the time ran out. But I had a test tomorrow, and I *had* to pass.

The routine unfolded like this:
- **Morning:** Wake up, coffee, breakfast.
- **7:30 AM:** Arrive at the library, start reading.
- **8:30 AM:** Lecture begins. Notes taken furiously, questions scribbled down.
- **10:00 AM:** Break time. Coffee refill, a quick walk outside to stretch my legs.
- **10:30 AM:** Lab or extra class. Experiments, group projects, or lectures that demanded active participation.
- **12:30 PM:** Lunch. A sandwich from the cafeteria, maybe a few friends over to chat.
- **2:00 PM:** Study session. Textbooks open, highlighters in hand, coffee in one hand, phone in the other for last-minute notes.
- **5:00 PM:** Home. Dinner, maybe a quick game of solitaire or a podcast to unwind.
- **7:00 PM:** Bedtime routine. Brush teeth, skincare, a quick glance at the day’s to-do list.

The first time it clicked was during my second week of classes. I realized that if I stuck to the routine, the chaos of uncertainty would disappear. It wasn’t about being perfect—it was about being *consistent*. Missing a study session one day didn’t ruin anything. Skipping breakfast once didn’t make me fail. The routine was a safety net, a way to make the chaos feel manageable.

I didn’t call it *routine* back then. I just called it *survival*. But by the end of that first semester, I had found a rhythm. It wasn’t about discipline—it was about *reliability*. And that was the first time I understood that even the most mundane things could become a kind of magic if you knew how to use them.
