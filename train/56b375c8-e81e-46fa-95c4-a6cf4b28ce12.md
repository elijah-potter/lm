**Character: Elias Veyne**, a former scholar turned rogue, now bound by a debt that consumes his mind.

The fire in the hearth crackled low, casting long shadows across the cramped study. Elias sat hunched over his ledger, fingers tracing numbers that no longer made sense. The ink smudged on the page as his vision blurred. Outside, the wind howled against the old window panes, but his ears were deafened by the whisper of his own thoughts.

*"You owe me,"* the voice in his head murmured. *"Not the debtors. Me."*

He exhaled sharply, the breath fogging the glass. His reflection in the mirror above the desk stared back—not at the scholar who once dissected texts, but at a hollow-eyed stranger. The debt wasn’t for coin or blood, but for *himself*. For the man who had abandoned his family, his purpose, the last truth he’d ever believed.

The door creaked open. His debtors stood there—two figures in borrowed cloaks, their faces obscured. One held a sealed letter, the other a knife. Elias didn’t look up.

*"You’re running out of time,"* the debtors said, their words thick with false pity.

He met their gaze. The knife was a threat, but the letter was worse. It was proof. Proof that the scholar he’d been had *failed*. Proof that the man who sat before them was already dead.

Elias closed his eyes. The weight of the ledger, the debt, the weight of his own guilt pressed down like a tombstone. He opened them again, but his hands trembled—not with fear, but with the slow, creeping understanding that he had already been lost for years.

The debtors watched him. Somewhere, a clock ticked. Somewhere, a life ended.

He reached for the knife.
