It was during the sacred time of Christmas that I, the tree, had a dream. Somehow, I sensed the arrival of the festive season, and in my dream, I imagined hearing the bells chiming from the nearby churches. Yet, it felt like a beautiful summer's day—mild, warm, and serene. Adorned with fresh green foliage, my mighty crown was basked in sunlight, as the playful rays danced among my leaves and branches. The air carried the sweet fragrance of herbs and blossoms, while colorful butterflies fluttered around, and summer flies joyfully danced. It was as if the whole world existed solely for their merry revelries.

In this dream, I witnessed the memories of my entire life unfold before me, like a grand procession celebrating my existence. I saw the noble knights and gracious ladies of old, riding through the woods on their majestic steeds, with plumes swaying in their hats and falcons perched on their wrists. The sound of hunting horns and barking dogs filled the air. Hostile warriors appeared in vibrant attire and shimmering armor, pitching and striking their tents with spears and halberds. Once again, watchfires blazed, and people sang and slept under my hospitable shelter.

Under the moonlight, lovers found solace and happiness near me, carving their initials into my aged trunk. Although years had passed, their guitars and Eolian harps, once hung on my branches by merry travelers, resonated once more, enchanting me with their marvelous melodies. The cooing of wood pigeons seemed to convey the tree's emotions, while the cuckoo counted the remaining days of my summers. It was as if new life surged through every fiber of my roots, stems, and leaves, reaching even the highest branches. I stretched and expanded, invigorated by the warm vitality coursing through my underground roots.

With each growth spurt, my topmost boughs widened and became fuller. Alongside my growth, a sense of self-satisfaction swelled within me, fueling a joyous yearning to ascend even higher and reach the warm, radiant sun itself. My uppermost branches pierced the clouds, which floated beneath like migrating flocks of birds or graceful white swans. Every leaf seemed to possess sight as if it had eyes to behold. Even in broad daylight, the stars shone brightly, resembling gentle, clear eyes. They reminded me of the familiar gaze of a child or lovers who once met beneath my sheltering branches. These moments were pure wonder and bliss, brimming with tranquility and delight.

Yet, amidst this happiness, a deep longing stirred within me—a desire for all the other trees, bushes, herbs, and flowers beneath me to rise as I had, to witness this grandeur and share in the same joyous experience.
