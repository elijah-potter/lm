AITA for insisting my sister return a borrowed wedding dress?

Okay, this is a bit complicated, and I’m honestly stressed about it. My name is Eleanor, and I’m 32. My sister, Beatrice (30), got married on June 12, 2021, at a vineyard in Napa Valley. I was her maid of honor, of course. The dress she wore – a stunning, ivory A-line with delicate beading – was a vintage find she’s always raved about. It cost her around $3,000, including alterations. 

After the wedding, Beatrice, in a moment of what I now see as impulsive generosity, offered the dress to me. She said, "You deserve something beautiful too, Ellie. Consider it a sisterly gift." I was touched, absolutely. But I also politely declined. I explained that I wasn't currently in a relationship and didn't have an immediate need for a wedding dress, and that I’m also a bit of a minimalist and didn’t want to store something so substantial. She insisted, saying she wouldn’t mind if I kept it. I eventually relented, feeling pressured, and said I'd keep it safe.

Fast forward to last October. Beatrice and her husband, David, decided to renew their vows in Charleston, South Carolina, on October 27th. David is a history professor, and Charleston held particular significance for him. Beatrice started talking about wanting to wear a new dress for the renewal, something "more modern" than her original gown. I didn't think much of it. 

Then, last week, on April 18th, I received a very casual text from Beatrice: "Hey, can I borrow your wedding dress for the Charleston renewal? It would be so perfect for the vibe." I was taken aback. I replied, “Beatrice, I’m really not comfortable with that. I’m still storing it, and I’m not sure it’s a good idea to lend it out.” Her response was immediate and, frankly, a little aggressive: "Seriously? You’re being ridiculous. It's *my* dress, and you’re just hoarding it. I’m going to wear it regardless."

I tried to explain that it’s not about her wearing it, but about the potential for damage and the fact that I never intended to be a long-term lender. I reminded her that I had stored it carefully, and lending it out now felt irresponsible. David got involved via text then, saying I was being “unreasonable” and “selfish” for not letting her wear something she once owned. He claimed I was holding onto the dress to be spiteful. 

I’m now feeling incredibly guilty. I feel like I’m being an awful sister, especially since she seems to believe it's rightfully hers. My parents have weighed in, and they’re telling me to just let her have it; they say I’m making a mountain out of a molehill. They’re suggesting I’m being overly attached to something that isn’t even mine anymore. I’d genuinely like to maintain a good relationship with my sister and her husband, but I also feel strongly that I shouldn’t be pressured into something I’m uncomfortable with. I’m worried that if I refuse, it will irreparably damage our relationship, but I also feel a sense of principle about this. This whole situation feels incredibly unfair. ESH, honestly.

So, AITA for refusing to let my sister borrow the wedding dress she gave to me?
