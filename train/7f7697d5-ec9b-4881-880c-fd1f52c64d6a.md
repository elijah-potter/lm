In dim, flickering light from an old lantern, a figure crouches beside a cracked wooden table, fingers tracing the grain of an empty chair. Their breath fogs the air, a whisper of frustration mingling with the scent of damp wool and stale tea. A pocket watch, once ticking steadily, now lies face-down in a bowl of water, its hands frozen at 3:17. The room hums with an unnatural silence, broken only by the distant *clink* of a glass—another attempt, another failure. The intangible they seek isn’t in the physical space, but in the cracks between the walls, the hollows of memory, the space between the lines of a love letter left behind on a desk. Their shadow stretches long and uneven, stretching toward the ceiling like a hand reaching for nothing. Outside, a single streetlamp casts a sickly yellow glow over the cobblestones, reflecting in puddles that mirror nothing at all.
