## Explain Like I'm Five: Why the Sky is Blue

Okay, let's talk about why the sky looks blue! It's a really cool question, and it has a pretty neat answer.

Imagine you have a big box of crayons. Let’s say you have red, orange, yellow, green, blue, and purple. Now, imagine you shine a flashlight through that box of crayons. What happens?

You see all the colors, right? Each color is bouncing off the crayons and coming out the other side.

Sunlight is kind of like that flashlight, and the air all around us is like our box of crayons. Sunlight isn’t just one color; it's made up of *all* the colors of the rainbow!  Red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, and violet – they’re all mixed together to make white light.

Now, here’s the important part. When sunlight comes down to Earth, it bumps into tiny bits of air.  These bits of air are so small you can’t even see them!  

Blue and violet light are like little bouncy balls. They bounce around *much* more than the other colors, like red and orange, which are more like bowling balls.  The bouncy blue light bounces all over the place, spreading it out across the whole sky. 

That’s why when you look up, you see blue!  It's because the blue light is bouncing everywhere.

Sometimes, when the sun is setting, like when it's almost bedtime, the sunlight has to travel through *more* air to reach us. Because it has to travel so far, the blue light gets bounced away even more, and we start to see the other colors – the reds and oranges – that were there all along! That's why sunsets are so beautiful and colorful.

So, the sky is blue because blue light is bouncy and likes to spread out! Isn't that neat?
