Two scientists in a dimly lit lab exchange glances over a half-empty coffee cup. The first, a skeptical physicist, traces equations on a whiteboard with a marker that’s nearly gone. The second, a philosopher, scribbles in the margins with a pencil that’s been sharpened too many times.

The physicist’s latest paper—*"Quantum entanglement violates Bell’s inequality in macroscopic systems"*—hangs between them. The philosopher’s notes read: *"The universe’s fundamental laws are not fixed; they are emergent from a deeper, unknowable order."*

The physicist taps the paper: *"Your interpretation assumes a deterministic universe. That’s just a model."*
The philosopher smiles, unfazed: *"But isn’t every model just a story we tell ourselves to make sense of the noise?"*

Outside, a distant siren wails—another experiment gone wrong, another discovery buried in bureaucracy. The physicist sighs, wiping the whiteboard clean. The philosopher picks up their pencil, sharpening it for the next question.
