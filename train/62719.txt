Rarely does a method of power generation end up being both functional and beautiful. In the case of this spherical glass lens from German architect André Broessel, both those qualities are at work. His ball lenses manage to be 35% more efficient than photovoltaic solar panels when used for power generation.
A traditional solar panel needs, at the very least, to be mounted facing south. This way it catches the most light throughout the day. Ideally, you’d want an optical tracking system that could angle the panels at the sun as it tracks its way across the sky. This is expensive, and imprecise at best. The spherical solar power collectors have no such problem.
Broessel’s ball lenses are either solid glass, or water-filled. They take advantage of the natural optics of spheres by concentrating even diffuse light into a tight ray. By placing a movable solar collector at the focal point, you can pull in a lot of power. Optical tracking would be employed here as well, but just for keeping the solar collector at the right spot. Since it’s just a big glass sphere, it can also be left out in inclement weather without fear of damage.
What’s the catch, you ask? Well, a photovoltaic panel weighs in at a few pounds per square-foot. A large ball lens like this would weigh a few hundred pounds and be harder to transport. These aren’t going to be available for purchase any time soon. The concept is still being actively developed, but it is an interesting (and lovely) approach.
