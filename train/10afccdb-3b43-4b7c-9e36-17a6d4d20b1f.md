The sky was a vast, unyielding canvas of gray, the kind of day that promised nothing but endless drizzle and the slow, creeping dread of what was to come. I sat in the dim glow of my desk lamp, fingers tracing the edges of my half-finished manuscript, each word a fragile promise of something I couldn’t quite name. The rain tapped against the window like a metronome counting down to the inevitable.

Then, the change.

It came without warning, a sudden burst of warmth that cut through the chill like a knife. My pen hovered over the page, my breath caught in my throat as the words spilled forth, raw and unfiltered, unburdening the weight I’d been carrying since before the storm began. The first half had been a slow, deliberate unraveling—observations, fears, the quiet desperation of a mind trying to make sense of itself. But now, the page was alive, electric, the words dancing across the paper like fireflies in the dark.

The rain still fell, but it was different now. It wasn’t the slow, suffocating wet of despair; it was the bright, relentless patter of something alive, something *fighting*. The manuscript had shifted—midair, mid-moment—and what emerged was a frenzy of energy, a reckless abandon in the way the sentences twisted and turned, as if the words themselves were screaming. The story wasn’t just about the weight of the world; it was about the weight of *not giving up*. The second half wasn’t just a descent into darkness; it was a leap into light, a refusal to stay buried.

And the rain? The rain was just that—rain. A reminder. A test. But no longer a prison.
