Title: The Effects of Introducing Telepathy in Non-Telepathic Species

Abstract: Telepathy has long been considered one of the most difficult evolutionary steps a species must take before crossing the threshold from the Sentience Epsilon to the Sentience Zeta level [Muktuk et al, Orion Calendar Year 6829]. In this paper, we examine the hypothesis that the level of difficulty of developing telepathy for a species is directly correlated to the level of individuality in their society. In our experiment, we temporarily induced telepathy in two sample species and observed their behavior for six months. We found that in the Homo Sapiens species (The Individualist Group), introducing telepathy significantly increased the level of conflict between members and led to a marked decrease in group cohesion. Meanwhile, in the Isoptera Rubecula species (The Harmony Group), introducing telepathy resulted in increased productivity and cooperation as well as deeper bonds between group members. Since the main difference between the two species is the level of group cohesion and individualism in their society, this clearly indicates that the path forward to helping lesser species evolve should directly address the lack of conformity in thought between members of the species (i.e. to strive towards the Hive Mind Ideal).
