A promise like this—one whispered in haste or broken by circumstance—lingers like a ghost, its weight carried not by the words spoken, but by the silence that follows. The moment it’s made, there’s always a doubt: *What if the world changes?* The weather forecast shifts, a deadline slips, or a well-intentioned heart grows cold under the pressure of reality. The one who promised finds their hands tied by unspoken rules—time, resources, or the quiet betrayal of their own ambitions. The other waits, not for fulfillment, but for the faintest echo of regret, like a candle guttering out before the flames could fully consume it.

Sometimes, the hardest part isn’t breaking the promise—it’s deciding to keep it, knowing the cost is already paid in blood, sweat, or stolen dreams. The truth is, not every promise needs to be kept. Some are merely seeds planted in the dark, waiting for the right soil, the right storm, or the right fool to water them. And if not? Then the earth will still turn, and the promise, though forgotten, will have been a story worth telling.
