The room was dimly lit, the air thick with tension. A man in a rumpled suit sat stiffly in the chair, fingers tracing the rim of a coffee cup. Across from him, a woman with sharp, assessing eyes leaned forward, her expression unreadable.

*"I’m just a guy,"* he said, voice low and hesitant. *"I don’t know why you’re asking."* His hands clenched the edges of the table, nails digging into wood.

*"You’re not just anyone,"* she replied, tapping a pen against her lips. *"Explain yourself."*

He exhaled sharply. *"I work at a warehouse. I deliver packages. That’s it."*

*"But you’re not delivering packages today,"* she said, voice like gravel. *"You’re here because you’re not who you say you are."*

Silence stretched between them. The man’s shoulders tensed, his jaw tight. Outside, rain tapped against the window. The weight of the question pressed down harder than the storm outside.

*"I’m not sure what you mean,"* he said finally, but his voice betrayed the lie. *"I’m just here to talk."*
