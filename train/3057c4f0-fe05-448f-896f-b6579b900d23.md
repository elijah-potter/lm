The moment was fleeting—just a whisper of doubt in the quiet after a dinner that had felt too warm, the clink of glass against the polished table reflecting a smile that wasn’t quite sincere. *"I should’ve said something,"* she thought, but her voice had been swallowed by laughter, the kind that masked more than it revealed. The next morning, her phone buzzed with a text from her sister: *"Where were you last night?"* The reply was a single, hasty line: *"Too tired."* The lie was as thin as the smile she wore at the office, where the cameras didn’t lie. She erased the unread emails from her inbox, the ones she hadn’t replied to in weeks, and replaced them with the polished responses she’d practiced in the mirror. The regret was a shadow—dissipating as fast as the morning mist over a lake where she’d promised herself she’d take a walk that afternoon. But the lake was already calling her name, the water too still, too inviting, and the boat was already tied to its dock, waiting.
