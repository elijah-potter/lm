A morning’s sleep slipped away in fragments, leaving only the weight of an unmade bed and the hum of the coffee machine long since brewed. The alarm, a distant siren, had barely stirred the room—only a whisper of caffeine and stale dreams. Outside, the city exhaled in slow, sleepy breaths, its streets still slick with dew. The clock, a stubborn sentinel, ticked past seven, its hands reluctant to move forward.

The first task was coffee, but the machine needed a refill, and the cup had forgotten its place. A quick trip to the kitchen—milk spilled, sugar clumped in the jar—and the day’s rhythm was set to crawl. The kitchen table groaned under the weight of a half-drawn sketch, its edges uneven, its lines uncertain. The pen, a traitor, had left its ink well before the paper.

A call from work found an answering machine, the voice a distant echo of the morning’s chaos. The afternoon stretched like a half-finished painting, its colors blending into a dull, uninspired gray. A podcast on the drive, its monotone narration the only thing keeping the mind from spiraling. The traffic was a symphony of honks and tailwinds, each beat a reminder of the day’s relentless march.

By evening, the sun had dipped low, casting long shadows over the city. The evening meal was eaten in silence, the food cold, the company of a single plate. The news on the TV was a parade of crises, each one a weight dragging down the evening. The bed felt like a stranger’s, its sheets damp with sweat and regret.

As the night deepened, the day’s exhaustion settled like a blanket. The screen lit up, a flickering promise of escape. A book, half-read, its words blurred by fatigue. The world outside was quiet, a stillness broken only by the occasional car passing in the distance. Sleep came like a thief, taking what it could while the day’s ghosts lingered.
