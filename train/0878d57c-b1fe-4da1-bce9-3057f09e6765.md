The alarm blared at 3:17 AM, its discordant chirps piercing through the static of the old radio. Outside, a low, rhythmic drumbeat echoed through the cracked windows of the abandoned warehouse, the kind of sound that made the hair on the back of your neck stand up even before the lights flickered on. The warning had been simple: *"Do not enter if you’re not prepared."* But the sign had been there for weeks, ignored.

The door creaked open. Inside, the air was thick with the scent of damp earth and something metallic, like old blood. Shadows stretched long across the concrete floor, moving when no one was looking. The first thing you noticed was the lack of sound—no distant voices, no muffled laughter from the next room. Just the silence that swallowed everything else whole. The warning had been a joke. Or a test. Or both.

You pulled out the knife from your pocket—the one your father had given you before he vanished three years ago—and checked the blade. The hilt was warm. The tip was already bloodied. The lights died.
