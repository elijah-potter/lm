A suffocating mist clings to the cracked pavement of a half-abandoned subway tunnel, its rusted metal ribs groaning under the weight of forgotten time. The air is thick with the scent of damp earth and old oil, the only sound the distant drip of condensation from a shattered skylight. Overhead, the skeletal remains of a crumbling ceiling loom like skeletal fingers, swaying slightly as if caught in an invisible current. Below, the flickering glow of a single, flickering streetlamp casts long shadows that seem to writhe and merge, pooling into distorted reflections of a city that never quite woke up. The ground trembles faintly—once, twice—like a heartbeat held too tightly, barely perceptible, as if the world itself is waiting for something to breathe. The silence is deafening, punctuated only by the occasional whisper of wind through the gaps in the walls, carrying fragments of a past that refuses to stay buried.
