At a small café in downtown Paris, a stranger sits across from a middle-aged woman at a dimly lit table. The woman’s face is expressionless, eyes focused on a stack of papers in front of her. The stranger, dressed in an old leather jacket, leans forward and speaks softly: *"I’m not who you think I am."* The woman raises her eyebrows, the papers shifting slightly in her grip. *"Explain yourself."* Outside, the street hums with distant laughter and the clatter of a café’s espresso machine. The stranger begins to describe their past—untruths, half-truths, and a life that defies easy categorization. The woman listens, her fingers tracing the edge of a half-empty coffee cup. *"You’re not just a story,"* she murmurs, *"you’re a question."* The stranger hesitates, then adds: *"I’m trying to find out what I am."* The café’s lights flicker, casting long shadows across the table. The stranger’s voice drops to a whisper: *"Tell me if I’m wrong."*
