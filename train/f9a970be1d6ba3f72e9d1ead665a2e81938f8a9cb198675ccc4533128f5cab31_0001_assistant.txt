Jimbo’s favorite thing in the world was cooking, or so his family thought. He would spend hours in the kitchen, mumbling to himself as he prepared three meals a day for everyone. As delicious as the meals were, Jimbo’s family thought he should consider trying out different hobbies - or at least going outside - now that it was summer.

Taking their advice, Jimbo fired up a compromise and lit the gas-powered grill out on the porch. This did not entirely satisfy his family, as he still spent all his time cooking and talking to himself, but at least he left the kitchen. It was true that Jimbo rarely went outside, but he was not talking to himself.

For as long as he could remember, Jimbo could communicate with inanimate objects and, at a young age, he befriended a spatula. Knowing his friendship was unorthodox and would concern his family, Jimbo figured he would learn to cook; this way, he would have an excuse to spend all day with his best friend who he called Spatty.

Out on the porch, Jimbo and Spatty were having a great time. They had never spent time together outdoors, so having new things to talk about was wonderful. They conversed about the birds, complained about the neighbor’s dog, and, not least importantly, they cooked meals they typically would not cook inside. Jimbo and Spatty began trying burger and steak recipes, and they even tested out some meat-smoking methods. Jimbo and Spatty could get used to being outside.
