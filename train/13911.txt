Although many species of the Quercus genus live up to the reputation of the oak as a mighty tree, renowned for its skyline height and wide spreading limbs, certain members of the oak tree family exhibit other charms while growing to more diminutive sizes. The height of an oak tree for a small yard is an important consideration, but the best trees have a narrow limb span.
The best deciduous oak trees for small yards in U.S. Department of Agriculture plant hardiness zones 5 through 8 are narrow in width compared to other oak tree species. Various cultivars of English oak (Quercus robur) fit this category, as there are columnar forms of this tree. "Kindred Spirit" reaches a mature height of 35 feet with a narrow width of 6 feet. "Skyrocket" and "Crimson Spire" each grow to a height of 45 feet and a width of 15 feet. Deciduous oak trees provide summer shade, and their leaves provide ornamental interest through colorful new growth and autumn color. Japanese feather oak (Quercus dentata "Pinnatifida") adds additional ornamental interest to the garden with finely cut leaves. Reaching a height of only 15 feet, Japanese feather oak makes a good specimen plant.
Evergreen oak trees have ovate or straplike, dark-green leathery leaves. The thick leaves of evergreen oaks are an adaptation to the climate, where they thrive in locations with moist or wet winters and dry summers. A dense growth habit makes evergreen oaks useful for windbreaks. Coast live oak (Q. agrifolia) is native to California, where it makes a good shade tree of 20 to 70 feet tall, or a dense hedge when sheared to approximately 12 feet tall. A less sturdy and more graceful evergreen oak is Japanese live oak (Q. myrsinifolia). This oak tree generally grows shorter in cultivation -- reaching a height of 20 to 30 feet with a similar spread -- than it does in its native habitat. Both of these oak trees grow in USDA plant hardiness zones 8 through 10.
Deciduous oak trees grow in sun or partial shade, but evergreen oaks require full-sun locations. Well-drained deep fertile soil is ideal for oak tree culture, as oaks are subject to root rot in wet conditions. Various soil types are tolerated, including alkaline soils. Fertilization is not generally required in soils of average fertility. However, if an oak tree grows poorly, fertilize in late winter or early spring with a balanced fertilizer blend, following manufacturer's instructions. Evenly sprinkle fertilizer in the root zone, which extends outward from the trunk of the tree to the dripline.
When planting an oak tree, locate the tree far enough away from hardscaping to prevent water pooling in the root area of the mature tree. If you plant perennials under your oak tree, use only drought-tolerant species, as irrigation to supply the needs of the perennials can introduce too much summertime water for the health of your oak tree.
