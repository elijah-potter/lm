The old café’s scent of fresh coffee and buttery croissants lingers, though the last customer left weeks ago. The worn wooden tables still creak under light footsteps, the high ceiling’s exposed beams slightly warped from age. The barista’s station—scuffed stool, cracked mugs lined up—reminds me of the way she’d hum while brewing, her hands steady despite the coffee rings. Outside, the sidewalk is slick with rain, puddles reflecting the streetlamps like the first time I walked through here, stumbling over a loose brick. The chalkboard menu, faded but legible, still lists the daily specials: the *Black Forest cake* (now with a single bite taken from the corner), the *grilled cheese* (still too salty after the last storm). The door’s old brass handle turns smoothly, but the lock clicks only when I press it just right. The *feeling* is the same—like stepping into a half-remembered dream.
