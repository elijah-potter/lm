The alarm buzzes at 5:47 AM, but my brain is already half-asleep. A day feels like a loop of 24 hours—too many minutes, too few hours. The morning is a blur of coffee and emails, each task a tiny step toward something that never quite feels complete. By noon, the sun is warm but the clock ticks relentlessly. Lunch is a sandwich that tastes like it was assembled by someone who hates their life, and the afternoon stretches like a bad dream. By evening, I’m exhausted but still have a thousand things to do. Dinner is a quiet affair, just another moment in the rush. The bed feels soft, but my mind races with unfinished thoughts. The night is the longest—endless hours of staring at the ceiling, wondering if tomorrow will ever start. Sleep comes, but it’s fragmented, like a day that never ends.
