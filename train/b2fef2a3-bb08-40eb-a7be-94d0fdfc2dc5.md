A dimly lit interrogation room. A man with sharp features sits across from a stern officer, a tablet in front of him displaying a name: *"Javier Martinez."* The officer leans forward, fingers steepled, eyes scanning the screen. "Why were you at the abandoned warehouse last night?" the officer says, voice low and measured.

Javier exhales slowly, rubbing his jaw. "I was there to retrieve documents—company records my firm was supposed to audit." He pauses, then adds, "The audit was delayed, and I had to act fast. No one would believe me if I said I was just passing through."

The officer’s pen pauses mid-air. "And why would you trust me over a company that might blackmail you?"

Javier’s grip tightens on the armrest. "Because I’m not a criminal. I’m a whistleblower. My partner was killed investigating fraud in that firm, and I had to act." He glances at the tablet, then back. "I’m not asking for your approval. I’m just telling you what happened." Outside, the city hums—traffic, distant sirens. The room feels heavy, but Javier won’t look away. "What do you want to know?" he says quietly. "The truth. But you’ll have to decide if it’s enough."
