## A Counseling Session

**Setting:** A comfortably furnished therapy room. Soft lighting, a view of a small garden.

**Characters:**

*   **Dr. Eleanor Vance:** A seasoned therapist, known for her calm demeanor and insightful guidance.
*   **Liam Davies:** A young professional experiencing significant stress and difficulty sleeping.

---

**Liam:** (Sighs, settling into the armchair) It’s…it’s been relentless, Dr. Vance. I feel like I’m constantly chasing something I can’t quite grasp.

**Dr. Vance:** (Nodding gently) Relentless is a powerful word, Liam. It sounds exhausting. Can you tell me a little more about what feels so relentless?

**Liam:** Work, mostly. The deadlines are tighter than ever, and there’s this constant pressure to perform. I feel like one mistake will unravel everything. And then I come home, and I just can't switch off. My mind races. I lie awake for hours.

**Dr. Vance:** So, the pressure at work is significantly impacting your ability to relax and recharge, even when you're not physically at the office. You’re experiencing a carryover of that stress. That’s understandable. It sounds incredibly difficult.

**Liam:** It is. I used to enjoy my job. Now, I dread going in. I feel guilty, too, because I know I'm lucky to have it. My friends are struggling, and I know I shouldn't be feeling this way.

**Dr. Vance:** It's common to feel guilty when experiencing challenges, especially when you perceive others as facing even greater difficulties. Guilt can complicate the situation. It’s important to remember that everyone’s experience is unique, and your feelings are valid regardless of what others are going through. It sounds like you’re holding a lot inside.

**Liam:** I am. I try to compartmentalize, to just push it down, but it just…it bubbles up. I find myself snapping at my partner, which isn’t fair to her. I'm not the person she signed up for.

**Dr. Vance:** That’s a very insightful observation, Liam. Recognizing how your stress is impacting your relationships demonstrates a level of self-awareness that is commendable. It’s also a clear indication that the current situation isn’t sustainable.  Tell me, when you're feeling overwhelmed, what, if anything, do you typically do?

**Liam:** Honestly? Not much. I scroll through social media, which I know is pointless. Sometimes I have a few drinks, but that just makes things worse the next day. I'm just…stuck.

**Dr. Vance:** It sounds like you're caught in a cycle, seeking temporary relief that ultimately reinforces the problem. That's a very common pattern. We can explore strategies to break that cycle. Have you considered any techniques for managing stress, such as mindfulness or exercise?

**Liam:** I’m not really a “mindfulness” person. And I used to run, but I haven’t had the time or the energy lately. It feels like another thing I *should* be doing.

**Dr. Vance:** It’s important to find strategies that feel manageable and sustainable, not another obligation. The pressure you're placing on yourself already seems significant. Let's not add more to that. Perhaps we can explore some smaller, more accessible steps. What’s one thing, no matter how small, that you think you could realistically incorporate into your routine this week to help you feel even slightly more grounded?

**Liam:** (Pauses, considering) Maybe… maybe just ten minutes of quiet time each evening. No phone, no work, just…quiet.

**Dr. Vance:** That sounds like a wonderful starting point. Ten minutes of intentional quiet. We can discuss how that feels next session. It's about creating space, even a small amount, to allow yourself to simply *be*.  It’s a beginning. And remember, progress isn’t always linear. There will be days when it feels harder than others.



**Liam:** (A faint smile appears) Thank you, Dr. Vance. It feels…a little less overwhelming, just talking about it.



**Dr. Vance:** You're very welcome, Liam. That’s often the first step. We’ll continue to work through this together.
