**Project Title:** *"Deciphering the Cryptic Metabolic Footprint of *Staphylococcus aureus* Under Sublethal Vancomycin Exposure: A Systems-Biology Approach to Antibiotic Resistance Mechanisms in Chronic Wound Infections"*

**Objective:**
Investigate how *S. aureus* adapts its metabolic pathways under chronic sublethal vancomycin exposure, particularly in biofilm-forming chronic wounds, to elucidate mechanisms of resistance and develop predictive biomarkers for personalized treatment.

**Key Focus Areas:**
1. **Metabolic Reprogramming:** Quantify changes in glycolysis, tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle, and amino acid metabolism via liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) and stable isotope tracing.
2. **Quorum Sensing & Resistance:** Analyze small-molecule signaling (e.g., autoinducer-2) and gene expression (e.g., *vanA*, *icaABC*) via qPCR and RNA-seq in wound exudate-derived biofilms.
3. **Epigenetic & Post-Translational Modifications:** Use mass spectrometry to identify modifications (e.g., acetylation, methylation) in vancomycin resistance-associated proteins (e.g., peptidoglycan-associated lipoprotein D).
4. **In Silico Modeling:** Construct a dynamic metabolic network integrating experimental data to predict vancomycin resistance thresholds in chronic infections.

**Methodology:**
- **In Vitro:** Grow *S. aureus* MRSA strains in subinhibitory vancomycin concentrations (0.1×MIC) for 72 hours, then analyze metabolic flux via ¹³C-labeling and proteomics.
- **In Vivo:** Isolate biofilm samples from chronic diabetic foot ulcers, culture under vancomycin exposure, and compare metabolic profiles to planktonic controls.
- **Bioinformatics:** Use *GENIE3* to infer regulatory networks from transcriptomic data, cross-referencing with CRISPRi screens to validate targets (e.g., *ltaS*, *atb(D)*).

**Expected Outcomes:**
- A metabolomic signature distinguishing chronic vancomycin-resistant *S. aureus* from acute infections.
- A decision-support tool (e.g., Python-based classifier) to predict treatment failure based on wound exudate biomarkers.
- Identification of repurposable compounds (e.g., dipeptidyl peptidase inhibitors) targeting metabolic vulnerabilities in resistant strains.

**Novelty & Impact:**
Unlike traditional studies focusing on *de novo* resistance, this proposal targets the **metabolic and signaling adaptations** of *S. aureus* under chronic subtherapeutic exposure—a gap exploited by biofilm-forming strains in chronic wounds. The proposed biomarkers could guide **adaptive antibiotic dosing** (e.g., vancomycin plus *ltaS* inhibitors) to curb resistance without disrupting biofilm dispersal.
