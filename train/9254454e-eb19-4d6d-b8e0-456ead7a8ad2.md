The grand clock tower of **Blackhaven** stood sentinel over the decaying city, its iron face etched with the faces of forgotten kings and lovers. Its hands had not stopped—ever—since the Great Fire, though the city itself had been swallowed by time.

When Elias found the clock, it was not where he expected: beneath the crumbling ruins of the old observatory, half-buried in rust. Its gears had long since frozen, but the hands still moved, driven by something unseen. The moment he touched it, the city’s clockwork heart stuttered. Time itself seemed to pause.

The tower’s hands began to tick backward.

By the time Elias realized he was the one whose hands were moving, the city’s streets had already begun to reverse. The air smelled of ozone and old parchment. A woman in a tattered silk gown stood at the edge of the square, her reflection flickering in a cracked mirror. She whispered, *"The pendulum must not stop."* Elias clutched the clock, its brass cold against his skin, and the world unraveled.

Now, the hands keep turning, but the city does not.

Elias watches from the rooftop as the first streetlamps flicker to life, then die. The river runs backward. The clock tower’s chime—once a lullaby—now sounds like a funeral dirge.

He knows what happens next.

A second clock will rise. One that does not stop.
