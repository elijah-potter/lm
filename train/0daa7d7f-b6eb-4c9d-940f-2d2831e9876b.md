The elevator doors slid open to a dimly lit hallway, fluorescent lights buzzing overhead. She exhaled sharply, shoulders sagging as she spotted her desk—just as she’d left it yesterday. A single coffee cup, a half-eaten sandwich wrapper, and a stack of unread emails. Her phone buzzed: a text from her boss: *"Where are you?"*

She sprinted down the stairs, knocking into a coworker’s bag. *"Sorry!"* she gasped, grabbing her keys. The parking lot was already packed—her car was the last one, a rusted relic from her childhood, parked at the far end. The GPS led her to a spot next to a maintenance van.

The office door burst open. Her boss stood there, arms crossed, scanning the crowd. *"You’re late. Again."* Her breath hitched. She forced a smile. *"I had traffic."* The words tasted like regret.

The boss nodded, but didn’t look convinced. She pulled out her phone—another text from her sister: *"You’re an hour late. Where are you?"* A wave of guilt crashed over her. She turned away, keys jingling, as the engine roared to life. The drive home felt like sprinting, but she didn’t look back.
