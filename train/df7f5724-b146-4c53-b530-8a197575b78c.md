The first light of dawn crept through the curtains like a hesitant hand, brushing gold across the room before the morning’s full weight settled over the city. I sat on the edge of my bed, the weight of everything pressing down—what if I’m wrong? What if this is all just noise? The phone buzzed again, its ringtone sharp enough to cut through the quiet. I didn’t answer. The screen glowed red, the message waiting: *"Meet at 9. Don’t be late."* My fingers hovered over the delete button, then hesitated. What if I was the one who was late? What if they’d already left without me?

Outside, the traffic hummed like a machine drowning in its own parts. I took a deep breath, but the air tasted of something sour—doubt, maybe, or the faint metallic tang of fear. The subway doors hissed open, and I stepped in, my pulse thrumming like a metronome counting down to the moment I’d have to choose. The train lurched forward, and I clutched the strap of my bag, my knuckles white. What if I got it wrong? What if I was supposed to stay? The doors closed with a finality that felt like a question: *Do you dare answer?*
