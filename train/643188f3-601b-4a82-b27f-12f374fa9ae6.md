In the dim glow of the attic’s rusted lamp, **Elias** sat hunched over a yellowed map, his fingers tracing the crumbling edges of parchment with deliberate slowness. The ink had faded to ghostly hues, but the path still held meaning—*"The Blackthorn Pass,"* it whispered, *"beware the echo of your past."* His memory was a labyrinth of whispers and half-remembered names: **Mother’s warning**, *"Trust no man who speaks of gold without a price."* **Father’s last words**, *"The plan was never to take it—only to hold it."* A voice from the shadows—*perhaps his own*—suggested, *"You already have it."* He exhaled sharply, gripping the map tighter.

Outside, the wind howled through the broken windows, carrying the scent of damp earth and something older, something that smelled like regret. His plan was simple: find the **Casket of Echoes** before dawn. But the map was incomplete—only the **three landmarks** marked with stars, each tied to a memory: the **old mill**, where he had first seen his mother’s bruises; the **abandoned chapel**, where she had kissed the cross and whispered secrets in a language no one understood; and the **river’s edge**, where he had drowned a boy who knew too much. The plan was a chain—break one link, and the whole thing unravels.

A knock at the door made him flinch. The latch creaked open. Standing there, clad in a coat that didn’t belong to him, was a man with eyes like polished obsidian and a grin that didn’t reach his teeth. *"You’re late,"* the man said, voice smooth as oil. *"The pass is already shifting."* Elias’ breath hitched. The man extended a hand—a skeletal, gnarled thing, wrapped in silver thread. *"I’m the one who spoke to you in the dark,"* he murmured. *"Your plan isn’t just about gold. It’s about the things you’re willing to bury."* The lamp flickered. The attic door groaned as if listening.
