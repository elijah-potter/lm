A team of microbiologists at MIT discovered a novel enzyme that catalyzes the fusion of water and carbon dioxide into crystalline cellulose under ambient conditions, bypassing traditional photosynthesis limitations.

The enzyme, **CryoCellF**, operates at near-zero temperatures, enabling plant-like structures to form from CO₂ without sunlight. Tested on synthetic polymer substrates, it produced fibrous networks with mechanical properties comparable to natural cellulose.

In a lab setting, it demonstrates how microbial life could theoretically engineer carbon-negative ecosystems without relying on photosynthesis. Economic potential includes carbon capture and biofabrication of sustainable materials, though further optimization is needed to scale production cost-effectively.

**Key takeaway:** A single discovery could redefine biogeochemical cycles and industrial chemistry by merging photosynthesis principles with enzymatic engineering.
