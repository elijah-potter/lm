The warehouse hummed with the low hum of machines, dust motes swirling in the flickering fluorescent light. Inside, **old rusted toolbox** sat abandoned on a high shelf, half-buried under dust. It had been there since the factory’s last shift before the shutdown, forgotten by the last workers who packed up their gear. The lid was cracked, a loose hinge creaking with each passing day.

A knock at the door. **Carlos**, the foreman, entered, his boots kicking up powdered grit from the floor. He glanced at the toolbox, then at the stack of paperwork on his desk. "You can’t find anything else to do?" he asked, but he knew better.

His gaze drifted to the box. A half-empty paint can lay beside it, its lid off, the surface already peeling.

He lifted the lid.

Inside, a single **blue circuit board**, cracked but intact, glinted under the light. It wasn’t labeled, just a hunk of metal and wiring. No serial number, no documentation. Just… *there*.

Carlos frowned. He’d seen this kind of thing before—components scavenged from old machinery, repurposed. But this one was different. The edges were smooth, not filed. Someone had *cut* it cleanly, as if it had been removed from something else.

He pocketed the board. The door creaked shut behind him.

That night, back at the apartment, he plugged it into his old laptop. The screen flickered to life, then went dark. Static. Then, a single line of text: **"RECONNECT."**

His breath caught. He’d seen that error before—on a prototype system from the old division. Something they’d scrapped.

Carlos’s fingers trembled. He knew what this meant. And he knew why it had been buried in the warehouse.
