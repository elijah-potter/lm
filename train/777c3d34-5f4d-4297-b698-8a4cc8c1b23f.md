## Explain Like I'm Five: Why the Moon Looks Different Sometimes

Okay, imagine you have a ball, like a bouncy ball! And you have a lamp shining on it. Now, if you stand directly in front of the lamp, you see the whole ball, right? It looks round and bright. 

But what happens if you move to the side a little bit? You don’t see the whole ball anymore. You might just see half of it, or even just a little sliver! 

The Moon is kind of like that ball, and the Sun is like the lamp. The Moon goes around the Earth, and as it goes around, we see different amounts of the sunlight reflecting off of it.

Sometimes, the Moon is between the Earth and the Sun. We see the side of the Moon that *isn't* lit up by the Sun, so it looks completely dark. This is called a "New Moon," and you can’t see it!

Then, as the Moon moves, a little bit of the Sun’s light starts to show. It looks like a tiny fingernail of light. This is called a "Crescent Moon."

As it keeps moving, more and more of the Moon becomes lit up. It looks like a bigger and bigger slice of pie! 

When the Moon is directly opposite the Sun, we see the whole side that's lit up. That’s when it looks like a big, round, bright Moon! We call that a "Full Moon."

Then, just like it got bigger, it starts to get smaller again. It goes through all those shapes again, a big slice, then a smaller slice, then just a little sliver, and then disappears again!

These different shapes the Moon makes are called “phases.” It’s just like the ball changing how much you see lit up because you’re looking at it from different angles. The Moon isn't actually changing shape; it's just the way we see it!
