**Quantum Dots in Semiconductor Nanostructures (e.g., a nanoscale semiconductor crystal with quantum confinement effects):**

Atoms arranged in a confined 3D lattice (e.g., CdSe) exhibit size-dependent photoluminescence where emission wavelength shifts predictably with particle diameter (quantum size effect). Below the exciton binding energy threshold, **direct-to-indirect bandgap transitions** appear via radiative recombination at surface traps, yielding a broadened emission spectrum. **Coherent phonon coupling** leads to Fano interference in emission lines, resulting in asymmetric spectral features. In **quantum wells**, electron-hole recombination is suppressed, but **exciton-exciton scattering** induces non-Boltzmann distributions under high excitation, altering emission dynamics. **Spin-orbit coupling** splits states asymmetrically, enabling spin-dependent emission (e.g., in GaAs-based systems). **Nonlinear optical effects** like second-harmonic generation emerge when light interacts with localized surface plasmon resonances, producing frequency-doubled emission from metallic nanoparticles embedded in the structure. **Thermally induced phase transitions** (e.g., from amorphous to crystalline) in nanocrystals can abruptly shift emission spectra. **Quantum dots in liquid environments** exhibit solvent-induced shifts due to dielectric screening, while **interfacial disorder** at core-shell interfaces can trap excitons, suppressing radiative recombination via nonradiative decay pathways.
