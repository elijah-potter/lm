A stealthy rulebreak occurs when an individual or system bypasses a norm without overt resistance, often through subtle adjustments or unintentional lapses. For example, an employee might exceed a work-hour limit by logging in 10 minutes late each day, assuming no one notices. Or a software update silently skips a required patch, assuming the risk is minimal.

Such behavior thrives in environments where enforcement is lax or where the cost of detection outweighs the effort. Over time, these quiet violations accumulate, normalizing the exception as the rule. The absence of confrontation and the assumption that others are also breaking the same rule create a self-perpetuating loop, where compliance becomes optional and enforcement becomes redundant.
