The rain tapped against the windows in rhythmic, weary pulses, each drop a whispered confession to the storm. Outside, the alleyways glistened under the sickly glow of flickering streetlamps, their beams stretching like skeletal fingers toward the distant silhouette of the old church spire.

Inside, the air was thick with the scent of aged paper and damp wool, the kind that clung to forgotten things. Clara sat hunched over her sketches, her fingers tracing the contours of a half-finished portrait with the precision of someone who had spent too long staring into the abyss. The canvas was a chaotic blend of blues and grays, the subject’s face half-erased by the time she’d paused to look away. A half-eaten sandwich lay on her lap, its crusts dusted with crumbs, the salt and pepper long since vanished.

The door creaked open.

Clara’s breath hitched. She didn’t look up.

A shadow shifted. Footsteps approached, slow and deliberate, like something testing its own weight in the dark. The door closed with a final, decisive click. The moment of silence that followed was heavier than any thunderstorm.

Then—movement. Clara’s chair scraped back, and the sketchbook clattered to the floor.

Someone knelt beside her, their presence so sudden it made her flinch. "You’ve been sketching for hours," the stranger said, voice low, rough with the kind of gravel that had seen better days.

Clara exhaled, rubbing her temples. "I was… trying to remember." She gestured to the half-finished painting. "The woman. She’s been following me."

The stranger leaned forward, their face half-lit by the dim glow of a cigarette ash burning between their fingers. "You’re not the first."

The door swung open again. A figure stepped inside, tall and broad-shouldered, their coat damp with rain. They carried a lantern, its flame casting long, wavering shadows across the room.

Clara’s hands trembled. "Who are you?"

The lantern light caught the edges of a name carved into the back of their hand: **Elias Veyne**.

And then, as if the moment itself had been waiting, the door closed behind them.
