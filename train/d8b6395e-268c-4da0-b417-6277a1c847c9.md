The old clock tower’s pendulum swung one last time before it cracked under the weight of its own rust. The chime echoed through the cobblestone streets, not with the usual rhythmic cadence but as a single, hollow *clang*. Below, Elias stood at the base, his breath fogging in the frigid dawn air. His fingers traced the scar running from his temple to his jawline—a souvenir from the war that had reshaped the world.

His father’s voice, rough as gravel, cut through the silence: *"You always were the reckless one."* Elias clenched his jaw. The letter in his pocket—his mother’s handwriting, the one he’d never sent—was gone. Not stolen, not lost. *Destroyed*. His eyes locked onto the broken hinge of the clock’s face, the one that had once kept time for a thousand years. He reached for it.

A shadow fell over them. The old woman from the bakery, her cheeks lined with wrinkles that told tales of decades, stepped forward. *"You’re breaking the last thing that hasn’t broken you,"* she murmured. Elias’ grip tightened on the hinge. The metal was warm to the touch, humming with energy he didn’t understand. He didn’t need to. The clock’s face was already bending.

The first crack split the glass like a knife through butter. The pendulum, suspended in mid-swing, hesitated—then plunged into the void below. Elias didn’t flinch. The clock’s gears, frozen in time, began to turn backward, rewinding the seconds, the minutes, the years. The woman’s hands hovered over his shoulder, her breath warm against his neck. *"You’ve always been the one who could see what others couldn’t,"* she whispered. *"Now, will you choose what comes next?"*

The hinge snapped free. Elias didn’t look away. The world dissolved into light.
