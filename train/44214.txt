What Is HIV? What Is AIDS?
Human Immunodeficiency Virus(HIV) is a virus that damages and weakens the body’s immune system of our body—the system your body uses to fight off infection and disease. Therefore having HIV or HIV-positive probably puts a person in danger of experiencing other life-threatening infections and certain cancers.
If we talk furthermore then AIDS is the very late stage of HIV infection that occurs when the body’s immune system is very badly damaged because of the virus.
In short, in the U.S., most people with HIV or HIV+ usually don’t develop AIDS because taking HIV medicine on a daily basis or regular basis as prescribed stops the progression of the disease.
A person who has HIV is considered to have progressed to AIDS when:
- The number of their CD4 cells falls below or down the 200 cells per cubic millimeter of blood (200 cells/mm3). (In someone with a healthy immune system, CD4 counts are between 500 and one,600 cells/mm3.) OR
- They develop one or more opportunistic infections regardless of their CD4 count in the body.
Without HIV medicine, people with AIDS typically survive about 2 to 3 years. Once someone has at the stage of dangerous opportunistic illness, life expectancy without treatment falls to about 1 year. Medicine can help, HIV medicine can still help people at this stage of HIV infection, and it can even be lifesaving. But people who start ART soon after they get HIV experience more benefits—that’s why HIV testing is so important.
