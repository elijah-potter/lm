## Explain Like I'm Five: Why the Sky is Blue

Okay, imagine you have a big box of crayons, right? And in that box, there are all sorts of colors: red, yellow, green, blue, and so on. Sunlight is kind of like that box of crayons – it's actually made up of *all* those colors mixed together! We can't usually see them all separate, it just looks white or yellow.

Now, imagine you're throwing those crayons at a bumpy trampoline. Some of the crayons bounce off easily, like the red and yellow ones. They go straight through. But the blue crayon? It’s a little bit like a bouncy ball – it doesn’t want to go straight! It bounces off the trampoline in all different directions.

The air around the Earth is like that bumpy trampoline. When sunlight comes down to us, it bumps into the air. The red and yellow colors are strong and mostly go straight through. But the blue color, like our bouncy blue crayon, bounces all over the place!

Because the blue color is bouncing everywhere, it fills up the whole sky. That's why when we look up, we see blue! It's not *really* that the sky *is* blue, it's that we see the blue color bouncing around the air.

Sometimes, at sunset, the sun is much farther away. The light has to travel through *lots* more air to reach us. By the time it gets here, almost all the blue has bounced away! Then we see the other colors, like orange and red, because they are the ones that can still make it through.



Isn't that neat?
