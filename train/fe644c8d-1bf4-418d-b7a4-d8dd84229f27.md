The team identified a novel enzymatic pathway in *C. elegans* where **mitochondrial-derived vesicles (MDVs)** fuse with lysosomes via a previously uncharacterized protein complex, **Lysosomal Fusion-Associated Protein-1 (LFAP-1)**. This discovery revealed that MDVs, typically thought of as apoptotic bodies, undergo **lysosomal degradation via a novel autophagy pathway**—**mitophagy via lysosomal fusion (MLF)**—triggering a cascade of metabolic and developmental reprogramming.

Key findings:
- **MLF** mediates the breakdown of mitochondrial remnants, releasing **mitochondrial peptides** (e.g., *mt-hsp60*) that act as **signals for stress adaptation**.
- In *C. elegans*, MLF was linked to **extended lifespan** via activation of **DAF-16 (FOXO)**, a master regulator of longevity.
- The process was **energy-dependent** and sensitive to **ATP depletion**, suggesting a role in **battery-like cellular homeostasis**.

This shift suggests that **lysosomal function extends beyond digestion** to **dynamic mitochondrial management**, potentially rewriting paradigms in aging, disease, and synthetic biology.
