The library was always quiet except for the soft hum of the fluorescent lights and the occasional rustle of pages. That was until the night Eli found the book tucked under his seat. It wasn’t a novel—it was a leather-bound journal, its pages yellowed with age. The last entry read: *"They’re watching. Find the code."* The ink was faded but unmistakable.

Eli had never been one for mysteries, but the words stuck in his throat. He checked the time—past midnight. His parents were asleep, and the stairs creaked under his bare feet. He slipped the book into his backpack and followed the faint glow from the hallway. The basement door was slightly ajar, its hinges groaning.

Inside, the air smelled of dust and something metallic. A single bulb flickered above a table where a circuit board hummed. At the center, a small, spinning device pulsed with an eerie blue light. His breath hitched. That wasn’t just a device—it was a transmitter.

His phone buzzed. A text from his older sister, Mia: *"You’re late. Mom’s making pancakes."* Eli’s pulse quickened. He shoved the transmitter into his pocket and ran upstairs. The door slammed shut behind him, but the basement’s glow followed him in his mind.

The next morning, he found the journal’s next entry in his backpack: *"They’ll ask. Answer only what they want."* His hands trembled as he opened the kitchen door. His mom’s face was set in a frown. "Where’s your phone?" she asked, her voice sharp.

Eli swallowed. "Out of battery." She didn’t buy it. "Where’s your book, then?"

The journal was gone. But the blue light from the basement flickered in his memory again.

---

The school’s Wi-Fi password was on the back of the teacher’s desk. Eli typed it into his laptop, his fingers flying. The screen lit up with a map—somewhere between the old mill and the river. His stomach twisted. The code was in the journal, but the last entry was crossed out. *"The bridge, third floor, 4765."* The numbers matched the coordinates he’d found online for the school’s abandoned storage room.

He took the elevator to the third floor, his heart pounding. The door creaked open. Dust motes swirled in the dim light. A ladder leaned against a wall, its rungs worn smooth. At the top, a door stood ajar. Inside, a room filled with shelves of old tech. His eyes landed on a box labeled *"Project Phoenix."* The cover was cracked, revealing a screen with a single line: *"Activate at 3:17 PM."*

His breath came faster. The journal’s last entry was a warning, but it wasn’t fear that gripped him—it was curiosity. He pulled out a USB drive from his pocket, the one he’d stolen from his dad’s desk. The device buzzed with static. He plugged it in, and the screen glitched. A message appeared: *"Welcome to the system."*

Eli’s fingers hovered over the keyboard. The time read 3:16 PM. He hesitated. The lights flickered. Then, the door behind him groaned open.

A figure stepped into the room—a girl with dark hair and a lab coat, her eyes sharp and unreadable. "You’re late," she said, voice like dry leaves. "The others are waiting."

Eli’s blood turned to ice. "Who are you?"

The girl smirked. "The code isn’t just numbers, kid. It’s a story. And you’re the first to read it."

Outside, the basement’s blue light pulsed again, brighter this time. The elevator doors hissed shut. The code was running out. And Eli was the only one left to decide what happened next.
