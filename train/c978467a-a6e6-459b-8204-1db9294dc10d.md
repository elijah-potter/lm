**1. The Forgotten Key**
The old lighthouse keeper found the rusted key beneath the sand, half-buried where the tide never reached. The beacon’s last beam flickered out as the storm swept in, but he pressed the key into the brass door of the abandoned keeper’s quarters. Inside, he found the ledger detailing the shipwrecks that had vanished without trace. The key opened the chest where the captain’s journal lay, its pages filled with warnings: *"They are not ghosts; they are waiting."* The next morning, the lighthouse burned to the ground, but the sea carried the key to the next shore, where it was found by a child who recognized its shape from a dream.

**2. The Clockmaker’s Error**
The clockmaker’s apprentice, Elias, built a mechanism that didn’t tick—it *un-ticked*, erasing time in bursts. When he tested it on the old town clock, the hands froze at 3:17, but the seconds remained. Elias realized the error: the mechanism didn’t undo time—it *reflected* it. The clock’s face mirrored the past, showing the clockmaker’s face as a child, smiling. Elias watched as the seconds coiled back, the clock’s gears whispering in a voice that wasn’t his. By sunset, the clock’s hands spun again, but the town’s children began speaking in perfect, unnatural harmony. The mayor declared it a miracle—until the first child vanished, their laughter echoing in the wind.
