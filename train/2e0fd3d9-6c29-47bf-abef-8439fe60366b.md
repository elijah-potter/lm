In a dimly lit, candle-lit room, an elderly woman—her fingers trembling over a worn leather-bound journal—flips through the brittle pages. The ink on the last entry, *"Promise kept"* in her child’s looping script, catches the flickering light. A photograph beside it shows a young boy, his eyes wide with fear, clutching a small wooden sword, while the woman’s own hand, once steady, now shakes as she traces the edge of his palm. The scent of old paper and lavender lingers. Outside, the church bells toll for an hour, marking the anniversary of the day she swore she’d never leave him. The boy, now a man, sits across the table, his voice thick with unshed tears. He tells her about the war, the battles he fought, the battles he lost. She listens, her mind replaying the moment they stood in the rain at the edge of the woods, the night the stars seemed to shine brighter than ever.
