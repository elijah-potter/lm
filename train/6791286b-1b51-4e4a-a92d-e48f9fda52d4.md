In a dimly lit conference room, holographic projections flicker over a table draped in translucent fabric. A group of figures, some in retro-futuristic attire, others in sleek, minimalist gear, exchange glances as a central display pulses with fragmented visions of past and future.

The speaker, clad in a tunic of what appears to be liquid silver, adjusts their device, their voice a blend of static and resonance. *"By the year 2187, the old world’s systems have crumbled—but not before they reshaped everything."* Behind them, a wall of shifting data streams depicts cities that no longer exist, replaced by skeletal frameworks of what was once built. The air hums with unseen energy as a second projection materializes—a child’s handprint glowing faintly against a backdrop of swirling constellations.

One attendee leans forward, fingers tracing the edge of a worn pocket watch. *"You’re saying… we’re not just watching the future. We’re building it?"* The room holds its breath as the speaker’s device crackles, revealing a whisper of a voice from beyond the screen: *"The choice was never yours to make."* The projection flickers again, dissolving into static. Outside, the distant chime of a clock—twenty years old—echoes like a warning.
