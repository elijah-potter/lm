A disgruntled warehouse clerk, Alex, adjusts his watch for the second time in an hour as he glances at the production line clock—it’s 3:12 PM. The automated sorting system has been down for twelve minutes, and the last batch of defective components was rejected without further notice. The manager, Mr. Thompson, never called in, but the team’s morale has been suffering. Alex subtly nudges the conveyor belt lever, causing a single, misaligned module to drift off the line and into a designated "minor defect" bin instead of the full rejection.

The forklift driver, Mia, notices the shift immediately—her eyes flick to the bin, then to Alex’s face, which doesn’t quite meet the usual grimace. She hesitates, then glances at the supervisor’s desk, where a half-empty coffee cup sits. The coffee machine’s timer reads 3:57 PM. A pause. Then, Mia picks up the misaligned module, returns it to the correct bin, and whispers, *"You’re not the only one."*

In the break room, three hours later, the team gathers around the microwave, half-eaten sandwiches in hand. The latest "incident" report, posted on the whiteboard, is titled *"Defective Component Adjustment Protocol—Under Review."* Below it, in scrawled notes, someone added: *"Alex, Mia, and the coffee machine."* The fluorescent lights buzz over the din, the hum of conversation masking the unspoken question: *How much longer?*
