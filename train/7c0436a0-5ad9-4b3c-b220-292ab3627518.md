**The Last Train to Eldermere**

The rain lashed against the grimy windows of the abandoned train station as Elias adjusted his worn leather gloves. The flickering gas lamps cast long shadows over the crates stacked beside the rusted platform. He had come here to retrieve his father’s last letters, hidden beneath the floorboards of the old freight car.

A knock at the door made him jump. It was the stationmaster, his face pale and trembling. "You shouldn’t be here," he whispered. "The police are looking for you."

Elias ignored him, stepping inside. The freight car was a tomb of forgotten things—jars of preserved herbs, a broken clock, and the faint scent of mildew. Beneath the floorboards, he found the letters. His father had written of the war, of the secrets he couldn’t share. His mother’s last words echoed in his mind: *"Burn them before they burn you."*

As Elias reached for the letters, the stationmaster grabbed his wrist. "You’re not leaving. Not today."

---

**The choice was his.**

1. **The Fire:**
   Elias ripped the letters from his pocket, crumpling them into a ball. He hurled them into the smoldering stove behind him. The flames erupted, turning the room into a sea of blue-black smoke. The stationmaster’s eyes widened. "You’ve done it," he breathed. "You’ve burned the past away." The train whistle wailed in the distance as the stationmaster helped him board the last train to Eldermere, where no one would ever find the letters—or the guilt.

2. **The Silence:**
   Instead of burning the letters, Elias stuffed them into his coat, then turned to the stationmaster. "I don’t know what to do," he admitted. "But I can’t let them stay buried. They’re my father’s work. They’re *mine*." The stationmaster’s hands trembled. "Then you’ll have to live with it," he said. Elias nodded and left, walking into the storm without looking back. The next morning, the stationmaster found the letters gone—not burned, but carried away by the wind. The police never found him, and the letters vanished into the mist, leaving only the echo of a story that could never be told.
