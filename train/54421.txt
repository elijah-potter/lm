Opportunities for learning and problem solving develop as the problem unfolds
(Edens, 2000). For example, in the problem-based learning model Roots and
Wings, students participate in simulations that require them to make decisions
(Slavin, et al., 1994). In one scenario, they are playing different roles in a debate
over environmental legislation, and they need to learn the necessary knowledge
and research skills in order to participate in an informed manner. Learning
Expeditions requires students to investigate questions like, “How can we tell if a
community is thriving?”
