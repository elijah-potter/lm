It’s estimated that one in five Americans get skin cancer in their lifetime. Most skin cancers aren’t serious and are treatable. However, in some instances, long-term effects or death is a possibility. Outcomes are largely influenced by how soon cancers are detected and treated, hence why prevention is incredibly important. 

SPOT skin cancer was launched by the American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) to raise awareness surrounding this issue. The campaign works with corporations, communities, medical partners, the media, and others to help lower the prevalence of skin cancers in the US. This is done through fundraising and acquiring grant funding for shade structures and offering free skin cancer screenings in communities and also through facilitating initiatives to further raise awareness about skin cancer. 

The movement behind “know your spots” is to encourage people to familiarize themselves with the warning signs of skin cancer. There are several resources available through the AAD to educate and inform people about how to self-examine their skin. Some “spots” are normal whereas some could be problematic. The key is to know what to look for and to keep monitoring potentially changing spots.
