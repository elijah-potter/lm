A U.S. citizen is a person who was born in the United States or specific U.S. territories, has parents that are U.S. citizens or has obtained citizenship through naturalization. The specific U.S. territories that this statement applies to include the U.S. Virgin Islands, the Northern Mariana Islands, Puerto Rico, and Guam.

A U.S. national mostly refers to a person born in an outlying U.S. possession such as American Samoa but can also be used to describe someone who is a U.S. citizen as well. Those born in outlying areas are protected by the U.S. government, able to obtain a U.S. passport, able to live in the U.S. and apply for naturalization, and are entitled to consular protection from the U.S. 

The main difference is that U.S. nationals are restricted from voting in any U.S. election or applying for jobs that require citizenship.
