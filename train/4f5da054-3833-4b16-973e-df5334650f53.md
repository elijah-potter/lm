A single misplaced screw in a nuclear reactor’s control rod assembly caused a cascade failure, triggering a partial meltdown that contaminated 10 square kilometers of surrounding land. The delayed response by safety protocols, compounded by bureaucratic delays in emergency protocols, resulted in 31 fatalities and forced the evacuation of 50,000 residents. The incident exposed systemic vulnerabilities in regulatory oversight and emergency response, leading to stricter international safety standards and a decade-long review of global nuclear plant maintenance practices.
