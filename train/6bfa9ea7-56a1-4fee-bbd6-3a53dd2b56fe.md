In the quiet corner of the old library, beneath the flickering glow of a single candle, a young scholar named Elias sat cross-legged, his fingers tracing the edge of a worn, leather-bound book. The cover bore an ancient sigil—a serpent coiled around a crescent moon—that had once belonged to his grandmother, a reclusive astrologer. The book was titled *"The First Echo"* and contained a prophecy Elias had long dismissed as nonsense.

A knock at the door shattered his focus. Elias stood, his breath catching as the door creaked open. A stranger stood there, dressed in a tattered cloak, their face obscured by a hood. "You’re Elias," the stranger said, voice rough with an accent Elias didn’t recognize. "They say you’re the only one who can see what the book predicts."

The stranger pulled a dagger from their belt. "The serpent must wake. The moon must bleed." Their eyes locked onto the sigil on Elias’s book. "You’ve been chosen."

Elias hesitated. "What if I refuse?"

The stranger smirked. "Then the moon will bleed anyway."

That night, Elias returned to the book. The candlelight pooled around the serpent’s image, and for the first time, he saw it—not as a symbol, but as a key. The pages turned by their own volition, revealing a map: a path leading to a ruined temple at the edge of the forest, where the first moon of the season had been stolen centuries ago.

The next morning, Elias followed the map. The forest was alive with whispers, the air thick with magic. He reached the temple’s crumbling stone arch, where a black stone altar stood, its surface carved with the same serpent and moon. At its center lay a vial of blood—old, dark, and pulsing. A voice, neither his own nor the stranger’s, echoed through the chamber: *"The serpent feeds on the moon. The moon feeds on the serpent."*

A figure emerged from the shadows—a woman, her skin like polished obsidian, her eyes twin moons. "You came," she said. "The time has come for the circle to close." She pressed the vial to Elias’s lips. The blood burned. When he opened his eyes, he stood on the library’s floor, the candle still flickering, the book open to the same page.

The stranger was gone. The candle guttered out. And on the library’s far wall, the serpent and moon were now joined—not coiled, but intertwined, a single entity bleeding into the night.
