A weathered wooden dock extends into a misty harbor, its planks groaning under the weight of time. The tide rolls in, swallowing the sand where the last summer’s fireworks left crimson streaks. Beneath the swaying palm trees, a row of crumbling shacks huddle close to the water’s edge, their corrugated roofs sagging under the relentless sun and storms. The children laugh as they chase the gulls, their laughter as fleeting as the shadows cast by the old lampposts, but the adults know this place—*this* sand, *this* wind, *this* scent of salt and wild thyme—isn’t lost to time. The ocean here doesn’t just wash ashore; it *remembers*. The waves, though constant, carve stories into the cliffs, but the stories themselves are older than the first settlers, older than the language that still whispers in the cracks of the old fishing boats. The time between here and the next shore is as permanent as the roots of the fig tree at the edge of the village, which bears fruit that tastes of the earth’s first harvests. This is not a place you leave behind. It’s the skin of a world that remembers.
