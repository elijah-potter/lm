It’s nothing more than a long strip of cement lined with metallic pink benches behind the husk of an old shopping mall, but for many, it is a gateway to new experiences. No matter the time of day, there are always people coming and going, hopping onto buses adorned with wacky advertisements and flashing white route numbers. Often times you can see people sleeping on or underneath the benches or feeding the already fat street pigeons. Ever since the mall closed down, it’s been kind of a free-for-all at this bus station. No security guards or rule enforcers, just one person crammed into a tiny green ticket booth behind bulletproof glass. Most people don’t mind. They’re just trying to get where they need to go, but every once in a while you’ll find someone who clearly isn’t accustomed to public transportation clutching their pearls at the rundown nature of it all. Where a seasoned traveler would look upon this place and think, “Sweet! A place to sit down!” The average person would be more concerned with the reddish brown splotches on the cracked sidewalk and the thick layer of dust and grime over everything. There was also the way the sun beat directly onto the metal benches without any barriers, meaning they were hot enough to scorch your skin in the dead of summer. Still, that seemed like a small price to pay in exchange for a $2 trip across a massive city. For many people, this “eyesore” is their ticket to freedom. It allows them to commute to school or work, go to the doctor, and visit their families, all  the things that so many people with cars take for granted.
