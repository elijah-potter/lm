The Met Office says the grass pollen risk is due to increase during the good weather this weekend. Nettle, plantain and dock pollen are forecast to also be airborne and fungal spores are increasing too.
But there is some good news – on Sunday the pollen levels are set to return to a medium level, and stay there on Monday, too.
Hay fever is an allergic reaction to pollen which affects up to one in five people at some point in their life.
Many people find themselves suffering from itchy, red or watery eyes, a runny or blocked nose, sneezing fits, itchy mouth, nose and ears or a cough.
Here are the top tips from both the NHS and the Met Office about how to reduce hay fever symptoms:
- Not going outdoors altogether isn’t realistic, but you can close your windows to stop pollen from getting into your house or car. Keep windows closed when at home and overnight. Most pollen is released in the early morning and falls to ground level in the evenings when the air cools.
- Keep car windows closed when driving and fit a pollen filter to reduce the impact of pollen spores.
- When indoors, there are a number of useful tips to reduce the impact of hay fever symptoms such as: vacuuming regularly, avoid bringing fresh flowers indoors, and be aware that pets can bring pollen in on their fur.
- When outdoors, wear sunglasses to keep pollen out of your eyes. Hay fever sufferers can experience itchy eyes when coming into contact with pollen spores. It is recommended that for any outdoor tasks, such as gardening, hay fever sufferers should wear a mask.
- Don't allow smoking in the house as this will irritate the lining of your nose, eyes, throat and airways, making your hay fever symptoms worse.
- After being outside, shower and wash your hair to remove pollen.
- Avoid drying your clothes outside when pollen counts are high. If you do, shake items before bringing them inside.
- Avoid grassy areas.
- Don’t mow your lawn when the pollen count is high.
- Put Vaseline, a nasal spray or nasal barrier balm inside your nostrils to stop pollen from sticking to the lining of your nose.
- Cut down on alcohol as this worsens hay fever. Beer, wine and spirits contain histamine, the chemical that sets off allergy symptoms in your body so alcohol can make you more sensitive to pollen.
- Try to avoid being outside when the air is warming up (8-10am) and cooling down (5-7pm) as this is when the pollen count is the highest.
- Exercise regularly.
- Eat foods rich in omega 3 and 6 essential fats such as oily fish, nuts, seeds and their oils as they contain anti-inflammatory properties which may help to reduce symptoms.
- Reduce stress - According to Allergy UK, hay fever can be exacerbated by stress levels. Almost seven out of 10 stressed-out hay fever sufferers rate their symptoms as unbearable or debilitating. As stress levels drop, symptoms become milder.
The Met Office says: “Hay fever sufferers can benefit from a wide range of medication which can be prescribed by your GP, or alternatively purchased over the counter from your local pharmacy.
“Your GP can help you find the most appropriate treatment for you especially if you also have asthma and other allergies. Treatments include antihistamine tablets or nasal sprays, corticosteroid nasal sprays and drops, nasal decongestants, eye drops, immunotherapy, and alternative therapies.”
