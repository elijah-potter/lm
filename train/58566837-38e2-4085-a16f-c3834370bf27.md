The old clock in the hallway ticked past midnight, its hands creeping like skeletal fingers across the brass dial. Outside, rain lashed against the grimy windows, turning the alley into a silvered river. **Elias** wiped the condensation from his glasses and leaned against the desk, fingers tracing the edges of the worn ledger where his notes still flickered in ink.

The memory was a half-remembered dream—*the library, the scent of aged parchment, the way the spines had whispered secrets if you listened too closely.* He had been a boy then, his father’s apprentice, but his mind had wandered when he should have been counting coins. Now, the words from that night clung to him like cobwebs: *"The past is a map, but the map is the key."*

His plan was simpler. The ledger held the names of those who could still remember the old ways—the ones who kept the forgotten books, the ones who whispered to the stars. But the city’s enforcers had been hunting him for months, and every step forward meant a step closer to the trap. The rain-soaked streets were his only ally now.

He pulled out the folded map from his coat pocket. The ink was faded, but the lines still held the path to the abandoned warehouse on the outskirts of town. The place where the last librarian had hidden the last surviving archives before they vanished. If he could reach it, he might find the answers—or at least a way to stop the next raid.

But first, he had to move. The clock struck three.
