The last time I saw the old stone bridge over the raging Blackwater, its weathered arches groaned under the weight of autumn leaves and rusted chains. The river below churned with icebergs, its surface a fractured mirror of forgotten battles. The air smelled of damp earth and pine—too familiar, too sharp. A single crow perched on the parapet, its black eyes fixed on me like a warning. The path beneath my feet led only to the crumbling ruins of the watchtower, where the wind howled through the broken windows, carrying echoes of the past. I held my breath, not out of fear, but because I knew what came next. The bridge was gone in an instant, swallowed by the water. The river claimed it whole, and the last thing I saw was the tower’s shattered spire vanishing into the mist.
