A cityscape at dusk glows with neon streaks—blue, violet, and amber filaments weaving through glass towers like liquid light. Streets hum with static, flickering from flickering streetlamps and glowing storefronts, casting eerie reflections in puddles. The sky bleeds through gaps in the urban canopy, a slow, fading aurora of pink and indigo, diffused by smog. Shadows are thin, almost translucent, dissolving into the glow of distant skylights. The air shimmers with heat haze, distorting light into prismatic shards where warm streetlights spill onto sidewalks. The absence of shadows marks the fading day; only light remains—a vast, shifting canvas of artificial and natural radiance.
