The fog curled around the ancient oak, its gnarled roots twisting like serpents beneath the damp earth. The path before her—narrow, crumbling—led straight to the village, its lanterns flickering like dying stars. But the other route, winding through the forest’s back alleys, was longer, its branches heavy with unseen weight. Her boots crunched gravel, the weight of every step a choice.

She hesitated. The clockwork hum of the mill’s wheels echoed in the distance, but the air here was colder, thick with secrets. The stars above were fewer, their light swallowed by the mist, as if the night itself whispered something urgent. The longer path called to her—not for its promise, but for the unknown.

She turned the corner. The forest exhaled.
