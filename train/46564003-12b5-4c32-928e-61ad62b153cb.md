**Exterior:** A misty autumn evening. The sky, a bruised canvas of indigo and gray, thick with the weight of unseen rain. Thunder rumbles faintly, its echoes swallowed by the stillness. A lone streetlamp flickers, casting long shadows across cracked pavement. Raindrops, slow at first, then relentless, patter against the windows like a whisper of sorrow.

**Interior:** Inside, the air is stale with the scent of damp wood and old paper. A fire burns low in the hearth, its embers reflecting in the cracked glass of the fireplace screen. The curtains, heavy with moisture, sway slightly in the draft. A single candle flickers on the table, its wick guttering out as if in defeat.

**A woman** sits at the kitchen table, her fingers tracing the rim of a cup half-filled with black coffee. She doesn’t drink it. The mug rests empty beside her, its surface slick with condensation. Outside, the rain hammers against the window like a drum solo. The wind howls through the trees, carrying the scent of pine and something sharper—bitter, like regret. Her breath fogs the glass, but she doesn’t move to clear it. The world outside mirrors her mood: heavy, aching, and drowning in its own weight.

A child’s laughter drifts from the hallway, high and careless, but the sound dies too soon, swallowed by the creak of a door closing. The woman exhales, a sound like the sigh of a sigh. The fire’s glow flickers. The rain keeps falling.
