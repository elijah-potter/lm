A soft-spoken apology, delivered with hesitant hesitation, slid between the cracks of a tense conversation. The words—*"I’m sorry I didn’t realize how much this hurt you"*—lingered in the air, heavy with the weight of a half-hearted effort. The other person’s lips thinned, their fingers tightening around their coffee cup, but they didn’t snap back. Instead, they exhaled slowly, as if the effort of saying anything else would cost them more than the words themselves.

The silence stretched, thick with the ghost of what might have been. The air smelled of stale coffee and the faint, lingering scent of the argument that had come before. The door creaked open, and the sound of footsteps—someone else’s—faded into the hallway. The moment of near-acceptance dissolved like mist in sunlight.

*"We should’ve handled this better,"* the apology whispered, but the room had already forgotten its weight.
