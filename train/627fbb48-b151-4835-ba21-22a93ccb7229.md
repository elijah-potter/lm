The weight of the promise pressed against my chest like a stone. Every morning, I woke to the same quiet ache—*keep it*—echoing in my throat. Words I had never meant to say, yet they had been spoken. A vow to someone who no longer needed me, a ghost in the room of my own life.

The terms were impossible: time was a thief, and I was its prisoner. The night was long, the days stretched thin as my resolve frayed. I measured hope in stolen breaths, counting the cracks in the ceiling like my own bones. The words were hollow now, but they were still there—stamped into the air like a debt I could never repay.

I tried to outrun it—work, distraction, the hum of screens drowning the voice that whispered, *"You lied."* But the echo followed. The promise was a wound that never healed, a scar that burned brighter than the truth I couldn’t give.

I was not the one who broke. I was the one who couldn’t.
