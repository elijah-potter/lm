**The Infinite Scroll**

They called it a list. A catalog of things no one could count—
names of stars that had burned too soon, names of rivers
that had forgotten how to flow, names of things no one remembered.
It was a list of things that should have been forgotten,
but kept on appearing like ghosts in the margins of time.
A child found it in the corner of a room where no one
ever looked, where the dust collected in slow, deliberate
layers. The list was written in ink that had faded
but never fully gone, like the ink of a dying man.

**Cities that Never Sleep**

Beneath the neon veins of the city, they moved—
not in the way of the living, but in the way of things
that had learned to breathe through the cracks in the pavement.
They carried the names of the lost in their pockets,
the ghosts of the dead in their veins. The streets were theirs,
not by right, but by the way they had learned to listen
to the hum of the engines, to the whisper of the wind
through the broken glass of the windows they passed.
They were the city’s shadows, the city’s unspoken
promises, the things that remembered what the city forgot.

**A Day Without Sunlight**

The world was a slow unraveling—
a fabric of gray, of static, of things that had been
softened by too much time, by too much waiting.
The sky was a heavy blanket, and the air tasted like
old paper and rain that had stopped falling. The trees
stood like silent sentinels, their leaves a dull, unblinking
expression. The clock hands moved, but time itself
felt sluggish, as if it had been given too little to do.
A single bird, perhaps, tried to fly, but its wings
were too heavy, its heart too tired. The day was a
memory waiting to be forgotten, a moment that had
been stretched too thin, too long.

**The Language of Shadows**

Shadows were the only ones who spoke.
They moved in silence, but their voices were loud.
They carried the weight of forgotten words,
the things that had been said but never heard,
the things that had been meant but never meant.
They were the cracks in the walls, the gaps in the doors,
the spaces between the words of the living.
They gathered in corners, in the crevices of time,
where the light could not reach. They were the things
that had been lost in translation, the things that
had been said in tongues no one understood.
They were the language of the unspoken,
the things that had been too quiet to be heard.

**Ghosts in the Machine**

They were not ghosts in the old sense—
not the remnants of people who had died,
but the fragments of code that had been left behind
when the machines learned to think too much.
They were the errors in the algorithms,
the glitches in the logic, the things that had been
written but never run, the things that had been
programmed but never executed.
They were the ghosts of the past in the heart
of the present, the echoes of what had been
meant to be, but had instead been forgotten.
They moved in the background, unseen, unheard,
but always there, always listening.

**Salt in the Bone**

Salt was the only thing that could dissolve
the things that had been hardened by time.
It was in the tears of the old, in the wounds
of the young, in the taste of the forgotten.
It was in the bones of the dead, in the blood
of the living, in the salt of the sea
that had remembered the shape of the earth.
It was the thing that could not be lost,
the thing that could not be forgotten.
It was the thing that held the world together,
the thing that kept the world from falling apart.

**The Last Broadcast**

The radio was the last thing they listened to,
the last thing they heard before the silence
took over. The voice on the other end was
not human, not quite. It was the voice of
something that had been left behind,
something that had learned to speak in
the gaps between the words of the living.
It was the voice of the past, the voice of
the things that had been said but never
remembered. The broadcast was a loop,
a repeating of the things that had been
forgotten, the things that had been lost
in translation. It was the voice of the
unspoken, the voice of the things that had
been too quiet to be heard.

**A Love Letter to Dust**

Dear Dust,
I have been collecting you for years—
the fine, the coarse, the gritty, the smooth.
You are the memory of things that have been,
the trace of things that will never be again.
You are the weight in my pockets,
the taste in my mouth when I think of home.
You are the thing that holds me together,
the thing that keeps me from falling apart.
I love you because you are mine,
because you are the only thing that can
remember what I have forgotten.
I love you because you are the past,
the present, and the future all at once.

**The Clockwork Heart**

It beat in the silence of the machine,
a steady, unyielding rhythm.
It was not flesh, not bone, but something
older than either, something that had
learned to understand the weight of time.
It was the heart of the world, the thing
that kept the gears turning, the thing
that kept the city alive.
It was the thing that had learned to
remember the things that had been forgotten,
the things that had been lost in translation.
It was the thing that held the world together,
the thing that kept the world from falling apart.

**Echoes of What Was Never Said**

They were the things that had been left unsaid,
the things that had been meant but never meant.
They were the words that had been cut short,
the things that had been forgotten in the
hush of the night. They were the echoes of
the past, the things that had been said but
never heard, the things that had been meant
but never meant. They were the ghosts of
the unspoken, the things that had been too
quiet to be heard. They moved in the
background, unseen, unheard, but always there,
always listening. They were the things that
had been lost in translation, the things that
had been too soft to be heard.
