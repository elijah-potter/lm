A shadow fell across the table as the first report was read aloud, the paper trembling slightly with the weight of the numbers. The air smelled of old parchment and damp leather. "The shipment is delayed by two days," the clerk repeated, voice steady but hollow. Outside, the rain hammered against the windows, a rhythmic, relentless drumming. The meeting’s leader exhaled sharply, fingers steepled. "Unforeseen complications," they murmured, but their gaze flickered to the clock—twelve minutes until the next briefing.

The plan, forged in secrecy over coffee and whispered confidences, now sat abandoned like a half-eaten cake on the floor. The map, once dotted with precise red pins, now lay blank. A single cigarette butt glinted in the corner of the ashtray, evidence of the man who’d just stood, stretched, and left without a word. The other members exchanged glances—some relief, some resignation. The door creaked shut behind him, sealing the failure.
