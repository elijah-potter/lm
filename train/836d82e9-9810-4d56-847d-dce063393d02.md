The rain started before dawn, turning the city streets into a slick, reflective mirror. I sat on the curb near the old diner, my coat damp, watching the neon sign flicker weakly as it struggled to compete with the streetlights. The sound of sirens had been distant, but now, closer. Then the crowd started forming.

A group of men in dark suits and tactical gear moved with purpose, their faces obscured by masks. They had a woman in handcuffs, her wrists already bruised. She was crying, but her voice was muffled. One of them took her by the arm and pushed her toward a waiting van. The others followed, closing the doors behind them. The van pulled away, leaving only the faint smell of ozone and fear in the air.

I stood up, my boots splashing in the puddles, and watched as they drove off. The diner’s windows were dark, but a flicker of movement inside caught my eye. Someone was watching, too. The sirens had stopped. The city was quiet again, but something was different.
