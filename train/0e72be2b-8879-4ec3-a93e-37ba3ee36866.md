You shouldn’t be here.

The neon glow of the alley flickered like a dying ember, casting long shadows across the cracked pavement. Your breath misted in the cold air, but you didn’t shiver—not because you were cold, but because your body remembered the weight of what you’d done. The key in your pocket felt heavier than it should. You had taken it from the dead man’s pocket, the one who’d been slumped against the dumpster, his fingers still clutching the rusted lock.

The door behind you groaned as you pushed it open. Inside, the air smelled of stale beer and cheap perfume. The bar was half-empty, the only light coming from a single, dim bulb hanging over the counter. Your fingers trembled as you reached for the drink on the bar—another stolen one. The bartender barely glanced up.

*"Another?"*

You didn’t answer. You just watched as the bartender slid the glass toward you, the ice clinking against the glass like a warning.

Outside, the alley was silent. The dead man’s body still lay there, the key still in his hand. You didn’t look back.

The bartender’s voice cut through the haze of your thoughts.

*"You really think you can keep running?"*
