A quiet village nestled in the valley of mist-shrouded mountains, where the church bell’s chime lingers like whispered prayer. Stepping into the old cobblestone plaza, the hum of daily life—rustling paper, distant laughter—dissolves into the soft murmur of a single candle’s flame flickering on a windowsill. The air is thick with the scent of damp earth and woodsmoke, carried by a breeze that carries only the distant cry of a sparrow and the occasional sigh of a draft in the stone arches of the cathedral’s cloister. Inside the library, shelves groan under the weight of leather-bound tomes, their pages turned slowly by the gentle press of fingers. The only sound is the turn of a quill in a well-worn ledger, the clink of ink on parchment, and the occasional, hushed voice of an old scholar correcting a young apprentice’s handwriting. A single lantern casts a warm glow on the floor, where a child plays with a wooden puzzle, their breath visible in the cold morning air. The village square at dusk, where lanterns cast long shadows, and the only conversation is the murmur of a mother’s voice, half-sung, as she tells her child a story.
