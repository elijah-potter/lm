**The storm raged outside—blackened clouds swallowing the sky, lightning splitting the air in jagged, electric seams. Rain lashed the glass like a thousand sharp fingers, each droplet a whisper of sorrow. Inside, the air was thick with the scent of damp earth and old books, the hum of the wind through the cracks in the windows muffled by the weight of despair.**

A single candle flickered on the table, its flame trembling as if afraid to burn too brightly. The walls seemed to breathe in the dim light, their corners hollow with the silence of a heart too heavy to speak. The curtains, once soft as silk, now hung like shrouds, caught in the relentless wind. Every shadow moved with purpose—each one a specter of the past, waiting.

The protagonist stood in the center of the room, arms crossed over a chest that felt too heavy, too empty. The weight of the world pressed down, not just from the storm but from the words they couldn’t say, the promises broken, the love left unspoken. The mirror above the fireplace reflected nothing—only the void between them.

Outside, the storm finally let out a breath, the rain turning to a drizzle. The sky, once a storm-wracked canvas, began to clear—just enough to reveal the grayness of a world that had grown weary. The wind died, leaving behind only the hollow ache of what had been.

And in the silence, the first light of dawn crept in, slow and stubborn, as if refusing to erase what had been buried beneath the tempest.
