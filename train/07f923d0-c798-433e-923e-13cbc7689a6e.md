The chronal fissure in the abandoned clock tower at Blackthorn Vale—its gears unbalanced since the 1897 earthquake. Time bends there: the sun sets *twice* in one day if you linger too long, and the hourglass runs backward in the broken lantern. Locals whisper that the tower’s founder, a mathematician named Elias Veyne, died when his own ticking clock stopped. Some say the clock doesn’t just show time—it *erases* it. The last recorded sighting of the tower’s "dissonant hour" was in 1943, when a group of astronomers vanished after studying the clock’s distortions. The valley itself seems to hold a pocket where physics unravels—objects freeze mid-motion, and shadows stretch like smoke. If you press the eastern clock face with your palm, the hour hand *speaks* in fragments of the future, though no one knows what to make of it. The air hums with static, and the wind carries the faint, metallic tang of rusted seconds. Some say the tower’s clock is still running. Others claim it’s been silent for decades.
