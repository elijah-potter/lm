A small, quiet village gathering in early autumn, where the usual rustic wooden huts and thatched roofs were adorned with mismatched, vintage decorations. A hand-painted banner in faded red and blue read *"First Annual ‘Unconventional Harvest Festival’"*—ironic given the lack of fireworks, live music, or the usual bustle. The centerpiece was a circular, half-buried "altar" in the village square, covered in cobwebs and half-eaten pumpkins, surrounded by mismatched lanterns, a single, cracked teapot, and a stack of books labeled *"For the Unseen Guests"* (with no names).

Guests—mostly elderly villagers, a few strangers in mismatched costumes (a man in a 19th-century military coat, a woman in a Victorian-era dress with modern neon socks), and one child in a tiny, patched-up spacesuit—danced around the altar to a playlist of obscure folk, electronic, and jazz tracks. The food was a medley of forgotten recipes: dried fruits, pickled vegetables, and a single, suspiciously "glowing" mushroom-shaped dessert. Conversations drifted between references to "the old ways" and whispered theories about a "hidden purpose."

The climax came when a figure in a tattered lab coat emerged from the woods, holding a rusted magnifying glass and a notebook with diagrams of celestial alignments. The crowd murmured, then gasped as the lanterns flickered—one by one, they extinguished, leaving only the single glowing mushroom in the center. The villagers parted to reveal a hidden platform where the "astronomer" stood, holding a small, golden orb. As the orb pulsed faintly, the festival’s banner slowly unfurled into a new design: *"This Year, We Honor the Void."* The crowd stood in stunned silence, some clutching their heads, others whispering about "the last gathering of the old order."
