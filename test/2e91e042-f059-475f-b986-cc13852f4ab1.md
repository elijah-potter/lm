The city of Maplewood had always been quiet, the kind of quiet that comes from living between the peaks of the Appalachian foothills and the endless fields of corn. The roads were smooth, the air smelled like fresh-cut grass and something metallic from the factories in the distance, and the neighbors were polite, even if they kept to themselves. But that was before Liam Carter found the abandoned house on Maple Street.

It was a small, two-story structure, its paint peeling in places, the windows boarded up. The neighbors had called it "The Hollow" for years, but Liam knew better. That house had a history, and it wasn’t just the stories passed down through generations of locals who avoided it.

One rainy afternoon in October, Liam was walking home from school when he spotted it—a flicker of light in the window, barely visible through the rain-soaked glass. He hesitated, then knocked. No answer. He tried the door, but it was locked. Still, curiosity got the better of him, and he climbed the rickety porch steps.

Inside, the air was thick with dust, but there was something else—an odd, metallic tang. The walls were lined with shelves filled with books, their spines worn from countless readings. On the table in the center of the room, a single candle burned, casting long shadows. Liam didn’t realize he was holding his breath until he heard it—a whisper, barely audible over the rain.

*"Find me."*

He turned, and there, half-hidden behind the shelves, was a figure wrapped in tattered black clothes. The face was obscured, but Liam saw the eyes—dark and glowing faintly in the dim light. The figure raised a hand, and the candle’s flame flickered.

"You shouldn’t be here," the figure said, voice like dry leaves.

Liam stumbled back. "I—I don’t know what you mean."

The figure stepped forward, revealing a face that wasn’t quite human. Its skin was pale, almost translucent, and its lips moved silently.

"Your ancestors," the figure whispered. "They left you here. They left *everything* here. But the Hollow doesn’t give up easily."

Liam’s hands shook. "What do you want?"

The figure tilted its head. "You’re the first in centuries to open this door. And the first to hear my name."

Liam’s pulse pounded in his ears. "Name?"

The figure’s lips curved into something that wasn’t quite a smile. "Kael. Kael of the Hollow."

That night, Liam dreamed of rain-soaked streets and whispers in the dark. He woke with a start, his sheets damp with sweat. The next morning, he told his parents he was going to the library, but he really went to the Hollow. He didn’t knock this time. He just walked in, his boots crunching on the broken glass beneath the door.

Inside, the air was still. The candle on the table had gone out, but the room felt warmer than before. Liam knelt down, brushing his fingers over the old floorboards. Something cold and hard pressed against his palm.

He pulled it out—a small, silver key, smooth and unmarked. His fingers tingled.

His phone buzzed. A text from his sister, Emma: *Liam, are you okay? You didn’t answer your phone yesterday.*

He looked at the key in his hand, then at the figure in the corner, half-hidden again. The candle flickered.

The Hollow wasn’t giving up. Neither was he.
