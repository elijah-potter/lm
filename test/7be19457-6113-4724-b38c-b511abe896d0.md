The street was too loud—screams, music, and a chaotic throng of people pushing past, their laughter and chatter drowning out the sound of the parade’s marching band. I kept my head down, elbows tucked tight against my sides, watching through the haze of sweat and the stench of overcrowded bodies. The floats passed by too fast, their designs whizzing by in a blur of colors and moving limbs, each figure in costume a blur of movement and artificial limbs. The crowd was a sea of faces—some smiling, some glaring, their expressions a mix of excitement and aggression. I had to move, but my legs wouldn’t listen, stuck in the grip of someone’s shoulder. A child’s toy cannon went off, sending a spray of confetti that stung my eyes. The noise was unbearable. I wanted to disappear. The last thing I saw was the crowd’s backs turning as the parade faded into the distance, but not before the weight of them pressed against me one last time.
