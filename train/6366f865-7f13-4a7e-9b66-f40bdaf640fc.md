Smooth, polished obsidian sheets reflecting fractured light like shattered mirrors, their edges sharp enough to cut through leather gloves. Underfoot, worn river stones glistening with damp moss, their rough surfaces absorbing the faint scent of wet earth. A rusted metal gate, its patina deep blue, creaks slightly under a creeping vine of ivy, whose silken threads weave between the bars. The air hums with the whisper of wind through ancient wooden planks—some cracked, others warped by centuries, their grain swirling in time-lost patterns. Warm, damp air clings to the skin like a second layer, thickened by the slow decay of centuries.
