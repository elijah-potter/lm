Quantum dot structures in semiconductor nanocrystals exhibit size-tunable plasmonic resonances where localized surface plasmon modes exhibit anomalous dispersion near the resonance wavelength. In superfluid helium-3 (³He), atoms can form a macroscopic quantum vortex lattice where light interacts with quantized vortices, exhibiting helical light propagation with helical polarization modes. At the perovskite phase transition in CsPbBr₃, spontaneous symmetry breaking causes light to couple with soft optical phonon modes, producing nonreciprocal optical activity akin to a magnetic field. In topological insulators, edge states allow light to propagate along helical paths with chiral polarization, demonstrating non-trivial topological protection. In graphene, plasmonic hotspots near metallic nanoparticles exhibit anomalous light absorption and emission where light interacts with collective electron oscillations beyond classical Mie scattering regimes.
