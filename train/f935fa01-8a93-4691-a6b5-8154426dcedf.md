A hollowed-out vow, like a glass half-empty where the liquid is frozen.
The words were sharp, but the weight of them felt like a promise carved into stone.
To keep it meant bending the rules of time—stretching the edges of belief until they snapped.
Every morning, she woke to the same quiet certainty: *I will do this one thing more.*
But the thing was always the thing she couldn’t.

The sky overtook the horizon, and the sun became a liar, painting everything in shades of gray.
They said, *"It’s not about the what, but the how."* But how was a question with no answer.
The hands that shook were tired of moving, the lips that spoke were dry with the weight of words they couldn’t undo.

She sat at the kitchen table, a half-eaten sandwich between her fingers.
*"One more day."* The words tasted like ash.
The clock ticked like a funeral bell, counting down to the moment the promise would be a memory.
Or the moment it would become something else entirely—
a wound that didn’t heal, a shadow that followed her everywhere.
The impossible was already there, waiting to claim what she couldn’t break.
