A rain-soaked alleyway hummed with the faint glow of flickering streetlamps. The cobblestones beneath **Kai**’s boots were slick with moisture, his breath visible in the cold air as he clenched his fists around the crumpled note tucked into his pocket—*"Meet me at the old mill at midnight. Bring the knife."* The name **Lena** had been scrawled in shaky ink above it, but the last lines were smudged, as if torn or erased.

He had waited weeks. Every evening, he checked the clock, calculating the minutes until the next full moon, when the mill’s rusted gates would creak open under the weight of the river’s tide. The promise had been made in a bar the night before, a storm raging outside, the air thick with the scent of whiskey and desperation. **"You’ll do it,"** she’d whispered, her fingers tracing the scar on his wrist where the blade had grazed him—his only warning that she wouldn’t keep her end.

Now, the mill stood abandoned, its skeletal structure half-collapsed into the marsh, the water lapping at the foundations like a living thing. The air smelled of damp earth and iron, the kind of scent that made his stomach twist. He had come prepared—his coat was soaked, his gloves damp, and the knife at his belt was the one he’d stolen from the old butcher’s shop, the blade cold and familiar.

A rustle from the reeds. A twig snapped.

Kai froze, his pulse hammering in his ears. The darkness swallowed the sound, but he knew it was there. A shape moved—tall, too still, the way people move when they’re not quite human.

*"You came,"* Lena’s voice slithered through the night, smooth as oil. She stood half-buried in the reeds, her cloak draped over her shoulders, her face half-lit by the sickly glow of a dying lantern. The note was gone. In its place was a grin that didn’t reach her eyes, her fingers curled around a different blade, one that gleamed like polished bone.

*"You always were a good liar,"* she murmured, stepping closer. *"But promises are just words until you’re forced to cut them."* The mill’s gates groaned, opening wider, the water rushing in to drown the last embers of hope.
