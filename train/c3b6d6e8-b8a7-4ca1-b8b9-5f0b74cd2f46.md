The kitchen light flickered as Maria wiped down the counters, her fingers brushing against the empty spot on the table where her favorite vase once stood. The ceramic glint of its polished surface had been a daily anchor, but now it was gone—silent, unseen. She paused, turning to the window, where the morning sun cast long shadows across the floor. Outside, the neighbor’s dog barked, its voice sharp and abrupt, as if echoing the same hollow ache.

She exhaled, running a hand over her dark curls. "Again," she murmured, turning back to the table. The absence wasn’t just a missing object—it was the weight of something else: the quiet hum of her mornings, the rhythm of her life. The vase had been more than glass; it had been a promise. And now, it was just the space where the promise had slipped away.
