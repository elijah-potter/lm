The library was always my safe place. The warm glow of the fluorescent lights cast long shadows across the stacks, and the scent of old books and coffee lingered in the air like a promise. I sat cross-legged on the floor, my fingers tracing the spine of *The Last Light of the Moon*, a book I’d read a hundred times before. Outside, the world was loud—cars honking, people shouting, the distant hum of the city. But here, inside these walls, I could disappear.

Today was different.

My dad’s voice cut through the quiet like a knife. "We’re moving to New York tomorrow." He didn’t sound angry. Just… finished. Like he’d run out of things to say. "The job’s there. Big opportunity. You’ll like it." He kept his eyes on his phone, but I saw the way his jaw tightened. "You can start tomorrow. We’ll pack up the apartment tonight."

I swallowed hard. The apartment was ours. It had been for as long as I could remember. My mom had left years ago, but the house still smelled like her perfume—lavender and something sharper, like salt and old paper. The couch where I’d spent my first sleepovers, the bookshelf where I’d memorized every line of *Harry Potter* and *Percy Jackson*. My bedroom was a mess of half-finished projects—sketches, half-assembled robotics kits, a journal I’d never finished.

I didn’t want to move.

But I also didn’t want to fight.

I packed my things quickly. My backpack was full of notebooks, a water bottle, a spare charger, and the one book I’d been saving for this exact moment: *The Last Light of the Moon*. I tucked it into my bag like it was a secret. Maybe it was. Maybe it was the only thing I could take with me.

The drive to the airport was worse than I expected. My dad didn’t say much, but his fingers drummed on the steering wheel, his knuckles white. "You’ll make friends," he said finally, like it was a given. "There’s a ton of people who need a job. You’ll find yours."

I didn’t answer.

At the gate, I stood in line, my stomach twisting. The flight was long. Too long. I’d been dreaming about New York for years—skyscrapers, Broadway, the smell of rain on pavement. But I’d never imagined it would feel like this. Like I was being handed over, like I wasn’t even in the picture anymore.

The plane took off, and for a second, I thought I might throw up. But then I remembered the book in my bag. The book I’d been waiting for. Because no matter what happened here, I had something else to fight for.

The window seat was empty. I sat down, my breath shallow. The flight attendant came over, and I ordered a soda, my hands shaking. Outside, the city stretched below us, a glittering city of lights. It was beautiful. It was terrifying.

And then I opened my bag.

The book was still there. *The Last Light of the Moon*. Inside, I found a note tucked between the pages:

*"If you’re reading this, you’re already lost. But that doesn’t mean you’re alone. Keep going. The light never goes out."*

I didn’t know who had written it, but I knew it was meant for me. Because sometimes, you don’t need a hero. Sometimes, you just need to keep walking.
