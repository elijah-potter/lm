The dim glow of the streetlamp flickered as **Lena** paused mid-step, her fingers brushing against the cold metal of the bench. **"You’re leaving?"** she whispered, voice cracking. **Jake** froze, his jaw clenched as he turned back—his coat sleeves rolled up, sleeves too short for his broad frame. **"I—I don’t know what came over me,"** he muttered, hand gripping the handle of his backpack. The words hung between them, raw and unfiltered.

A beat. Then, softly: **"We’ve been pretending this never happened."**

Silence. The weight of unsaid things pressed between them like a shared secret. Lena’s breath hitched. Jake exhaled, slow and steady. **"Then let’s say it,"** he said. **"I don’t just want to be friends."** She stared at the cracked pavement, her heart pounding. **"I know,"** she admitted. **"I’ve been waiting for you to say it."**

He reached out, fingers brushing hers—just once—before stepping closer. The moment stretched, thick with possibility, until the streetlight’s glow blurred into something brighter. They stood there, two ghosts of the past, finally seeing each other for the first time.
