## A Counseling Session

**Setting:** A comfortably furnished therapist's office. Soft lighting, neutral colors, and a view of a small, well-tended garden.

**Characters:**

* **Dr. Eleanor Vance:** A seasoned therapist, known for her calm demeanor and insightful approach.
* **Liam Carter:** A young professional experiencing significant stress related to his career.

**(The session begins with Liam seated across from Dr. Vance. He appears visibly tense.)**

**Liam:** I feel… overwhelmed. That feels inadequate, actually. It's more than overwhelmed. It’s like I’m constantly bracing for something, and I don't even know what it is.

**Dr. Vance:** (Nods gently, maintaining eye contact) It sounds incredibly difficult to live with that constant feeling of anticipation, especially when you’re unsure of what you're anticipating. Could you tell me a little more about what’s been contributing to this feeling?

**Liam:** Work, mostly. I recently took on a new project, a really significant one for the firm. It's high-profile, and the pressure to deliver is intense. I keep thinking about all the things that could go wrong. The potential for failure feels enormous.

**Dr. Vance:** It’s understandable that a high-stakes project would generate anxiety. It sounds like you’re carrying a considerable weight of responsibility. You mentioned potential failure – can you elaborate on what that looks like to you? What are the specific consequences you’re imagining?

**Liam:** Losing the client, obviously. Damage to my reputation. Letting my team down. It’s a cascade of negative outcomes that keeps running through my head. I find myself replaying scenarios, analyzing every email, every meeting… trying to anticipate and prevent any possible misstep.

**Dr. Vance:** (Leans forward slightly) So, you’re actively engaged in problem-solving, trying to mitigate those potential negative outcomes. That’s a proactive approach, and it suggests a strong sense of responsibility. However, it also sounds exhausting. Are you finding it difficult to switch off, to relax, even when you’re not at work?

**Liam:** (Sighs) That’s the worst part. I used to enjoy evenings, weekends. Now, I find myself thinking about the project constantly. Sleep is… sporadic, at best. I'm snapping at my partner, and I hate that.

**Dr. Vance:** (Offers a reassuring smile) It’s clear this stress is impacting multiple areas of your life. It's admirable that you recognize the effect it’s having on your relationships. It takes courage to acknowledge that.  Let’s explore this a bit further. You mentioned snapping at your partner. What’s the underlying emotion you believe is driving that behavior?

**Liam:** I think… it’s frustration. Frustration with myself, mostly. I feel like I’m failing, even though I haven’t actually failed at anything yet. It's the *potential* for failure that's consuming me.

**Dr. Vance:** That's a very insightful observation. You’re experiencing anxiety about a future event, a hypothetical failure. It sounds like your mind is creating a narrative of potential doom. We can work on challenging that narrative, on separating your thoughts from reality.  Have you ever considered how you typically approach challenges in other areas of your life?

**Liam:** I'm a planner. I like to have contingencies, to anticipate problems. It’s always served me well.

**Dr. Vance:** That’s a valuable skill, and it’s clearly been a strength for you. However, it seems that in this situation, that tendency towards planning and anticipating has become a source of distress. We can explore ways to harness that planning ability in a more balanced way, to acknowledge potential challenges without allowing them to consume you. Perhaps we can examine the difference between proactive preparation and unproductive worry.  For this week, I’d like you to try a simple exercise: When you notice yourself spiraling into these anticipatory thoughts, try to pause, take a deep breath, and ask yourself, "What is the *actual* probability of this negative outcome occurring?"  We can discuss your observations next session.

**Liam:** (Nods slowly) Okay. That sounds… manageable. It’s just a different way of looking at things.

**Dr. Vance:** Precisely. It’s about shifting your perspective. Remember, you're not failing. You’re facing a challenge, and we’re here to navigate it together.



**(The session concludes with Liam appearing slightly less tense, a glimmer of hope visible in his expression.)**
