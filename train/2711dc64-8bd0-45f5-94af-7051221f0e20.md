The café was dimly lit, the hum of conversation a distant murmur through the thick glass. I sat there, pen hovering over the empty page, staring at the half-finished first paragraph. It was supposed to be about the old library on the edge of town, its shelves whispering secrets to those who dared listen too closely. Instead, my fingers had traced the words too quickly, the lines too sharp, like I was writing someone else’s story.

I should have started with the fire escape, the way it creaked under the weight of a hundred years. The way the wind carried the scent of damp stone and old books, how it felt like the place still breathed. But no. I was too eager, too sure. I’d written about the girl who found the lost novel under the floorboards, how she traced the same handwritten note I had seen in the library’s archives. How she held it like a talisman, her fingers trembling with the weight of the story she carried.

I should have waited. Should have let the scene unravel, let the reader piece it together like a puzzle with too many missing pieces. But the first draft was a rush—too much, too fast. I wanted to finish. I wanted to be done.

Now, the page is blank again. The pen sits idle, a relic of a decision I’ve since outgrown. The story shifts again. This time, it begins with the fire escape, the first time I saw it from the window, the way it stretched down, disappearing into the alley like a thread leading to something else entirely.

The girl isn’t the one who finds the book. It’s the librarian, old and sharp-eyed, who recognizes the note. She doesn’t tell anyone. She keeps it in her pocket, presses it against her ribs like a secret she’s too afraid to share. The novel isn’t lost. It’s already out there, buried in the cracks of time, waiting for the right hands to dig it up.

The café door swings open. A customer walks in, carrying a bag of stale pastries. The scent of cinnamon and something sweet fills the air. Outside, the city pulses—a thousand lives, a thousand stories, all waiting to be told. I should start there. Start with the noise, the chaos. Start with the chaos of a story that doesn’t begin with a girl, but with a librarian who knows too much and doesn’t know how to stop.
