A floating market in a storm-wracked harbor, where wooden stalls drift on the tide, their sails half-mast and canvas torn. Vendors in patched raincoats haggle over lanterns that flicker between flame and shadow, their wares—clothes stitched from salvaged fabric, fish that still twitch in cracked iceboxes—shifting with the currents. The sky is a bruise of lightning and dusk, the air thick with the scent of salt, damp wood, and the distant cry of gulls. The docks, once bustling, are now ghostly, the only movement a child’s laughter lost in the wind, followed by the creak of a ship’s hull as it tilts, finding its new balance. The water runs black with oil and secrets.
