**The Whispering Walls**

The first half of the room smelled of rain and old books, their spines cracked like dried leaves. The air hummed with the quiet rhythm of a typewriter’s keys, the kind that hadn’t been used in decades. Outside, the city pulsed with a distant, unnatural pulse—siren notes that had long since lost their urgency. This was no place for the living, just for ghosts who remembered the world before the storm.

I sat at the desk, fingers tracing the edge of an open ledger where names had been scrawled in ink that had faded to silver. The paper beneath me was thick with the weight of forgotten decisions. The clock above the fireplace ticked like a metronome counting down to something that had already passed. The fire burned low, casting long shadows that danced like figures from a forgotten play.

Then the door creaked.

A shift.

The scent of copper and damp earth replaced the must of parchment. The walls, once so still, seemed to breathe. The room was no longer a museum piece but a stage for something that had been waiting. The air thickened, pressing against my skin like the first frost of winter. The typewriter’s keys, silent for so long, now rattled with a sound that wasn’t human.

The ledger’s pages fluttered open on their own. The names above them had been crossed out, replaced by new ones—names that had never been written before. The ink glowed faintly, as if still alive, as if they were whispering.

Outside, the city’s pulse sharpened. The sirens weren’t distant anymore. They were here, wrapped in the same breathless silence that had settled over the room. The fire roared—not from embers, but from something that burned hotter, deeper. The clock’s hands spun backward, and when they stopped, it was not at midnight but at the dawn of something that had never been.

The typewriter’s keys clicked in unison, one by one, forming words that weren’t yet written. The words spilled into the room like water from a broken dam, filling the space until there was no room left for doubt. The walls were no longer walls. They were a screen, a stage, a stage for a performance that had been rehearsed in the dark since the beginning of time.

And then—

The first half of the story was over.

The second half began.
