The library was quiet except for the hum of the fluorescent lights and the occasional creak of the old floorboards. Liam sat on the edge of his desk, his fingers tracing the spine of the book he’d stolen from the shelf behind him—*The Alchemist* by Paulo Coelho. He had read it cover to cover three nights in a row, but today, he wanted to rewrite it.

He opened his notebook and scribbled the first line in the same handwriting he used for homework:

*"One summer, when the world was too loud and the future was too far away, a boy named Mateo found himself standing in front of a door he’d never expected to open."*

The door was his bedroom. The room was a mess—piles of clothes, a half-eaten pizza on the floor, and a half-drawn sketch of a dragon on the wall. Mateo had drawn it that morning, while his mom was at work and his dad was on his phone. The dragon was supposed to be his lucky charm, but today, it looked like something a five-year-old would draw if they’d seen a fireworks show.

Liam flipped to a fresh page and wrote:

*"The dragon was named Veyra. It had eyes like polished river stones and wings that shimmered in the dim light of his closet. Mateo had found it under his bed the night before, half-buried in a pile of old socks and a broken stuffed tiger. It wasn’t real, but it felt like it was watching him sleep."*

He paused, staring at the words. The dragon’s eyes—pale, almost colorless—made him think of something he’d read in a science book: *How do we know what we see isn’t just our brains filling in the gaps?* The dragon was just a sketch, but for some reason, Mateo believed it was real. Like the time he’d found a coin under his pillow that wasn’t his dad’s. Or the time he’d heard voices in the walls that weren’t there.

Liam’s phone buzzed. It was his best friend, Carlos. His name was already typed in the message field:

*"You up? My mom’s gonna kill me if I’m not home by dinner."*

Liam typed back:

*"Yeah. Just thinking."*

*"About what?"*

*"About if I’m going to be the guy who gets everything right or the guy who’s just trying."*

*"Dude, you’re already the guy who’s trying. That’s more than half the battle."*

Liam smiled. He typed:

*"True."*

---

Outside, the sun set behind the mountains, painting the sky in shades of orange and pink. Mateo sat on the edge of his bed, the notebook in his hands. The dragon’s sketch was still there, faded now but still recognizable. He flipped to the next page and wrote:

*"The first day of sixth grade, Mateo had a new rule: No more secrets. His mom had told him that day—after the coin under the pillow, after the ‘voices,’ after the way his stomach always felt like it was being squeezed when he thought about the dragon. ‘If you keep it from me, I can’t protect you,’ she’d said. ‘And you can’t protect yourself if you don’t tell me what’s bothering you.’"*

He hesitated. He didn’t want to write about his mom’s new rule, but the story was supposed to be about Mateo’s journey. Maybe he’d figure out how to include it later.

---

The next morning, Mateo arrived at school early, his backpack slung over one shoulder. He was late again, but he didn’t care. He’d promised himself he’d be there before the bell this time. His usual spot was by the lockers, but today, he sat on the bench outside the bathroom, staring at his reflection in the cracked mirror.

His eyes were dark, almost black, like the dragon’s. His dad had said he looked like him when he was younger, but Mateo knew the truth. He looked like the things his mind kept hidden away.

A girl in the hallway stopped in front of him, her brown hair pulled into a messy bun. She was wearing a sweater that was too big for her, the sleeves hanging past her wrists.

*"You look like you’re thinking about something,"* she said.

*"Just… trying to figure out if I’m going to mess up or not,"* Mateo said.

She tilted her head. *"You’re not the only one."*

Mateo blinked. *"What?"*

*"Everyone’s doing it. Everyone’s second-guessing themselves. But you know what? Most of them are just as scared as you are. And that’s okay."*

She turned to walk away, but then turned back. *"I’m Sofia. You’re not alone."*

---

Later that week, Mateo found himself in the school’s empty gym after classes. The basketball hoop loomed over him, the floor cold under his sneakers. He’d always been good at basketball, but today, he was playing worse than ever. He kept missing shots, his hands shaking too much.

He sat on the bench, his arms crossed, watching the other kids practice. Some were good. Some were terrible. Some were just trying.

Then he saw her.

Sofia was standing in the center of the court, her arms outstretched, her eyes focused. She didn’t have a ball, but she moved like she was. Like she knew exactly what she was doing.

Mateo watched her for a few minutes, then walked over.

*"You can’t just stand there,"* she said without looking at him. *"You have to move."*

Mateo hesitated, then stepped forward. He didn’t know what she meant, but he tried.

Sofia watched him carefully. *"You’re not moving fast enough. Your feet are too far apart. Your arms aren’t strong enough."*

Mateo’s face burned. *"I don’t know what I’m doing."*

*"Then don’t think about it,"* Sofia said. *"Just do it. And when you mess up, just get up and do it again."*

Mateo looked at her. She was right. He was just trying. And that was enough.

---

The summer was long. Mateo and Sofia spent most of it practicing in the gym, late at night when the other kids were gone. They didn’t talk much—just about basketball, mostly. But Mateo started to feel like he wasn’t alone anymore.

One evening, as they stood outside the gym, the stars above them twinkled like tiny diamonds. Sofia turned to him, her expression serious.

*"I don’t know what’s happening with you,"* she said. *"But I think you’re finally ready to face it."*

Mateo nodded, his throat tight. *"I don’t know what that means."*

*"Just that,"* she said, her voice soft. *"You’re not hiding anymore."*

---

The dragon.

Mateo remembered the first time he’d seen it. It was under his bed, half-buried in a pile of old clothes. He’d thought it was just a toy, but then he’d touched it. It was warm. Almost alive.

He’d never told anyone about it. Not his mom, not his dad, not even Sofia. But today, he was going to.

He went into his room and found the notebook where he’d started his story. He opened it to the first page and wrote:

*"The dragon was real."*

Then he closed the book and put it away. He didn’t know what that meant. But for the first time, he didn’t care.

---

The school year ended, and Mateo didn’t go back. He stayed home, watching the sky, listening to the wind. The dragon was still there, under his bed. It didn’t move, but it didn’t seem to care.

One day, Sofia came to visit. She knocked on his door, her backpack slung over her shoulder.

*"I heard you’re not coming back,"* she said.

Mateo sat on his bed, watching her. *"I’m not."*

*"Why?"*

*"Because I’m done hiding."*

She smiled. *"Good."*

She stayed for a while, talking about basketball, about the future, about the things that made them different. Then she left, and Mateo stood at the window, looking out at the world outside.

The sun was setting, painting the sky in colors he’d never seen before. The dragon was still there, under his bed, watching him.

Mateo closed his eyes. He didn’t know what would happen next. But for the first time, he felt like he was ready.
