Feeling like an outsider in a familiar crowd—every eye on you, every gesture a silent question: *"Why are you here?"* The hum of conversation fades into static, replaced by the weight of unspoken expectations. Clad in a jacket that doesn’t quite match the room’s casual vibe, you adjust your posture, fingers trembling as you clutch your coffee. The scent of the city—smoke, rain, the faint metallic tang of exhaustion—hits you like a physical presence, but the air around you feels *clean*, unmarked by the kind of tiredness that seeps into your bones. A glance at your reflection in the café window: eyes wide, shoulders hunched. The mirror doesn’t lie—you’re not *them*.

A server’s voice cuts through the silence: *"You okay?"* The question lands like a brick. You force a smile, nodding too fast. The noise of the place swells again, but you’re still listening for the sound of a door closing behind someone who *does* belong. The wait is endless. The world moves on without you, and for the first time in years, you realize: you’re not just *lost*. You’re *unseen*.
