Quantum tunneling occurs in subatomic particle accelerators like the Large Hadron Collider (LHC), where particles pass through energy barriers that classically (non-quantum) physics forbids. Photons exhibit anomalous dispersion in metamaterials where their wave behavior can reverse direction under specific conditions.

In nonlinear optics, light can form solitons—self-reinforcing waves that maintain shape despite dispersion, demonstrating coherent behavior akin to fluid dynamics. Superconducting cavities at cryogenic temperatures trap light in a vacuum via cavity quantum electrodynamics (QED), enabling strange interference patterns.
