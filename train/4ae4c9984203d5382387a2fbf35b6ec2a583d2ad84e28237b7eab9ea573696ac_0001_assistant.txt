Turner called herself a "Buddhist-Baptist." As a child, she was brought up in the Baptist church, where her father was a deacon, but as an adult, she converted to Buddhism. In 2016, she told Lion's Roar magazine that she considered herself a Buddhist. As far back as February 15, 1979, Jet ran a picture of Turner with her Buddhist altar on the cover of their magazine. Turner said that the Liturgy of Nichiren Daishonin and Soka Gakkai International was what introduced her to the spirituality of Buddhism. 

In her biography, I, Tina, she said that her friend Valerie Bishop introduced her to Nichiren Buddhism. Bishop taught her the chant nam-myōhō-renge-kyō in 1973. According to Turner in her memoir, Happiness Becomes You, her son Ronnie suggested that chanting might be good for her. She joined her neighborhood chanting group through Soka Gakkai International, and afterward, she noticed positive changes in her life. She said, "I realized that I had within me everyone I needed to change my life for the better." She would chant for four hours a day during the most difficult times in her life. Even though she didn't chant for as long as she grew older, she still made sure to do it every day. In fact, Turner said that chanting was like singing that touched a place inside her, the subconscious mind. She believed this was the "highest" place, and if she communicated with it through chanting, it would tell her what to do. Her chanting is shown in the films What's Love Got to Do with It and Tina.

On August 2, 2005, she met with the 14th Dalai Lama in Einsiedeln, Switzerland. She also got to meet the Swiss-Tibetan Buddhist singer Dechen Shak-Dagsay and cocreated a spiritual music project called Beyond with him and Regula Curti in 2009.
