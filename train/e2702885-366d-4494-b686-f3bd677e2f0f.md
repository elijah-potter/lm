The angle of a scene shifts subtly when you step back, revealing details hidden from closer view. A single tree, once a backdrop, now frames a landscape like a painter’s brushstroke—its bark, once unremarkable, becomes a study in textures. The sun, once a warm glow on your shoulder, casts long shadows that dance across the earth like liquid gold.

Perspective isn’t fixed; it’s the alchemy of what you choose to see. A streetlamp, from one viewpoint, is a source of light; from another, a towering silhouette. Even silence is a perspective—one moment it’s the hum of a city, the next, the whisper of an empty alley. The world isn’t static; it’s a map drawn by the mapmaker inside you. What’s the question you’re asking it to answer?
