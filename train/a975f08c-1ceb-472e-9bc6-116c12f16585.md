A stillness hums with the soft breath of evening,
silent as a breath held tight against a dusk’s embrace.
The world breathes slower—leaves rustle faintly against the wind,
whispering secrets only the trees understand.
A single lullaby, faint and drifting,
echoes through the garden where fireflies
gather like scattered stars, glowing in time.

The hum of distant traffic fades into static,
replaced by the distant chirp of crickets,
a chorus of tiny, unheard voices singing in the grass.
The air smells like damp earth and warm stone,
caressed by the first whisper of rain or the cool press
of morning dew on a forgotten face.

A cup of tea sits untouched, half-empty,
the steam curling into the golden light,
a quiet promise of warmth and rest.
The world is a painting left unobserved—
only the quiet in between the colors.
