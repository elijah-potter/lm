The void hums with an emptiness so vast it warps perception, stretching into horizons that defy measurement. Shadows stretch diagonally across the floor, their edges dissolving into the blackness as if pulled by an unseen force. The walls, smooth and unbroken, recede into the distance at an impossible angle, their curvature blending seamlessly with the sky, which hangs like a bruised bruise above, unnaturally still.

A single flickering light casts long, wavering shadows, but they never settle—always shifting, as though the light itself is moving in a direction that isn’t quite visible. The silence is a physical presence, pressing down like a weight, muffling even the faintest sound from the distant corridors. The air is thin, carrying a faint metallic tang, as if the space itself is slowly dissolving. The floor tilts subtly, as if tilting gravity ever so slightly, making standing feel precarious. Objects, if there were any, would appear to defy gravity entirely, suspended in mid-air or drifting toward the ceiling.

The ceiling, if it can be called that, is a swirling expanse of dark matter, its edges bleeding into the abyss. Stars—if they’re stars—are scattered haphazardly, some too bright to focus on, others too faint to see at all. The perspective is wrong, as though looking up is the same as looking down. The entire space feels like a living, breathing entity, breathing in and out in a rhythm that doesn’t quite match any known physics. Time seems to stretch, or compress, or both—moments feel like ages, ages feel like moments.

The weight of it all presses against the mind, a suffocating quiet that makes thought feel impossible. The void is not empty—it is *alive*, a sentient absence that watches, waits, and eventually, relentlessly, *consumes*.
