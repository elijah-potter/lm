A janitor noticed a single, dust-covered bead of silver on the floor near the old grandfather clock in the lobby. He had been sweeping for twenty minutes without finding anything unusual. The bead glinted faintly in the dim light from the broken ceiling fixture, catching his eye as he paused to wipe his hands on his apron. It was the first time he’d seen it in weeks—ever since the clock’s pendulum had stopped swinging entirely, leaving the hands frozen at 11:11.

Curious, he picked it up. It was cold, heavier than it should have been. When he pressed it against his palm, the metal felt warm. His breath caught as he realized: the bead was not just a relic, but a key. The clock had been designed to release its mechanism only when a specific object was placed at the exact moment its pendulum stopped—every 11:11. The janitor had just discovered the secret to its revival.
