In 2004, Berlin-based architects J Mayer H won a competition to redevelop the Plaza de la Encarnación in Seville, Spain. They proposed the Metropol Parasol -- a four-level timber structure, "the largest ever wooden construction". But making it proved a challenge: the wood could be made to support the building only if the various forces were modelled using powerful software.
"The amount of computing required was enormous because the system uses 8,000 timber elements," says Andre Santer, the project's 48-year old director. "If you change a single parameter, it has implications for almost all the others." Rather than being bolted together, the structure uses steel rods and glue. But that posed another problem: under the baking Seville sun, there was a risk that the adhesive would melt.
"The usual glues liquefied at temperatures higher than 60°C," says Santer. "Sowe investigated a technique to reinforce the glue by reheating it so that it can withstand temperatures up to 75°C." Completion is expected in early 2011.
