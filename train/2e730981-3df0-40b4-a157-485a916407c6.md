Post-flood marshlands stretch across the once-dry valley, blackened silt caked with algae and rust. The air hums with cicadas, their chorus muffled by the new standing water. The old stone bridge, half-submerged, cracks under the weight of driftwood and broken glass. Willows lean toward the sky, their roots exposed like skeletal fingers, while the river—now a slow, silver vein—meets the horizon in a mirror of shifting ripples. The wind carries the scent of damp earth and iron, the taste of something both familiar and alien. The earth remembers its depth.
