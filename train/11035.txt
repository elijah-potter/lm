More than fifty years after Brown v. Board of Education promised equal educational opportunities for all children, the nation must confront a growing crisis of separate and unequal education. Millions of students are still not getting the basic education they need to survive and compete in the 21st century.
Indiana is the 20th most segregated state for African Americans, with 20.9% of Black students in extremely segregated schools (those with a 90-100% minority student body).Indiana is the 19th most segregated state for Hispanics, with 10.6% of Hispanic students in extremely segregated schools (those with a 90-100% minority student body).
A growing body of research documents the educational benefits for all students of racially and ethnically diverse schools. Segregated minority schools are much more likely to be in poor neighborhoods, have lower graduation rates and offer Indiana's minority students far fewer opportunities to take the kinds of academic enrichment courses that prepare them for college.
