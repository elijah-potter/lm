When the first ink spilled across the page, the words were just words on paper.
The plot was a skeleton—bare, unfeeling—until a single line of dialogue cracked it open.
A character’s name, scribbled in the margin, became a living thing when given breath.

The first draft was chaos: a map of ideas, some sharp, others jagged.
Then came the revisions—edits, cuts, a rewrite where the protagonist’s voice emerged like water through stone.
The story was still a plan, but now it was a key, turning the door of a forgotten story into a door of its own.

The writing became telling. A moment of dialogue—*She hesitated, but he didn’t stop*—became a scene where tension coiled like a snake.
The world unfolded not from the writer’s mind, but from the characters’ mouths, their choices bleeding into the pages.

Now, the story is a narrative: a river of words, not just a destination, but a journey.
The plan became a story when it learned to breathe.
