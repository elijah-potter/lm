The morning fog clung to the old mill like a forgotten promise. The river, usually so swift, now moved at a deliberate pace, as if tasting its own sluggishness. Inside, the machinery groaned—not with urgency, but with the quiet resignation of something that had outlasted its purpose.

The painter’s brush moved with the same measured caution, each stroke a half-hearted effort. The canvas, once vibrant with bold strokes, now held only the faintest echoes of color, like memories fading in the corners of a room long abandoned.

Outside, the clock struck noon without fanfare, its chime muffled by the weight of unspoken things. The sky, usually a canvas of fleeting clouds, was today a uniform gray, as if the world had decided to be polite about its own dullness.

A single leaf, detached and unnoticed, drifted toward the edge of the path, a silent testament to the cycle of things—how little things grow, how little things fall.
