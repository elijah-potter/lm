**The Last Broadcast of KXWN-97.3**
Static hissed between channels as the final transmission crackled to life. The voice of **Dr. Elias Voss**—engineer, cryptographer, and the last man standing—was clear, though his hands trembled slightly over the controls.

*"You’re not hearing this for entertainment. You’re hearing it because the signal was jammed by something we didn’t account for. The transmitter’s core is failing—again. The blackout’s coming in five minutes. Listen carefully: the core’s core is unstable. If we don’t reset the fail-safes manually, the reactor will self-destruct. The backup power is dead. You have until the next full moon to reach the abandoned bunker below the station. The key is in the server racks—look for the one with the **blue LED** blinking. It’s not a standard circuit. It’s a *watch*. The watch that tells the time of the storm."*

A sudden **click** from the speaker—then silence. The screen flickered to black.

Then, a single line of text appeared in the bottom corner, glowing faintly:

***"RUN."***

**End of Transmission.**
