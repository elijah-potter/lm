Reverberations of indecision lingered as the decision chair creaked. His fingers traced the edge of the half-eaten sandwich—once crisp, now soggy from the second helping. The call had come in a blur: *"They want you at the meeting at 3 PM."* His stomach clenched; the *"but"* was already forming.

The clock ticked. The elevator doors hissed open. His mind, a storm of conflicting voices, whispered, *"What if they’re wrong? What if I’m right?"* He shoved the last bite into his mouth, swallowed hard, and stepped into the hallway. The door clicked shut behind him. A single sentence, half-formed, escaped his lips: *"I think I’ll stay."* The words were gone before they reached his lips, swallowed by the weight of the moment. The silence that followed was his undoing. The elevator doors closed, and the elevator ascended. The meeting room loomed ahead, a judgment seat. He hesitated. The pause was eternal.
