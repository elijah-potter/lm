A winding road flanked by overgrown thorn bushes, the pavement cracked and pockmarked with decades of neglect. The asphalt is faded, a dull gray under a perpetual overcast, casting long, shadowless shadows. Dirt and gravel spill from the edges, where the pavement once held a more defined path. At regular intervals, the road curves sharply, its trajectory lost to memory. The surrounding landscape is desolate—no signs of life, no buildings, no vehicles. The horizon is a flat, monotonous stretch of what appears to be nothing but more of the same: endless, featureless emptiness. A lone, rusted metal sign, half-buried in the dirt, bears an illegible inscription. The air smells of damp earth and something metallic, like old rainwater. The absence of any landmarks or landmarks that could guide one forward makes it feel like the path spirals into the ground.
