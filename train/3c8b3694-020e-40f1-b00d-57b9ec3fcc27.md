Lena had always been the quiet kid in class, so when she found the strange blue light pulsing under her desk, she didn’t tell anyone. It was the day her chemistry teacher mentioned that the school’s old lab was being renovated. The light flickered like a dying bulb, and when she reached out, her fingers brushed against something cold and smooth. A small, smooth metallic case with a lock. Inside, she found a single, perfectly intact note: *"Meet me at the old clock tower at midnight. Bring the key."*

She hesitated. Then she grabbed her phone and recorded the address of the clock tower, hidden behind the school’s empty storage closet. That night, she slipped out through the back door, her breath fogging in the cold air. The tower stood silent, its face cracked and rusted, but the clock’s hands had stopped at 11:59. The door was locked, but her key fit the old brass lock perfectly. Inside, the room smelled of old wood and something metallic. At the center of the tower’s inner chamber, a device hummed faintly—a device she recognized from her science projects: a time capsule.

Inside, she found letters, photos, and a single, tarnished coin. The oldest was dated 1998: *"If you’re reading this, you’ve found the last message from the experiment. The tower was a test of time manipulation. The device inside will reset the world’s clocks if activated. Do not touch it."* The most recent was from 2023: *"The clock is broken. The experiment failed. But I left the key for you in case you’re the one who fixes it."*

Lena’s hands trembled as she picked up the device. The note said to activate it, but what if it didn’t work? What if the world was already out of sync? She looked around the tower, remembering the way her science teacher had warned them about "time anomalies" in the lab. She had ignored him then. Now, she wasn’t so sure. The light from the device pulsed again, stronger than before. She hesitated, then pressed the button.

The world around her blurred. The clock tower’s hands spun wildly, and the air grew thick. Then—silence. The tower was gone. Lena gasped, her head spinning. When she opened her eyes, she was back in her room, the blue light gone. But her phone was warm in her pocket. She checked the time: 12:01 AM. The experiment had failed. The clock hadn’t reset. But something else had changed. On her desk, beside the empty desk she’d left it in, was a single, smooth metallic case. The lock was open. Inside, the light was still there, pulsing faintly.
