In the dim glow of an old, flickering lamp, Elias sat hunched over the worn desk, his fingers tracing the edges of a yellowed map. The parchment smelled of age and old tea. *"This place is a labyrinth of forgotten paths,"* he muttered, but no one could hear him. The memory of his mother’s last words—*"Don’t trust the clockmaker"*—whispered through the silence like a ghost.

His plan, carved into the margins of a notebook, lay spread before him: *"Find the pocket watch. It’s not where the clockmaker says. The memory is in the walls."* He traced the words with a quill dipped in ink, the stroke deliberate. Outside, the wind howled through the broken windowpanes, but Elias did not flinch. He had spent too long chasing ghosts to let the world scare him away.

The pocket watch was in the attic, not the cellar. And the memory was the scent of roses—his mother’s favorite—stuck in the hollow of the old wardrobe. He clenched his fists. The clockmaker would not give up so easily.
