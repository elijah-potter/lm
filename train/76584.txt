Protect The World's Genetic Diversity
Mon Oct 19 00:40:01 BST 2009 by Rory Galloway
I strongly agree with the domestication of a great deal more species. It is essential to look at the wider picture. The domestication of wild animals at present may cause suffering to a few, however, in the long run it is the survival of the species which should concern us all.
For instance if we managed to domesticate properly the rarer sub-species of Zebra and Orix we may reduce or reverse their decline far faster than we could hope for using the regular captive breeding methods. Other species of mammal which never take to captive breeding at all could be saved by this promising new research.
With it becoming increasingly clear that the world will suffer from major environmental changes in the coming century it is important that we look outside the box to properly protect rarer species in any way we can. We could use this research to completely halt the decline in mammalian species.
(This is not to mention the wonderful potential obvious through the domestication of wild animals; kangaroos could be as common in the household as dogs! Who knows, perhaps there is a potential for the conservation and breeding of rare species to be brought to a commercial scale.)
It is a greater good to save species using ethically suspect methods than it is to leave a saveable species to become extinct.
