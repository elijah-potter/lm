A hollowed-out oak tree in a crumbling courtyard, its bark split by time into whispering cracks. The moss-covered roots pulse faintly with damp air, like a slow heartbeat. The air hums with static—sounds caught between the leaves, voices dissolving into static. A rusted metal gate creaks open at the slightest touch, revealing a narrow stone path descending into the earth. The walls of the abandoned church next door are stained with the same eerie blue-green light as the tree’s canopy, reflecting in puddles that never quite dry. At night, the wind carries the scent of damp stone and something metallic—like rusted nails or old keys. The silence isn’t empty; it’s a chorus of things waiting to be heard.
