The fluorescent lights in the dimly lit office flicker as an elderly man in a stained apron polishes the same glass door handle, repeatedly, with a damp cloth. His movements are precise, yet the hand holding the cloth trembles—too much like a child’s, or perhaps a machine’s, despite his age. Behind him, the fluorescent tubes groan like distant sirens, casting a sickly yellow glow over the sterile white cubicles. In the corner, a single plant withered leaves curls slightly, as if whispering inaudible words to the ceiling fan. The printer jams every third page, spitting out identical blueprints—maps of the city’s underbelly, where the streets twist into labyrinths no one remembers. A clock on the wall ticks backward, its hands frozen at 3:17 AM, while the door’s lock hums an eerie, slow melody—like a choir of static.
