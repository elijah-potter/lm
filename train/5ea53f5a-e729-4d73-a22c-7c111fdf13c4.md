**The Garden’s Secret**

The morning light spilled like slow honey through the cracks in the old windows, warming the cobblestones beneath the bare feet. She had come to the garden not to seek, but to remember—how the scent of damp earth and crushed lavender lingered even when the first frost had seeped into the bones of the world. The roses, though withered, still bent their heads toward the sun, as if whispering to the sky that endurance was their only true language.

She knelt, pressing her palm against the cracked earth where her father’s favorite daisies had once grown. The roots coiled like forgotten dreams, still tender beneath her fingers. A bee, too small to notice her presence, buzzed between her fingers, carrying pollen that had already forgotten its own origin. She let her breath soften into the air, a prayer for what could not be changed.

Outside, the wind carried the sound of distant laughter—children’s voices, careless and bright, like the first notes of a song that had not yet been sung wrong. She stood, watching them run, their laughter the only thing in this world that moved without hesitation. The garden had taught her that even in stillness, there was a return to calm: not the quiet of exhaustion, but the quiet of being seen.

And so she walked back to the house, her hands still warm, her heart full of the quiet that had always been there, waiting. The garden had not given her answers, only the memory of what was already known—somewhere, deep within her, the return to calm had always been a door she had only needed to open.
