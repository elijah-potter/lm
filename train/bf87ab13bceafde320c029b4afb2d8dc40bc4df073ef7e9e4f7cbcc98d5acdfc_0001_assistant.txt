Mr. Peterson couldn’t stand his neighbors, the Smiths. They had moved from the next town over a month ago, and ever since then, Mr. Peterson was annoyed by them. They were just so different from the other families in the neighborhood! They always wore blue while the neighborhood wore black; they had brown hair while everyone else in the neighborhood was blonde; and they would have barbecues on Saturdays while everyone in the neighborhood had them on Sundays. Mr. Peterson decided he really didn’t like them, and so he spent every day steering clear of them.
One day when he came home from work, he saw his son Michael playing with the Smith children. Outraged, Mr. Peterson called Michael over. His son came running over with his soccer ball, wondering why his father had interrupted their game.
“Were you playing with the neighbors?” Mr. Peterson asked worriedly.
“Yes,” Michael replied with a grin. “They’re great fun!”
“Unbelievable! How can you play with them when they wear black and when we wear blue? They have brown hair when we are blonde. And they have barbecues on Saturday mornings when we have them on Sundays!”
Michael looked at his father, quite confused, and then asked, “So what?”
Mr. Peterson was stunned. He had no answer for his son – really, so what? So what if they wore blue, had brown hair, or preferred to have barbecues on Saturdays?
“Well, nothing, I suppose,” Mr. Peterson said gingerly, beginning to see the error of his ways. “I’m sorry for interrupting your game, Michael. You go have fun with your new friends.”
