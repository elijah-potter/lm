Granite is a very hard and durable stone, it is resistant to scratches and heat, making it a popular choice for kitchen counters and other high-use surfaces. It's also resistant to acidic substances, which makes it ideal for use in areas that may come into contact with acidic liquids such as lemon juice or vinegar.
Granite has a long and rich history that dates back thousands of years. Granite was commonly used in ancient Egypt for the construction of monumental structures such as pyramids, temples and statues, as well as in ancient Greece and Rome, where it was used for monuments, public buildings, and architectural elements.
During the Middle Ages, granite was used primarily in the construction of castles, churches, and cathedrals.
In the 19th century, the Industrial Revolution brought new technologies that allowed for the large-scale quarrying and processing of granite.
This made granite more readily available and less expensive, and it became a popular choice for a wide range of construction projects.
