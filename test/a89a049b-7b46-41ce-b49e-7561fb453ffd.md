**"Revolutionary AI Voice Assistant: The First Commercial Speech Synthesis Engine (1984) – *DigiSynth-9000***
**A Glimpse into the Future of Human-Computer Interaction**

This compact, hand-held prototype was one of the first commercial speech synthesis systems, capable of generating synthetic speech at near-human cadence. Developed in 1984, it predated modern voice assistants by over a decade, marking a pivotal moment in bridging the gap between digital data and intelligible human language.

**Technical Specs & Cultural Impact:**
- **Engine:** Customized TTS (Text-to-Speech) chipset with waveform modulation.
- **Accuracy:** Recognized commands like *"Play Track 7"* or *"Set Alarm for 8 AM"* with ~80% success rate (error-prone by today’s standards).
- **Usage:** Primarily tested in call centers, military training simulations, and early accessibility tools for the visually impaired.
- **Legacy:** Though limited by hardware constraints (e.g., 5-second playback, static vocabulary), it demonstrated the potential for seamless voice-driven interfaces. Today’s smart speakers—from Alexa to Siri—directly trace their lineage to this foundational innovation.

**Interactive Exhibit Concept:**
*"Try It Yourself!"*
Visitors could listen to pre-recorded clips of the device’s synthetic voice (e.g., a weather report or a simple command) alongside a comparison of its output against modern AI voice synthesis. A side panel explains how early TTS algorithms approximated human speech using *phoneme-by-phoneme* synthesis, a technique still used in niche applications today.

**Why It Matters:**
This exhibit underscores how incremental technological advancements—from hardware limitations to algorithmic trade-offs—paved the way for today’s voice-activated ecosystems. From the "Hello, Siri" of 2008 to the 1984 "Play Track 7" command, the story of AI voice assistants is one of iterative discovery, where the impossible became inevitable."
