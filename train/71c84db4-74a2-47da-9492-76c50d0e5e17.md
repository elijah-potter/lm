The air hummed with a static borrowed from another time, the scent of damp earth lingering where no rain had fallen—just the ghost of a summer’s end. She stood at the window, fingers tracing the frame, though it was never hers to hold. The view shifted between two worlds: a garden of thorns that grew in her father’s backyard, and a city street where strangers passed like shadows through the cracks of memory.

Her reflection in the glass was a stranger’s reflection—eyes too wide, lips pressed into a line that wasn’t hers. She had borrowed the chair from her mother’s study, the one with the carved lion’s head, and now it sat empty, its weight forgotten. The book she read was one she’d never written, the words plucked from a story that wasn’t hers to tell.

Outside, a clock struck midnight, but the hands moved too fast, too slow, as if caught in a loop. She wondered if the world was a borrowed moment, too, a performance where no one ever quite belonged. The borrowed moment was the only truth left—fragile, stolen, and impossible to keep.
