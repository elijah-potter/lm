A quantum dot in a semiconductor matrix under ultra-low temperatures (near absolute zero) exhibits non-classical light emission with spontaneous symmetry breaking. Fluorescence shows stochastic wavelength fluctuations and polarization states, forming a self-organized light emitter with non-Gaussian statistics. The emission spectrum shifts unpredictably between discrete spectral lines (Wigner–Aronszajn effect), and photon antibunching is observed, revealing strong quantum interference effects.
