The clock struck five. Rain tapped against the windows of the cramped café, turning the neon glow of the streetlights into a shimmering veil. Across the room, Elias sipped his cold coffee, fingers tracing the rim of the cup. His phone buzzed—an unread message from his mother: *"You’re late again."* The screen flickered as he ignored it, staring at the blurred faces of strangers in the window.

A shadow moved behind him. The woman at the next table, a silhouette against the dim light, stood abruptly. *"You’re always late,"* she said, voice like static. Elias didn’t turn. *"Late for what?"* she asked. *"For the world, maybe."* His breath fogged the glass. The café door burst open. A commotion erupted—someone screamed. Chaos spilled into the room. The woman grabbed his wrist, pulling him toward the exit as sirens wailed outside.

The street was a riot of flashing lights. Elias stumbled, his shoes slipping on wet pavement. He turned—she was gone. The air smelled of burnt rubber and fear. A police officer grabbed his arm. *"You know what happened?"* Elias shook his head, but the last thing he saw was the café’s window shattered, the neon sign flickering like a dying heartbeat. Then—silence. A single second passed. The world held its breath.
