In the quiet village of Eldermere, where the mist clung to the ancient oak trees like whispered secrets, there was a boy named Elias who collected strange trinkets from the woods. One evening, while gathering firewood, he found a small, cracked mirror hidden beneath the roots of an elder tree. The reflection in it was blurred, showing nothing but endless twilight.

Elias, fascinated, kept the mirror and began to examine it closely. Each time he looked, the reflection shifted—sometimes showing a forest of towering spires, other times a bustling market where the air smelled of spiced tea and distant laughter. One day, he noticed something strange: the mirror seemed to fade and reform around the same image—a young girl with golden hair, standing in a meadow bathed in golden light.

Curious, Elias traced the girl’s path in the reflection, following the golden light until it led him to a hidden door in the woods. Inside, the walls pulsed with colors he’d never seen, and the air hummed with unseen energy. There, he met the girl—a woman named Lyra, who had been his reflection all along.

"Elias," Lyra said, her voice echoing through time, "you’ve been searching for something that was always right in front of you. The mirror shows you the choices you’ve made, the paths you’ve walked. The golden girl was you at your happiest."

Elias realized the mirror wasn’t a reflection at all—it was a portal. The girl in the light was his past self, and the forest of spires was his future. The mirror had always been there, waiting for him to see it.

As he stepped back into the village, the mirror shattered into a thousand pieces. The last fragment he held still flickered with light—a faint golden glow, like a promise.

Years later, when Elias grew old, he found the same cracked mirror beneath the same elder tree. The reflection was the same: a boy in the woods, holding a mirror that showed him the past and future. The golden girl stood once more in the meadow, waiting.
