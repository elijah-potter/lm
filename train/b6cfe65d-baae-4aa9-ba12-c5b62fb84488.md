**Notes on Choices & Alternatives:**

*   **Profession Assumption:** I assumed software engineering. If your profession is different, please adjust the details about technical debt, code reviews, and architectural design to be relevant.
*   **Specificity vs. Generality:** I provided two paragraph options regarding the reasons for leaving. Option 1 is more detailed and specific, which can be helpful if you want to provide concrete feedback. However, it also carries a higher risk of creating conflict. Option 2 is more general and avoids direct criticism. Choose the option that aligns with your relationship with your boss and the company culture.
*   **Tone:** I aimed for a professional and respectful tone, avoiding emotional language. You can adjust the tone to be slightly more or less formal as needed.
*   **"Voiced Concerns":** The phrase "I have voiced these concerns" is important. It demonstrates that you attempted to address the issues internally before resigning. If you haven't voiced concerns, consider whether it's appropriate to do so in the letter (or whether it's better to omit the statement entirely).
*   **Smooth Transition:** Offering to help with the transition is a professional courtesy. However, be realistic about how much time and effort you can realistically offer.
*   **Legal Review:** *Crucially*, before submitting any resignation letter, especially one containing critical feedback, it's highly advisable to have it reviewed by an employment lawyer or HR professional to ensure it complies with local laws and doesn't create any legal risks.
*   **Company Policy:**  Always review your company’s resignation policy before submitting your letter.
