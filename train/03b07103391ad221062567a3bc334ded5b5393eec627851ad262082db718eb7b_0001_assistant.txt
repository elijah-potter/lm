1802: Humphry Davy invented the first electric light known as the Electric Arc lamp, but it was too bright and didn't last long enough for practical use.
1840: Warren de la Rue creates a platinum filament light bulb. Although it is efficient, the cost of platinum made it commercially impractical.
1850: Joseph Wilson Swan created a carbonized paper filament light bulb, but the lack of a good vacuum and adequate electricity resulted in a bulb with a very short lifetime.
In the 1870s: Better vacuum pumps became available, allowing Swan to continue experimenting with light bulbs.
1878: Swan developed a longer-lasting light bulb using a treated cotton thread and simultaneously solved the problem of early bulb blackening.
July 24, 1874: Henry Woodward and Mathew Evans filed a Canadian patent for their lamp with carbon rods and nitrogen-filled glass cylinders. They were unsuccessful in commercializing it and eventually sold their patent to Edison in 1879.
