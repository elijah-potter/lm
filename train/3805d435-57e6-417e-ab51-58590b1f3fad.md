A subtle, half-hearted plea scribbled on a crumpled napkin in the diner’s back corner, the ink smudged from the heat of the coffee cup. *"Sorry I’m late. Traffic was… chaotic."* The server’s pen paused mid-draw, her fingers twitching as she studied the words. She’d seen this apology before—*all* of them. The one that lands between "I’m sorry" and "I’ll make it up to you," the one that’s neither real nor fake but a hollow placeholder between regret and dismissal.

The waitress set down a fresh espresso, the steam curling into the air like a warning. *"No worries,"* she said, but her voice was the kind that could melt steel. The apology lingered on the table, a ghost of what it could have been—something more, something *felt*—but instead, just the ghost of a *trying*.
