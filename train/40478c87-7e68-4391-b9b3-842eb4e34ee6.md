The first time a routine became a routine was likely in prehistoric hunter-gatherer societies, where predictable seasonal cycles influenced repetitive tasks like foraging, hunting, and shelter-building. Over generations, these activities evolved into standardized practices due to necessity, leading to the early emergence of structured routines. By the Neolithic era, farming and craft production formalized daily labor patterns, embedding repetition in human experience. The Industrial Revolution later codified these routines into structured work schedules, further institutionalizing repetitive tasks as a standard part of societal function.
