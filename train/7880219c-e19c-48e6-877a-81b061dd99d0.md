A dilapidated clock tower in a forgotten city square, its gears rusted but still turning, each tick and toll echoing a cadence that doesn’t align with the metronome of modern life. The streets below hum with the discordant rhythm of a society that has long since abandoned timekeeping. Pedestrians move in loops, their faces frozen mid-expression, their footsteps synchronized to a beat that was never meant to be heard. A café’s neon sign flickers, displaying the same hour—*5:23*—for days, yet the sun moves as if unaware of its existence. The air smells of ozone and something metallic, the scent of time itself unraveling. Inside a museum, a single, misaligned clock in the corner chimes every hour, but the hands don’t stop—each tick a whisper of what could’ve been lost. A child’s laughter drifts from an empty playground, the sound of a world that never grew old. Shadows stretch unnaturally, casting figures that move backward in the flickering light of a dying bulb. The clock tower’s bell tolls once, then again—*twice*—at the same moment the world outside resets.
