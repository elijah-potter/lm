As the shuttle approached the edge of the black hole, Astronaut Jim Field watched through one of the portholes in anticipation of things to come. Nothing was known about what would come next; only conjecture, theory, and scientific "best guesses." These were all that was offered to Field when he attended his first briefing before becoming the first human to ever traverse through a black hole. Now, after innumerable briefings, tests, and dry runs over three years, Field was in a metal egg that was heading toward the black hole. A large, stable, black hole formed several years earlier and prompted scientists to create this project. Sending a human rather than a robot or other measurement devices had already been done in the past with positive results. On one occasion the video camera capturing its descent into the black hole was able to reach the other side of it unharmed, and it recorded invaluable information within that would lead them to determine that travel through the black hole would be safe. However, the scientists still wanted a real person going through this, to act as a pioneer who would venture into a new (metaphorical) land and discover what was inside.

Field quickly approached the black hole's event horizon but felt no adverse effects. Scientists determine before the trip, that Field would not experience the normal "spaghettification" of his body in this particular black hole. The geometry of it which made it stable also acted as a sort of tunnel that protected matter going into it and preserved information and matter. This phenomenon allowed the matter to come back out of the black hole at a later time.

As Field circled the drain of the black hole, he began to see bright lights deep in its center, like radiation. Field continued his journey to the center of the black hole as cameras and other instruments documented his descent into darkness. It had been several days since Field was swallowed up by the black hole, and there was no sign of him yet.
