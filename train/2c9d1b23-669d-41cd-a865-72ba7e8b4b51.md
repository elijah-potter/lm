The café’s muted hum faded as she glanced up from her latte, her fingers still tracing the rim of her cup. His hand brushed the back of her neck—not a touch meant to be noticed, but one that sent a jolt through her chest. His voice, usually sharp with frustration, softened: *"You remember the way the light hits the old bridge at dusk?"* She nodded, recalling the exact angle, the way the water glinted like fractured glass. His smile was rare, a quiet thing, and in that moment, she understood why he’d never mentioned it before. The unspoken bond between them, forged in shared silence and the way he’d once stolen her sandwich from the cafeteria line years ago, suddenly felt like a language she’d been speaking all along. The coffee between them vanished. The world narrowed to this—two people who’d been trying to read each other for years, finally seeing the map before them.
