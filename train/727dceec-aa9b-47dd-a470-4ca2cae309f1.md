The old clock tower stood silent at the edge of Blackthorn Hollow, its rusted hands frozen at 11:11. Inside, Elias adjusted his gloves, his breath misting in the cold morning air. The first image he remembered was a child’s drawing of a clock with hands that spun endlessly—his own, drawn in crayon on the back of an envelope from his grandmother.

Outside, the wind howled through the hollow’s skeletal trees, carrying whispers of the past. He knelt, tracing the grain of the old oak where he’d once pressed his fingers into the bark, searching for the same spot where he’d scribbled his first story.

A crackle—his grandmother’s voice, still warm in his memory. *"Every story starts with a moment, Elias. Even the ones that loop."*

He turned back to the tower’s creaking iron door. The clock’s hands moved now, not frozen, but spinning in slow, deliberate circles. The door groaned open, revealing a narrow staircase descending into darkness. At the bottom, a single candle flickered—a child’s candle, like the one he’d lit that night years ago, watching the stars.

The first words he wrote, *"Once upon a time…"*, echoed through the air. His fingers trembled as he reached for the last line he’d ever finish: *"…and so it began again."*

The clock tower’s hands stopped. The moment held. The drawing on the envelope, now aged, glowed faintly in his mind’s eye—one hand pointing to the clock, the other to the staircase.

Elias exhaled. The story had returned to its beginning. The cycle was complete.
