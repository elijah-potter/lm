**The door creaked open before I could reach for the handle.**

*"Help me,"* the voice inside whispered, *"before it’s too late."*

The flickering candle cast jagged shadows across the walls—dust motes swirled in the dim light, like ghosts of forgotten things. My fingers trembled as I traced the edge of the old ledger, its pages yellowed with age. The ink had run, but the words were still there, blurred but unmistakable:

*"They’re coming. The ones who don’t sleep. The ones who wear the same face as you."*

I swallowed hard. The mirror above the fireplace showed my reflection—pale, hollow-eyed—but it wasn’t me. It was *them*. The voice inside was louder now, urgent, desperate.

*"Look around you,"* it hissed. *"The clock tower chimes at midnight. The blacksmith’s hammer falls at dawn. You have to move before the next full moon."*

My breath hitched. The floorboards groaned beneath my feet. Something *shifted* in the corner—no, not shifted. *Watched.* Too close.

*"Where do I go?"*

The voice sighed, like a breath held too long. *"Anywhere but here. The river’s the only thing that’s safe. But you’ll need a boat."*

A cold hand clutched my shoulder. The ledger pages fluttered open—*pages I never wrote.*

*"Or,"* it whispered, *"you could stay. And let them take what’s theirs."*

The candle snuffed out. In the dark, the door slammed shut. Behind it, something *laughed*—a sound like breaking glass.

I stumbled toward the window, my pulse roaring in my ears. Below, the river was still. Too still.

*"What do I do?"*

The answer was already there—*on the other side.*
