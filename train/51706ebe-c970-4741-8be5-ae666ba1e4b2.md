The abandoned half-finished tower loomed over the village, its stone foundations cracked and crumbling, exposed to the elements. Only the skeletal remains of a spiral staircase, its rungs missing in places, hinted at the structure’s original intent. A single, weathered signpost near the entrance bore faded inscriptions: *"To be continued."* Dust coated the broken windows, and rusted gears, still partially assembled, lay scattered across the ruined workshop floor. The scent of damp wood and old metal lingered in the air, a ghost of progress abandoned mid-motion.

Inside, the skeletal remains of a desk—half-built—sat in the center of the room, its surfaces blank except for a single, unfinished sketch: a half-drawn cityscape with jagged lines where buildings should have been. The air hummed with the faint, unresolved sound of an unplayed piano, its keys still angled as if held mid-performance. Outside, the wind howled through the broken eaves, carrying whispers of what could have been. The unfinished was not just a failure of craftsmanship, but a silence—one that refused to let the past stay buried.
