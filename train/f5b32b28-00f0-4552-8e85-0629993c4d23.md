In the dim glow of an abandoned library, the scent of aged paper and damp stone clings to the air. A librarian, hands trembling, retrieves a dust-covered book from the back row. The spine cracks under her grip. As she flips through the brittle pages, a whisper slithers through the cracks of the ceiling: *"You were here once."* The words echo like a ghost’s breath, but the book’s last page is blank—until she opens it to a place she never visited. A photograph of herself as a child, seated beside a stranger whose face flickers in and out of focus. The stranger smiles, their teeth too sharp, their eyes black as ink. The librarian’s reflection in the old mirror above the desk mirrors the stranger’s grin, but it isn’t her. The past isn’t gone—it’s woven into the present, stitching the past and future into a single, tangled thread.
