A person sat stiffly in the corner, fingers curled around a coffee cup, the steam rising lazily. Their gaze flicked toward the speaker—an older woman with sharp features and a voice that carried weight—before returning to a notebook, pages filled with scribbles. The woman’s question hung in the air, unspoken but clear: *"Did you really steal that painting?"*

The evader exhaled through their nose, a sharp inhale, then met her eyes with a flicker of something—guilt, deflection, or sheer exhaustion. They opened their mouth, hesitated, then said, *"I don’t remember."* The words were clipped, the lie too neat, too obvious. The woman’s lips thinned, but she didn’t press. Instead, she tilted her head, studying the edges of the room—where shadows pooled like secrets. The evader’s hands trembled slightly, the cup trembling too, as if the act of avoiding had become a physical weight.
