The Jews of the Silk Road– Bukhara, Uzbekistan’s Jewish Legacy

Situated far beyond the reaches of Judea, one would never expect a small Uzbek city to be the place thousands of Jews call home. However, Bukhara has been a hub of Jewish life for well over a millennium. 
They’ve inhabited the city for so long that their origins are the subject of legend. However, historians believe that they’ve traced their arrival to 586 BCE. The first groups came seeking refuge in the wake of the Babylonians exiling them from Israel.

Over the centuries, various upheavals and conquests in the region shook the community. They were the subject of discrimination under some rulers, but some, such as the Russian Tsar, looked upon them favorably. 
Many have left the region since the collapse of the Soviet Union in the early 1990s due to economic instability and opportunities in faraway places like Israel and the United States. However, it’s estimated that roughly 50,000 Jews lived in Bukhara at the peak of their population. 

Despite the economic exodus, a group of 100-200 Bukharan Jews remains, keeping local traditions alive. A walk around Bukhara’s historic center will no doubt bring you to the Jewish district where you can find their old synagogue. Those who remained still hold traditional Jewish weddings and funerals for community members, too.
