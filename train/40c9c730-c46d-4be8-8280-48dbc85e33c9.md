The crowd moved in a blur—faces pressed close, laughter muffled by scarves, the weight of unseen expectations pressing against the shoulders of strangers. She had spent years learning to see the unspoken: the way a man’s fingers trembled when he counted coins, the hollow beneath a woman’s smile when she’d been told she couldn’t have the job. They walked past her, oblivious to the way the light caught the veins in their wrists, or how their breath hitched when they thought no one was watching.

She had been taught to listen beyond the noise of their words, to read the exhaustion in their eyes when they thought she couldn’t see. The others moved in the same rhythm, but they never paused. They never paused to wonder how many hands had touched the same scar, how many tongues had tasted the same bitter medicine. She paused. And in that pause, the world shifted—just slightly—for her.

Outside, a child’s laughter cut through the hum of the street, bright and unfiltered. She had noticed the way the sound bounced off the pavement, how it lingered in the air before fading like a promise. The others had missed the way the sound *was*, not just the sound of a child, but the sound of freedom. They had missed it because they never paused to listen.

She turned her head, let the sound wash over her, and wondered what else they had missed. Because the world wasn’t just what they saw. It was what they didn’t. And she would find it.
