The skeletal remains of a crumbling clock tower stand in the center of an abandoned village square, its brass hands frozen at 3:17 AM. The streets, once bustling with cobblestones, now carry only the dust from decades of neglect. Half-finished murals, half-buried under layers of peeling paint, depict scenes of the village’s past—one shows children playing in what was once the central fountain, another depicts a grand feast with empty chairs and missing tables.

The air smells of damp earth and rusted nails. The few remaining windows in the dilapidated buildings are boarded up, but gaps between the boards let in shadows that stretch like fingers across the cracked pavement. The river that once flowed through the town now runs sluggishly, its banks lined with overgrown reeds and the occasional rusted tire or discarded bicycle.

At the edge of the woods, a hand-painted sign reads **"This Place Belongs to the Past"**, its paint chipped and fading. The trees here are gnarled, their branches knotted with time, their leaves whispering secrets in a wind that carries no voices. Some say the village still breathes in its sleep, though no one dares to wake it. The unfinished is not just a ruin—it’s a wound that never fully heals.
