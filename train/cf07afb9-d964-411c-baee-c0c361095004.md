**The Last Light**

The city slept under a weight of silence,
each street lamp casting shadows too long to fade,
while the moon hung like a bruised and forgotten eye,
drifting past the broken glass of shattered dreams.

I sat at the café’s empty table,
whispering to the coffee that didn’t stir,
the steam curling up in slow, deliberate spirals,
a ghost of something that had once been warm.

*"Why do you stay?"* the dark said,
not with words, but with the way it clung to the walls,
the way it let the shadows dance where there was no light.

Then—

The door creaked open.

Not with the sound of footsteps,
but with the sound of a promise,
something sharp and hungry,
something that had been waiting too long.

The café erupted in noise.
Screams, laughter, the clatter of chairs—
a storm of voices, a feast of colors,
a world that had forgotten how to be quiet.

The moon was gone.
The city was awake.
And somewhere, the last light burned out.
