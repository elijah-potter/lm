The waiter bowed slightly as she approached, a smile stiff on her face. "Good afternoon, sir. May I take your order?" Her voice was polite, but her posture lacked warmth. She had memorized the lines but failed to connect with the customers.

He glanced at the menu, then at her, his expression unreadable. "I’ll have the steak, black coffee," he said, voice monotone. "And could you please not mention the butter?"

She blinked, adjusting her apron. "Of course, sir." Her hands trembled slightly as she placed the order, her smile too wide, her manners too stiff. The diner noticed, but no one dared correct her—no one wanted to be the one who ruined the illusion.

As she turned away, her reflection in the mirrored wall showed her face: eyes too bright, lips too forced, the weight of a job that demanded more than just words. She swallowed hard, knowing she had failed at something she had never truly understood.
