A cracked coffee cup lay shattered on the worn linoleum floor, its porcelain shards glinting dully under the dim hallway light. Nearby, the half-eaten sandwich wrapper twisted into a crumpled mess, its edges singed where it had been tossed too hastily. A loose button, no bigger than a thumbnail, had rolled under a stack of old newspapers—papers from the week the landlord had moved out. It was the only thing that hadn’t been tossed or forgotten in the chaos.

She knelt, fingers brushing the fabric of her jeans, combing through the debris. A rustle. The button was there, buried beneath a faded receipt from last month’s grocery run. She picked it up, turning it over in her palm. It was the same as the one on her jacket—her old one, the one she’d lost track of after a late-night shift. The one that matched the faded name on her driver’s license, the one that had belonged to her before she’d moved here.
