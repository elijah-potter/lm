The task of refining a dataset’s label consistency for a large corpus of unstructured medical records proved iterative and resource-intensive. Early stages required manual review of 200 records, identifying ambiguities like "chronic fatigue" vs. "post-viral syndrome" through clinician feedback. As iterations progressed, the scope expanded to 5,000 records with additional criteria (e.g., temporal context, symptom severity), necessitating specialized tools for nested classification (e.g., rule-based NLP for patient history + symptom overlap). Data drift emerged, forcing retraining of a hierarchical ensemble model (3 classifiers: basic, intermediate, and expert-level). Performance metrics (precision@10=78%, recall@5=65%) underscored diminishing returns: each additional record added ~0.2% improvement, while false positives rose due to edge cases (e.g., "depression" vs. "dysphoric mood"). Final batch processing required 48 hours on a cluster, with parallelized validation pipelines to handle real-time feedback loops from domain experts. The cumulative cost (time + computational) reached $12K/week for 12 weeks, with diminishing marginal gains. Key insight: exponential complexity in multi-label, hierarchical systems often demands trade-offs between accuracy and scalability.
