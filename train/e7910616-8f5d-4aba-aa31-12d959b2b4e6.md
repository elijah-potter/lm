**The Silent Room**

The city hummed outside, a lazy pulse of neon and traffic, but inside the old bookshop, the air smelled of aged paper and damp leather. The scent lingered like a ghost—something you could almost taste. Outside, people moved in a rush, their footsteps echoing against the pavement like a metronome counting down the end of the world. But here, in the corner where the light filtered through stained glass, the time had stopped.

You sat on the floor, knees drawn to your chest, a half-eaten apple in your lap. The book in your hands was one of those things—thick, heavy, full of words that made your eyes ache if you read too long. The title had been buried beneath layers of dust, but you remembered the promise: a story about a girl who learned to listen when everyone else was too busy talking.

Outside, the world had gone mad. Cars honked, laughter erupted from a pub down the street, and the occasional scream carried on the wind, sharp as a knife. But here, in this quiet, you were just a shadow, a flicker in the corner of someone else’s memory.

**The Storm**

The sky split open.

Not the kind of storm that came with warnings—no sirens, no thunderheads rolling in like a slow, angry tide. This one was different. It started with a whisper, a breath against the window, then a gust that tore the curtains from the frame. Rain came down in sheets, so thick you could barely see the street below, and the air smelled of ozone and something metallic, like blood after a fight.

You were supposed to be outside. The weather report had said nothing would happen, but then again, what did they know? The wind howled through the alleyways, carrying with it the sound of laughter—real, unfiltered, the kind that made your stomach clench. Someone was alive. Someone was *happy*.

But you were stuck inside, watching the storm roll in, your hands trembling around the coffee cup in front of you. The TV was on, playing some news channel you didn’t recognize, but the words blurred together, and all you could see were the flashes of lightning, the way the streetlights flickered like dying stars.

Then the power went out.

Darkness. Complete. The only sound was the rain, the distant crash of something hitting a window, the low, steady pulse of your own heartbeat. Outside, the city was alive—loud, reckless, *alive*. And you were just a ghost in the corner, listening. Waiting.

Waiting for what? The question gnawed at you, sharp and hungry. You had no idea. But for the first time in a long time, you didn’t care.
