Fernão Lopes, a Portuguese soldier, found himself marooned on the uninhabited island of St. Helena in the southern Atlantic in 1516. After betraying his homeland and suffering severe punishment at the hands of his former comrades, Lopes sought refuge on the island. Living in self-imposed exile, he spent several years alone, accompanied only by a rooster he befriended. Despite refusing to meet visitors, Lopes gained a legendary status among Portuguese mariners, who left offerings for him whenever they visited the island. Eventually, he returned to Europe, where he received a pardon from the King of Portugal and the Pope's absolution. However, he chose to go back to St. Helena, living in solitude until his death around 1545, having spent approximately 30 years on the island.
