In the quiet of the old library, the last book was returned to the shelf. The spine cracked under the weight of forgotten knowledge, and the pages whispered secrets to the wind that carried them away. A single candle flickered, casting long shadows on the walls where time had etched its own stories.

Outside, the first light of dawn painted the sky in hues of gold and rose. A stranger passed by, carrying a bag of coins and a half-finished letter addressed to no one. The air smelled of damp earth and rain, the kind that promised new beginnings.

She paused at the corner of the street, where the cobblestones had been worn smooth by centuries of footsteps. The letter slipped from her fingers, unopened. The coins clinked softly against the pavement. The candle had burned out, and the library doors stood wide, inviting.

She turned away, but not before catching the reflection of the stranger—a woman with eyes like storm clouds, standing at the edge of the river, her hands clutching the same letter. The river was still, mirroring nothing but the sky and the distant church bell tolling for the day.

The stranger wrote again, this time in ink that glowed faintly under the moonlight. The words formed a circle, and within it, a new story began. The river ran deep, carrying the echoes of the past and the whispers of the future.

The library doors closed with a final, heavy sigh. The shelves were empty now, save for the one book that had never been returned. Inside, the pages turned, and the stories within stretched out into the endless night.
