## A Counseling Session

**Setting:** A warmly lit, comfortable therapy office. Soft music plays quietly in the background.

**Characters:**

* **Dr. Eleanor Vance:** A seasoned therapist, known for her calm demeanor and insightful approach.
* **Liam Harding:** A client in his late twenties, visibly burdened by stress.

---

**Liam:** (Sighs, looking down at his hands) It just feels… overwhelming, Dr. Vance. Like I’m constantly juggling a dozen things, and I’m dropping them all.

**Dr. Vance:** (Leans forward slightly, maintaining eye contact) I hear that, Liam. It sounds incredibly taxing to feel that sense of constant pressure. Can you tell me a little more about what these “dozen things” are that you’re juggling?

**Liam:** Well, work is demanding. I’m on a project with a really tight deadline, and my boss has been… less than understanding about the workload. Then there’s my father; he's been needing more support lately, and I feel guilty when I can't provide it as much as he expects. And, honestly, my relationship with Sarah… it's been strained. We're just not connecting the way we used to.

**Dr. Vance:** (Nods slowly) So, a significant workload, family responsibilities, and challenges within your relationship. It's understandable that you're feeling overwhelmed. It sounds like you’re carrying a considerable weight. You mentioned feeling guilty about your father; can you elaborate on that feeling? What specifically are you feeling guilty *about*?

**Liam:** I feel guilty when I can’t visit as often as he wants, or when I can’t immediately help him with whatever he needs. He’s always been so supportive of me, and I feel like I’m failing him now that he’s getting older.

**Dr. Vance:** It’s natural to want to reciprocate the support you’ve received, especially from a parent. It’s a sign of a loving and respectful relationship. However, it sounds like you might be setting an impossibly high standard for yourself. Do you believe your father expects more than is realistically possible, given your own commitments?

**Liam:** (Hesitates) I think… I think he might. But I also feel like I *should* be able to do it all. I should be able to be there for him, excel at work, and maintain a fulfilling relationship with Sarah.

**Dr. Vance:** That "should" can be a powerful and often unhelpful burden, Liam. It implies a level of control and capability that rarely exists. It’s admirable to strive for excellence, but the "shoulds" often lead to self-criticism and feelings of inadequacy. Let's explore where that belief originates. Have you always felt this pressure to be everything to everyone?

**Liam:** (Looks thoughtful) I think so. My parents always emphasized hard work and responsibility. They wanted me to succeed, and I always felt like I needed to prove myself.

**Dr. Vance:** So, a strong emphasis on achievement and responsibility from a young age. It’s likely that you internalized those values and now apply them to yourself with considerable intensity. It's important to recognize that those values, while positive in many ways, might be contributing to your current stress. We can work on disentangling those ingrained beliefs and developing a more balanced perspective. How do you think Sarah would describe the changes she's noticed in your relationship?

**Liam:** (Sighs again) She says I'm distant. That I seem preoccupied and emotionally unavailable. She misses the… the ease we used to have.

**Dr. Vance:** That's valuable feedback, Liam. It suggests that the pressure you're putting on yourself is impacting your ability to connect with those you care about. It’s a clear indication that something needs to shift. Let’s start by examining some practical strategies for managing your workload. Perhaps we can explore prioritization techniques and boundary setting, both at work and within your family dynamics. Would you be open to discussing those approaches next session?

**Liam:** (Nods slowly) Yes, please. I think… I think I need some help figuring out how to breathe again.
