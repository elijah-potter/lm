A dimly lit chamber, its walls lined with faded tapestries depicting forgotten ceremonies. The air hums with the scent of burnt incense—once sacred, now stale. A lone figure kneels before a cracked altar, tracing a symbol in ash with trembling fingers: the same mark from a hundred years ago, but now meaningless. The ritual’s steps are mechanical:

1. **The Offering** – A hollowed-out bone (once a sacrifice’s skull) sits in a dish of cold water, its hollowed eye sockets filled with dirt. The kneeler whispers the same prayer, *"May the old world remember,"* as they press a single black feather into the dish.

2. **The Light** – A candle, unlit since the last failed attempt, is struck from a rusted matchbox. The flame flickers weakly, casting long shadows. The ritual demands fire, but the ritual’s purpose is lost. The candle burns too short, too bright, too faint—it just *burns*.

3. **The Silence** – A drum, its skin stretched taut over a frame of rotting wood, is struck once, twice. No melody, no beat. The sound is a static hiss, like static on a dead radio. The kneeler claps their hands twice, then stops. The silence is not prayer—it is the absence of sound.

4. **The Passing** – A bowl of water sits on the altar, its surface rippled by unseen hands. The kneeler dips a finger into it, then presses it to their lips—no taste, no memory. The ritual demands a blessing, but what is left is just the act of touching.

5. **The Return** – The figure stands, clutching the ash in their palm. They walk away without looking back. The ritual is complete, but the world remains unchanged. The candle’s ember snuffs out, the drum falls silent, the bowl’s water dries in the corner. The ritual has stopped because it was never about the act—it was about the *why*, and that has vanished.

The chamber is empty again. The tapestries hang askew. The ritual is dust.
