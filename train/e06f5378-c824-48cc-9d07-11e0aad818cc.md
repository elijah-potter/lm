You should have stayed home in the dark. The old oak door creaked as it swung shut behind you, sealing the last of the autumn leaves against the cold. Your breath fogged in the frigid air, but the warmth of the hearth had already faded. A single candle flickered on the mantel—its flame trembling like a dying ember. The walls were thick with cobwebs, the kind that hadn’t moved in decades.

A voice, smooth as polished steel, whispered from the shadows: *"You shouldn’t be here."* The words weren’t meant for you, but the air itself seemed to hum with their meaning. The candle’s flame dipped, then extinguished, plunging the room into silence so absolute you could hear your own pulse. The door had no lock. No chain. Just a promise, whispered by something that had watched you come.

From the corner, a figure moved—tall, cloaked in tattered robes stitched with silver thread. Their face was a mask of sharp angles, the hollows of their eyes black as ink. They lifted a hand. The moment their fingers brushed your sleeve, the air turned to ice. The world narrowed to the sting of frost on your skin and the scent of old blood.

*"The key was in your pocket,"* the figure said, voice like dry leaves skittering across stone. *"And now, you’re the one who carries it."* The light from the candle’s ghostly remains pooled on the floor, reflecting off something buried beneath the rug. Your stomach twisted. You hadn’t even *felt* the weight of it before.

Then the door groaned open on its own. Beyond it, the night stretched endlessly, alive with the wrong kind of light. The figure smiled. *"Welcome home."*
