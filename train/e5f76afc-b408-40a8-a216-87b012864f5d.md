**Location:** A dimly lit attic in an old Victorian home, dust motes swirling in the flickering lamplight.
**Time:** Late evening, after the last guests have departed for the night.

The air smelled of aged wood, polish, and something faintly sweet—old books, perhaps, or the remnants of a forgotten meal. On the cracked floor, a half-empty glass of bourbon sat beside a half-mended photograph in a tarnished frame. The glass had been raised in toast before, but this time, the glass was empty, and the photograph bore a new, deliberate crack along the edge.

Across the room, **Eleanor** knelt beside a mahogany desk, fingers tracing the edge of a ledger where she had jotted notes in her hurried, looping script. The paper was yellowed with age, but the words were still legible: *"Meet me at midnight. Don’t be late."* The date was three years ago.

A soft knock at the door made her jump. **Daniel** stood in the doorway, his coat damp with rain, his expression unreadable. He had arrived just as the storm had begun, his boots caked with mud, his breath visible in the cold night air.

Eleanor’s pulse quickened. "You’re late," she said, voice tight.

Daniel didn’t answer. Instead, he reached into his coat and pulled out a single silver locket, its chain frayed at one end. He pressed it into her hands. "It was yours," he murmured. "Found it in the rain."

She opened the locket. Inside was a miniature portrait of a woman—her mother, perhaps?—wearing a dress that matched the one she had worn on the night they met.

Daniel’s eyes flicked to the photograph on the desk. "You remember," he said, almost to himself.

Eleanor didn’t look away. "Of course I remember."

The lamplight flickered. The storm outside howled, the wind rattling the windows. Somewhere, a train whistle wailed in the distance, carrying echoes of a city far away.

She reached for the photograph. "Let’s go downstairs," she said. "We need to talk."

Daniel didn’t argue. He led the way down the stairs, the weight of years pressing between them like a silent promise.
