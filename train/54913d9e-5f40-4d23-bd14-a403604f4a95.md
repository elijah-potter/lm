The river bends softly, carving its path through the mist-laden morning light. Each ripple carries whispers of forgotten things—old stories, secrets wrapped in water, secrets the wind carries back and forth like a shared sigh. The reeds sway in time with the current, their leaves whispering secrets to the sky, to the earth, to the unseen things that linger in the hollows of the trees.

I sat beneath the willow’s shade, fingers tracing the grain of an old book where the pages have yellowed with time. The ink from the margins still glows faintly, like embers in the dark. The book’s title is lost to dust, but the spine holds the weight of centuries—each page a story, each turn a chapter, each breath a pause between them.

A child once sat here, too, their laughter echoing in the stillness, though now only the wind carries it. The willow’s roots coil around the stones, steady and unyielding, as if they’ve been here forever. The river’s current tugs at my thoughts like a gentle pull, and for a moment, I let myself drift.

The clouds part. The sun breaks through, not as a harsh glare but as a warm embrace, painting the sky in hues of gold and silver. The world breathes again. The river hums, the reeds sing, and the silence between the sounds is the space where everything else belongs. The weight in my chest lifts, not with relief but with understanding—the kind that settles like the tide at low tide, quiet and full.

I lean back in the grass, the cool earth beneath my palms. The world is still. The river is still. And for the first time in a long time, I don’t feel the need to fill the silence. Instead, I feel the quiet itself, the quiet that holds all the stories, all the stories that were never meant to be told but were meant to be heard in the spaces between. The return to calm is not the end of anything but the beginning of the next breath.
