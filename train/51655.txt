The essay is one of the most important parts of an application to graduate school. It is also something that you have control over. The essay is often referred to as a Statement of Purpose, Personal Statement or Autobiographical Statement. The vast majority of schools require them because it helps explain your reasons for applying. Most people find writing them to be painful. These tips will help provide the institution with the necessary information to reach your objective – acceptance to the best program that meets your needs.
Clearly understand why you are applying to the school’s program. Begin by reviewing your past, brainstorm ideas and then develop an outline. If possible, talk to students or alumni of the program for their input. Read sample essays that can serve as models. Think intentionally about the three to five key messages you want to send. Ask yourself, “How will the committee evaluate this essay?” Don’t expect to write the essay in one sitting. Take some time away from it to gain a fresh perspective, and invest the time to do it right. Put your heart and enthusiasm into it. Create your very best piece of writing. Be honest and most importantly, be yourself.
Read the directions carefully and do exactly what you are told to do. Determine if there is a word limit or other specifications. For example, they may request that you submit it with your full name, Social Security number, attach a form as a cover sheet, sign and date it. If they say double-space it and write three to five pages then don’t make it single spaced or longer because you think it looks better that way. Some schools limit the word count to 500 or even 250 which makes getting your key points across challenging with so few words. In this case you have to choose every word very carefully, ensuring that each one counts and has an impact.
Format and Style
The goal is to make the reader’s job easier and to convince them that you are an exceptional candidate. Make this writing sample visually appealing and easy to read. Make the reader’s job easier. This may seem obvious, but be sure you type your application. Believe it or not, some candidates actually handwrite it. I suggest using such standard fonts as Times New Roman or Arial. I recommend half inch margins and font sizes either 11 or 12; font size 10 is too small for some readers.
The paragraphs should not be too long. They ought to have a theme and make a selling point to the institution. Leave a space between the paragraphs for easier reading. Don’t print it on flashy or colored paper. Instead use white or ivory. Answer ALL the questions the school requires even if you don’t want to or find the questions too challenging.
Remember the essay is about you, but you are writing it for someone else. Put yourself in their shoes. Ask yourself, “What do they want to hear?” Grab the reader’s attention early so that they will want to read on. Use you resume be a reference to help provide specific and concrete examples to support any generalizations. Write in a positive an optimistic style. Showcase your strengths and never highlight your weaknesses. In order to write a winning essay, these sample questions should be addressed and can serve as a guide for constructing a balanced statement detailing your background, experiences and interests.
- How did you become interested in the field of…?
- What personal, professional and educational experiences have influenced or confirmed your interest in the field of…?
- If you were involved in another career field, what were the reasons for such a change?
- How have your family background and relationships contributed to your interest in the field of…?
- What are your reasons for pursuing graduate education in the field of…at this time?
- What appeals to you the most about this program?
- What expectations do you have for graduate studies? What area(s) of difficulty do you anticipate? Why?
- What are your major accomplishments and how have they prepared you for this program and field?
- Discuss your personal qualities and abilities that you could bring to the program?
- Discuss the reasons why you think you will be successful in this field?
- Discuss your career interests and goals?
- In what area of this field do you think you would like to work after you complete your degree?
- Briefly discuss what you think is the most important issue facing the field of…?
End the essay with a strong conclusion; one that makes them want to accept you. This is your last chance to persuade them that you will be an excellent addition to their institution. Hold yourself accountable by saying to yourself, “Is this a convincing essay? Would I accept this person?” I strongly suggest having the essay critiqued by a Career Counselor, a faculty member or some other professional you trust. You will have been writing and revising it for a long time, and so it is critical that you have a fresh set of eyes to review it for grammatical errors and helpful suggestions that will strengthen it. The activity of writing a graduate school essay can be a fun and a valuable process of self-discovery. You will learn a lot about yourself as you develop the key elements. If you are deliberate and patient, you can write a killer essay that can increase your chances of acceptance.
Tom’s Tip: “Good writing is rewriting.” – Truman Capote
