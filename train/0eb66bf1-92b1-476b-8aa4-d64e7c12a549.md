**The Weight of Silence**

She traced the rim of her coffee cup with the tip of her finger, watching the steam curl into the morning light. Outside, the city breathed in slow, uneven pulses—car horns, distant laughter, the occasional scream cut short by sirens. Inside, she was learning to listen.

He said the first thing that mattered was *time*, how it stretched and coiled like a snake, how it could be bent or broken, but never truly escaped. She believed him then, because he had once been a man who knew the weight of words before they were spoken.

Now, she wondered if he had known the weight of *truth* too, or if she had just been the first to stumble over it.

The mirror showed her a face that had once been young, but now held the hollows of things unsaid. She pressed her palm against the glass and whispered: *"I’m sorry."* The echo faded. The words were already gone.

Outside, a car door slammed. Somewhere, a child laughed too loud. The world kept turning.
