High cholesterol increases your risk for cardiovascular disease, which affects about 50 percent of all men and 33 percent of women at some point in their lives, according to Harvard Health Publications. Lowering your LDL cholesterol levels -- LDL is the harmful variety of cholesterol -- is as simple as eating low-cholesterol foods. To make your low-cholesterol diet more effective, you must also eliminate foods that spike LDL levels.
Foods rich in soluble fiber help lower your body’s cholesterol levels and reduce your body’s absorption of LDL cholesterol. MayoClinic.com states that consuming 10 or more grams of fiber per day decreases your cholesterol levels. [See Ref 3] Whole grains, fruits, vegetables and legumes are all excellent sources of fiber -- plant-based foods contain minimal, if any, cholesterol and are safe on a low-cholesterol diet. Rolled oats are a healthful choice; a 1/3-cup serving, measured dry, contains 102 calories and 2.7 grams of fiber. Add 1/2 cup of fresh raspberries for an additional 4 grams of fiber and just 32 calories. For a lunch or dinner side dish, opt for cooked beans; 1 cup of cooked black beans has 227 calories and 15 grams of fiber.
Consuming fatty fish provides abundant omega-3 fatty acids, which help reduce LDL levels, prevent blood clots and lower your blood pressure. Fish containing the highest amounts of omega-3s include lake trout, herring, mackerel, salmon, sardines, albacore tuna and halibut. Replacing cholesterol-laden red meats with fish will help lower your LDL levels and make your diet healthier. A 3-ounce helping of trout provides 126 calories, 17 grams of protein, 5.6 grams of mostly heart-healthy fats and just 49 milligrams of cholesterol. To compare, 3 ounces of sirloin steak has 161 calories, 17 grams of protein, 9.5 grams of fat and 61 milligrams of cholesterol without providing a high amount of omega-3s to aid in reducing your cholesterol levels.
Nuts and Oils
Mayo Clinic recommends consuming approximately 1.5 ounces of nuts each day to help reduce your blood cholesterol levels. Adding nuts to your diet can also help reduce your risk of heart disease. To avoid consuming too much sodium, eat unsalted nuts, and control your calorie intake by limiting yourself to just one serving per day. A 1.5-ounce helping of almonds, or about 34 almonds, has 245 calories, 9 grams of protein and 21 grams of mostly heart-healthy fats. Nuts do not contain any cholesterol. When you cook with oils, opt for olive oil -- it helps lower your LDL cholesterol without reducing your HDL, or beneficial, cholesterol levels. Extra-virgin olive oil is the best type for your cholesterol levels. As with nuts, you should limit the amount of olive oil you use to prevent overconsumption of calories. In 1 teaspoon of olive oil, you consume 4.5 grams of fat and 40 calories, with no cholesterol.
What to Eliminate
Eliminating foods that are high in saturated and trans fats will help reduce your cholesterol levels. Examples of these foods include red meats, full-fat dairy products, butter, shortening, margarine and anything fried. Eliminating inactivity from your lifestyle can also help; being overweight and inactive can increase your LDL cholesterol levels. Exercising and losing weight reverses the LDL-increasing effects of inactivity, reports Harvard Health Publications.
