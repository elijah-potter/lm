More than a dozen war First World War memorials across Britain have been given listed status and added protection to commemorate the centenary of the Commonwealth War Graves Commission.
The 15 memorials were designed by Sir Herbert Baker, the renowned architect celebrated for designing 113 cemeteries on the Western Front, including Tyne Cot near Ypres, in Belgium.
The memorials upgraded to Grade I listing to give them additional protection include the War Cloister at Winchester College, designed in the unusual form of a medieval cloister.
Listed for the first time is Hatfield War Memorial in Old Hatfield, which includes a shelter pavilion similar to the shelter buildings built in the cemeteries of the Western Front, and the cross in the East Sussex village of Etchingham, commemorating 15 young men from the village killed in battle.
Rudyard Kipling, who lived in the neighbouring village of Burwash, decided to cover the cost of the architect’s fees and unveiled the Etchingham memorial himself, on 28 April 1920.
The 15 memorials have been listed or upgraded by the Department for Culture, Media and Sport on the advice of Historic England.
Roger Bowdler, Director of Listing for Historic England said: “Sir Herbert Baker was one of the Commonwealth War Graves Commission’s principal architects, and designed some of their most important cemeteries, ranging from the utterly exceptional War Memorial Cloister for Winchester College to the simple, yet poignant, cross in his home village at Cobham in Kent.”
