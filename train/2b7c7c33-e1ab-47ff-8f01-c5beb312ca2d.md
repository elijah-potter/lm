**The Last Light**

The city hummed beneath its own weight, a slow pulse of neon and dust. I sat on the edge of the docks, my boots sinking into the wet stones, the saltwater clinging to my fingers like a second skin. The air smelled of fish and old oil, of things that had been forgotten and things that were still being made. A fishing boat drifted past, its hull glinting under the flickering streetlights, and for a moment, the world was just the rhythm of the waves and the distant cry of gulls. There was no hurry here, no destination but the endless horizon. The tide would come, the tide would go, and the night would stretch out like a blanket over everything.

Then, the phone rang.

It was my sister, her voice sharp with the weight of something unsaid. *"Dad’s in the hospital,"* she whispered into the line, her breath ragged. The words hit me like a physical blow, and for the first time in years, I felt the cold fingers of fear gripping my chest. The docks vanished. The boat was gone. All that remained was the sound of her voice, thin and trembling, and the unspoken terror that I wasn’t there for her. The world had shifted. The calm was gone.

I ran.

The hospital was a beast of steel and fluorescent lights, its halls echoing with the distant screams of machines and the hollow hum of alarms. My father lay in a room that smelled of antiseptic and old blood, his face pale as the sheets, his fingers twitching like insects trapped beneath glass. He didn’t see me when I walked in, didn’t recognize the voice that called his name. But I saw it—the way his chest rose, the way his lips parted, the way his eyes flickered open just for a second, like he was trying to remember where he was. Then they closed, and he was gone.

The doctor stood over him, her voice a dry rasp. *"It’s over, son. He’s done."*

I didn’t cry. I didn’t scream. I just stood there, my hands clenched into fists, and let the weight of it sink into my bones. The boat was still there, the docks still there, but they meant nothing now. The night had swallowed the world, and there was only this—this moment, this pain, this silence.

I left.

Outside, the city was still. The same boats drifted past, the same gulls cried into the wind. But this time, when I looked up, I saw the stars. They burned brighter than ever, like a promise in the dark. And for the first time in a long time, I thought maybe, just maybe, the world wasn’t as cruel as it seemed.
