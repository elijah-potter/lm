In a dimly lit, rain-soaked alleyway, flickering neon signs cast eerie reflections on wet pavement. The air hums with the distant droning of a city’s pulse, a mix of distant traffic and distant laughter from a nearby tavern. A lone figure moves through shadows, their silhouette barely defined against the flickering glow of a flickering streetlamp. The ground is littered with discarded bottles, rusted cans, and a single, half-buried newspaper. A discarded pair of gloves lies near a shattered bottle, their fabric stained with dried mud and something darker. A faint scent of damp earth and copper lingers, marking the passage of time and weather. The walls, lined with peeling posters and graffiti, whisper forgotten stories. The absence of movement, the weight of silence, tells more than any spoken word ever could. The environment itself seems to hold a story, a memory etched in every crack, every stain, every angle of light that dare not touch the dark corners.
