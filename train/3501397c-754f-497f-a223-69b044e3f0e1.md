The abandoned subway station, half-buried beneath a concrete overpass, hums with a perpetual low murmur. Doors sealed with rusted metal groan open at random intervals, revealing flickering fluorescent lights and the faint scent of damp earth and old food. Somewhere inside, a radio plays a loop of static with occasional whispers—no one knows what they’re saying. The platform is littered with discarded items: a half-empty energy drink, a crumpled newspaper, a pair of scuffed sneakers. No schedules, no maps, just the rhythmic clatter of shoes on metal. Some arrive alone; others come in groups, some talking, some silent. No one checks IDs. No one asks why. The station’s echo swallows every word, every step, every reason. The air is thick with the tension of unseen rules and the weight of something unsaid.
