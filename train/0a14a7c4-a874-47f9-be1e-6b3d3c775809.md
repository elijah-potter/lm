When the decision was made to act, the hesitation dissolved in a wave of urgency.
A single glance at the clock—just past midnight—marked the threshold beyond which the night’s shadows stretched too long.
The call came from an unknown number, a voice that had been ignored until then, whispering urgency: *"They’re coming."*

The choice wasn’t deliberate; it was a reaction to a flicker of danger, a spark of instinct that refused to be extinguished. A car door slammed outside, metal grinding against concrete, and the weight of the past two hours pressed on the mind—*what if they find me here?*

A key turned in the lock, the old door creaking open. The hallway beyond was dark, but the light from the streetlamps beyond the window cast long, jagged shadows. The choice wasn’t about courage; it was about survival. One step forward, two quick breaths, the sound of boots crunching gravel behind them. The moment of doubt dissolved into motion.

Now, the air was thick with the scent of damp earth and the faint metallic tang of fear. The decision had been made not by planning, but by necessity—by the need to be gone before the dawn revealed too much.

The path ahead was uncertain, but the past was already a memory. The act had begun.
