The air is thick with the scent of damp earth and rusted iron, suffocating in the stillness of an abandoned cathedral’s nave. The stained-glass windows, once vibrant, are now cracked and fogged with time, their colors muted into a sickly, unnatural green. The stone floor, slick with years of moisture, mirrors the dim light filtering through the broken panes. The air hums with an eerie, rhythmic vibration—like the distant, labored heartbeat of the building itself. Shadows stretch unnaturally long, clinging to the walls like living things. The silence is not absolute; it is a whisper, a sigh, a breath held too long. Even the wind, if there were one, would be gone, swallowed by the silence. The entire world feels suspended, suspended in a moment that is both frozen and collapsing inward.
