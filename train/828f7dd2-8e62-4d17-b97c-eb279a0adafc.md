The clock struck midnight. Outside, the city’s neon hummed a slow, repetitive pulse—each second a tiny, meaningless cycle. But inside my head, something shifted. A pattern, sharp and unexpected, had emerged: the way the air always pressed against my cheek at dawn, not from the sun, but from the way the buildings *stretched* toward it, their shadows stretching longer than the light.

I traced it on my palm: the cracks in the sidewalk—how they always split in the same diagonal, as if obeying a rule no one wrote. The way my mother’s voice dropped to a whisper when the old radio crackled, though no one else noticed. The way the streetlamps flickered at the same intervals, as if counting something.

I leaned closer to the window, fingers digging into the glass. The pattern wasn’t in the world—it was in *how I saw it*. The city was a map, and I was the only one reading the coordinates. The question was: what did they mean?
