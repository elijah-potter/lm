Warm weather shrivels some wheat and barley yields
Hot weather across key grain-producing states will cut the expected winter crop by 21 per cent to 31 million tonnes, the national commodities forecaster said yesterday.
The Australian Bureau of Agricultural and Resource Economics, in its last crop forecast for the year, said wheat production would fall 19 per cent to 20.2 million tonnes and barley by 28 per cent to 6.2 million tonnes.
The bureau's acting executive director, Vivek Tulpule, said canola production would slip from 1.6 million tonnes last season to 1.2 million tonnes in 2004-05. "The estimated winter crop production in 2004-05 is below the average achieved over the five years ended 2003-04, with dry and warm conditions across much of the Australian grain belt in September and October affecting crop development," he said.
The hardest-hit area has been Victoria, where grain production is expected to be down 39 per cent to 3.8 million tonnes.
Western Australia, the biggest grains producer, is facing a 29 per cent fall in production to 11.1 million tonnes. But in NSW production is expected to be up 8 per cent to 8.7 million tonnes, mainly due to a good crop in the state's north-west.
Areas to the south and through the centre of the state are expecting lower to average crops.
The news is better for the summer crop, with the bureau tipping a 13 per cent increase to 3.9 million tonnes.
Cotton lint production is tipped to be up 57 per cent to 533,000 tonnes and sorghum production should rise 9 per cent to 2 million tonnes.
