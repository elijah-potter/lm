The warehouse’s fluorescent lights flickered as the foreman yelled, *"Last call—last call!"*—then abruptly stopped. The forklift’s motor coughed to life, but the operator hesitated, clutching the wheel. The foreman’s clipboard clattered to the floor as he scribbled something on a napkin before tossing it aside. The forklift driver, a grizzled man with a scar across his knuckles, stared at the new sign taped to the ceiling: **"NO STRIKES, BUT NO RULES EITHER."** The foreman grinned, wiping his brow. *"You’re in the new system, kid. We’re testing."* The forklift’s brakes screeched as it lurched forward, skidding onto the wrong dock—where the new crew, still in their blue caps, had already begun stacking crates differently, faster. The air smelled of spilled coolant and anticipation. The foreman clapped the driver on the shoulder. *"Welcome to the future."* The forklift’s engine roared to life, but the driver didn’t look back. The rules had changed, and this time, there was no way to unlearn them.
