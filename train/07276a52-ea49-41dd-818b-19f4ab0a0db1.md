The morning light flickered through the curtains like a dying ember, casting jagged shadows across the floor. You sat at the kitchen table, fingers tracing the rim of your empty coffee cup, the steam long gone. A book lay open on the table—*The Alchemy of Us* by someone you’d forgotten—but the page was blank, the words blurred into static. Outside, birds sang a chorus that sounded too loud, too cheerful for the quiet ache in your chest.

You reached for your phone, thumb hovering over the screen like a ghost. The notification for your mom’s birthday message had been ignored for three days. The number blurred together with the static in your head. Then it clicked—not the phone, but the memory: the way her laugh had been sharp with something you couldn’t name, the way she’d said, *"You’re going to make something of this,"* as if you’d already failed. The words settled into your bones like rust.

The book’s spine cracked under your palm. You exhaled, slow and sharp, and the air smelled like old coffee and something metallic. The clarity—*the thing you’d been chasing*—wasn’t the answer you’d expected. It was the weight of everything you hadn’t said. The phone buzzed again. You didn’t answer. The light outside dimmed. The moment stretched, unraveling like thread, leaving only the hollow ache and the half-formed decision to let it go.
