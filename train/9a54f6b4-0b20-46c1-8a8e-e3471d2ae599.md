In a dimly lit alley, a shadowy figure crouched beneath a flickering neon sign that read **"Last Chance."** The cobblestones beneath them glistened with rainwater, and the air smelled of damp wool and old secrets. The figure pulled a single, silver key from their pocket—its tarnished surface etched with strange, spiraling designs.

A door creaked open ahead, its brass knocker hammered with a single word: **"Home."** Inside, a child sat at a desk, scribbling frantically on a notebook. The child’s eyes, wide with wonder, locked onto the key. The figure hesitated—then slipped inside, closing the door behind them.

Years later, the child, now an elderly man, stood before the same alley, the same sign, the same cobblestones. The silver key was tucked into his coat pocket. He reached for the door of **"Last Chance"** and turned the handle. The child from his past stepped out, breathless with surprise, holding a fresh notebook and a pen.

The figure handed the key to the child. **"You found it,"** they said. **"Now, what do you do?"**

The child grinned, the pen hovering midair. **"I open the door."** The figure smiled. **"Then we go home."** The door creaked open, and the child stepped through, the first step of the cycle repeating itself.

The alley was empty now, but the sign still glowed. **"Last Chance"** awaited another soul who might find the key—and another story to begin.
