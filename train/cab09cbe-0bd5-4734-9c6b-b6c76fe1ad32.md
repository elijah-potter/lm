In the dim glow of a half-lit room, the walls whisper secrets that only I hear—some clear, others tangled like vines wrapped around my resolve. I stand before the mirror, fingers tracing the lines of my own face, though I know better than to read them. The reflection does not lie, but it does not speak either. It is just another shape in the glass, just another version of myself that might as well be someone else entirely.

I used to believe in the certainty of choices. Now, I see them as threads, each pulled with hesitation, each one stretching the fabric of my decisions thinner. The past is a book I cannot turn back, its pages smudged with ink that has faded but never fully disappeared. And the future? It is a blank canvas, but I am the one who must paint it, though my brush trembles with doubt.

Sometimes, I wonder if I am even the same person who once thought they knew what they wanted. The voice in my head is a chorus of conflicting whispers—one telling me to trust my instincts, another cautioning me to question everything. I remember the first time I felt this way: a spark, a flicker of something new, something uncertain. It was fear, maybe, or hope, or both tangled together.

Now, I am caught between them, a boat adrift in the sea of my own thoughts. The waves rise and fall, and I do not know which direction to steer. The wind carries words that I do not fully understand, but I am learning to listen to the ones that make sense, even if they are not always the loudest.

Outside, the world moves on. People act, decisions are made, lives are lived. But for me, every step feels like a gamble, every choice a leap into the unknown. The doubt is not a flaw—it is part of the process, the messy, uncertain part that makes everything feel alive.

I do not know if this is a curse or a blessing. Maybe it is just the way things are. And perhaps, in the end, that is enough.
