## Project Chimera: Rulebook

**A Cooperative Game of Scientific Crisis Management**

**Introduction**

Project Chimera places you in the roles of specialized researchers at the Global Research Initiative (GRI), a multinational organization dedicated to addressing existential threats. A rapidly evolving, genetically engineered virus – designated "Chimera" – has emerged, exhibiting unpredictable mutation and devastating effects on global ecosystems and human populations. Your mission is to understand Chimera’s behavior, develop countermeasures, and ultimately contain the crisis before it overwhelms the world. Success depends entirely on your ability to collaborate, prioritize research, and make difficult decisions under immense pressure.

**1. Components**

*   **Game Board:** A world map depicting key regions, each with varying levels of Chimera infection and research potential.
*   **Researcher Cards (8):** Each card represents a researcher with unique skills and starting resources (e.g., Virologist, Epidemiologist, Geneticist, Logistician).
*   **Chimera Mutation Cards (60):** Represent the virus's unpredictable evolution, introducing new challenges and altering its characteristics.
*   **Research Cards (45):** Represent potential research projects, categorized by focus area (e.g., Diagnostics, Therapeutics, Containment).
*   **Resource Tokens:** Represent funding (blue), samples (green), and data (yellow).
*   **Infection Markers:** Indicate the severity of Chimera infection in each region.
*   **Event Cards (30):** Introduce unexpected complications and opportunities.
*   **Crisis Track:** Measures the overall global situation, escalating as Chimera spreads.
*   **Dice (6):** Used for research attempts and event resolution.
*   **First Player Marker:** Indicates the current player's turn order.



**2. Setup**

1.  Place the Game Board in the center of the playing area.
2.  Shuffle the Chimera Mutation Cards and place them face down to form the Mutation Deck. Draw the top three cards and place them face up next to the deck. These represent the current mutations affecting Chimera.
3.  Shuffle the Research Cards and place them face down to form the Research Deck. Reveal the top three cards and place them face up next to the deck.
4.  Shuffle the Event Cards and place them face down to form the Event Deck.
5.  Place Infection Markers on designated starting regions as indicated on the Game Board.
6.  Place the Crisis Track marker on the starting position.
7.  Each player chooses a Researcher Card and takes the corresponding starting resources as indicated on the card.
8.  Determine the first player randomly and give them the First Player Marker.



**3. Turn Structure**

Each player’s turn consists of the following phases, performed in order:

1.  **Event Phase:** Draw the top card from the Event Deck and resolve its effects. These effects may introduce new challenges, provide opportunities, or impact the Crisis Track.
2.  **Action Phase:** You have three actions to spend. You may choose from the following actions:
    *   **Research (1 Action):** Attempt to complete a face-up Research Card. Roll a number of dice as indicated on the card, modified by your Researcher’s skills and any applicable regional bonuses or penalties. Success is determined by rolling a target number or higher on the dice.
    *   **Gather Resources (1 Action):** Collect Resource Tokens from a region. The type and quantity of resources available depend on the region’s characteristics.
    *   **Move (1 Action):** Move your Researcher to an adjacent region.
    *   **Containment (2 Actions):** Reduce Infection Markers in your current region.
    *   **Share Resources (1 Action):** Transfer Resource Tokens to another player in the same region.
3.  **Mutation Phase:** Draw the top card from the Mutation Deck and apply its effects. These effects can alter Chimera's characteristics, introduce new challenges, or worsen the global situation. Replace one of the face-up Mutation Cards with the new card.
4.  **Pass the Marker:** Pass the First Player Marker to the player on your left.

**4. Trade-off Mechanics**

Project Chimera incorporates several mechanics that force difficult choices and strategic trade-offs:

1.  **Mutation Prioritization:** When a new Mutation Card is drawn, the team must immediately decide which of the three face-up Mutation Cards to discard. This decision requires evaluating the immediate threat posed by each mutation and considering the long-term consequences of ignoring any of them.
2.  **Resource Allocation Dilemma:**  At the end of each round (after all players have taken a turn), the team collectively decides how to allocate a limited number of “Global Funding” tokens. These tokens can be used to accelerate research, improve containment efforts, or mitigate the effects of Events. The team must prioritize which projects or regions receive funding, knowing that neglecting one area may have severe repercussions.
3.  **Containment vs. Research:**  Regions with high Infection Markers generate "Chaos" tokens each round. These tokens hinder research attempts. The team must decide whether to prioritize immediate containment efforts to reduce Chaos, or focus on research, risking increased Chaos and potentially accelerating the crisis.



**5. Win/Loss Conditions**

*   **Win Condition:** The team wins if they successfully complete a designated number of "Cure" Research Cards (typically three, depending on difficulty – see Scaling Difficulty below).
*   **Loss Condition:** The team loses if:
    *   The Crisis Track marker reaches the final position.
    *   A designated number of regions become “Overrun” (indicated on the Game Board).
    *   The team runs out of Event Cards.



**6. Scaling Difficulty**

The game’s difficulty scales based on the number of players:

*   **Two Players:** Reduce the number of "Cure" Research Cards required to win to two. Start with fewer Infection Markers on the Game Board.
*   **Three Players:** Play the game as described in the core rules.
*   **Four Players:** Increase the number of "Cure" Research Cards required to win to four. Start with more Infection Markers on the Game Board.  Introduce a “Mutation Surge” – after every two rounds, draw *two* Mutation Cards instead of one.



**7. Advanced Variants**

*   **Specialized Research Focus:**  At the start of the game, each player secretly draws a "Focus Card" that dictates a specific research area they must prioritize.  This adds a layer of hidden objectives and encourages specialization.
*   **Political Interference:** Introduce a "Political Influence" track. Certain Event Cards will shift this track, introducing penalties or restrictions based on global political pressures.
*   **Dynamic Mutation Effects:** Implement a system where Mutation Cards have cascading effects, triggering additional events or modifying existing challenges based on the current game state.




**Designer’s Note**

Project Chimera was designed to engage players in a challenging cooperative experience that fosters collaboration and strategic decision-making. The game aims to subtly explore the complexities of scientific research, the importance of risk assessment, and the ethical considerations involved in responding to global crises. While the narrative is fictional, the underlying themes—the unpredictable nature of scientific discovery, the potential for unintended consequences, and the necessity of international cooperation—reflect real-world challenges. The trade-off mechanics are intended to highlight the difficult choices that scientists and policymakers face when dealing with existential threats, encouraging players to consider the broader implications of their actions.
