It wasn’t supposed to happen like this. The first time she saw him, he was a blur of motion through the café’s steamy windows—his tie half-untucked, the way his fingers traced the rim of a coffee cup as he leaned against the counter. She’d been there for the same reason he was: waiting, half-listening to the hum of the city outside. The air smelled like stale espresso and something metallic, like old keys or the first frost of winter.

He asked her name before she could think of one. She’d forgotten how to breathe. Now, the days stretched like loose threads, each one a question mark. She’d spent months planning something—*something better*—and instead, she was here, in a room that smelled like dust and regret, watching him laugh too loud, his laugh cracking the silence between them like glass.

She told herself it was the coffee. That it was the city. That it was just another story she’d outrun. But the truth was simpler: she didn’t know how to stop.
