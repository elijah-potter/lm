The meeting ended abruptly when the sudden alarm blared through the office. The door burst open, revealing storm clouds rolling in from the west. The power flickered as rain pounded against the windows. The plan for the supply drop was scrapped immediately, with everyone scrambling to secure personal belongings as the manager hurried to assess the situation.

Rain-soaked windows, half-eaten sandwiches, and a half-drunk coffee on the desk became the new backdrop for a tense huddle. The team debated whether to delay the next shift or improvise, but the urgency of the weather forced a quick decision. The original plan—coordinated at midnight—was abandoned. The logistics manager’s notes were hastily scribbled over with new ones, outlining a modified route, extra caution, and a reinforced team dynamic.

Outside, the wind howled through the empty streets, and the first flash of lightning illuminated the chaos inside. The plan was not dead, but it had been rewritten in real-time, adapting to the storm’s unpredictable grip.
