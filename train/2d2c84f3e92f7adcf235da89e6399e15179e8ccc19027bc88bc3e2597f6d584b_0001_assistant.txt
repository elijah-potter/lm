Lieutenant Gary Greeves was an American military hero. He'd served in the Air Force since 2009 before being bestowed the honor of becoming a Marine Recon in 2015. He was tough, strong, and took care of others. He was at a standstill with his enemy. He was gridlocked and isolated from his team. He needed to quickly get back around the wall. He bravely ran into the line of fire to protect and help his combat buddy, who had fallen in the line of duty.

Greeves took an oath and swore to protect the lives of his brothers in arms. He would leave no man behind. But now, he needed to get back with the body. To do so, he'd have to run out of cover, exposed, to a tree he was pretty sure was occupied by a hostile enemy force. He had a feeling that the hostile Tango was exposed and isolated from his team, too. It was a matter of who would make the first strike.

Ahmed al-Rahman Arif was a Taliban soldier and a leader in his group. He had no choice but to join the group to support his family, way back in 1998. His duty was to institute a strict interpretation of Qur'anic instruction and jurisprudence. He believed that by taking more control, this duty would be accomplished. This was the "why" that brought him to every decision that he did. It was his calling, his purpose, and something he would die doing.

A cousin had served alongside him since they were children. The cousin sacrificed himself in the line of fire to cause the enemy to pause or retreat. Ahmed rushed to aid the cousin, who appeared to have eliminated one enemy. Ahmed felt the rush of bullets next to him and quickly climbed into a tree. He did not have much cover, so he sat very still. Just out of sight was another enemy. Ahmed needed to get back to his group but would need to get out of the tree to do so. He believed the enemy might see him, so he waited. It was a matter of who would make the first strike.
