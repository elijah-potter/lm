**Scene: The Weight of Silence**

The hallway smelled of damp wood and old books—an echo of forgotten conversations. Elias clenched his fists against the wall, his reflection in the cracked glass not moving. Outside his window, the city hummed, indifferent to his unraveling. His breath hitched as he turned toward the study, the door ajar like an open wound. Inside, the desk was a maze of half-finished drafts, a half-eaten sandwich, and a single empty coffee cup. His fingers trembled as he reached for the first page—*The Letter*—his last attempt at closure.

The words on the page were raw, honest. *"I don’t want to die like this."* His hands shook as he tore it in half, then folded it into a tight ball. He hurled it against the wall, watching it shatter into confetti of paper. The sound was hollow, like the silence inside him. The mirror in the hallway caught his reflection one last time before he turned away, his jaw set, his throat tight.

The elevator doors hissed open. Below, the streetlights flickered, but none of them would ever see the truth in his eyes—the truth that he had stopped believing in the stories he was supposed to tell himself. The doors closed behind him, sealing him in the dark.
