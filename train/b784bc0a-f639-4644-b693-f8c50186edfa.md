In the hollowed-out core of the old mill, where the gears groaned like forgotten prayers, a single candle flickered against the damp stone. Its flame, though weak, cast a ghostly path along the walls—carved with symbols long forgotten, some still humming faintly with the echoes of their past lives.

The miller, his face a mask of sunken cheeks, wiped his brow with a rag that smelled of mildew and old coffee. He had come here not to work, but to wait. To listen. The river below whispered secrets in the dark, and the wind carried whispers from the trees—some human, some not.

At the center of the mill stood a small, rusted box, its lid slightly ajar. Inside, something glinted: a single, smooth pebble, not like any found in these waters. It had been rolled into the box by an unseen hand, perhaps years ago.

The miller exhaled slowly. He reached in, his fingers brushing the pebble, and when he pulled it out, his thumb brushed against something else—a tiny, silver key, worn smooth by time. It wasn’t meant to unlock this box. But it would open the next one.

Outside, the first light of dawn crept through the cracks in the stone, painting the mill in gold. The river still murmured, but the wind had changed its tune.

The miller pocketed the key. Tomorrow, he’d find the next box. And the next.
