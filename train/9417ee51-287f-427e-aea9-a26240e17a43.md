A sunrise that lingers too long before the alarm sounds, stretching the morning like syrup on toast.
The coffee brews too slowly, its aroma thick as the tension in the shoulders that ache from sitting too still.
The clock ticks away like a metronome set to the speed of a child’s wobbly first steps—each minute feels like a half-step, a false start.
The list of tasks stretches like an uncut rug, each item demanding more energy than the last, while the daylight fades into evening before the day’s first proper end.
Even the quiet moments—the pause after a sentence, the space between breaths—feel like stolen seconds, too brief to linger, too precious to waste.
The world moves faster than the mind can catch up, and the days blur into a blur of "almost" and "maybe" and "too soon."
By nightfall, the day is done, but the exhaustion lingers like a half-finished thought, unspoken and unresolved.
It’s not that time is running out; it’s that time is running *too fast*.
