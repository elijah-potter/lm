**Notes on Decisions and Potential Adjustments:**

*   **Specificity:** I deliberately included specific examples (scope creep, resource allocation, requirements gathering) to provide concrete reasons for leaving. This demonstrates that the decision wasn’t impulsive and highlights systemic issues. Adjust these examples to accurately reflect *your* reasons.
*   **Positive Framing:** I started and ended with expressions of gratitude and well wishes. This maintains a professional tone, even when delivering difficult news.
*   **Focus on Systems, Not People:** The letter focuses on *processes* and *systems* rather than criticizing individual colleagues. This is crucial for avoiding unnecessary conflict. While personal frustrations might exist, they are best left unstated in a formal resignation letter.
*   **Transition Offer:** Offering to help with the transition demonstrates professionalism and a willingness to leave on good terms.
*   **Two Weeks' Notice:**  I assumed a standard two weeks’ notice. Adjust this if your contract or company policy dictates otherwise.
*   **Legal Review:** *Crucially*, before submitting this letter, it's wise to have it reviewed by an employment lawyer, especially if you have concerns about potential legal ramifications or if you anticipate a negative reaction from your employer.
*   **Delivery Method:** Consider how you will deliver this letter. A formal, printed copy, delivered in person, is often the most professional approach, followed by an email copy for documentation.
