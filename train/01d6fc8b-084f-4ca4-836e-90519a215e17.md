Long, wavy shadows stretch from a crumbling stone archway at dawn,
where morning mist curls like smoke through fractured mortar.
The sun casts deep, jagged lines across moss-stained pavement—
each crack a silent story, every shadow a forgotten key.
Midday turns them to sharp, brittle veins of light against the north wall,
where a cracked window glows faintly, its glass like a broken mirror.
Evening paints them gold, bending toward a courtyard’s rusted iron fence,
where lanterns flicker—just their light and the slow, creeping dark beyond.

No names, only the weight of time in the space between.
