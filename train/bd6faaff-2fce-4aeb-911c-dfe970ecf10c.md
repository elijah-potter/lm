You should have left hours ago. The storm raged outside, turning the alley into a slick, whispering void. Neon reflections off the broken glass of a half-collapsed storefront flickered like dying stars. The air smelled of wet concrete and old cigarette smoke—things that weren’t supposed to be here.

She pressed the button on her phone, the cold metal biting her palm. The screen lit up, displaying a single number: **213-789-5642**. No name, no context—just a voice mail. She hadn’t left a message yet. Hadn’t *needed* to.

A floorboard creaked behind her. The sound was wrong—too deliberate, too *aware*. She spun, fingers tightening around the grip of her knife. The alley was empty. The storm still howled. But the air hummed with something else—something like a warning.

Then the lights died.

Darkness swallowed her whole. The knife slipped from her grip. The only sound was her own ragged breathing, the distant wail of sirens, and the faint, distorted voice of a stranger’s message playing through her speaker:

*"They’re looking for you. You shouldn’t be here."*

Silence.

Then—footsteps, fast and heavy, approaching from the shadows.
