The road ahead was a winding dirt track, flanked by towering pines and misty hollows. The signpost, half-buried in the undergrowth, read **"Main Road: 3 miles"** in bold, weathered letters. Beside it, a smaller sign, faded but still legible, proclaimed **"Forest Path: 5 miles"**—though the true distance was likely longer, winding through unmarked trails.

She hesitated, her boots crunching on loose gravel. The main road was safer, smoother, and would take her to town in half the time. But the forest path—it hummed with stories. The scent of damp earth and pine resin filled her lungs as she turned away from the sign. The trees pressed closer, their trunks gnarled with age, their branches weaving a canopy of shadows. A single beam of sunlight slashed through the foliage, illuminating a path that vanished into the undergrowth before reappearing again, like a secret.

She stepped forward, her boots sinking slightly into the damp earth. The longer path wasn’t just distance—it was choice. And choices, she knew, were rarely simple.
