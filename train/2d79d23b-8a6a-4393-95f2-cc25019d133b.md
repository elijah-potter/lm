At dusk, elongated shadows stretch diagonally across cracked pavement, merging into the cracks like veins of black dust. The nearest ones cast by a lone streetlamp flicker sharply, their edges jagged as if carved by unseen hands. To the west, the church steeple looms tall, its shadow a slow, steady descent toward the horizon, dissolving into the fading light like ink in water. The alleyways, lined with rusted dumpsters, cast deeper, denser shadows—some fragmented by the uneven ground, others pooling like ink in the mud. The streetlights here are few and far between, their beams cutting through the gloom like distant stars. In the distance, the silhouette of a bridge arches over a river, its shadow a faint, wavy line across the water, reflecting only the last remnants of the sunset.
