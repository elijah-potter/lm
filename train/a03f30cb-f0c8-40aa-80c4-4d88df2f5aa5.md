In the hollowed-out core of the old city, where the streets were cobblestone and the air smelled of rust, there was a clock that didn’t tick. Its brass face, cracked with time, showed a single hand frozen at 3:17 AM, a time no one remembered. The clock tower’s chains had long since rusted away, and the pendulum hung still, as if holding its breath.

Every morning, the city’s inhabitants would wake to find the clock’s hands had moved, though no one had touched it. Some whispered it was cursed; others claimed it was a ghost. But the truth was simpler: the clock was alive. It fed on the moments stolen from those who passed by, its gears grinding softly as it absorbed the seconds they had never claimed. By sundown, its hands would slow, and the city would forget the time had passed.

One evening, a thief named Elias stole a pocket watch from a merchant’s shop, unaware of the clock’s power. That night, as Elias lay awake, the clock’s face glowed faintly. The hands began to move on their own, dragging time backward. Elias awoke the next morning to find the thief’s watch had stopped at 12:00 AM, but his own had returned to 11:55 PM. He had spent an hour in a world where time was a river, flowing backward.

Elias realized the clock wasn’t just a timekeeper—it was a thief of moments, a sentinel guarding the unspent. He tried to destroy it, but the moment he reached for the hammer, the clock’s face pulsed brighter, and the seconds reversed. The tower collapsed in a rush of falling stone, but the clock survived, its gears spinning faster than ever, hungry for more.

Years later, the city’s people still told stories of the clock that didn’t tick, of hands that moved without hands, and of time itself bending to its will. Some said the clock had been broken beyond repair, others that it still waited, patient and relentless, in the shadows of the old city, counting down the moments they refused to live.
