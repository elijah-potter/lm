The Grand Clock of Eldermere stood silent at the end of the Hollow Street, its brass face etched with a thousand years of forgotten time. Its pendulum, once a vibrant emerald, had dulled to a rusted brown, marking the last century of its existence. The town had long since forgotten its purpose—until the day the wind carried whispers of a thief who had stolen the clock’s heart.

The thief, a young clockmaker named Elias, had been tasked by the Council to repair the Great Clock. But when he lifted the lid, he found not gears but a single, glowing blue gem embedded in its center. The gem pulsed with energy, and the clock’s hands began to move on their own, rewriting time in a spiral of golden light. Elias, terrified, dropped the gem into the river—but the river, too, began to weave time into a single, endless stream.

By the time the Council found him, the town had unraveled. The river ran backward, children aged backward, and the streets shifted like sand. Elias, now a man of no age, found himself standing in the center of a clock face where the hands had stopped—forever.

Outside, the world paused. The clock’s hands, now frozen, were the only thing that remained.

And the thief, the only thing that mattered.
