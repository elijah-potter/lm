A crumbling stone bridge arched over a narrow, moss-covered stream where time seems to bend. Its weathered planks whisper secrets of forgotten storms, yet its joints still hold firm. Nearby, a rusted bench sits half-buried in ivy, its surface etched with the names of those who once paused here. The air hums with the sound of distant laughter, but the laughter fades into the wind like smoke. This place feels like a half-remembered dream—roots of the past clawing at the soil, yet the earth beneath it endures, unchanged. The stones remember the years, but the sky above shifts endlessly, as if the land itself is waiting to be rediscovered.
