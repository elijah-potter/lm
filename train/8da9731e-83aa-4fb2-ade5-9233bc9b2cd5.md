The wind carried the scent of rain over the cracked asphalt of the old warehouse district. Inside, the flickering neon of a half-dead sign pulsed like a dying heartbeat—*LOST & FOUND*—while the echoes of a keyboard clattered against the walls, each keystroke a ghostly whisper in the dark. The air smelled of aged paper and something metallic, like old blood dried in the shadows.

The door at the end of the dimly lit hallway creaked open, and for a heartbeat, the silence stretched too long. Then footsteps—slow, deliberate—approached. The person behind them was wrapped in a coat that had seen better decades, the sleeves rolled up to reveal hands that moved with practiced ease, fingers tracing the edge of a worn leather satchel.

"Lost," the voice said, low and measured, as if the word itself was a confession. The person in the room—someone who had been staring at the flickering screen for far too long—didn’t move. The screen’s glow cast long, jagged shadows across their face, half-masked by the hood of their own coat.

"Found," came the reply, dry as dust.

The satchel was heavy. Inside, the person pulled out a single, unopened letter—the kind that had been waiting for years, sealed with a date that didn’t match the year’s calendar. The other person didn’t ask why it had been found. They just took it, their fingers brushing against the paper, the ink already blurred with age.

Outside, the rain began again, a slow, rhythmic patter against the rusted roof. The neon sign flickered once, then died. The warehouse door swung shut behind them, sealing the past in the dark.

The first step was already taken.
