The office clock struck seven, and the fluorescent hum of the cubicle walls faded into static. Across the room, Sarah adjusted her tie, fingers tracing the hem like a second skin. Her eyes flickered to the ceiling—then back down to her watch. Each time she glanced, the numbers aligned just so: 7:04, 7:08, 7:12, the minutes incrementing by four.

She exhaled sharply, almost missed the next beat. The pattern was there: the seconds ticked in perfect increments, but the minutes never strayed from the fourth mark. A mistake in her watch? No—she’d checked it at noon. The office’s air conditioner groaned, the thermostat blinking red. Sarah’s fingers tightened around her mug of coffee, black, unadulterated. The numbers on the wall clock mirrored her heartbeat: steady, predictable, counting down.

She stood, swaying slightly, and whispered to herself: *"Four. The pattern’s four."* The door creaked open. A stranger in a trench coat stepped in, eyes darting like trapped spiders. The moment their gaze locked, the clock stopped. The seconds stretched, then—

*Four.*
