In the quiet town of Eldermere, where the cobblestones whispered secrets of forgotten wars, there lived a boy named Elias who could see the ghosts of the past. His eyes, sharp as a hawk’s, caught glimpses of shadows that others could not—memories of battles long buried, names of the lost, and the unspoken bargains made between the living and the dead.

One evening, as Elias sat by the old well, the water within it began to shimmer like liquid moonlight. He reached in and pulled forth a single, perfect rose, its petals the color of the dawn. The moment his fingers brushed them, the well’s surface rippled, revealing a face—his own, but older, with hollow eyes and a smile that did not reach them. *"You carry the weight of the past,"* the voice in his mind whispered, *"but you have yet to ask what it costs to carry it."* The rose crumbled to dust in his palm.

Elias looked down at the empty space where the petals had been. The well had vanished, leaving only the scent of roses lingering in the air. He stood, his heart pounding, and asked himself the question that had been gnawing at him since childhood: *What do I owe the dead for the gifts they leave behind?*
