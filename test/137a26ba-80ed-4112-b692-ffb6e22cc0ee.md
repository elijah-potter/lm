**Version 1: Formal & Detailed**

[Your Address]
[Date]

[Boss's Name]
[Boss's Title]
[Company Name]

**Subject: Resignation – [Your Name]**

Dear [Boss's Name],

Please accept this letter as formal notification that I am resigning from my position as Project Manager, effective two weeks from today, [Date of Last Day].

This decision has not been reached lightly. While I appreciate the opportunities afforded to me during my time at [Company Name], I have become increasingly concerned about the prevailing project management methodologies and their impact on the team's effectiveness and the quality of our deliverables. Specifically, the consistent pressure to expedite project timelines without commensurate adjustments to scope or resource allocation has resulted in a pattern of compromised technical debt and unsustainable working hours for the development team.  My attempts to advocate for more realistic planning and risk mitigation strategies, supported by data demonstrating the correlation between rushed timelines and increased defect rates, have unfortunately not resulted in meaningful change.

Furthermore, the recent shift towards a purely Agile framework, implemented without adequate training or support for the team, has introduced significant operational challenges. The lack of clarity regarding sprint goals and the absence of a dedicated Scrum Master have contributed to a sense of confusion and reduced overall productivity. I believe a more measured and consultative approach to process implementation would be beneficial.

I am committed to ensuring a smooth transition during my remaining two weeks. I am available to assist in training my replacement and documenting ongoing projects to minimize disruption.  I wish you and [Company Name] continued success in the future.

Sincerely,

[Your Signature]
[Your Typed Name]

**Notes on Version 1:** This version is highly formal and detailed. It explicitly states the reasons for leaving, providing specific examples. The language is professional and avoids any emotionality. This is appropriate if you have a relatively distant relationship with your boss or if you feel it's important to provide a thorough explanation for your departure.  It's also suitable if you anticipate a potentially difficult reaction and want to have a clear record of your concerns.

Dear [Boss's Name],

I am writing to formally notify you of my resignation from my role as Project Manager, with my last day of employment being [Date of Last Day], two weeks from today.

I’ve genuinely appreciated the opportunities I’ve had at [Company Name], and I’ve learned a great deal during my time here. However, I've reached a point where I feel I can no longer effectively contribute to the team’s success given the current project management practices. The ongoing pressure to accelerate project timelines without adjusting scope or resources has consistently led to technical compromises and placed considerable strain on the development team's workload. I’ve tried to address these concerns by suggesting more realistic planning and highlighting the impact of rushed timelines on defect rates, but unfortunately, these efforts haven't resulted in a change in approach.

I'm also concerned about the recent move to a purely Agile framework. While I believe in the principles of Agile, the implementation has lacked the necessary training and support for the team, which has created some challenges. A more collaborative approach to adopting new processes would, in my opinion, be more effective.

I’m committed to making my departure as seamless as possible. I’ll be happy to assist with training a replacement and documenting ongoing projects to ensure a smooth handover. I wish you and the team all the best.

Sincerely,

[Your Signature]
[Your Typed Name]

**Notes on Version 2:** This version maintains a professional tone but is slightly less rigid than Version 1. The language is a bit more conversational ("I'm committed to making my departure as seamless as possible" instead of "I am committed to ensuring a smooth transition"). It acknowledges the positive aspects of the experience more explicitly ("I’ve genuinely appreciated the opportunities…"). This is appropriate if you have a reasonably good relationship with your boss and want to leave on a positive note while still explaining your reasons for leaving.
