The air smells faintly of rain and old wood—earthy, warm. The cobblestones beneath my boots are slick with morning dew, a familiar crunch beneath my feet. The grand archway at the entrance stands slightly ajar, its ironwork rusted but still proud, as if holding its breath after years away. Inside, the dim glow of the streetlamps casts long shadows across the worn wooden floors.

The café’s counter hums with the usual clatter of teacups and the occasional murmur of conversation. The barista, always the same—same dark circles under their eyes, same way of swirling the latte art just so—takes my order with the same tired smile. The scent of fresh-baked croissants lingers in the air, mixed with the faint, nostalgic aroma of the old library’s bookshelves behind me.

I step onto the terrace, where the same wrought-iron railing curves around the edge of the garden. The roses bloom heavier than when I last saw them, their thorns sharp against my fingers. A child’s laughter drifts from somewhere inside, and for a moment, I forget how long I’ve been gone.
