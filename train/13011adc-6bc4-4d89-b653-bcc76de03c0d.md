**The Fireplace Lounge, late autumn evening.**

The air smelled of damp wool and burnt tobacco. Elias sat hunched over a half-empty whiskey, his fingers tracing the rim of his glass. Across the room, a woman with silver-streaked hair and a cloak too warm for the season stirred the embers of the hearth. She had been listening—*really* listening—as he recounted the tale of the Hollow Man, but she didn’t interrupt. Not yet.

She leaned forward now, her voice a whisper but sharp enough to cut glass.

*"You said he didn’t speak,"* she murmured. *"But he sang."*

Elias exhaled smoke, the curl of it curling into the shadows. *"Not with words. With… music. The kind that doesn’t need ears to hear."* He paused, then added, *"The notes in his throat were like the wind through an empty church."*

The woman’s fingers traced the rim of her own glass. *"What did the church hold?"*

*"Nothing,"* Elias said softly. *"Just dust and echoes. And once, a child who thought he saw his mother’s reflection in the stained glass."* He swallowed. *"She vanished mid-step."*

A beat. Then, the door creaked open.

*"You’re telling stories,"* a voice said, dry as parchment. *"Again."*

The woman’s gaze flicked to the newcomer—a man in a coat patched with stolen leather, his eyes sharp as flint. *"We’re listening,"* she said.

The Hollow Man’s voice slithered from the shadows, a whisper so fine it might have been a breath: *"The child was the last to see her. The others… they were already gone."* The air hummed. The fire roared. The man in the patchwork coat exhaled, a laugh that sounded like a rusted hinge. *"You’re not the first to say that, are you?"*

Elias looked at the woman. *"She’s been here before,"* he said. *"With him."*

The Hollow Man’s laughter was a chorus of bells, then nothing. The room still. The woman stood, her cloak billowing like a ghost’s. *"Next time,"* she said, *"you’ll tell us what he left behind."*

Outside, the wind howled.
