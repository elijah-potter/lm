A crumbling stone archway at the edge of an abandoned forest, its moss-covered surfaces whispering secrets in the damp breeze. The walls pulse faintly with an unseen hum, like static on a radio tuned to an empty frequency. At night, the wind carries the sound of distant laughter—echoes of voices long vanished—resounding through the hollows of the trees, as if the place itself is keeping silent company. The air smells of old wood and something metallic, like copper and damp earth. Shadows move where no one is, and the ground beneath your feet seems to breathe, shifting ever so slightly when you stand still. The trees lean in, their branches forming a living curtain that filters light like a slow, deliberate sigh. The silence isn’t absence; it’s the sound of everything waiting.
