How Do Leak Detection Sensors Work On Double Wall Tanks?
Tanks that have two walls and a space (called an interstitial space) in between them are known as double-walled tanks. These tanks are sometimes referred to as interstitial tanks. This area’s mission is to serve as an additional line of defence in the event that the tank containing the liquid in question springs a leak or otherwise becomes compromised. Tanks with two walls are common in many different kinds of businesses, including those dealing with chemicals, oil and gas, and environmental management.
When choosing a sensor for detecting leaks in double-walled tanks, it is vital to take into consideration the kind of liquid that is being held, the environment in which the tank is situated, and the amount of sensitivity that is sought. For instance, a chemical storage tank may require a sensor with a higher level of sensitivity to detect small leaks, whereas an oil storage tank may require a sensor with a lower level of sensitivity that can withstand harsh environments. Both types of storage tanks, however, may require a sensor with the ability to detect leaks.
