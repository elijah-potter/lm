Critical thinking fosters your ability to reflect
Critical thinking is the ability to think about your own thinking and the thinking of others. It’s not just about being able to analyze a situation and make logical conclusions. It also involves being able to reflect on your own thoughts, questions them, and critique them so that you can improve how you reason.
Critical thinkers are able to consider all sides of an argument before concluding. If their initial conclusion is wrong, they’re willing to change their minds when presented with new information.
They realize that no one has all of the answers, so they use good judgment when making decisions based on incomplete, yet available information.
The result is higher-quality thought processes. Critical thinkers are more likely than most people are at finding flaws in an argument or identify assumptions behind statements made by others.
They’re better at making sense of complex situations; they think more clearly under pressure or when under stress. And they tend at avoiding common mistakes made by those who don’t practice reflection regularly (like jumping too quickly into conclusions).
