A barren valley stretches beneath a low, hazy sun, its cracked earth a dull ochre. Rust-colored stones jut from the ground in jagged formations, remnants of what once grew tall. The air is stale, thick with dust and the scent of damp earth after the last monsoon vanished. A few stubborn patches of green thread through the barrenness—weeds clinging to sun-baked soil. The wind carries no sound, only the distant, faint whisper of a river long since dried up, its bed now a shallow channel of blackened water. The sky is a heavy, bruised hue, punctuated by the occasional drift of black clouds that linger like forgotten shadows. The horizon is unclear, the land rolling endlessly toward the faint glow of a distant, unfamiliar city.
