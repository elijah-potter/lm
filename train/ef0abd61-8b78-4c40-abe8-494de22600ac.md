A morning rain pattering against the window left the garden floor damp. The child’s small hands, still stiff from sleep, reached for the damp patch and pressed the toe of a discarded shoe into the soil, marking a tiny circle. No one saw it—no adult noticed the child’s quiet attempt to engage the world, no elder commented on the effort, and the rain kept falling.

Later, the child found the shoe’s edge still embedded in the earth, a ghostly print in the dampness. They picked it up, turned it over, and pressed the toe again—this time, the mark stayed. The victory wasn’t grand; it was the persistence of a child who, against the odds, had learned to make something from nothing. The soil remembered.
