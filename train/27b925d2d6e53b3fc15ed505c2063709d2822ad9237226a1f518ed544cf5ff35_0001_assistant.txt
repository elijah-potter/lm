In Chicago, tavern-style pizza originated as a way for the city's workers to have a little bite with their post-shift beer. Let's dig into what makes Chicago's tavern-style pie so delicious: 

- Crust: The crust of a tavern-style pizza is inarguably its defining characteristic. Thin and crispy, it has a slightly thicker and sturdier base than a traditional thin-crust pizza. This comes from the dough being pre-stretched and rolled out before being topped and baked. The end product is often referred to as a "cracker-like" crust due to its crispy texture. It's not uncommon to see a crust that crumbs and flakes like a saltine!

- Shape and Size: Tavern-style pizza is typically baked in a round shape, cut into small square pieces. These are commonly known as "party-cut" or "tavern-cut" slices.

- Cheese: Unlike traditional thin-crust pizza, tavern-style pizza usually features a layer of cheese extending to the edge of the crust. The cheese is typically a combination of low-moisture mozzarella and cheddar, which melts and caramelizes, creating a slightly crispy and flavorful cheese edge. Combine that with the crispiness of the thin crust underneath, and it's easy to understand why some folks sprint to the corner pieces.

- Toppings: Tavern-style pizza often features traditional toppings such as Italian sausage, green peppers, onions, and mushrooms. However, the toppings are usually applied generously and heartily, creating a more substantial pizza compared to some other thin crust styles. It can make those center square pieces feel much heavier as well!

- Baking Method: It's common to see old-school electric or gas ovens. Remember that tavern-style pizza is traditionally made in—you guessed it—taverns! You're not likely to find imported Italian ovens here! 

There's nothing like a hot slice of tavern-style pizza, ideally with an icy beer on the side. We hope this guide helps you know exactly what to look for when you try it and what makes it so special.
