The rain tapped against the window like a hesitant hand, its rhythm too familiar yet strange. I should have left by now, but the coffee stayed warm in my hands, and the half-eaten sandwich on my lap was still there, untouched. Outside, the city pulsed with life—laughter, distant sirens—but inside, the shadows swallowed my voice.

I was supposed to be bold. To step forward, to take the stage, to *be* something more than the quiet girl who sat here, her fingers tracing the rim of her mug. But the words stuck like glue. Every sentence felt like a lie, every thought a compromise. What if I failed? What if I was wrong? The mirror above the sink reflected not my face, but a stranger’s—one who had already crossed the line, one who had chosen, and now I was left with the weight of *not knowing*.

The door creaked open. My name was called. My name, and the fear that followed it, like a shadow that refused to fade. I exhaled, the breath sharp and uncertain. Outside, the city waited. Inside, I was still waiting.
