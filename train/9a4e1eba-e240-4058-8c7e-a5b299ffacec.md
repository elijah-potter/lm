The waiter bowed slightly, fingers nearly brushing the napkin in his lap.
"Can I get you anything else, sir?" he asked, voice too soft, like a question he'd already decided on the answer.

The man shifted in his seat, adjusting his tie. "No, thank you," he said, but his eyes darted to the corner of the table. The waitress, who had been standing beside him, turned away abruptly.

"Just—no dessert?" the waiter prompted, voice trembling slightly.

The man looked down at his plate. "No, thank you." His fork clattered against the plate as he stabbed the last bite of his salad, which was now soggy with forgotten dressing.

The waiter swallowed hard, his knuckles turning white around the pen in his apron. "Of course. I’m sorry if I—"

The man stood abruptly, knocking over his glass of water. It spilled across the table, staining the white tablecloth. "Oh!" he gasped, reaching for a napkin, which was already halfway to the floor.

The waitstaff exchanged glances. One of them picked up the pen, while the other bent to retrieve the napkin. The man wiped his mouth with the other hand, muttering, "I’m so sorry. I’ll—"

The restaurant’s manager, who had been sitting in the corner, stood up. "Sir, I really don’t think this is necessary—"

"Wait," the man said, voice rising. "I’ll pay for this."

The manager’s eyes narrowed. "It’s already been covered."

The man’s face flushed. "No, I—"

Silence. The waiter stared at the mess, then at the man, who was now pacing the floor, hands clenched at his sides.

"Fine," the man said, finally. "But I’ll make it up to you."

The waitstaff exchanged another look. The manager sighed and nodded. The waiter took a deep breath. "What can I do?" he asked, voice breaking.

The man hesitated, then said, "I’ll leave you a tip."
