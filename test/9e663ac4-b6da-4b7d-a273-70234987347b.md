The city of New Haven was quiet on this particular Tuesday evening, the kind of quiet that felt like a heavy blanket draped over everything—except for the faint hum of traffic in the distance and the occasional chirp of a night bird. Inside the dimly lit diner across from the park, Leo sat at a corner booth, a half-eaten slice of pizza and a cup of black coffee in front of him. His phone buzzed under the table, a notification from his algebra teacher: *"Homework due Friday. Late submissions lose 20%."*

He ignored it. Leo had never been great with math, but he was good at other things—like stealing the last slice of pizza from the guy at the next table every Friday, or sneaking into the old abandoned warehouse behind the park on the weekends to tinker with old electronics. Tonight, though, he just wanted to stay put, finish this coffee, and maybe watch an episode of his favorite show.

Then the phone buzzed again. This time, it was a text from his best friend, Mira.

**Mira:** *You up? The warehouse’s been on fire.*

Leo’s fingers froze over the keyboard. Fire in the warehouse? That place was supposed to be empty. He hadn’t been back in months. He didn’t even know who owned it anymore.

**Leo:** *What?*

**Mira:** *Your dad’s there. Said he needs you.*

Leo’s stomach dropped. His dad had been in and out of trouble since Leo was little, but he’d never asked for help like this. Unless something was wrong.

He grabbed his jacket, slung his backpack over his shoulder, and stuffed his keys into his pocket. Outside, the night air was cool, the kind that made your breath visible in the dark. The park was empty, the usual nighttime scuffle of kids playing tag or parents arguing over homework replaced by silence. Leo hurried past the park, weaving through the shadows of the alleyways until he reached the warehouse’s rusted gate.

The fire was already spreading, tongues of orange and yellow licking at the wooden beams. His dad stood near the entrance, arms crossed, his face pale. "Leo," he said, voice tight. "We need to get out now."

Leo didn’t hesitate. He kicked open the door, the hinges groaning, and stepped inside. The warehouse was a mess—wires snaked across the floor, half-burned circuit boards littered the floor, and smoke curled up from the charred remains of whatever had been inside. His dad was already running, pulling him behind a stack of crates. "You were supposed to be safe here," his dad muttered, his breath ragged.

Leo’s heart pounded. *This wasn’t supposed to happen.*

"Where’s the rest of the crew?" Leo asked, his voice steady despite the adrenaline.

His dad didn’t answer. Instead, he grabbed Leo’s wrist, his grip tight. "They’re not coming back. Not like this." He pointed to the flames, now reaching the ceiling. "We have to go."

They ran, Leo’s shoes slapping against the concrete, his dad’s hand guiding him through the smoke. Outside, the night was still, the firelight casting long shadows. His dad didn’t say another word until they were both breathing hard in the dim light of the alley.

"You’re sure this is your dad?" Leo asked, voice shaky.

His dad looked at him, then at the ground. "Yeah. I just… I didn’t think this would happen."

Leo swallowed hard. "What do we do now?"

His dad hesitated, then pulled out a crumpled piece of paper from his pocket. "There’s a meeting at the old diner. They want to know what happened. You go with me."

Leo’s stomach twisted. He didn’t want to go. But he didn’t have a choice. He took the paper, his dad’s hand still warm against his own.

As he turned to leave, he heard his dad whisper, "Tell them the truth. About everything."

Leo nodded, but his mind was racing. *Truth? What was he supposed to tell them?*

The diner was packed, the usual hum of conversation replaced by a tense silence. The people around the table—some he recognized from the warehouse, others not—glanced at him nervously. His dad sat beside him, his face set in a grim line.

Leo cleared his throat. "Okay. So, last night…" He paused, his fingers tracing the paper in his hand. "The warehouse was on fire. I ran out with my dad because he said we had to get out. We didn’t know anyone else was inside."

A murmur rippled through the room. His dad’s shoulders tensed.

Leo continued, his voice steady despite the weight of it all. "The fire spread fast. I don’t know what happened to the others. My dad says they’re not coming back."

A few people nodded, some looking relieved, others still wary. His dad exhaled sharply, his grip on Leo’s hand tightening.

"Anyone else want to say something?" a voice cut through the tension. It was Mira, her usual confidence back, though her eyes were wide with something Leo couldn’t quite read.

Leo glanced at her, then back at the table. He didn’t know what to say. But then, Mira spoke up again. "We need to figure out what happened. Who started the fire? Who was inside?"

Leo’s mind raced. *Fire. Someone knew. Someone was trying to hide something.*

His dad leaned forward. "It wasn’t an accident. Someone set it. And they were planning something bigger."

A beat of silence. Then, one of the guys at the table—a guy Leo had never really paid attention to—leaned in. "Who?"

Leo’s blood ran cold. He looked at his dad, then at Mira. *Who?*

His dad’s jaw tightened. "We don’t know yet. But we’ll find out."

Leo’s stomach dropped. *They were going to find out. And they were going to ask him.*

He took a deep breath. "I don’t know. But I’ll help."

The room was quiet. Then, Mira stood, her voice firm. "Then let’s get to work."

Outside, the night was still. But somewhere in the distance, Leo could hear the faint sound of a siren—something that made his skin prickle.

Leo didn’t sleep that night. He sat on the park bench, staring at the empty space where the warehouse used to be, his mind a whirl of questions. *Who was inside? Who started the fire? And what were they really trying to hide?*

His phone buzzed again. A text from Mira.

**Mira:** *We need to meet tomorrow. Early.*

Leo’s heart raced. *Tomorrow. They were already planning something.*

He pocketed his phone, his mind racing. Whatever was coming, he was ready. He just wasn’t sure what it was yet.
