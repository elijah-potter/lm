The old clock tower creaked past noon,
a pendulum’s slow, steady groan.
Outside, the garden swayed in golden light,
where petals kissed the breeze like whispered delight.

A child’s laughter spilled from a window high,
echoing soft against the summer sky.
The wind hummed through the trees, a lullaby,
carrying joy where once there was only sky.

In the hollow of a sunlit field,
something ancient stilled the restless mind.
The world had paused—just for this moment—
a breath held tight, a pause in storm.

Now the sky is clear, the air is sweet,
no thunder left, no storm to meet.
The calm had come like a quiet tide,
and time, once wild, now softly glides.
