A dimly lit alleyway at dusk, the cobblestones damp with recent rain. A lone figure—shoulders hunched, coat collar turned up against the chill—stands motionless near a flickering neon sign advertising a closed store. Their fingers trace the edge of a half-empty bottle of whiskey, the glass too heavy to hold steady. The air hums with the distant wail of sirens, a sound that feels both familiar and meaningless. Their reflection in a rusted metal barrel is a ghostly blur, their face a mask of unresolved anticipation. A single cigarette burns in an ashtray, its smoke curling into the night. Time stretches like a taut string, each second a weight they dare not lift. The door of a nearby abandoned building creaks open, but no one steps out. Just the sound of their own breath, slow and shallow.
