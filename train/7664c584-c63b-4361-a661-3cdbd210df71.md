The clock tower’s chime struck midnight, and the old street lamps flickered to life, casting long shadows across the cobblestones. I sat at the café’s worn table, my fingers tracing the rim of a half-empty espresso cup. The waitress placed a fresh croissant in front of me, its golden crust glistening. I exhaled slowly, watching steam rise from the steam.

Outside, the city slept, but I couldn’t stop thinking about what I’d almost forgotten—until now.

She was the only one who saw it. The way her breath hitched when I laughed too loudly, the way her fingers trembled when she held my hand. But I’d buried it under the weight of my own lies, the ones I told to keep her safe, to keep her from seeing the cracks in me. The truth was simpler than I’d made it sound: I loved her. And I’d been too afraid to admit it.

The waitress cleared her throat. "Your order’s ready."

I took a bite of the croissant, savoring the buttery crunch. The café’s hum filled the silence, but the weight of my confession pressed harder than ever.

Outside, the first light of dawn crept over the horizon, painting the street in soft gold. I closed my eyes, listening to the rhythm of the city waking up—ignoring the guilt that still lingered like a ghost in the corners of my mind.
