Philosophical inquiry into the nature of knowledge, perception, and existence. Examples include:
- **Free Will vs. Determinism**: Does human decision-making arise from choice or predetermined causes?
- **The Nature of Consciousness**: How does subjective experience emerge from physical processes?
- **The Boundary of Science**: What cannot be explained by empirical observation or theoretical models?
- **Temporal Paradoxes**: Is time linear, or does it introduce unsolvable contradictions (e.g., grandfather paradox)?
- **Meta-Ethics**: Are moral judgments objective or subjective, and what defines right/wrong?
- **Existence of God**: Is divine existence provable, or is it inherently unanswerable via natural reasoning?
- **Identity Across Time**: Can a person remain the same through aging, or is identity a fluid concept?
- **The Observer Effect**: Does measurement alter the system being studied (quantum mechanics, physics)?
- **Unsolvable Equations**: Existential equations (e.g., in math/physics) that may have no finite solution.
- **The Limits of Language**: Can words capture reality, or do they introduce distortions (e.g., "The sound of one hand clapping")?

These questions transcend empirical methods, requiring synthesis of philosophy, science, and introspection.
