A decision postponed weighs like a stone in the stomach—each passing moment a subtle erosion of resolve. The clock ticks, but the mind lingers on the edges of doubt, where questions like *what if?* or *why now?* spiral into an unspoken calculus of fear. The weight of indecision isn’t just the unknown of the choice itself, but the certainty of the *not choosing* at all.

Outside, the world moves—some decisions made, others resolved with quiet finality. The air carries the scent of resolve, while yours remains tangled in the static of hesitation. You could say you’re waiting for clarity, but clarity, in this case, is less about answers and more about the absence of them. The future, like a half-opened door, seems both distant and impossibly close, each moment a pause in a story that isn’t over yet.

In the silence between yes and no, something shifts: the weight of the pause itself becomes the decision. The question isn’t whether to act, but whether to act now—or whether the act of waiting is the act itself. The future, like a half-remembered dream, is neither lost nor gained, but suspended in the space where decisions are meant to be made.
