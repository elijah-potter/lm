"Would you like any tea?"

I shook my head, unable to quite meet her gaze. The fire crackled merrily away, popping and hissing. The cottage was small but homely - feline-related knick-knacks and trinkets lined every shelf, and the floral wallpapered walls were crowded with paintings and art depicting cute cats. The overstuffed couch I sat on had an outdated pattern, but it matched the aesthetic of the rest of the room. 

"Would you like some milk instead? Maybe coffee?" 

She smiled sweetly down at me, her eyes crinkling the same way Michael's do. Her eyes are the same color too, a sea-foamy gray-green. I just got her dark hair. 

"Well, just say it. What did you want to talk about, dear? What is it"

The words were stuck somewhere in my throat. I had no idea what to say. I mean, how do you say this to a complete stranger? It wasn’t a good idea. Dad told me not to come, but I was adamant, and now I was sitting all alone in some adorable little cottage in the woods with a lady whose taste in interior decorating was firmly lodged in "crazy-cat lady" territory. I didn't even like cats. Micheal didn't even like cats. It must have skipped a generation.

"Umm, I don't know how to…" I stopped to breathe. "I'm just going to say this. My name is Naomi, and you're my mother..."
