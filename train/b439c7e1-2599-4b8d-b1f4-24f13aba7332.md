A dimly lit apartment in a rain-slicked city. The flickering neon glow of a broken streetlight casts long shadows across the cluttered floor. **Old photographs**—yellowed, half-buried in a cardboard box—are the only evidence of a life that no longer belongs here. The scent of dust and damp wool lingers, a ghost of a childhood shared between two worlds.

On the kitchen counter, a half-empty glass of whiskey sits beside a **worn leather wallet** with a faded photo of a woman’s face inside. Her eyes follow you as you pass, though you know she’s not here. The door creaks open, and a man in a **dust-coated trench coat** steps inside, his breath fogging the glass.

His hands shake—not from age, but from something older. He reaches for a **rusted pocket watch**, its chain frayed, and turns it over. The face is blank, but the second hand moves in slow, deliberate circles. **"I was supposed to meet you at midnight,"** he says, voice rough with the weight of a promise broken decades ago. **"But the past has a way of finding us."** Outside, a siren wails—too close, too familiar. The city is alive with echoes of a time that never was.
