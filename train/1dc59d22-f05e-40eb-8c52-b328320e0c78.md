I was standing at the corner, waiting for the bus, when I heard the scream. It wasn’t a sound I’d ever forget. It came from the alley behind the old hardware store, the one with the peeling paint and the flickering neon sign. My first thought was that it was probably just some drunk throwing a fight or a stray dog getting into trouble, but then I heard the *thud*—something heavy hitting the ground. The scream cut off abruptly.

I turned and saw the alley. A man in a torn leather jacket was lying on his back, his hands splayed wide. He wasn’t moving. His eyes were closed, but his mouth was open in a silent, terrified gasp. Then, from the shadows, a figure emerged—a man in a black trench coat, his face half-hidden by a scarf. He crouched over the victim, his hands moving swiftly, like he was already reaching for something. I don’t know what I was expecting—maybe a knife, maybe a gun—but the way he moved was too precise, too controlled. He didn’t seem to be fighting.

The second scream was louder, sharper. It came from the other end of the alley, where a woman in a frayed dress was standing, her face pale and trembling. She was holding a child by the arm, who was screaming and clawing at her. The woman’s voice was raw with fear as she shouted, *"No! Please don’t—"*

The man in the trench coat stood abruptly, his expression unreadable. He turned toward them, his eyes locking onto the woman. Then, in one fluid motion, he grabbed the child and threw them both into the air. They hit the ground hard, the child’s small body crumpling. The woman screamed again, her hands flying to her mouth. The man in the trench coat didn’t look at her. He just stood there, like he was waiting for something.

Then, the woman’s screams stopped. The alley was silent except for the child’s wet, whimpering breaths. The man in the trench coat turned and walked away, his coat swallowing him whole. The woman and child stayed where they were, trembling, until the alley was empty again.

I should have left. I *should* have left. But my feet didn’t move. I stayed there, watching, my stomach twisting. The woman’s hands shook as she tried to get up, her voice breaking when she said, *"Where’s my baby?"*

I didn’t know what to say. I didn’t know what to do. So I just stood there, waiting for the next scream.

Then, the child started to cry. Not the quiet, broken sound anymore. This was a sound that echoed down the alley, raw and desperate. The woman’s face crumpled. She dropped to her knees, her hands flying to her child’s back, her voice raw with grief. *"Oh God, oh God—"*

I turned away. The bus was coming. I boarded it, my mind racing. What had just happened? Who was that man? What had he done? I didn’t know, but I knew I’d never forget it.

The bus pulled away, and the alley faded into the night. But the memory stayed. And the question: *Who was next?*
