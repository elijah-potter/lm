**The Last Transmission**

We didn’t mean for this to happen.

The static hissed between the wires, a ghost of the signal we’d tried to bury. The console flickered—blue and cold—while the captain’s voice, once sharp with command, now cracked like old parchment. *"Protocol breach detected. Terminal failure imminent."*

The door hissed open. Not quietly. Not smoothly. It *unraveled*, like a thread cut in the dark. Inside, the crew stood frozen, their faces pressed against the glass, their breath a slow, uneven rhythm. The air smelled of ozone and something older—something that hadn’t been here before. The lights dimmed. Then, one by one, the screens went black.

*"This isn’t part of the plan,"* whispered Engineer Voss. His hands trembled. *"Not in any way we’ve ever imagined."*

The ship groaned. A sound like nails on metal. The engines, usually a steady hum, now whined like a dying animal. The alarms blared—somewhere, far away. The captain’s chair tilted backward. His fingers, rigid with fear, gripped the armrests.

*"We need to—"* His voice cut off. The last words were drowned out by the scream of the thrusters, a deafening, metallic wail that sent the remaining crew sprawling.

Outside, the void stretched endlessly. Not the cold, endless dark of space—this was different. This was *breathing*, pulsing, like a wound that had been left open too long. And then, from the darkness, something *moved*.

Not with purpose. Not with intent.

Just… *there*.
