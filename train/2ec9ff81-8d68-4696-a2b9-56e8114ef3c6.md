## Project Chimera: Rulebook

**A Cooperative Game for 2-4 Players**

**Introduction**

Project Chimera casts you as a team of specialized researchers at the Global Crisis Response Initiative (GCRI). A rapidly evolving, synthetic pathogen, designated "Chimera," is spreading across the globe, exhibiting unpredictable mutations and defying conventional treatment. Your mission is to understand Chimera's nature, develop countermeasures, and ultimately contain the crisis before it overwhelms civilization. Success requires careful planning, resource management, and a willingness to make difficult choices under immense pressure.

**1. Components**

*   **Game Board:** A world map depicting key regions, each with a Stability rating.
*   **Researcher Cards (8):** Each card represents a researcher with unique abilities and starting resources.
*   **Chimera Mutation Cards (40):** Detail Chimera’s evolving properties and the challenges it presents.
*   **Crisis Cards (30):** Introduce unexpected events and complications.
*   **Research Tokens (60):** Represent progress in understanding Chimera.
*   **Resource Tokens (120):** Represent funding, equipment, and personnel. Divided into three types: Scientific (blue), Logistical (yellow), and Security (red).
*   **Stability Markers (20):** Used to track Stability levels in each region.
*   **Containment Markers (10):** Placed to temporarily halt Chimera’s spread.
*   **Dice (3):** Six-sided dice used for various checks.
*   **First Player Marker:** Indicates the active player.
*   **Rulebook (this document).**

**2. Setup**

1.  Place the Game Board in the center of the playing area.
2.  Shuffle the Chimera Mutation Cards and place them face down in a designated draw pile. Reveal the top three cards and place them face up beside the draw pile. These represent Chimera’s current mutations.
3.  Shuffle the Crisis Cards and place them face down in a designated draw pile.
4.  Place Stability Markers on each region of the Game Board according to the starting Stability ratings printed on the board.
5.  Each player chooses a Researcher Card and takes the corresponding starting resources indicated on the card.
6.  Place the First Player Marker with a randomly chosen player.

**3. Turn Structure**

Each player’s turn consists of the following phases, performed in order:

1.  **Draw Crisis Card:** Draw the top Crisis Card and resolve its effects immediately. These effects often introduce new challenges or complications.
2.  **Researcher Action Phase:** You may perform two actions. The same action may be performed twice. Available actions include:
    *   **Research:** Spend Scientific resources to gain Research Tokens. The number of tokens gained depends on the region’s Stability rating and the researcher’s abilities.
    *   **Deploy Resources:** Move Resource Tokens (Scientific, Logistical, or Security) to a region. This can be used to improve Stability, prepare for containment, or support research.
    *   **Containment:** Spend Logistical and Security resources to place a Containment Marker in a region. This temporarily halts Chimera’s spread.
    *   **Investigate Mutation:** Spend Scientific resources to attempt to understand a face-up Chimera Mutation Card. A successful die roll (modified by researcher abilities) removes the card from play, providing a long-term benefit.
    *   **Trade Resources:** Exchange Resource Tokens with another player. The exchange rate is determined by mutual agreement.
    *   **Move:** Move your researcher to an adjacent region.
3.  **Chimera Spread Phase:** Draw the top Chimera Mutation Card and resolve its effects. This usually involves reducing Stability in one or more regions. If a region’s Stability reaches zero, it is considered "Compromised" and incurs further penalties.
4.  **Pass the First Player Marker:** Pass the First Player Marker to the player on your left.

**4. Trade-off Mechanics**

Project Chimera incorporates several mechanics designed to force difficult trade-offs under uncertainty:

*   **Mutation Prioritization:** With only three face-up Mutation Cards, you must collectively decide which poses the greatest threat. Focusing on one mutation might ignore another, potentially leading to unforeseen consequences.
*   **Resource Allocation Dilemma:** Limited resources force you to prioritize between Research, Containment, and Stability improvements. Investing heavily in one area often comes at the expense of another. For example, deploying Security resources for Containment might mean fewer Scientific resources are available for Research.
*   **Stability vs. Speed:** Certain actions require sacrificing immediate Stability improvements to gain a crucial advantage, such as quickly deploying resources to a critical region before it is compromised. This creates a tension between short-term stability and long-term crisis management.

**5. Win and Loss Conditions**

*   **Winning:** You win the game if you successfully remove all face-up Chimera Mutation Cards from play *and* all regions on the Game Board have a Stability rating of at least 2.
*   **Losing:** You lose the game if any of the following conditions are met:
    *   A predetermined number of regions (based on player count – see Difficulty Scaling) are compromised.
    *   The Chimera Mutation Card draw pile is exhausted.
    *   You are unable to resolve a Crisis Card due to lack of resources or available actions.

**6. Difficulty Scaling**

The game’s difficulty scales with the number of players:

*   **2 Players:** Reduce the number of regions required to win to 6. Increase the number of regions required to lose to 5.
*   **3 Players:** Standard setup as described in the rules.
*   **4 Players:** Increase the number of regions required to lose to 6.

**7. Advanced Variants**

*   **Rapid Mutation:** After each Chimera Spread Phase, discard one of the face-up Mutation Cards and draw a new one. This accelerates the game and increases the challenge.
*   **Limited Communication:** Restrict communication between players during the Researcher Action Phase. This simulates the pressures of a crisis situation and forces players to rely on their individual skills and judgment.
*   **Specialized Research:** Introduce optional "Specialization" cards that grant additional abilities but also impose limitations on a researcher’s actions.



**Designer’s Note**

Project Chimera was designed to be more than just a game; it’s a simulated exercise in collaborative problem-solving under pressure. The mechanics are intended to highlight the complexities of responding to global crises, emphasizing the importance of communication, resource management, and the difficult choices that must be made when facing uncertain threats. The game aims to foster critical thinking, teamwork, and an appreciation for the challenges faced by those working to safeguard global health and security. We hope you find the experience both engaging and thought-provoking.
