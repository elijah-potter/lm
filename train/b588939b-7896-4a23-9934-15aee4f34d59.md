The crowd hummed beneath the flickering neon, their faces half-lit by the glow of distant billboards. I watched as they stepped over cracked pavement, oblivious to the faint scent of damp earth beneath their heels, the way the wind carried the cold from the river’s edge. They moved in sync—rushing, pausing, never pausing long enough to notice the weight of the city’s silence pressing against them.

I lingered near the edge of the sidewalk, fingers tracing the grain of an old bench where a stranger had once sat too long. The others passed by, their breath visible in the chill, their footsteps echoing like a chorus of unheard voices. They missed the way the streetlamps cast long, skeletal shadows that stretched into the alleyways, where no one dared to linger.

I counted the cracks in the sidewalk where the pavement had split to let the roots of something ancient rise. They missed the way the pavement itself had learned to whisper—each fissure a secret kept for centuries. The city was a museum of forgotten things, and they walked through it like ghosts, blind to the ghosts within the walls.

I watched them all, and for the first time, I realized how much there was to see—and how little anyone else saw.
