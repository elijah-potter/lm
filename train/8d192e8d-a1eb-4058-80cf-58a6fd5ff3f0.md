The valley now stretches with cracked earth, fissures of exposed bedrock winding through the once verdant fields. The once-gentle slopes of the hills are now scarred by a network of erosion channels, some dry, others still holding shallow, stagnant pools reflecting the sky in muted tones. The air hums with a persistent, low drone—no birds, only the occasional distant wind sighing through the skeletal trees, their branches gnarled and twisted, some leaning precariously toward the earth. Dust swirls in lazy spirals where the wind finds weak resistance. The old orchard stands abandoned, its fruit trees stripped of leaves, their trunks blackened at the base. The river, a ribbon of light against the parched earth, has carved new paths through the land, its banks lined with mud and driftwood. The horizon is a blurred line where the sky meets the land, a vast, unbroken expanse of ashen blue and burnt gold, broken only by the faintest glimmer of distant ridges.
