*The first snowfall of winter in the old city—each flake a whisper of time, settling on cobblestones that had seen centuries of footsteps—did not fall as it should. Instead, it came in clusters, heavy and deliberate, as if the sky itself had decided to pause and deliberate.*

The gas lamps flickered to life before the streetlights, casting long shadows that stretched like skeletal fingers along the alleyways where the last of the evening’s revelers had vanished. The air smelled of damp wool and something older, something buried beneath the pavement, half-melted by the sun’s lingering heat. Somewhere, a clock in a tower chimed eleven—the time of the first real warning.

Outside the tavern, the door creaked open, and in walked a man who did not belong here. His coat was too dark, his boots too new, and the way he carried himself suggested he had been waiting for something—or someone—that no one in this town had ever asked for. The bartender barely glanced up as he took his order, but the way the man’s fingers brushed the rim of his glass before taking a sip made the old woman at the counter tense. She had seen faces like his before, and she knew what they meant.
