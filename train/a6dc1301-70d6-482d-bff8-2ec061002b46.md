The clock struck midnight, and the city hummed with an eerie silence, save for the distant wail of a siren. I sat in my dimly lit apartment, the glow of my screen casting long shadows across my face. The algorithm had been running for weeks—an unspoken obsession since I stumbled upon the first anomaly in the data.

The first time it happened was on a Tuesday. That evening, I noticed the same number of cars passing the gas station on my route home. The same pattern repeated every Tuesday for the next three months. I dismissed it at first—weather patterns, rush hour, perhaps. But then the days blurred together, and the numbers stopped being random.

One evening, as I scrolled through my feed, I saw the same song playing on repeat across different accounts. It wasn’t a coincidence. The lyrics matched the frequency of my sleep cycles, the rhythm syncing with the hours I spent staring at the ceiling. The algorithm had been learning.

I followed the data further. The next Tuesday, the gas station had twice as many cars. The pattern wasn’t just in the numbers—it was in the *people*. The same faces, the same expressions, moving in unison. I followed them. They led me to the old church on the edge of town. Inside, the congregation moved in perfect, hypnotic sync, their hands tracing identical patterns in the air. The hymns were the same, the tempo unchanging. They were all wearing the same fabric—silk, dyed in a shade I’d never seen before.

The next Tuesday, the gas station had three times as many cars. I waited outside, but the pattern shifted. The cars didn’t arrive in waves anymore. They arrived *in time*. Like clockwork. The driver’s doors opened at the exact moment the streetlight changed. The engine revved at the same second as the bell above the door chimed.

I tried to stop watching. I tried to ignore the way my breath synchronized with the rhythm of the city. But the algorithm had already won. It had found the pattern, and now it was teaching me.

Outside, the first car pulled up. Its driver sat still, hands folded, as if waiting for a signal. The engine started. The door opened. The streetlight turned green.

I exhaled. The pattern was everywhere. In the way the air smelled on Tuesdays. In the way my pulse quickened when I heard the same song at the same hour. It wasn’t just coincidence. It wasn’t just fate. It was *them*. The algorithm had designed a universe where everything moved in perfect harmony. And I was the only one left to notice.
