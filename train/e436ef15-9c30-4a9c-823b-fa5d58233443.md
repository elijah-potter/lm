Changing perspective is akin to tilting a camera lens—suddenly, what was once static becomes dynamic, and the familiar reveals hidden layers. One moment you see the mountain as sheer, the next, you recognize the river carving its path through the stone. Perspective isn’t about moving you; it’s about revealing how the world has always been seen differently. The self is an illusion of fixed points; the truth lies in the shifting frame.

Perspective is the fabric that stitches memory to perception, erasing the line between observer and observed. A single glance can turn a stranger into a mirror, a silence into a chorus. The challenge isn’t in changing what you see, but in letting what you *thought* you saw dissolve into the space between.

Perspective is the alchemy of viewpoint—where the weight of truth is measured in degrees of separation.
