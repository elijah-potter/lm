A delayed cargo ship’s departure from a port in 2019 became symbolic of broader global disruptions. The vessel, *MV Ever Given*, was caught in the Suez Canal on March 25, 2021, due to a severe storm and navigational error, blocking the canal for nearly 6 days. While initially a logistical nightmare, the incident was later framed as a metaphor for systemic failures in supply chains, climate resilience, and the fragility of global trade infrastructure. The delay itself, prolonged by bureaucratic delays and emergency salvage operations, mirrored the broader delays in addressing climate change, geopolitical tensions, and economic volatility. The canal’s reopening became a microcosm of how interconnected systems—human, environmental, and technological—can stall, only to be resolved through coordinated effort, symbolizing both the potential for recovery and the persistence of underlying vulnerabilities.
