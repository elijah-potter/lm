A dimly lit cottage nestled between overgrown gardens, its wooden shutters slightly ajar, whispered about the evening breeze. Inside, the fireplace had burned low, casting long shadows over the worn leather chairs. The old radio hummed a forgotten lullaby, its static the only sound besides the soft crunch of leaves underfoot outside.

On the kitchen table, a half-empty coffee cup sat beside a single cigarette butt. The ashtray, empty but for a single, charred match stub. A newspaper, folded in half, lay open to an article about a quiet town that had lost its last resident two years past.

Outside, a gentle rain began to fall, tapping against the windowpane like distant footsteps. Inside, the curtains swayed slightly, and a single candle flickered to life on the mantle, its flame trembling in the draft. The air smelled of damp earth and something faintly sweet—old wood and old memories. No one moved. No one spoke. Only the steady, unhurried rhythm of the rain outside.
