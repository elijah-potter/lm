The **Tidal Basin** in coastal cities, particularly during high tide, exhibits dynamic human movement patterns. During low tide, pathways are exposed, reducing congestion, while high tide floods access points, forcing crowds to disperse or queue along elevated walkways and bridges. Seasonal events (e.g., festivals) amplify density, creating surges in foot traffic. Urban planners model this as a **wavefront analysis**, where pedestrian flow oscillates between concentrated clusters and dispersed dispersion zones. Spatial data (e.g., GPS tracks, sensor grids) captures real-time density metrics: baseline activity (30–50% occupancy), peak surges (150–200% during events), and post-event resettlement. The system is studied via **agent-based simulations** to optimize infrastructure (e.g., ramps, barriers) and reduce bottlenecks, emphasizing adaptive crowd control algorithms.
