The annual **Harvest Festival** was no longer a feast but a solemn ritual: the same old field, the same worn-out sickle, the same crimson grain stored in the same wooden bins. The first step was the **burning of the old grain**—a slow, deliberate ritual where the remnants of last year’s harvest were consumed in the fire pit, their smoke curling into the autumn sky. The second was the **blessing of the new seeds**, whispered over by the elders, their fingers tracing the earth with a prayer. The third was the **feast of thanks**, where only the finest of the new crop was shared, and the rest was stored with the same care as the last harvest’s leftovers.

Now, the cycle repeated, but the hands that tended the land moved with a heavier weight, knowing the ritual was no longer just tradition—it was a prayer.
