The silence hums like a tuning fork half-shattered—each note lingers too long, a ghost of what was never said. The air smells faintly of damp wool and old books, as if the house itself has been holding its breath for years. Mirrors reflect not faces but hollow places where someone once stood, now just an empty chair at the dinner table.

She’s not there in the way you imagine her—she’s *everywhere*, pressing against the edges of your thoughts like a memory you can’t quite name. The clock ticks, but time stretches thin, warped into something that isn’t linear. You reach for her hand, and it’s cold, a ghostly touch that dissolves into static.

Some days, you forget she’s gone. Then the weight returns—first as a whisper, then a full-body ache. The absence is a physical presence, a draft that never goes away, even when you’re alone. You learn to live with the gaps in the story, the silences that echo like a question no one wants to answer.
