## A Counseling Session

**Setting:** A comfortable, softly lit therapy room.

**Characters:**

*   **Dr. Eleanor Vance:** A seasoned therapist, known for her calm demeanor and insightful approach.
*   **Liam Davies:** A young professional, experiencing significant stress related to his career.

---

**Liam:** (Sighs, looking down at his hands) It just feels… overwhelming, you know? Like I’m constantly chasing something I can’t quite reach.

**Dr. Vance:** (Nods gently, maintaining eye contact) I understand. That sense of chasing, of striving without a clear endpoint, can be profoundly draining. Can you tell me a little more about what feels overwhelming specifically?

**Liam:** It’s my job. I’m a project manager, and lately, the projects have been… demanding. The deadlines are tighter, the expectations higher, and I feel this constant pressure to perform. I'm worried about letting people down.

**Dr. Vance:** It sounds as though you're carrying a significant weight of responsibility. Letting people down is a common concern, and it’s natural to want to meet expectations. But tell me, what happens when you *do* feel you’re falling short? What thoughts or feelings arise?

**Liam:** (Hesitates) I start to doubt myself, I guess. I replay situations in my head, wondering what I could have done differently. I get irritable, and I find it hard to relax, even when I’m not working. My sleep has been terrible.

**Dr. Vance:** That self-criticism can be very harsh. It’s understandable that you’re experiencing physical symptoms like sleep disturbance when you’re constantly replaying events and questioning your abilities. It sounds exhausting. You mentioned irritability; do you find that impacting your relationships?

**Liam:** (Looks up briefly, then back down) Yeah, it has. My partner has noticed. I'm short with her sometimes, and I feel awful about it afterward. I just… I don’t want to disappoint her either.

**Dr. Vance:** It’s admirable that you’re aware of how your stress is affecting your relationship. That self-awareness is a valuable starting point. It takes courage to acknowledge that. What do you think might be contributing to this heightened pressure, beyond the demands of your work?

**Liam:** I don’t know. I’ve always been a “people-pleaser,” I suppose. I want everyone to be happy with me. I'm afraid of conflict, so I often take on more than I can handle to avoid it.

**Dr. Vance:** That desire to ensure everyone’s happiness is a beautiful quality, Liam, but it sounds like it's also creating a significant burden for you. It’s important to recognize that you’re not responsible for the happiness of others. That’s a very difficult lesson to learn, but a crucial one.  We can explore some strategies for setting boundaries and prioritizing your own well-being. Would you be open to discussing that?

**Liam:** (Nods slowly) Yes, please. I… I really need to find a way to manage this. I don’t want to lose myself in it.

**Dr. Vance:** (Smiles warmly) That’s a very insightful observation, Liam. It's a sign of strength that you’re recognizing that. We're here to work together to help you reclaim that sense of self. Let's start by examining some of those boundaries you mentioned. Can you think of a recent situation where you felt you overextended yourself?
