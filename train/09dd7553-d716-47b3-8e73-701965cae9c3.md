## TIL The “Smell of Rain” Has a Name: Petrichor

Today I learned that the distinctive, earthy scent that arises after rainfall, particularly on dry ground, has a specific name: petrichor. It’s derived from the Greek words *petra* (stone) and *ichor* (the fluid that flows in the veins of the gods in Greek mythology).

For centuries, people have appreciated this aroma, but its scientific explanation is relatively recent. In 1964, Australian scientists Isabel Joy Bear and R.G. Thomas investigated the phenomenon and published their findings. They discovered that petrichor is primarily caused by two factors. Firstly, oils secreted by plants accumulate on surfaces during dry periods. These oils, particularly from eucalyptus and conifers, are released into the air when raindrops impact the ground. Secondly, the scent is amplified by geosmin, an organic compound produced by soil-dwelling bacteria (specifically *Streptomyces*). Rain releases geosmin from the soil, creating a uniquely earthy fragrance.

The phenomenon is especially noticeable after a prolonged dry spell, making the scent of rain a welcome and recognizable signal of change.
