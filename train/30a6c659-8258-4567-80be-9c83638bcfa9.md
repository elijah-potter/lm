The library’s high ceiling hummed with the weight of forgotten books, their spines cracked like old bones. On the central desk, a lone figure—cloaked in a moth-eaten coat—paused mid-stroke, pen hovering over a blank page. The air smelled of aged parchment and damp wool. Outside, the city’s heartbeat—a distant chug of traffic and the occasional cry of a pigeon—faded into static.

Then, it happened.

The room fell still. Not the absence of sound, but the absence of *meaning*. The air thickened, as if held by an invisible force. The figure’s fingers twitched, but the pen stayed suspended. The only sound was the slow, rhythmic rise and fall of the person’s breath—a single, unspoken breath, like a sigh from a world that had stopped turning.

A door creaked open. No one stepped inside. No voice called out. The silence wasn’t broken—it *persisted*, unbroken by the weight of human noise. The clock on the wall, its hands frozen mid-minute, cast long shadows on the floor.

From the shadows behind the desk, a whisper slithered forward—*not words, but the absence of sound itself*, vibrating against the ears like a ghost’s breath. The figure exhaled. The pen dropped to the page.
