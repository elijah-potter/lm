THAT reptile is hot stuff! The tegu can heat its body to as much as 10 °C above its surroundings, making it the first lizard known to do this. But it flicks on its internal heating only for the reproductive season.
The Argentine black and white tegu (Salvator merianae) lives across much of South America and grows to between 60 and 90 centimetres long. It was thought to be like most other cold-blooded reptiles and stay the same temperature as its surroundings. Indeed, a team led by Glenn Tattersall of Brock University in Canada and Cleo Leite at the Federal University of São Carlos in Brazil has found that, for much of the year, tegus cool down when the sun sets, reaching a low similar to that of their burrows in the early morning.
But during the reproductive season, the lizard’s breathing and heart rate rise and its temperature reaches as much as 10 °C above that of its burrow (Science Advances, doi.org/bb3j).
No one knows how the tegu generates its heat. It may secrete a hormone that causes tissue to go into overdrive, producing heat in the process.
The discovery may provide a clue to how warm-bloodedness evolved, says Tattersall, because tegu might represent a transition state from cold- to warm-bloodedness.
