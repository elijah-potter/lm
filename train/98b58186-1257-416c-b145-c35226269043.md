An expansive void humming with silent echoes, corridors stretching endlessly like veins of cosmic dust, their surfaces etched with forgotten constellations. Shadows move unnaturally—twisting, dissolving—while distant machinery hums a monotone, a heartbeat too slow for any life to sustain. The air carries the faint, metallic tang of something long since dead, yet no bodies remain to confirm its source. At your periphery, the horizon blurs into a shifting, liquid mass of static, a storm of forgotten signals. The walls pulse faintly, as if breathing, while the floor beneath you is a maze of uneven planks or something far stranger: a lattice of light, too fine to see, too vast to comprehend. Gravity feels off—sometimes too light, sometimes like it’s pulling you toward an unseen center. The walls whisper when no one is listening.
