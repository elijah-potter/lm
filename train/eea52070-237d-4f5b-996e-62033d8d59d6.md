A fleeting encounter at the café where the barista’s voice carried the same warmth as last week’s—same coffee order, same slow swirl of sugar in the morning latte. The city hummed outside, but inside, the rhythm of the machine, the clink of cups, the hum of the espresso grinder felt like a shared secret. The moment of connection wasn’t mine; it was a quiet theft of time, borrowed from another’s routine, another’s habit of pausing just long enough to pour.

The phone buzzed. A text from home: *"Where are you?"* The screen glowed like a reminder that the borrowed moment was temporary, that the world kept turning, and the café would empty, the barista would move on, and I’d have to find another way to feel like I belonged there.
