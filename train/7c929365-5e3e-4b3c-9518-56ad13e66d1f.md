**The Last Light**

The clock struck twelve, and the city swallowed the neon like a beast starving for its next meal. The streets, slick with rain, reflected the flickering glow of abandoned billboards—ghosts of advertisements that had once promised dreams but delivered only dust. I stood on the corner, the cold seeping through my boots, my breath misting in the damp air.

A woman passed, her coat too tight for the season, her eyes sharp with the weight of something unsaid. She didn’t look at me. She never did.

Then, the lights dimmed.

Not all at once—like a slow unraveling—but in stages, each one a whisper of surrender. The streetlights blinked out first, their bulbs glowing one last time before surrendering to darkness. Then came the neon signs, their colors bleeding into the night like ink in water. The last to fade were the car headlights, their beams stretching into the distance like dying embers.

And then—silence.

I crouched, my fingers brushing the pavement, feeling the weight of forgotten things. The air smelled of ozone and old metal. My phone, still warm from the pocket, buzzed—a text from an old friend: *"You coming?"*

I didn’t reply.

Instead, I walked toward the river.

The water was black, mirroring the sky’s endless void. Beneath the surface, something moved—something that wasn’t quite fish, not quite shadow. It pulsed, like a heartbeat, and for a second, I thought I saw a face in the depths.

Then the water turned silver.

A reflection of a city that had never existed.

A city that had always been here.
