The rain had been falling steadily for three days, turning the cobblestone streets of Old Wharf into a slick, reflective mirror. Elias stood at the window of his cramped shop, staring at the same old view—docks, warehouses, and the distant hum of the city’s heartbeat. The wind carried the scent of salt and something sharper, like old paper and forgotten promises.

"Elias?" The voice was familiar, but the words made his blood run cold. "You’re supposed to be in the warehouse."

He didn’t turn. "I’m here. I’m always here."

A rustle from the floorboards. His sister, Maren, stood there in her worn apron, her dark eyes darting between him and the door. "You’re not supposed to be *here*." Her voice cracked. "You’re supposed to be with the crew. You promised."

Elias exhaled, slow and deliberate. "I promised. And I’m keeping it."

The shop door creaked open, and his father stumbled in, his face streaked with soot and exhaustion. "Elias," he rasped, "the ship’s gone. The captain’s lost his mind. We don’t know where they’ve taken the cargo."

Maren’s hands clenched at her sides. "They took *him*, too. The one who was supposed to be safe."

Elias turned, his jaw set. "And I’m still here."

The words hung between them, heavy and unbreakable. The shop felt smaller than ever. The rain kept falling.
