The library at night was silent except for the hum of fluorescent lights and the distant creak of the old floorboards. Lily sat cross-legged on the floor, her notebook open in her lap, pen poised over a half-finished story. Outside, the city lights flickered through the grimy windows, painting everything in shades of blue and gray. It wasn’t her first night here, but tonight felt different. The words kept slipping out of her fingers, leaving behind only blank pages.

She had always loved stories—how they let you escape, let you believe in magic even when the world felt too real. But lately, every time she started one, it ended the same way: with her staring at the ceiling, wondering if she’d ever finish. Her teacher had said she had a knack for it, that her imagination ran wild like a river. But rivers ran out, too.

Then there was her dad’s old typewriter, rusted and half-buried under a pile of boxes in the attic. The first time she’d found it, she’d touched the keys, imagining the sound they’d make if pressed. The second time, she’d started typing something—*The Last Letter*—and it had felt like coming home. The words spilled out without thinking, like ink bleeding onto paper.

She closed her notebook and stood, stretching her arms above her head. The attic was a maze of forgotten things, but the typewriter was her anchor. She picked it up, its weight familiar despite how long it had been since she’d last used it. The keys were smooth, the ribbon still shiny in places. Maybe she’d write something new tonight. Something that didn’t end in silence.

---

The elevator doors dinged open, and the scent of rain and old paper filled the air. Lily stepped out into the damp hallway, her fingers brushing against the cold metal of the typewriter’s lid. She didn’t know what she’d write, but she knew she had to keep going. Somewhere, in the dark between stories, there was a beginning that hadn’t started yet.

She opened the lid, and the first word she typed was *Once*.

The machine whirred to life, and the city’s glow blurred into something brighter—something that waited to be found.
