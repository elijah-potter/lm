In the hollowed-out tower of St. Crux, the clock hands had grown skeletal, their movements slow as a dying heartbeat. Each tick of the second hand whispered secrets to the wind, while the minute hand—once bold and vibrant—had faded into a ghostly blur, erasing all but the past. The hour hand, a jagged blade, had begun to spin backward, carving the future into fragments of forgotten time.

Mira found it by accident. She had come to St. Crux to retrieve a stolen relic, but the moment she stepped inside, the clock stopped. The tower’s stained glass shattered into shards of fractured time, and the air hummed with the sound of a thousand clocks chimes lost in the abyss. The relic was a pocket watch, its face cracked, but its hands had not stopped—only paused, suspended in a moment between past and present.

She took it, but as she turned to leave, the clock’s gears turned again, pulling her back. The hands spun faster, and the time around her dissolved like sugar in water. The walls of the tower became layers of time—somewhere between when she arrived and when she left. A child’s laughter echoed from the past, and a woman in a tattered dress whispered to her in the future. The pocket watch’s hands pointed to **11:59:59**, a final moment before the tower’s time unraveled entirely.

Outside, the sun was setting, but the sky had no color—only the slow, creeping shadow of a clock dragging the world backward. The relic was gone. The tower’s clock was broken. And somewhere, a new one was already ticking.
