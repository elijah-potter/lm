• 2000 Championship winning season - This season consisted of a hard battle with McClaren driver Mikka Häkkinen. Schumacher had much more success when it came to winning races, but the team struggled with DNF results. In the end, Schumacher secured the Championship at Suzuka after a dramatic race in which Schumacher passed Häkkinen to clinch the title.
• 2001 Championship winning season - This season was dominant for Schumacher. No other driver posed much of a challenge, and he won 9 times during the season. Schumacher would clinch the championship with four races to go and ended up finishing 58 points ahead of second place.
• 2002 Championship winning season - This was another dominant season for Schumacher and Ferrari — perhaps the most dominant. Schumacher would clinch the championship with six races left and won a record-breaking 11 races. There were 17 races that year and Ferrari won all but two. This was also the season where Schumacher tied the long-standing record of Juan Manuel Fangio's 5 World Drivers' Championships.
• 2003 Championship winning season - The 2003 season was a season of regulation changes designed to make the racing more competitive, and it worked. Schumacher just barely won the championship with a two-point lead over his season rival, Kimi Räikkönen. Schumacher became the driver with the most Formula One World Drivers' Championships in history.
• 2004 Championship winning season - Despite regulation changes the previous year and a much more competitive 2003 season, this would be another dominant season for Schumacher and Ferrari. The level of dominance was on par with his 2002 season, and Schumacher would once again break the record for most races won in a season. In the first 13 races of the season, Schumacher only lost once. He went on to rack up an astounding 13 wins in 18 races over the course of the season. He once again had no serious rival with his teammate, Reubens Barrichello, taking second place in the championship standings.
