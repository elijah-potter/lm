A tiny pocket watch, its brass face etched with a forgotten date,
kept in a grandfather’s drawer, its hands frozen mid-seconds—
each tick a heartbeat between generations.
The chain, frayed yet sturdy, bears the weight of a lifetime’s stories:
the way his mother’s hands trembled when he asked for it,
the stolen glances from a high school hallway,
the buried promise from a love that outlived the war.
Not just time, but memory—trapped in a movement too slow to move.
What it measures isn’t seconds, but the silence between them.
