The janitor noticed the faint, unpolished edge on the corner of the new chair in the break room—the one they’d barely used—and quietly tucked it into the corner of the floorboard.

It wasn’t the chair itself, but the *exact* angle where the wood met the floor, where the grain in the grain stain matched the subtle twist in the ceiling’s old planking. The detail was so small it wasn’t even *wrong*—just an unexpected echo of something older, forgotten.

Later, while wiping down the kitchen counters, they traced the mark with their thumb, realizing how easily overlooked such things can be, how they linger in the cracks between what’s seen and what’s *seen to be*. The chair remained unused. The detail stayed hidden.
