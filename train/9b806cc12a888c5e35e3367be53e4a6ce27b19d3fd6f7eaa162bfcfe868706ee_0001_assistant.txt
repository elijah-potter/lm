1. Hydrogen: I, Henry Cavendish, discovered hydrogen which I called "inflammable air". I think it is important to be very precise when doing research.
2. Helium: Oui, my name is Jules Janssen and in 1868 I observed a solar eclipse through my spectroscope. I noticed a yellow line coming from the chromosphere of the sun. I thought it was sodium, but in October of 1868, Norman Lockyer and Edward Frankland realized it was a new element and named it after the Greek word for sun, which is helios. Therefore the element's name is helium. 
3. Lithium: My name is Johan August Arfwedson and I am from Sweden. In 1817, I visited my friend Jöns Jakob Berzelius's laboratory. While there, trying to analyze the mineral petalite, I discovered lithium. 
4. Beryllium: I am Louis Nicolas Vauquelin and I was an assistant to A. F. Fourcroy. We worked together and published together for many years. In 1797 I discovered chromium. In the next year, 1798, I was working with emeralds and I discovered beryllium oxide.
5. Boron: We are Sir Humphry Davy, Joseph Gay-Lussac, and Louis Jacques Thénard. The three of us discovered boron, although I, Sir Davy, was able to confirm that it was a new element. I named it boracium, joining the word "borax" with the ending -ium.
