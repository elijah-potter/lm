**BREAKING:** Mayor’s Office Announces Emergency Shelter Expansion in Harmony Heights After Historic Rainfall

Harmony Heights residents have been evacuated from low-lying neighborhoods due to catastrophic flooding triggered by a rare late-season monsoon storm. City officials confirm the storm, classified as a "monstrous downpour," dumped over **18 inches of rain** in 24 hours, exceeding all historical records. The National Weather Service has issued a **Red Alert** for the region, warning of potential landslides and debris flow risks. Authorities report at least **12 confirmed fatalities** and **47 missing persons**, with search-and-rescue teams still working in remote areas. The city’s flood barriers, which were operational for the past two months, were overwhelmed, leading to widespread water damage.

The newly expanded emergency shelter at the **Harmony Heights Community Center** has already accommodated over **1,200 displaced residents**, including families displaced from their homes near the old canal system. Volunteers from the Red Cross and local churches are distributing food, medical supplies, and emergency rations. The state has deployed two military helicopters to assist with rescue efforts in inaccessible areas.

Residents near the **Blackthorn River overflow zone** are advised to avoid all floodwaters, which remain at dangerous levels. The city has suspended all non-essential services, including public transportation, until further notice. Authorities urge anyone with critical documentation (passports, medical records) to seek shelter immediately. The mayor has declared a state of emergency, and residents are urged to register for updates via the city’s official app.

Ahead of the storm, Harmony Heights implemented **mandatory water rationing**, reducing daily allocations to **10 gallons per household** to prevent infrastructure strain. The city’s water treatment plant, located near the flood zone, is operating at maximum capacity but remains functional. No major power outages have been reported yet, though blackouts are expected in high-risk areas within 48 hours.

The storm has also caused severe damage to the **Harmony Heights Farmers Market**, which was forced to close indefinitely. Local businesses along the riverfront report extensive structural damage, with several shops and restaurants reporting water intrusion. The city is coordinating with FEMA for disaster relief funding, though delays are expected due to the region’s remote status.

For updates, residents are directed to the **Harmony Heights Emergency Alert System (HEAS)** or the city’s social media channels. No official word has been released on when normal operations will resume, but officials promise a full investigation into the storm’s impact and potential causes.
