A dim hallway flickered with the glow of an old fluorescent light, casting long shadows that seemed to stretch into the unknown beyond the cracked door. The air smelled of damp wool and old paper, the kind of scent that lingered after the world had stopped making noise. On the table by the window, a single cup sat empty, its surface etched with the faint imprint of a teacup—too precise to be accidental.

She had been waiting for hours. Not for words, but for the way his fingers brushed against the doorknob just before he turned it, the way his breath hitched when he saw her name on the list. He didn’t say it—she’d already known. The unspoken weight of it settled between them like a stone in a well. The silence wasn’t empty; it was full of the things that needed to be said, but the words were already there, coiled tight in the space between them.
