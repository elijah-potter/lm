The server room’s fluorescent hum grew louder as the power surge fried the last of the redundant fans. Engineer Elias wiped his brow, staring at the blinking error on the console. *"Mistake #42-ERROR-ALERT: Primary cooling loop overheat detected."*

He should have called for backup. Instead, he grabbed the emergency kit from the shelf—just a spare circuit breaker and a wrench—and bolted upstairs.

The first floor was chaos. Customers’ orders, frozen mid-transaction. The AI’s voice, static-laced, repeated the same error: *"System integrity compromised. Full shutdown imminent."*

Elias’s hands shook as he fumbled with the wrench. The breaker was the last thing he needed. He slammed it. Nothing.

Then the lights flickered.

Outside, the city’s neon glow pulsed through the broken windows. A voice crackled over the radio: *"Elias? You there?"*

He exhaled. "Yeah."

*"We’re on our way. But you—"*

The radio cut out.

The server room’s air conditioning died. Steam coiled from the exposed cables. Elias grabbed the nearest cable—an old, rusted one—and yanked. The machine died with a final, satisfied *click*.

Silence.

From the floor below, footsteps echoed. The door creaked open.

A woman stood in the glow of a streetlamp, her coat damp. "You made it," she said, voice rough. "But you’ll have to walk."
