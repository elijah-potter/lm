The last time I saw the crumbling stone arch of the old mill, its once-vibrant moss and ironwork now faded into a sickly green, peeling like old skin. The river beneath it groaned, its waters thick with sediment, carrying with it the last remnants of the dam’s forgotten foundations. Beyond, the forest had swallowed the fields whole—tall, gnarled trees bent toward the earth, their branches weaving like skeletal fingers across the sky. The air smelled of damp earth and decay, the scent of something that had long since lost its life. The wind carried whispers from the ruins of the abandoned farmhouses, their windows shattered, their walls sagging like old men’s bones. The last light of dusk painted everything in a sickly, ashen hue, making the place look both beautiful and final. I stood at the edge of the last path, my boots sinking into the mud, watching the last light fade. The river’s current carried away the last echoes of my footsteps, and I knew this was it.
