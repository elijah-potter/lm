The first mistake was the cracked egg on the floor, its shell shattering like brittle glass. Inside, the yolk spilled in slow, dark rivers across the kitchen tiles. The chef, Elias, knelt, his fingers brushing the remnants before they pooled into a messy, half-cooked mess.

He wiped his brow, a habit from years of stress, and muttered, *"Another one."* The stove’s burner hissed, a sound that had grown familiar—like the ticking of a clock that couldn’t be stopped.

The recipe had been perfect once, a dish he’d perfected in his late mother’s kitchen: *Duck Confit with Wild Mushroom Ragu*. The first batch had been a masterpiece, served at the Blackthorn Feast, where the Duke himself had praised it. But today, the duck was too crispy, the mushrooms overcooked, and the sauce had turned to sludge.

Elias tossed the ruined dish into the sink, where it joined the previous week’s failures: a half-baked risotto, a burnt cheesecake that had earned him a slap across the back of the head from his father-in-law. He pulled a face at the reflection in the fogged window.

*"This is how it starts,"* he thought, staring at the egg’s remnants. *"A mistake. And then another. And then—"* The microwave beeped. Inside, a bag of frozen peas. *"Perfect."*

He grabbed the peas, tossed them into the pan, and closed his eyes. The first one was already brown.
