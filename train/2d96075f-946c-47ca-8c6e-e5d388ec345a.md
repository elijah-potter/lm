**The Last Transmission**

We didn’t mean for this to happen.

The console flickered to life in the dead of the night, its blue glow cutting through the static hum of the ship’s systems. Dr. Elias Voss adjusted the focus of the scope, his fingers trembling—not from cold, but from the weight of what he was about to see. The transmission was encrypted, but the urgency was unmistakable.

*"We didn’t mean for this to happen."*

The voice was distorted, muffled by the ship’s own noise, but the words were clear enough. They were coming from the *Aurora-7*, the research vessel that had gone missing three months ago. The coordinates led to a black hole’s edge, a place where even the most advanced sensors should have failed.

Captain Lien had ordered the scan. The AI had flagged anomalies—unnatural readings, energy signatures that didn’t match any known celestial object. The crew had been told to prepare for a routine inspection. But Elias knew better. He’d seen the same thing in his research: gravitational distortions that defied physics. And now, the crew of the *Aurora-7* were calling for help.

His thumb hovered over the transmit button. He could wait. He could send a general alert, a standard distress call. But something in his gut told him this wasn’t just another lost expedition. This was a warning.

*"We didn’t mean for this to happen."*

The screen cut to static. Then, a single, unmistakable sound—a deep, resonant hum, like the universe itself shifting. The AI’s warning beeped in his ear. *"Critical anomaly detected. Proceed with extreme caution."*

Elias exhaled. He had a choice. He could turn this off. He could let the *Aurora-7* be the first to learn what they already knew.

Or he could press the button.

And hope they didn’t all end up like the others.
