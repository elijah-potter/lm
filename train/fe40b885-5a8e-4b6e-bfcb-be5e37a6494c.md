The neon glow of the alley flickered against the damp pavement, casting long shadows across the cracked asphalt. A single cigarette burned in the ashtray of the empty bench, its smoke curling lazily upward.

Javier adjusted his coat collar, the cold seeping through despite the heavy fabric. Across the way, the flicker of a distant radio played softly, its voice muffled by the static—*"You don’t have to say it, but I know."*

He didn’t move. The words hung between them, unspoken, yet clear as a bell. The tension in his shoulders eased slightly, just enough to let the weight of the silence dissolve. The air smelled of rain and exhaust, but for the first time in weeks, it felt like breath.
