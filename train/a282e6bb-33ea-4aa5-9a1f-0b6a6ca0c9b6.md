A small, intricately carved wooden spoon—its surface worn smooth by years of use—slides from the fingertips of a child into the warm palm of an elder. The elder’s fingers, gnarled with age, trace the spoon’s curve before passing it to a young woman passing by, her eyes alight with curiosity. She catches it just as the elder’s hand begins to falter, then slips it into the pocket of a stranger in a bustling market, where it nestles beside a cracked pocket watch and a half-empty vial of ink. The stranger, distracted by a vendor’s wares, does not notice until the spoon is whisked away by a passing thief. The thief’s grip is quick; the spoon vanishes into the crowd, its next owner unknown, its origin lost in the chaos of the street.
