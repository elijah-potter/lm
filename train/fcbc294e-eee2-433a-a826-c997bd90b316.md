The old clock tower creaked as rain drummed against its rusted face. The weight of its chime had long since stopped—yet every quarter-hour, a ghostly echo still resonated through the cobblestones, as if the past had never faded. A woman in a faded wool coat stepped from the alley, her breath fogging in the damp air. She clutched a torn photograph, its edges singed. The photograph showed a boy—just her age—standing in the same alley, his hands trembling as he clutched a similar frame. The boy’s eyes were wide, his smile strained. The woman’s fingers traced the faded image, her voice cracking: *"You were here too."* The alley’s shadows swallowed the boy whole before the photograph dissolved into static. A car horn blared from a passing vehicle; the woman stumbled, her grip slipping. The photograph fell to the ground, its edges now scorched black. The boy’s face flickered in the rain-slicked glass of the car’s windshield, his smile frozen mid-air. The horn cut off abruptly, but the boy’s voice whispered from the glass: *"Wait."* The woman’s breath hitched. The past had found her again.
