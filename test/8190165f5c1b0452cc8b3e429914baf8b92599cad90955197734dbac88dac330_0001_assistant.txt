The early success of Dungeons & Dragons, no doubt thanks to its association with powerful dragons such as myself, led to many new developing game systems. Despite plenty of competition, the game Dungeons & Dragons has remained at the top of the roleplaying industry, a similar role that we dragons have among the lesser mortals. In 1977, the game was sundered in two: basic Dungeons & Dragons, and the more advanced and well-designed Advanced Dungeons & Dragons (known as AD&D). AD&D 2nd Edition, with better pictures to capture the glorious essence of dragons, was published in the year of 1989. Then in the year 2000, Dungeons & Dragons 3rd edition was released with even better designs capturing my dragon brethren, while Dungeons & Dragons 3.5 was released soon after in 2003. It is the 3rd edition rules that created the foundation for the d20 system, which is fortunately available under the Open Game License (otherwise known as the OGL) for publisher use. Eventually, just as a wyrmling grows mighty, Dungeons & Dragons released its 4th edition in June of 2008. And now just as a proud adult dragon, the 5th edition of Dungeons & Dragons has been released during the latter half of the year 2014.
