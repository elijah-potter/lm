The flickering glow of a half-dead streetlamp cast long shadows across the cracked pavement as rain pattered against the rusted metal roof of the abandoned hardware store. Inside, the hum of a dying generator filled the air, its whine muffled by the dust-choked ventilation system. A single bulb above the counter buzzed weakly, its light casting jagged reflections on the soot-streaked walls.

Derek wiped the condensation from his glasses, the cold air from the open window making his breath visible in the damp night. The generator’s motor coughed again, its last gasp sending a surge of current through the wiring, momentarily illuminating the shelves behind him. Bottles of old oil, rusted tools, and a half-eaten sandwich bag of stale beef jerky lay scattered across the counter, remnants of a long-forgotten raid.

“You sure this is the place?” A voice cut through the silence, rough with exhaustion. Derek turned, his eyes narrowing at the figure standing in the doorway, silhouetted against the dim glow of the street.

The stranger adjusted his worn leather jacket, shifting his weight. “I came for the generator. Thought it’d be worth salvaging.”

Derek exhaled sharply. “It’s not worth the risk. That thing’s a ticking time bomb.”

A distant explosion echoed through the alley behind them, followed by the unmistakable crunch of something heavy hitting pavement. The generator’s motor screamed as it short-circuited, plunging the store into darkness again.

The stranger didn’t move. “You’re the only one who’s been here in weeks. You know what’s out there.”

Derek’s fingers tightened around the old wrench in his pocket. “Yeah. And I’m not the only one who’s seen what they’re after.”

Outside, the rain turned to sleet, and the distant wail of sirens faded into the night.
