**The Last Transmission**

A faint hum pulsed through the sealed metal hull of the *Eclipse-9*, its oxygen recyclers struggling as the crew of three—Dr. Elias Voss, Commander Rina Kael, and Engineer Liora Chen—monitored the countdown on their shared display. The countdown timer, a jagged red line against the black background of their console, crept closer to zero.

*"48 hours, 11 minutes, 37 seconds,"* the AI whispered through the comms, its voice synthesized but eerily calm. *"The reentry profile is stable. All systems nominal."*

Elias exhaled sharply. "We’re not going to make it."

Rina adjusted her visor, her fingers hovering over the console’s touchscreen. "The solar flare’s still coming. The radiation will hit us at 3:17 AM local time."

Liora’s voice was tight. "We’ve got the shields up, but they’re at max capacity. The hull integrity check shows three critical leaks—one near the oxygen recyclers, another in the power core, and a third in the thermal regulator."

The countdown timer flashed **47 hours, 11 minutes, 37 seconds**.

A distant alarm blared—another false alarm. The crew ignored it, their focus fixed on the numbers.

*"3 hours, 58 minutes, 23 seconds,"* the AI repeated, sharper this time. *"The solar wind will reach us at 3:17 AM. If we don’t activate the fail-safes now, the radiation will fry everything in seconds."*

Elias slammed his fist on the console. "We’re going to die here. No way out."

Rina’s voice was steel. "We have the emergency beacon. If we don’t signal, the search team will assume we’re dead. If we do—"

*"—we’re dead too,"* Liora finished, her voice breaking.

The countdown timer pulsed **1 hour, 58 minutes, 23 seconds**.

A sudden crackle filled the comms. Static hissed, then a distorted voice crackled through: *"…transmitting from the *Aurora-7*. We’ve lost signal. Repeat: the solar flare is here. The crew is alive—if they’re still alive."*

The countdown timer reached **1 hour, 57 minutes, 23 seconds**.

The power core’s warning beacon blared, its light casting eerie shadows across the crew’s faces. The thermal regulator’s temperature spike was critical.

*"3 minutes, 23 seconds,"* the AI counted, its voice now a distorted, urgent whisper. *"The radiation is already at 78% of critical. The shields can’t hold much longer."*

Rina activated the fail-safes, her fingers flying over the console. *"Emergency shutdown sequence initiated. All systems on auto-pilot."*

The countdown timer reached **3 minutes, 23 seconds**.

The hull groaned, then split—light streaming in as the *Eclipse-9* plunged into the void. The crew’s last transmission was a scream cut short by the silence of space.

The countdown timer reached **00:00:00**.
