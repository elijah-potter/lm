The last note of the broken guitar was a whisper against the wind’s scream. Outside, the city’s pulse throbbed—neon veins bleeding into the street, where the ghosts of forgotten promises still danced in the flicker of streetlamps. Inside, the walls were lined with half-finished sketches of a world that had already begun to unravel.

She found the first page of the book in the pocket of the jacket she’d worn to the concert three nights ago. The title was scribbled in ink that had faded to silver: *"The End is Where It All Began."* The words were already half-erased, as if the paper had been dipped in time and washed away.

The first line read:
*"They say the universe is infinite, but I know better. It’s just another loop, and this time, I’m the one spinning it."*

She traced the lines with her fingers, and the ink under her thumb *moved*—not like static, but like liquid. The page curled inward, revealing a new section beneath the first. The text there was different, sharper, as if written in a language she’d never learned but understood instantly:

*"The beginning is always a door. Yours is cracked open."*

A knock at the door. The wind rattled the window. The city’s pulse turned to a hum, a rhythm she’d grown tired of but couldn’t ignore.

She turned the page. The room was empty. The city’s veins had thinned to static. The last line of the first page was now the first line of something else:

*"The end is where it all began."*
