The café’s hum was a shared breath—everyone’s coffee, everyone’s conversation, the air thick with the scent of stale espresso and pastries. My hands trembled as I placed the last coin into the machine, fingers clutching the empty cup. The barista’s eyes flicked up, unreadable, before turning back to her phone. I knew that moment well: the one where you realize you’re not the first to feel this way, not the first to be invisible in a space that’s already full.

The door creaked open, and the morning sun spilled in, casting long shadows across the floor. Outside, a child’s laughter echoed through the window, sharp and unfiltered. I exhaled slowly, the weight of the borrowed moment pressing down like a second skin. Somewhere, another person was standing in this exact same spot, watching the same door, waiting for the same thing to happen.
