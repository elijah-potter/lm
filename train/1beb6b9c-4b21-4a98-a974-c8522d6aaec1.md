A stranger’s quiet laugh in a crowded elevator, the warmth of a mug shared with someone who isn’t yours—yet feels like a secret. The way time slows when you’re not the one driving it, when the path you tread is someone else’s shadow. The ache of stepping into a room where the scent, the furniture, the hum of voices all whisper *this was here before you*. How the absence of your name in a text chain, or the way a song plays without you singing it, makes the present feel like a borrowed space. The tension between wanting to claim what isn’t yours and knowing it’s only yours if you let it. The ghost of a memory that wasn’t yours to hold, yet you cling to it like a lifeline. How the borrowed moments are the only ones that feel *real*—because they’re not yours to erase.
