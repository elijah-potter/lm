The morning sun spilled through the windows of the old house on Maple Street, painting the bedroom window glass in gold. Liam sat cross-legged on the floor, staring at his hands—clawed, dark, and trembling. His mother’s voice came from downstairs, muffled through the thick wood: *"Liam, where’s the milk?"*

He didn’t answer. The milk was already gone. Always gone.

Outside, the wind howled through the broken screen door, rattling the dust motes trapped in the light. The neighbors’ dogs barked at something, something that wasn’t there. Liam pressed his palm to his chest, feeling the old scar—a jagged line where the last thing he’d seen before the world turned black had ripped him apart. His fingers curled into fists.

His phone buzzed. A text from his best friend, Jake: *"You up? I’m at the diner. Bring the bag."*

Liam exhaled, the breath catching in his throat. He had to go. The diner was where he belonged, even if the rest of the world felt like it was falling apart.

---

The diner smelled of coffee and grease, the kind of smell that made Liam’s stomach grumble without him trying. Jake sat at the counter, his usual spot, flipping through a comic book with a smirk. He didn’t look up when Liam slid into the seat across from him.

*"You look like you’ve seen a ghost,"* Jake said, tossing the comic onto the table.

Liam didn’t answer. He just stared at the coffee mug in front of him, the steam curling like a ghost of its own.

*"You haven’t eaten in days,"* Jake pressed. *"Or slept. Or—"* He trailed off, seeing the way Liam’s eyes darted to the door. *"What’s eating you?"*

Liam’s jaw tightened. *"Nothing."*

Jake leaned forward. *"Liar. Last time I checked, ghosts don’t eat. You’re the one with the hollow cheeks and the way you keep staring at the walls."*

A knock at the door. The diner owner, Mrs. Henderson, pushed it open. *"Liam, you got a minute?"*

Jake groaned but stood. *"I’ll be back later."*

Liam nodded, but his mind was already elsewhere.

Mrs. Henderson had a habit of knowing things before they happened. She was the only one who called him by his real name—Daniel. The one that didn’t taste like ash. The one that didn’t make his stomach twist when he said it.

*"You’re frowning like you’re about to cry,"* she said, setting a steaming mug in front of him. *"Here. Black coffee. Strong enough to wake a dead man."*

Liam took the cup, the heat of the coffee seeping into his fingers. He sipped it, the bitter taste cutting through the stale air in his mouth.

*"What’s wrong?"* she asked again.

*"I’m not sure,"* Liam lied.

She studied him for a long moment, then reached into her apron pocket and pulled out a small, worn notebook. *"You ever read this?"*

She flipped it open, and Liam’s breath caught. The pages were filled with sketches of people—faces he didn’t recognize, but the way they moved, the way their eyes seemed to *watch* him. He’d seen them before, in the cracks of the sidewalk, in the reflections of his own eyes when he wasn’t looking.

*"No,"* he whispered.

Mrs. Henderson closed the book. *"That’s okay. Some things are better left unseen."*

Liam’s throat was dry. *"What are they?"*

She didn’t answer. Instead, she slid the book across the counter. *"Here. Keep it."*

---

The city was quiet tonight. Too quiet. The kind of quiet that made the bones in Liam’s legs ache. He walked slowly, his hands shoved deep in his pockets, the notebook tucked under his arm. The sketches inside were all the same—faces, but not faces. They were like shadows with too many teeth.

He stopped at the old abandoned theater on the corner. The doors were boarded up, the windows shattered. He pressed his palm against the glass, feeling the cold seep through. The theater had been empty for years, but sometimes, when the wind was right, he swore he could hear laughter inside.

His fingers trembled as he turned the page. Another face. This one was younger, a girl with dark curls and a smile that didn’t reach her eyes.

*"She’s not real,"* he muttered.

The notebook slipped from his fingers, landing with a soft *thud* on the cracked pavement. He bent to pick it up, but the pages had scattered. He reached for one, his fingers brushing against the girl’s face.

For a second, he saw her.

She was standing in the middle of the street, her hands pressed against the glass of a store window. The sign above her read *"Gone with the Wind."*

Then she was gone.

Liam’s breath hitched. The notebook fell to the ground. He knelt, gathering the pages, but they were gone. All of them.

He stood, his legs unsteady, and walked back to the diner.

---

Jake was waiting for him at the counter, a half-eaten burger in his hand. *"You okay?"*

Liam didn’t answer. He just stared at the floor.

*"Liam?"*

He looked up. *"I’m fine."*

Jake’s face softened. *"You’re not."*

Liam shook his head. *"I’m just… tired."*

Jake reached into his pocket and pulled out a cigarette, lighting it with a flick of his lighter. He took a drag, exhaled slowly. *"You know, sometimes the things that scare us most aren’t the monsters under the bed. Sometimes they’re the ones right in front of us."*

Liam didn’t laugh. *"What are you talking about?"*

Jake didn’t answer. Instead, he tapped his fingers against the counter. *"You ever think about the people who don’t remember you? The ones who only see what they want to see? The ones who forget you exist?"*

Liam’s stomach twisted. *"What are you saying?"*

Jake finished his cigarette, stubbed it out in the ashtray. *"I’m saying you might be the only one who sees them."*

Liam’s eyes burned. *"See who?"*

Jake didn’t answer. He just looked at Liam, really looked at him, like he was seeing something he’d never seen before.

The bell above the door chimed. Mrs. Henderson pushed it open, her hands full of takeout boxes. *"Liam, you forgot your usual."*

Liam didn’t move. *"I’m not hungry."*

She set the boxes down in front of him. *"Eat. Then you’ll see."*

---

Liam ate, but he didn’t taste anything. The food was cold, the coffee bitter, the burger dry. His stomach was a hollow place, and he knew if he kept eating, it would just get worse.

Outside, the city pulsed with life. Cars honked, people laughed, children ran down the streets. Liam watched them from the window, his hands clenched into fists.

He thought about the notebook. About the faces. About the girl in the window.

He thought about Mrs. Henderson’s voice, soft and quiet: *"Some things are better left unseen."*

But then he thought about Jake’s words, sharp and honest: *"You might be the only one who sees them."*

Liam looked down at his hands. They were dirty, his nails bitten to the quick. He ran his fingers over the counter, the wood grain smooth beneath his touch.

For the first time in months, he smiled.

*"I don’t know if I can do this,"* he whispered.

The diner owner turned. *"Do what?"*

*"Find them."*

She didn’t answer. Instead, she reached into her apron pocket and pulled out another notebook. This one was blank, but the pages were lined with the same strange, jagged lines that had been in the first one.

*"You’re not going to like what you find,"* she said.

Liam didn’t ask. He just took the book, the weight of it familiar in his hands.

*"Where to?"*

She pointed to the door. *"Out."*

---

The night air was cold, biting against Liam’s skin. He walked slowly, the notebook tucked under his arm, the blank pages filling with the shapes of faces he didn’t know.

The city was a maze of shadows and light, and somewhere in the middle of it all, he was looking for something.

And he didn’t know what it was.
