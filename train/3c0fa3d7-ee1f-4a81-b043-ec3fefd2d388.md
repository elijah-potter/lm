A recent study identified a novel enzyme in extremophilic archaea capable of degrading synthetic polymers at near-zero temperatures, defying conventional catalytic efficiency models. This discovery challenges long-standing assumptions about thermodynamics in enzymatic reactions, prompting speculation about undiscovered catalysts in non-ideal environments.

Detailed follow-up experiments revealed the enzyme’s active site contains an unusual hydrogen-bond network stabilized by non-canonical amino acid residues, suggesting previously uncharacterized protein fold motifs. The discovery also highlights potential environmental roles: microbial communities in permafrost soils may rely on such enzymes for recycling plastics over geological timescales.

Key unresolved questions:
