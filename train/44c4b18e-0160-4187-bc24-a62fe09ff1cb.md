## Proposal: The Willow Creek Pocket Park – A Climate-Resilient Community Oasis

**Executive Summary:** This proposal seeks $75,000 from the Municipal Parks and Recreation Department to transform the currently underutilized and degraded lot at the corner of Elm Street and Willow Creek Drive into a vibrant, climate-resilient “pocket park” – The Willow Creek Pocket Park. This project directly addresses the city’s strategic goals of enhancing green infrastructure, promoting community well-being, and mitigating the impacts of increasingly frequent and intense rainfall events. The park will incorporate native, drought-tolerant landscaping, a rainwater harvesting system for irrigation, and permeable paving to reduce stormwater runoff.  Through collaborative design and construction, this project fosters community ownership, provides a safe and accessible green space for all residents, and serves as a replicable model for future urban revitalization efforts. The total project cost is $100,000, with $25,000 secured through local business sponsorships and volunteer labor valued at $15,000.



**Project Plan & Timeline:**

The project is divided into four phases, each with designated responsible partners and measurable deliverables. 

**Phase 1: Design & Planning (Months 1-2) – Responsible Partner: Willow Creek Neighborhood Association (WCNA) & City Planning Department**
* **Deliverables:** Community needs assessment report (WCNA), site assessment and topographic survey (City Planning), preliminary park design incorporating community feedback (WCNA & City Planning), finalized design plans approved by the City Parks Department.
* **Activities:** WCNA will conduct surveys and host community forums to gather input on park design preferences. The City Planning Department will provide technical expertise and ensure compliance with zoning regulations. 

**Phase 2: Site Preparation & Infrastructure (Months 3-4) – Responsible Partner: GreenThumb Landscaping & City Public Works**
* **Deliverables:** Site clearing and grading completed (GreenThumb), installation of permeable paving (GreenThumb), rainwater harvesting system installed and tested (City Public Works).
* **Activities:** GreenThumb Landscaping will handle all physical site preparation, including removal of debris and grading. City Public Works will oversee the installation of the rainwater harvesting system, ensuring proper connections to existing drainage infrastructure.

**Phase 3: Planting & Landscaping (Months 5-6) – Responsible Partner: Willow Creek Garden Club & Volunteer Teams**
* **Deliverables:** Native plant installation completed (Willow Creek Garden Club & Volunteers), irrigation system operational (Willow Creek Garden Club), mulch application and initial site stabilization (Volunteers).
* **Activities:** The Willow Creek Garden Club will provide horticultural expertise and oversee the planting of native, drought-tolerant species. Volunteer teams, organized through the WCNA, will assist with planting and site preparation.

**Phase 4: Community Launch & Education (Month 7) – Responsible Partner: WCNA & City Parks & Recreation Department**
* **Deliverables:** Grand opening celebration event (WCNA), interpretive signage installed (City Parks & Recreation), community education workshops on water conservation and native plants (WCNA & City Parks & Recreation).
* **Activities:** The WCNA will organize a community celebration to officially open the park. The City Parks & Recreation Department will install interpretive signage to educate visitors about the park’s features and ecological benefits.



**Budget:**

The total project cost is $100,000. The requested municipal funding of $75,000 will be supplemented by $25,000 in secured funds.

* **Site Preparation & Permeable Paving:** $30,000 (GreenThumb Landscaping) – This includes clearing, grading, and installation of permeable pavers to manage stormwater runoff.
* **Rainwater Harvesting System:** $18,000 (City Public Works) – This covers the cost of materials and labor for the installation of a 2,000-gallon rainwater storage tank and associated plumbing.
* **Native Plants & Soil Amendments:** $12,000 (Willow Creek Garden Club) – This includes the purchase of native trees, shrubs, and groundcover, as well as organic soil amendments to improve soil health.
* **Interpretive Signage & Educational Materials:** $5,000 (City Parks & Recreation) –  This covers the design, fabrication, and installation of educational signage highlighting the park’s features and ecological benefits.
* **Tools & Equipment Rental:** $3,000 (WCNA) – This covers the cost of renting necessary tools and equipment for volunteer workdays.
* **Project Management & Contingency:** $17,000 – This allocation covers project coordination, administrative overhead, and a 10% contingency fund to address unforeseen expenses.
* **Secured Funds:** $25,000 (Local Business Sponsorships & Volunteer Labor – Valued at $15,000) – This demonstrates community commitment and reduces the financial burden on municipal funding.

The contingency fund will be utilized to address unexpected material cost fluctuations or unforeseen site conditions.  Detailed receipts for all expenditures will be maintained and readily available for review.



**Community Engagement & Equity Impacts:**

Community engagement has been central to this project from its inception. The WCNA has actively solicited input through surveys, public forums, and online platforms. The design incorporates features requested by residents, including accessible pathways, a small seating area, and a designated children’s play space. The project prioritizes equitable access by ensuring the park is located within a historically underserved area with limited access to green space.  The use of native plants reduces the need for chemical fertilizers and pesticides, promoting a healthier environment for all residents. Volunteer opportunities are actively promoted to encourage participation from diverse community members.

**Risk Mitigation:**

Potential risks have been identified and mitigation strategies developed.  Unfavorable weather conditions during construction could delay the project timeline. To mitigate this, the schedule incorporates buffer time and contingency plans for inclement weather. Fluctuations in material costs are addressed through the contingency fund and proactive procurement.  Potential safety hazards during volunteer workdays are minimized through comprehensive safety training and the provision of appropriate personal protective equipment.  Liability insurance will be maintained throughout the project duration.

**Post-Grant Sustainability:**

Long-term sustainability is ensured through a multi-faceted approach.  The WCNA will assume responsibility for ongoing park maintenance, including watering, weeding, and litter removal.  The City Parks & Recreation Department will provide technical support and guidance.  Community education workshops will promote responsible park usage and encourage volunteer participation in ongoing maintenance efforts.  The rainwater harvesting system reduces reliance on municipal water sources, minimizing long-term operating costs.



**Call to Action:**

The Willow Creek Pocket Park represents a unique opportunity to enhance community well-being, improve environmental resilience, and demonstrate the power of collaborative urban revitalization. We respectfully request the Municipal Parks and Recreation Department’s approval of this proposal and allocation of $75,000 in funding. This investment will yield significant and lasting benefits for the Willow Creek neighborhood and serve as a model for future green infrastructure projects throughout the city.
