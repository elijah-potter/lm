A quiet café in the early morning. The hum of distant traffic and faint coffee machine drip is the only sound. **Lena** leans against the counter, her fingers tracing the rim of her empty cup, eyes closed. A half-smile lingers on her lips as she mutters to herself, *"I should have realized this sooner."* She turns to **Daniel**, who stands across the room, arms crossed, watching her with unspoken tension.

He exhales sharply, almost as if the words have been stuck in his throat for years. *"You don’t know how long I’ve been waiting for you to say that."* His voice is rough, the words raw. *"I’ve been running through every version of this moment—how you’d react, what you’d say—and now it’s real."

She opens her eyes, startled. *"You remember?"*

*"I’ve been thinking about you for months."* He steps closer, voice dropping to a whisper. *"Not like this—when you were little, laughing at my jokes, or when you stood up for me in class. But the way you look at me now? Like you’ve been waiting too."* A beat. Then, softer: *"What if we’re wrong?"*

Lena’s breath catches. For the first time, she doesn’t have to translate. She doesn’t have to guess. She just *sees*—the way he’s holding his breath, the way his hands tremble just a little. The way his eyes are full of everything she’s never let herself admit.

She reaches out, fingers brushing his. *"We’re not,"* she says. And for the first time, neither of them has to finish the sentence.
