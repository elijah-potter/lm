The quiet afternoon sun filtered through the half-drawn curtains of the old apartment, casting long shadows across the worn rug. Elias sat at the kitchen table, fingers tracing the rim of his empty coffee cup. Outside, the distant hum of traffic and the occasional call of a pigeon faded into a rhythm that felt familiar, almost comforting. He had chosen this moment to wait.

The letter had been in the mail for two days, and each morning, he had checked the mailbox, half-expecting it to be there. But it wasn’t. He had called the office twice, each time the same receptionist repeating the same message: *"We’re still reviewing your application."* His stomach twisted. Waiting was not his nature—action was. He had built his life on moving forward, on solving problems before they became too big to ignore.

Yet here he was. His phone buzzed—a text from his sister: *"You shouldn’t be here. It’s too quiet."* He smirked, but the message made no sense. Waiting was supposed to be about patience, about letting things unfold naturally. But patience was a luxury he didn’t have.

He stood, stretching his arms overhead, the weight of the moment settling over him like a shroud. The coffee was cold. The mailbox was empty. And for the first time in years, he felt something he hadn’t in a long time: stillness. It was terrifying. It was also the first step.

He picked up the phone again, dialed the office number one last time. The receptionist’s voice was polite, but firm. *"We’ll get back to you soon."* Elias exhaled slowly, letting the silence take over. Maybe waiting wasn’t about passivity. Maybe it was about choosing where to place his energy. And for now, he had decided to place it here.
