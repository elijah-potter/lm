Changing perspective involves reframing one’s viewpoint through observation, questioning, and empathy. Initially, the same scene may appear as a threat or limitation, but by shifting focus—whether through curiosity, new information, or emotional detachment—opportunities or truths emerge. For example, a person stuck in a routine might find inspiration in others’ challenges, while a conflict could reveal hidden collaboration if approached differently.

Key shifts include:
- **Cognitive reframing:** Recognizing biases and assuming others’ perspectives.
- **Contextual awareness:** Understanding cause and effect beyond immediate perception.
- **Emotional detachment:** Observing emotions without being defined by them.

This process fosters growth, problem-solving, and deeper connections. It’s not about rejecting old views but expanding them to include unseen possibilities. The goal isn’t perfection but adaptability—balancing consistency with flexibility in thought.
