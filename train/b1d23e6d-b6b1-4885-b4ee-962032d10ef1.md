A dim, flickering bulb cast long shadows across the cracked floor of an old apartment. The door creaked open, barely shutting behind a tired man who stepped inside. His fingers traced the worn edges of a newspaper, pages yellowed with age. He exhaled slowly, the scent of stale coffee and damp wood thick in the air.

A single phone rang. He ignored it at first, but the sound persisted—unmistakable, urgent. He snatched it up, the screen dark. A notification glowed red: **"BREAKING: Accident at Sector 9 Bridge—12 confirmed fatalities."** His breath hitched. He dialed the number again, hands trembling. A woman answered, breathless: *"It’s him. They found him near the railing, arms outstretched. His last words were ‘not safe.’"* The line went dead. He dropped the phone, fingers clawing at the air.

Outside, the rain came down harder. He stood in the doorway, watching the storm roll in, until the last light faded.
