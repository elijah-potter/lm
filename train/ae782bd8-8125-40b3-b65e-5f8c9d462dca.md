A worn leather wallet slipped from the pocket of an old coat as the protagonist hurried through the rain-slicked alley, keys jingling behind them. The coat pocket had been stuffed with crumpled bills, a frayed photo of their mother, and a single, oddly shaped stone—polished and cold. The stone was too heavy to be a coin, yet too smooth to be a gem. The protagonist bent, retrieving it just as the coat’s collar flapped against their leg, exposing the wallet’s contents. Inside: a faded letter addressed to their grandmother, a rusted key, and a receipt from a shop in the city they’d left months ago.

They pocketed the wallet and the stone, the rainwater pooling in the crevices of both. The stone felt familiar, like a ghost from another time, while the wallet’s contents whispered of a life half-lived. They turned, the stone still in their palm, the rain swallowing the alley behind them.
