**"The Last Light"**

The city had forgotten how to breathe by the time she arrived, its neon veins pulsed with hollow promises. The subway doors hissed shut behind her like a door left open too long. The scent of damp concrete and old coffee clung to the air, a ghost of something once warm.

She found the bench in the park where the elders sat, their hands wrapped around cracked mugs of something that wasn’t tea. One of them—old enough to remember the war, their fingers trembling—leaned forward and whispered, *"You’re not supposed to see this."*

She followed the line of them, the way they pressed closer as if the sky had opened a secret door. The clouds were no longer cotton, but inked with stars that didn’t twinkle—they *burned*, slow and steady, like embers in a dying fire. The air smelled of ozone and something metallic, the scent of a world that had stopped turning.

Above them, the horizon split. Not dawn, not dusk, but something older, a fracture in time. The sky was a map of light—some parts still golden, others already ash. The stars were ghosts of their own reflections, drifting like leaves in a storm. The earth below was a pulse, a heartbeat too fast to count.

And then, the silence.

She sat there until her bones ached, watching the last of the light bleed into the dark like ink in water. The park was empty now, save for the birds—no longer singing, just watching, waiting for the sky to remember how to fall.
