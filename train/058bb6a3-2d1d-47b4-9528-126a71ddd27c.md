The unspoken pact among the elders of this village was a silent contract etched in the bones of tradition. No words were exchanged, yet the understanding was as clear as the unbroken chain of their lineage. They agreed to preserve the sacred grove’s secrets, to leave the cursed well untouched, and to pass down the knowledge that the river’s song carries warnings, not warnings, but the weight of history itself.

They did not speak of the night when the last healer vanished without a trace, or the children who stopped growing after eating from the forbidden figs. The agreement was not a promise, but a ritual—one where silence itself was the oath sworn. Some whispered that the pact was a trap; others believed it was a mercy. The village endured, and the unspoken bond held them together like the roots of the old oak, deep and unyielding.

The agreement was never spoken aloud, but it was never broken either.
