**Mayor’s Office Announces New Sustainability Initiative in Riverport**

Residents of Riverport are encouraged to participate in the city’s latest eco-project: the **"Green Canopy Initiative"**—a pilot program aimed at replacing 500 urban trees with drought-resistant varieties over the next six months. City officials cite declining city park attendance due to extreme heat as the primary driver, emphasizing that the initiative will reduce urban heat islands by 20% while improving air quality. Volunteers are needed for tree planting sessions, scheduled for weekends at City Hall and the Central Park entrance. Applications for volunteer spots are open until **September 15**.

Additionally, the **"Riverport Waterfront Revitalization"** project has begun construction along the new pedestrian walkway, featuring solar-powered lighting and shaded seating areas. Public feedback sessions will be held this Thursday, August 22, at the Old Mill Cultural Center. The first phase, slated for completion by December, will include a floating market space and expanded docking for electric boats.

A reminder for residents: The city’s **"Zero-Waste Fridays"** program, which mandates compostable food waste collection, will expand to include additional trash/recycling bins in apartment complexes starting next month. Violators may face fines up to $150. Residents are urged to report improper disposal by calling the **311 Hotline**.

The Riverport City Council has also approved a $200,000 grant for the **"Youth Horticulture Program,"** which will train local teens in urban gardening and sustainable landscaping. Interested students can apply for scholarships to cover equipment costs. Applications close **September 1**.

For updates, visit the city’s website or follow **@RiverportSustainable** on social media.
