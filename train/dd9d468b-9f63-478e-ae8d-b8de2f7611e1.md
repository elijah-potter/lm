The skeletal remains of a crumbling archway loom over cracked asphalt, its stone slabs tilted at impossible angles, half-buried in ivy that coils through rusted reinforcement bars. Concrete pillars, half-formed, jut from the earth like stumps, their surfaces slick with moss and mildew. The roof’s wooden planks, warped and rotting, sag precariously, half-lifted by rusted nails that still cling to the edges like ghostly fingers. A series of half-dug foundation trenches, muddy and overgrown, spiral outward in a spiral staircase that should have been a ramp. The air smells of damp earth and old wood, thick with the scent of forgotten effort. Above, the sky peeks through the gaps between the skeletal timbers, a thin, bruised ribbon of blue. No one lives here anymore, but the bones of the structure still whisper of a past that wasn’t finished.
