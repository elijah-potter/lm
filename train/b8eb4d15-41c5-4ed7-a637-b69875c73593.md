The air in the dimly lit waiting room was thick with tension. A single fluorescent bulb flickered, casting long shadows across the worn armchairs. Outside, a distant siren wailed, punctuated by sporadic gunfire. The door creaked open, and a uniformed officer stepped inside, his face pale beneath the helmet’s visor.

"Mr. Carter?" he asked, voice hoarse. "You’re the one waiting for the news."

James Carter clenched his fists. His wife, Emma, had gone missing three days ago. The search had been chaotic—fires, explosions, the scent of smoke clinging to the city like a shroud. The officer’s gaze flicked to the wall where a map of the area was pinned, half-drawn in ink. A name, *"Emma Carter,"* had been circled in red.

The officer cleared his throat. "She was found in Sector 7. Alive. But… not well."

James’s breath hitched. The officer handed him a folded piece of paper. The words were scrawled in hasty handwriting:

*"She’s in recovery. They’re working on her. Be there soon."*

A nurse’s voice echoed from somewhere deep in the facility. "Mr. Carter? You’re next."
