The dimly lit study smelled of old books and polished wood. Emma adjusted her glasses, her fingers tracing the edge of her coffee mug as she leaned slightly forward in her chair. Her posture was stiff, her posture deliberately relaxed, her eyes darting between the speaker’s lips and the screen, her lips forming faint, unintelligible sounds.

Across the table, Daniel smirked, his gaze flickering toward her before returning to the conversation. "So, the project’s behind schedule," he said, tapping his pen against the desk. "I’m not sure if we’re even going to meet the deadline."

Emma’s mouth opened slightly, but no sound came out. Her hands trembled slightly, her fingers tightening around the mug’s rim. She nodded once, her eyes glued to the screen, the sound of his voice muffled by the distance between them.

"Yeah," Daniel continued, oblivious to her discomfort. "I don’t know what to do. Maybe we should—"

Emma’s chair creaked as she leaned back sharply, her face turning pale. She cleared her throat, her voice barely audible above the hum of the air conditioning. "I—uh—uh, I think we should definitely discuss this further. When you’re done with that email." She pointed at his phone, her fingers twitching. Her tone was flat, her words clipped, but the lack of enthusiasm was clear.

Daniel laughed softly, amused by her display of effort. "Sure thing. I’ll—" He paused, his brow furrowing. "Emma? Are you okay?"

Emma’s eyes darted to his, a sudden realization dawning in her expression. She swallowed hard, her hands stilling. "Yeah. Just—thinking about something else." She gave a shallow, unconvincing nod, her voice dropping to a whisper. "Like, uh, the weather outside."
