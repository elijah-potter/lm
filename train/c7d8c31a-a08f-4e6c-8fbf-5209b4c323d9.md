The metropolis of **Neon Haven** pulses with autonomous transit hubs where self-driving pods weave through canyon-like streets lined with adaptive facades that shift colors based on energy demand. Skyscrapers grow taller with modular, AI-managed construction, their glass and steel skeletons rearranging midday to optimize sunlight and airflow. The air hums with the static of neural lace interfaces, a quiet majority of citizens choosing to sync with city algorithms for basic needs—dining, housing, even social interaction via holographic avatars. Meanwhile, a subculture of "Static Keepers" hoards analog relics in repurposed shipping containers, clinging to the past as if it were a physical thing. The streets are alive with drones delivering biotech-enhanced food—grown in vertical farms that recalibrate crop yields in real-time. The river beneath flows with algae-powered biofuel, its banks lined with solar-charged walkways that adjust elevation to avoid flooding. Festivals happen in the sky, where floating platforms host concerts and markets, while below, underground "dust lanes" (former subway tunnels) serve as black markets for rare, pre-digital tech. Governance is decentralized: city councils answer to algorithms that predict behavioral trends, though dissenters are often "reassigned" to off-world colonies. The city breathes faster than its inhabitants—some are already ghostly echoes of previous versions, their neural prints fading as new bodies replace old.
