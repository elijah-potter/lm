A crumbling stone bridge arches over a river, its rusted metal girders leaning precariously. The archway itself is jagged, half-buried in ivy that coils around its edges like a forgotten memory. The river below flows silently, its waters stained with sediment and the ghostly remains of past debris.

The ground beneath is a patchwork of uneven slabs, cracked and warped by time. A single, rusted bench sits abandoned at the edge of the bridge, its seat long since collapsed. Nearby, a small, tattered flagpole stands, its fabric frayed and torn, as if it has been there since the bridge was first built.

In the distance, a cluster of old buildings loom, their windows dark and boarded up. The walls are marked with graffiti, some faded, others still legible: *"DO NOT ENTER"*, *"This place is cursed"*, and a name scratched out in jagged letters: *"KARL"*. The air smells of damp stone and old wood, and the wind carries whispers that seem to come from nowhere in particular.

At the center of the ruins, a single, abandoned ladder leans against a half-collapsed tower, its rungs worn smooth by time and neglect. The ladder’s top is missing, leaving only a jagged hole in the sky. The ground around it is littered with half-buried tools, broken lanterns, and the occasional rusted nail.

A faint, rhythmic sound—like distant rain or the distant echo of footsteps—drifts from the buildings, but no one is there to hear it. The place feels like it was paused in time, half-constructed, half-abandoned, as if whatever was meant to be built here was never finished. The air hums with an unsettling quiet, the kind that makes the skin prickle.
