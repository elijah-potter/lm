Dental fillings are usually made from composite resin, metallic amalgam, or a special type of gold alloy. Though some are made from special dental grade porcelain that initially looks just like real tooth enamel. They are cemented to the surrounding tooth enamel to treat a small area of tooth decay or a physical defect like a chipped tooth. 

While they are meant to last a long time, all dental fillings have a finite lifespan. If you struggle to maintain a good oral hygiene regimen, the demineralization effect on your tooth enamel can gradually weaken the seam between the natural tooth enamel and the dental filling material. As this continues to progress bacteria could gain increasing access to the area. 

As the bacteria starts to affect more and more of the tooth, you might notice discomfort or newly developed sensitivity. In some cases, you might also notice a change in surface texture or color. Without professional treatment from a dentist, the tooth could suffer from worsening complications. 

If you’re lucky and it’s just a small dental filling that’s just started to fail, the dentist might be able to simply replace it with a new dental filling. This usually involves removing a small amount of the surrounding tooth enamel before applying a slightly larger dental filling. 

If the bad dental filling is large or a new area of tooth decay has developed in the adjacent tooth enamel your dentist might recommend treating the tooth with a dental crown restoration. This will call for completely replacing the entire tooth enamel layer. 

Though one thing's for sure. The longer you wait to get a suspected bad filling treated, the more likely it is to fail. The more time that goes by without a dentist's attention the more likely tooth decay is to develop under the bad dental filling. In some cases, it could even allow bacteria to invade the root of the tooth causing a toothache, which might require a root canal.
