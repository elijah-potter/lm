The rain-soaked garden path led me to an old wooden bench where a single forgotten book lay half-buried in the soil. The title, *"The Weight of Whispers"*, was smudged with age. Inside, a single, unopened page faced me: *"I didn’t realize how much I’d miss the way the wind carried my mother’s voice."* I picked up the book, my thumb brushing the worn paper, and carried it home. That night, I tucked it into my nightstand beside my journal. The next morning, I opened it again, writing beneath the same words: *"I didn’t realize how much I’d miss the way the bench held my thoughts when I was alone."* The act of keeping it, of writing there, changed nothing—yet it became the first thread in a quiet, unspoken chain.
