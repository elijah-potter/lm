**The Clockmaker’s Secret**

In the quiet town of Eldermere, where cobblestone streets hummed with the weight of history, old Elias Veyne ran his clock workshop. His grandfather’s clocks had always told time accurately, but Elias’s latest creation—a tower clock with rotating hands—kept drifting slightly off. The townsfolk whispered it was cursed.

One evening, as Elias tinkered late, a child named Mira found a tiny brass key in his workshop. It glinted under the lantern light, its engravings twisted like a cipher. Mira, a bookish girl who loved puzzles, recognized the pattern: **"SOS"** in Morse code was hidden among the symbols—though the letters were distorted by the key’s strange design.

Elias, suspicious, followed her. "What are you doing with my key?" he demanded.

Mira’s eyes lit up. "It’s not just a key—it’s a cipher. The clockmaker’s grandfather hid a message in the gears of his clocks. The key unlocks the first part of the message."

Elias frowned. "And what is it?"

Mira held up the key. "The first letter is here: **E**."

A shadow crossed Elias’s face. "The *E* is the key itself. The message starts with the symbol on the key’s edge—**⚡** (a lightning bolt). The next letter is the **shape of the clock’s second hand at midnight**—a curved line resembling **L**."

Elias’s fingers tightened. "The message is about the *time* the clocks were made. The workshop’s old ledger says the grandfather’s first clock was struck at **11:11** on the anniversary of his wife’s death. The message was hidden to warn of the curse."

Mira’s voice trembled. "But the key says *12:12*—the time he buried her."

Elias exhaled. "Then the message is: **‘The clockmaker’s wife died at 12:12. The key is the past. The clock is the future.’**"

A gust of wind rattled the workshop window. Outside, the tower clock struck midnight.
