When the last light of the evening dimmed the office’s fluorescent glow, Maria paused before the closed door of her boss’s office, the weight of her words pressing against her throat like sand. She had spent months suppressing her frustration, smoothing the edges of her concerns into polite requests, half-hearted suggestions—anything to avoid the storm that would follow if she spoke plainly. But then came the email: *"Project deadlines are tight. Prioritize X over Y."* She had already spent too long on Y, and the team’s workload had ballooned.

The next day, as the office buzzed with tension, she found her way to the break room. The coffee machine hissed, and the hum of idle chatter masked the quiet ache in her chest. She cleared her throat and said, *"I’m worried about Y. It’s not getting enough time."*

Silence.

Then, the door creaked open. Her manager, hands on hips, stared at her. *"What’s that supposed to mean?"*

Maria exhaled, steadying herself. *"I’ve been pushing too hard. The team’s already stretched thin, and if we don’t adjust, things will fall apart."* She hesitated, then added, *"I don’t want to fail you, but I need you to hear me."*

Her manager’s expression shifted—first confusion, then something like understanding. *"Okay,"* he said, voice gruff. *"But we’re doing this my way."* He turned, but Maria pressed on. *"Actually, I think we should pivot to X. It’s more critical than we realized."*

For the first time in months, the office held its breath. The tension in the air shifted, lighter, but heavier with the weight of truth. The manager nodded. *"Fine. But we start tomorrow."* Maria smiled weakly. The truth had been a spark in the dark, and though it had burned, it had also lit the way forward.
