The sweet smell from the apple blossoms clung to the summer breeze as it passed by and hung on all the way to the second-floor windows of the pale blue house at the end of the lane. It was always there at this time of year. Mild and pleasant and never overpowering, the occupant of that second-floor room hardly noticed it anymore. 
Wyatt listened to his mother's footsteps recede down the hall. Her leather-soled slippers scraped ever so slightly on the wood floor as she made her way to the stairs. He counted as she went down the fifteen steps to the first floor. There was a gentle tap of leather on wood for each and a soft thud when she reached the bottom. Once she turned towards the kitchen he heard no more. 
The moon had passed to the far side of the house and only dim light came through the windows. A streetlight down the road gave off a glow that betrayed the quiet presence of the neighborhood and a few cast-off stars lingered after their constellations had departed for the evening.
Wyatt fingered the hem of this blanket between his thumbs and forefingers as he focused on a single point on the ceiling. There was a worn spot in the texture of the eggshell finish just to the left of the light fixture. His father had bumped his head there a few times while changing a bulb or cleaning out the flies that always seemed to show up there, and each time he came away with a few crumbled pieces of ceiling paint stuck in his jet-black hair. Wyatt and his mother would giggle and then take turns picking the pieces out.
This was the spot where it all started. Where the change stemmed from.
