In a dimly lit attic above an old bookstore, a scavenger named Elias found an envelope tucked beneath a loose floorboard. The paper was yellowed, the ink faded but legible. Inside was a single sheet bearing a cryptic message:

*"The map is not where the treasure is, but where you forget to look. Seek the place where time runs slowest—where the river bends, but not as you’d expect. Bring only what you can carry, but leave behind what you fear. The first clue is the name of the town where the bell tolls twice on New Year’s Eve."*

Elias traced the faded ink with trembling fingers. Below, the street below was a blur of holiday lights, but the message spoke of a secret hidden in the river’s bend—a place where the river’s flow slowed to a whisper, where the water’s edge was lined with stones that hummed with forgotten stories. He knew the town as Hollow’s Bend, though no one there remembered the bell tolling twice on that night.

The second clue awaited him in a hollowed-out acorn, buried beneath the roots of an ancient oak near the river’s edge. Inside were two words: *"The mirror holds the key."* Elias pressed his palm against the river’s stone bank and saw, for a heartbeat, his reflection—distorted, as if caught in a slow-motion blur. The river’s current had always been swift, but now, for the first time, it felt as if it were holding its breath. The stone beneath his palm pulsed faintly, like a heartbeat.

The treasure wasn’t gold or jewels, but something far more rare: a silver locket, its surface etched with symbols that pulsed in the dim light. Inside was a portrait of a woman who had vanished decades ago, her eyes still holding the memory of a promise. Elias opened the locket’s other side and found a single, unbroken promise:

*"I will return when the river’s song is heard again."*
