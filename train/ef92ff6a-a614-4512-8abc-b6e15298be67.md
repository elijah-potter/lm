I remember the way the old clock tower’s chimes
drew the rainbows into the hollow air,
how the wind sighed through the rusted keys of a forgotten piano.
I remember the scent of damp earth after thunder,
the way the candlelight flickered like embers in a dying fire.

I remember the hollow ache between my ribs,
the weight of silence when the last whisper faded,
the way the stars pressed too close, like they were watching.
I remember the taste of salt on my tongue after the storm,
the way the world felt smaller, but not safer—just softer.

I remember the hands that held mine, the words that didn’t quite stick,
the way the night stretched thin like paper, then tore.
I remember the weight of unspoken things,
the way the past is a ghost that won’t stay gone.

I think of the cracks in the mirror now,
how light bends through them like secrets I’ve let slip.
I think of the way the world keeps spinning,
even when it feels like it’s falling apart.

I think of the time I stopped believing in time itself.
I think of the things I still don’t know.
I think of the things I thought I understood.
I think of the way I used to be—and the way I am no longer.
I think.
