A train passenger, scheduled to arrive at a distant city for a business conference, noticed an unmarked compartment with a single nameplate: *"Dr. Elias Carter."* The seat was free, and the compartment door opened without a key. Inside, a leather-bound journal titled *"The Alchemy of Chance"* lay open to a passage describing how "a single unplanned moment can rewrite destiny."

The passenger, a data analyst named Naomi, recognized the name—Dr. Carter was her former professor, known for his radical theories on quantum probability. His research suggested that coincidences aren’t random; they’re *calculations* in an unseen system.

As the train pulled into the station, Naomi found a single envelope on the seat: a postcard from her hometown, dated a year earlier. The handwriting read: *"Meet me at the library. The book on the shelf is for you."* She had no memory of returning that book.

Later, at the conference, she overheard Carter mentioning a project on "the mechanics of serendipity." The next day, her presentation on predictive algorithms was interrupted by a stranger—a woman who handed her a slip of paper with coordinates: *"The library. Tomorrow. At noon."* The book was titled *The Physics of Coincidence*, and its first page quoted: *"The universe does not play dice with reality—it hands you cards."*

By evening, Naomi had returned to her hometown, found the library, and discovered the book on the shelf. Inside, she read Carter’s final notes: *"You were supposed to meet me here. The plan was always yours to rewrite."* The cover was embossed with a symbol: a die with six faces—each marked with a different possible outcome.
