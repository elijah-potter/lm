The half-finished sketch on the easel—a landscape with a half-bleached sky and a single tree trunk leaning toward a horizon line—spreads across the studio wall. The paintbrush strokes on the canvas are uneven, some thick with swirls of ochre and blue, others barely there, as if the artist paused mid-stroke. A half-empty cup of tea sits on the desk, its steam long since condensed into a mist. The chair beside it is pushed back, its legs scraping against the floor, and the notebook on its armrest bears only a few scribbled lines: *"The wind carries what we can't name."* The rest of the page is blank, waiting. The unfinished piano at the other end of the room hums a single, unresolved note, its keys out of tune, as if the music itself is waiting for a second part that never comes. The clock on the wall ticks past the time the artist stopped, marking a silence where words, colors, and sounds should have continued.
