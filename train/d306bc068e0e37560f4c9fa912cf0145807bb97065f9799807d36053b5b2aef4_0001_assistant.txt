

1) Eat more protein - Protein provides the building blocks of muscles. A study by the Journal of Applied Physiology suggests eating about 1 gram of protein per pound of your body mass every day.

2) Eat more calories - To pack on the muscle, you need to consume a caloric surplus, that is, eat more calories than you use in a day.

3) Focus more on compound movements - Exercises such as deadlifts and squats target several exercises and these should form the backbone of your workouts, as opposed to isolation exercises like bicep curls.

4) Lift heavy - Low rep exercises with heavy weights build more strength and size than high rep exercises with lower weights

5) Try a carb loaded, amino acid packed shake pre-workout - Drinking a pre-workout shake with carbohydrates and amino acids increases protein synthesis, according to a 2001 study by the University of Texas. You need to add 10 to 20 grams of protein (about a scoop of whey powder) to your shake.

6) Take time to recover - Don't work out to the point of exhaustion more than 3 times a week, and never on consecutive days. This gives your muscles time to recover and grow.

7) Eat carbs after working out - Research shows that post-workout meals with carbs stimulate insulin production, thus stimulating muscle growth.
