**Celsius:**
- **-50°C to -40°C** – Arctic tundra winters (e.g., Antarctica, Siberia).
- **-10°C to 0°C** – Subarctic winter (e.g., Fairbanks, Canada; Novosibirsk, Russia).
- **-5°C to 5°C** – High-altitude tundra (e.g., Mount Everest base camp in winter).
- **0°C to 5°C** – Polar desert (e.g., parts of Greenland; some Arctic coastal regions).
- **-10°C to -2°C** – Alpine winter (e.g., Swiss Alps, Rocky Mountains).
- **-5°C to 0°C** – Coastal tundra (e.g., Iceland’s far north).

**Fahrenheit:**
- **-58°F to -40°F** – Extreme Arctic cold.
- **-4°F to 32°F** – Subarctic/subpolar frostbite risk.
- **32°F to 41°F** – Glacier ice shelf or high-altitude frozen lakes.
- **32°F to 50°F** – Coastal polar desert (e.g., wind-chill amplified).
- **-4°F to 14°F** – Alpine snowy meadows (below treeline).
- **0°F to 32°F** – Northern tundra (e.g., Canada’s Northwest Territories).

**Key characteristics of such places:**
- **Permafrost** dominates soil structure.
- **Ice-capped or snow-covered** year-round (except brief summer melt).
- **Low biodiversity**, dominated by hardy plants (e.g., mosses, lichens).
- **Wind-chill** exacerbates cold; temperatures may feel **-30°C/-50°F** with gusts.
- **Extreme seasonal contrast**: Summer maxes at **5°C/41°F** (briefly).
- **Few human settlements** except for research stations or Indigenous communities.
- **Water sources** are ice-locked or frozen rivers/lakes (e.g., Lake Baikal in winter).
- **Animal life**: Arctic foxes, snow geese, or polar bears in summer; ice seals in coastal areas.

**Examples by region:**
- **Antarctica’s interior**: **-60°C (-76°F)** year-round.
- **Greenland’s inland**: **-30°C (-22°F)** in winter.
- **Alaska’s interior**: **-20°C (-4°F)** in winter.
- **Svalbard (Norway)**: **-10°C (14°F)** winter, **5°C (41°F)** summer.
- **Northern Siberia**: **-25°C (-13°F)** in winter.
