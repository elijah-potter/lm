The weight of silence presses like a coat too tight,
each breath a whisper lost in the hollow of time.
I trace the edges of the moon—sharp as a blade—
against the fabric of the night, where stars
unfold like pages from an old, unread book.
The earth is a wound, and the sky a stitch
holding it together, though the thread is thin.

A child’s laughter, caught in the wind,
drifts away like smoke through a cracked window.
The world does not remember. The world does not care.
Only the sky remains, vast and indifferent,
a canvas of void where the lost are erased
and the forgotten are written in ink that fades.

The horizon bends like a lover’s promise,
unfulfilled, undone. The air hums with the ghosts
of voices that never spoke, of hands that never held.
The stars are not just lights—they are echoes,
delayed, distorted, carrying nothing but
the silence of what could have been.

And then—
the sky, a vast, shifting tapestry of blues,
where the deepest indigo drowns in twilight’s sigh
and the gold of dawn is still dreaming its first sigh.
The clouds are not clouds but rivers of mist,
slowly unspooling into the endless blue.
Above, the first light touches the earth like a hand,
softening the edges of the world, of everything,
into something less sharp, less certain.
The day is born. The night is learning to let go.
