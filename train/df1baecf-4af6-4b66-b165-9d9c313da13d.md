A clock struck six. The old brass bell ticked once against the cold wind as Elias pushed open the door of the bakery, his fingers numb. Inside, the scent of fresh bread and sugar caramelized with cinnamon clung to the air like a second skin. The counter glowed under the dim light of a single bulb, casting long shadows.

A woman in a frayed apron turned, wiping her hands on a cloth. "Late again?" she asked, voice rough as gravel.

Elias exhaled, rubbing his temples. "Five minutes."

She smirked. "Same as always. But today’s bread’s better." She tossed him a loaf wrapped in wax paper, the crust golden, the crumb soft. "For the road."

Outside, the city hummed—a distant, rhythmic pulse. Elias took a bite, the warmth melting his jaw. The last of his coin was in his pocket, just enough for a train ticket. He pocketed the bread, the loaf heavy in his hand.

The train arrived at seven.
