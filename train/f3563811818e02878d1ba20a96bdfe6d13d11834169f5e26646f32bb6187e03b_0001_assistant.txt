Exploration of the Bunny Ears Method of Shoe Tetherage

Peter
Aged 4
Sandbox College at Oxford Park, NC

Hypothesis

A pair of loops, hereafter referred to as "bunny ears", when crossed at a common point and pulled taught, might affix a shoe to its appropriate foot for a period of up to twelve hours, or until manually released.

Materials and Methods

One pair of tennis shoes - Laced
One pair of tennis shoes - Velcro (Control)
Twelve-Inch nylon braided shoe lace - Brown
Two feet.

We will run six trials, three with the laced shoe on the left foot and the velcroed shoe on the right, and three in the reverse configuration.  The velcro method has already demonstrated the ability to remain affixed to the foot for the assigned period and will serve as a control.  We will first form two bunny ears with our lace.  We will cross the lace at a point approximately centered on the foot.  We will pull the lace through the juncture point until it is tight.  We will proceed to walk around in our shoes and record whether the shoe becomes loose before the end of the day.

Results

For our three trials with the left shoe laced, the lace was successfully tied three times.  Twice it remained tied for the full day.  Once it was loosened on a branch.
For our three trials with the right shoe laced, the lace was successfully tied twice.  Once it was pulled loose during the attempt to tighten the knot.  Both times the knot lasted for the full day.
All six trials with the velcro remained tightened for the full day.

Possible Sources of Error

One possible source of error is external debris.  Another possible source of error is human error resulting from a lack of finger dexterity.

Conclusion

The Bunny Ears method shows some promise, successfully attaching a shoe to the appropriate foot for the full duration of one day on four out of six trials.  It still did not achieve the same efficiency as the Velcro method.  More testing is needed.
