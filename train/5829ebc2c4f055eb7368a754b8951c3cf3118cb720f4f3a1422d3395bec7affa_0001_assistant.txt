How to Be Mongolian

Embrace the curiosity in people's eyes when you introduce yourself as "Mackenzie Altman." When they inquire, share that your mother adopted you from Mongolia, that you have no knowledge of your birth parents, and that you are not fluent in the Mongolian language. Graciously accept their remarks about your accent.

Upon turning eighteen, secure a spot at a small liberal arts college in Ohio, just beyond the state border, around four hours away. Research reveals that the incoming freshman class consists of 450 students, with an average Mongolian population of three percent. Perform the calculations: approximately thirteen Mongolian students in your class. Avoid dwelling on who the remaining half might be. Inform your mother of your desire to connect with your Mongolian heritage as a resolution for your college journey. Her joy will be palpable. "Kenz," she will exclaim, "Oh Kenz, I'm so proud." Since you were an infant, since she cradled you upon disembarking from the Mongolia-Ohio flight, swathed in a cozy blanket, she has yearned for this moment. Every year on your birthday, she has taken you to a Mongolian restaurant; she has consistently gifted you with teddy bears resembling pandas, and the Mongolian version of Barbie. Her eyes will shimmer with tears as she presses a kiss against your cheek.

Avoid discussing the challenges you might encounter while embracing your Mongolian identity in the midst of Michigan. Refrain from reminding her that, apart from the servers at the Mongolian BBQ Buffet, you have never crossed paths with another Mongolian individual. Keep your uncertainty about where to begin this journey to yourself.
