The Grand Clockmaker’s Apprentice
At the edge of Eldermere, where the mist clung to the cobbled streets like forgotten threads, there stood an old clock tower—its iron face etched with runes of forgotten time. Inside, a boy named Tali found it abandoned, its gears rusted, hands frozen at twelve. He touched the rust and whispered, *"Tell me why you stop."* The moment his voice reached the tower’s hollow, the hands began to move again—each tick and toll a whisper from a dying city.

The tower’s heart was a paradox: it counted backward. Every hour, it erased a memory from the world. Tali, desperate to save the forgotten stories of his village, pressed his ear to the bell. The clock chimed once, then twice—then the city’s echoes returned: the laughter of children who had never grown old, the last words of the blind poet who had sung of the sun setting in the north, the sigh of the wind that carried the scent of lavender from the gardens where the roses never bloomed. By midnight, the tower had consumed all of Eldermere’s past. The clock stopped again, silent.

The next morning, Tali woke in the ruins of his village, but the air smelled of blossoms. A stranger handed him a single rose—its petals shimmered with the light of stars he’d never seen. *"The clock doesn’t erase,"* the stranger said. *"It only asks."* The clock tower stood in ruins, its gears still whispering, but the hands now pointed to the future. Somewhere, a new story was beginning.
