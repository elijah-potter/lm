Philip Ashton, a fisherman from Massachusetts, was captured by the infamous pirate Edward Low in 1722 after refusing to join their ranks. For nine months, Ashton served as a slave while the pirates pillaged across the Atlantic and along the coasts of Brazil and Central America. In March 1723, he managed to escape when the pirates stopped for water on a small island off the coast of Honduras. Stranded and alone, Ashton constructed a shelter and survived on fruit and turtle eggs. In November 1723, he encountered a British man who soon disappeared but left behind essential supplies. Ashton endured months of extreme conditions, including heat, insects, snakes, hunger, fever, and an attack by the Spanish, until he was rescued by a British ship in June 1724. He later wrote a popular book recounting his harrowing experiences with Low's pirates and his 16-month period as a castaway.
