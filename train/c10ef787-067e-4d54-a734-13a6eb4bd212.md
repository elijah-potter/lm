## A Counseling Session

**Setting:** A comfortable, warmly lit therapy office. Soft music plays quietly in the background.

**Characters:**

*   **Dr. Eleanor Vance:** A seasoned therapist, known for her calm demeanor and insightful guidance.
*   **Liam Walker:** A young professional, experiencing significant stress and difficulty sleeping.

---

**Liam:** (Sighs, looking down at his hands) It's just… a lot, you know? I feel like I'm constantly chasing something I can’t quite reach.

**Dr. Vance:** (Nods gently) I understand. It sounds incredibly draining. Can you tell me a little more about what "a lot" entails for you, Liam?

**Liam:** Work, mostly. Deadlines, expectations… my boss seems to think I have unlimited time and energy. And then there’s the pressure I put on myself. I always feel like I need to be doing *more*.

**Dr. Vance:** That drive to excel is admirable, Liam, but it sounds like it’s taken a toll. You mentioned difficulty sleeping. Is that a recent development?

**Liam:** It’s been getting worse over the last few months. I lie in bed, my mind racing. I analyze every email I sent, every meeting I had. I just can’t switch off. I’m exhausted, but wired.

**Dr. Vance:** It's a common response to prolonged stress, Liam. Your body is trying to tell you something. It’s signaling a need for balance. You're essentially keeping yourself in a state of hyper-vigilance. Can you identify any specific situations at work that trigger this feeling of overwhelm?

**Liam:** There's this project, the Henderson account. It's high profile, a lot of responsibility. My boss keeps adding layers to it, shifting priorities. I feel like I’m constantly firefighting.

**Dr. Vance:** So, the shifting priorities and increased responsibility are significant stressors. It sounds like you’re experiencing a lack of control in a situation that feels very important. It’s natural to feel anxious when you perceive a lack of control. Have you attempted to communicate these concerns to your boss?

**Liam:** I’ve tried, a couple of times. But I worry about seeming incompetent or uncommitted. I don't want to damage my reputation.

**Dr. Vance:** It's understandable to be concerned about professional perception. However, maintaining your well-being is paramount. It sounds like you're prioritizing your boss's perception over your own needs, which isn’t sustainable. Perhaps we can explore some strategies for assertive communication. It’s not about being confrontational, but about clearly stating your limitations and needs in a respectful manner.

**Liam:** That… that makes sense. I just don't know *how* to do that. I’m not good at saying no.

**Dr. Vance:** That’s something we can definitely work on. It’s a skill, like any other. We can practice role-playing scenarios, so you feel more comfortable setting boundaries. For now, let's focus on understanding the root of that difficulty. Where do you think that reluctance to say no stems from?

**Liam:** (Pauses, considering) I think… I think I’m afraid of disappointing people. I always want to be seen as helpful and reliable.

**Dr. Vance:** That’s a very generous impulse, Liam. However, consistently sacrificing your own needs to please others can lead to resentment and burnout. It’s important to recognize that you’re not responsible for other people’s expectations. We can explore the origins of that need for approval, and how it impacts your current situation. In the meantime, I’m assigning you a small exercise. Each day, identify one small task or commitment you can realistically decline or delegate. It’s a starting point for regaining a sense of control. How does that feel to you?

**Liam:** (Takes a deep breath) It feels… a little daunting, but also… hopeful. Thank you.

**Dr. Vance:** You’re very welcome, Liam. Remember, this is a journey, and I’m here to support you every step of the way. We’re focusing on building resilience and finding a more sustainable approach to managing your responsibilities. Let's schedule our next session for the same time next week, and we can discuss your progress with that exercise.
