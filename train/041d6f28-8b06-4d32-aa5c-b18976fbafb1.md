A flicker of tension crossed his shoulders as the phone buzzed—an unread message from his partner. He ignored it, but the vibration on his wrist told him he shouldn’t. The clipboard in his hands trembled slightly as he glanced at the half-finished schematic on the table, the ink still damp from the pen he’d been using for hours.

*"Last-minute change. Cancel the extraction."* The words were short, clipped, the kind of message that didn’t need explanation. His stomach tightened. The plan had been running on autopilot for weeks, but this was different. This felt like betrayal. Or worse, like he’d been set up.

He exhaled sharply, the breath steadying his hands as he reached for his jacket. The door creaked open before he could knock—his boss stood there, arms crossed, eyes sharp. *"You’re leaving. Now."* The words were clipped, but the implication was clear. The operation was over. His part was done. The team was being reassigned. The city would soon know the truth.

The elevator doors closed with a hiss as he stepped inside, the weight of the decision pressing down on him. He hadn’t expected this. Not today. Not like this.
