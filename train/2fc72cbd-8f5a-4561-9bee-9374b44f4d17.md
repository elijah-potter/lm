In the neon glow of a Tokyo subway tunnel, a man—mid-40s, hands calloused from years of typing—slipped his phone into his pocket. The train’s doors hissed open, and he stepped onto a platform where a woman in a designer coat leaned against a pillar, watching him with eyes like polished obsidian. She said nothing, just tilted her head.

The train lurched forward. Outside, the city pulsed—headlights, sirens, the distant hum of a crowd. His watch chimed 5:37. The woman’s gaze locked onto his phone screen: a single tweet from his last job, *"Team, last-minute change. Be at 6:00 sharp."* She didn’t move as the train sped away, but her fingers traced the edge of her coat like she was memorizing his silhouette.

At the station, he exhaled. The woman was gone. His phone buzzed—an email: *"Urgent. Meet me at the bridge at 5:59."* He ignored it. Instead, he stepped into the rain, his boots splashing against puddles, and watched the city lights blur into streaks of gold as the minute turned into two.
