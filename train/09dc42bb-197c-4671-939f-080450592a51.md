In the hollowed-out shell of an abandoned cathedral, where stained-glass windows long since shattered like the dreams of a forgotten age, the last candle flickered against the damp stone. The air smelled of damp earth and something older—burnt incense and the faintest trace of iron, as if the very air had once been a battlefield.

Lena adjusted her gloves, her breath visible in the cold. The heavy wooden door groaned as she pushed it open, the hinges screaming like a wounded animal. Beyond the threshold, the grand nave stretched like a wound in the sky, its vaulted ceilings supported by skeletal columns that whispered secrets in the wind.

She stepped inside, her boots sinking into the crumbling floor. The cathedral was silent—too silent. Then, a rustle. Not from the shadows. From *her* coat.

A figure stood at the altar, wrapped in a tattered cloak, their face half-hidden beneath a hood. They moved slowly, deliberately, as if studying the room before them.

"Late," they said, their voice like gravel and honey. "But the stars are right."

Lena’s pulse quickened. The stranger didn’t turn. The cathedral, for all its decay, was still a stage. And tonight, the performance began.
