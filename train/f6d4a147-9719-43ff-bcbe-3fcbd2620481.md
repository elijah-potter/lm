A dimly lit, mist-shrouded alleyway glows faintly under a single flickering streetlamp, casting long shadows across weathered brickwork. The air hums with the distant, rhythmic drip of water—somewhere above, a gutter leaks into the pavement. A loose cobblestone shifts beneath a boot, revealing an old, half-buried plaque with faded inscriptions: *"Here stood the old mill, 1872"*. The scent of damp earth and aged wood clings to the air, thick with the metallic tang of rust. A rusted metal gate, half-sunken in the ground, hangs open, its hinges groaning in the wind. The street beyond is lined with sagging, ivy-covered buildings, their windows dark except for a single, warm glow in a third-story window, hinting at life within. The silence is only broken by the occasional crunch of footsteps on loose gravel, and the occasional, eerie whisper of wind through the alley’s narrow gaps. The absence of movement tells more than any action ever could.
