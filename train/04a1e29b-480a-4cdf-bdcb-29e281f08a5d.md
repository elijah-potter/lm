The first time Mia saw the strange lights in her backyard, she thought it was just the old oak tree flickering in the wind. But then the shadows moved. Not like anything alive—more like they were *waiting*.

Her mom had warned her about the old man who lived across the street, the one who never came out at night. He’d been gone for years, but the rumors said he’d left something behind. Something that called to people like Mia.

One evening, after school, she found a key taped to the back door. It wasn’t hers. Her mom had lost it at the market months ago. The key was rusted, but it fit the old shed behind the house. Inside, she found a half-empty bottle of something dark and sticky, and a note scrawled in what looked like her own handwriting: *"Find the box. It’s under the floor."*

The next day, her phone buzzed. A text from an unknown number: *"You shouldn’t have touched it."* She deleted it, but the next night, the lights flickered again. This time, the shadows *twisted*, forming shapes—her little brother, her grandpa, even a girl she’d never met, all whispering the same words: *"The box is ours."*

Mia’s dad found the note the next morning. He was a detective, and he knew the kind of things that happened when people dug too deep. He told her to stay away, but Mia couldn’t. She kept finding clues: a hidden room in her attic, a map drawn on her grandpa’s old notebook, a single word repeated in every message—*"Lily."*

Then the lights stopped. The shadows vanished. But the next morning, her backpack was missing. Inside, wrapped in her old sweater, was a small wooden box. When she opened it, she found a single silver locket. Inside was a picture of a girl who looked just like her. The words on the back read: *"You were always meant to find us."*

The police said it was a prank. Her dad said it was over. But Mia knew different. She followed the clues to an abandoned factory in the woods, where the girl from the picture stood waiting. The girl’s name was Lily. She’d been there for years, trapped in a world that wasn’t hers. And now, Mia had a choice: run away with her, or leave her behind.

The box was heavy in her hands. She could feel something inside it moving, humming. The locket pulsed. The shadows stretched, forming shapes again—this time, *them*.

Lily’s voice whispered in her ear: *"What do you think you’re doing?"*

Mia looked down at the box. And for the first time, she wondered if she was the one who was supposed to be found.
