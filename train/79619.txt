Written by Nancy Dunn Thompson, Ph.D.
Falling can be a significant fear among older adults and those who love them. There’s a reason. Falls are the leading cause of traumatic brain injury (TBI), and those 75 years and older are at risk for higher rates of hospitalizations and death after a fall.
How can a fall be prevented? Begin by making the home or surroundings safer since about half of all falls occur in the home. Clear pathways. Use anti-skid material on rugs. Put mats in the shower and tub. Closely evaluate the home. Wear shoes with traction soles. Exercise. Exercise can improve balance and coordination. Get your vision checked. Review all the medication (including over the counter meds) and supplements. with your physician. Know the side effects and note any changes such as drowsiness or light-headedness.
Learning about TBI can help you enhance the care you provide for the older adult you love. It can also make your life safer. What you learn can help your loved one and you avoid a fall and thus stay independent longer and live a better life.
To learn more about TBI, visit this CDC website Centers for Disease Control and Prevention.
