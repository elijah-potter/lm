The first time Mia saw her own reflection in the cracked glass of the old mirror, she thought it was a trick of the light. But this time, it wasn’t just her eyes—it was the way her reflection’s lips moved, whispering something she couldn’t hear. Her stomach twisted. *What if I’m not the only one who sees it?*

She shoved the mirror aside and stormed into the kitchen, where her little brother, Leo, was already at the table, scribbling in a notebook. "You scared me," he said without looking up. "What’s wrong?"

Mia sat down hard. "Nothing." She grabbed a slice of pizza from the fridge and stuffed it in her mouth, but her taste buds betrayed her—the first bite tasted like dirt. Leo’s eyes widened. "You okay?"

She forced a laugh. "Yeah, just hungry." But her hands shook as she wiped her mouth.

That night, Mia found Leo’s notebook open on his bed. The pages were filled with sketches of her face—twisted, unrecognizable. At the bottom, in bold letters, it read: *"She’s not you."* Her breath hitched. She snatched the notebook and hurled it across the room, shattering it on the wall. Leo’s voice came from downstairs, muffled. "Mia? You okay?"

She didn’t answer. She knew he’d follow, and when he did, he’d see.

---

The next morning, Mia woke to the sound of her name. "Mia!" Leo’s voice was urgent. She grabbed her backpack, ignoring the way her fingers trembled. Outside, the school bus was already parked in front of their house. As she climbed on, her reflection stared back at her from the window. This time, she *heard* it.

*"You don’t belong here."*

The bus driver turned. "You alright, kid?"

Mia’s voice was small. "Yeah."

Leo was already inside, whispering to his friends, but none of them saw the way she stared at her own shadow on the window.

---

In the science lab, Mia sat alone at a table, her notebook open. She’d been copying equations from the board—*x + y = z*—but her mind was elsewhere. She’d been told to leave. The principal had called her into his office, hands shaking, and told her to stay home. But she’d stayed, just in case.

Leo’s voice cut through the quiet. "You’re not doing that again, right?"

She snapped the notebook shut. "What?"

"You know what," he said, pointing at her. "The way you’re looking at things."

Mia’s pulse pounded in her ears. "I’m just… thinking."

Leo’s eyes were serious. "Then think about this. What if you’re not the only one who sees the same thing?"

Mia’s stomach dropped. "What?"

Leo didn’t answer. He just looked at her like she was something broken.

---

The next day, Mia found a note in her locker: *"Meet me at the old mill. After lunch."* Her heart raced. Who would dare send her a note like that?

She hurried to the mill, the wind whipping her hair. The place was abandoned, the doors hanging crookedly. Inside, the air was thick with dust. She found a child’s drawing on the wall—a girl with her own eyes, but her mouth stretched too wide. Her breath caught.

Then she saw him. A boy in a hoodie, sitting at a desk, sketching something in a notebook. He looked up, and his eyes locked onto hers. *"You’re not the only one,"* he said.

Mia’s voice was a whisper. "How do you know?"

He hesitated. *"Because I see it too."*

Mia’s hands clenched. "What do you see?"

He leaned in. *"You’re not her."*

The words echoed in her skull. She turned and ran, her sneakers slapping against the cobblestones. Behind her, she heard laughter—too loud, too cheerful. She didn’t look back.

---

That night, Mia sat on the edge of her bed, staring at her reflection in the bathroom mirror. The girl staring back wasn’t her. Her mouth was too big, her eyes too bright. She pressed her fingers to her temples, trying to push away the pain. But it didn’t work.

Leo’s voice cut through the darkness. "Mia? You in there?"

She didn’t answer. She just watched as her reflection’s lips moved again, whispering something she couldn’t hear.

*"You’re not the only one."*

Leo’s voice was softer. "What’s wrong?"

She opened her eyes. "Nothing."

Leo was already gone. The house was quiet. But she knew he was still watching.

---

The next morning, Mia found an envelope tucked under her door. Inside was a single photo—a girl with her own eyes, but her mouth stretched too wide. The words on the back read: *"Meet us at the river. After school."*

She didn’t hesitate. She grabbed her backpack and ran.

The river was cold, the water rushing against the rocks. She found them there—a group of kids, all whispering, all looking at her like she was something strange. One of them, a girl with dark curls, held out a sketch. It was a map. *"Follow this,"* she said.

Mia’s heart raced. "What is this?"

*"The way out,"* the girl said. *"But you’ll have to be brave."*

Mia swallowed hard. "Where does it lead?"

The girl just smiled. *"Somewhere you don’t belong."*

She took the sketch and ran, her feet pounding against the dirt. Behind her, she heard laughter—cheap, forced. She didn’t look back.

---

The path led to a cave, dark and damp. Inside, the walls were lined with sketches—faces like hers, mouths stretched too wide. A girl in the corner was drawing something in a notebook. When she saw Mia, she stopped. *"You’re late,"* she said.

Mia’s breath came in ragged gasps. "Who are you?"

The girl didn’t answer. She just pointed to the sketches. *"We’re all like you. We just don’t know it yet."*

Mia’s hands trembled. "What do you want from me?"

*"We want you to see,"* the girl said. *"And then you can decide what to do."*

Mia’s vision blurred. She looked around—at the faces, at the walls, at the sketches. She was not alone.

Leo’s voice echoed in her head. *"You’re not the only one who sees."*

She turned to the girl. "What do I do?"

The girl’s eyes were kind. *"Find your voice."*

Mia nodded, but her mind was racing. She was not just a girl with a secret. She was part of something bigger.

---

The next morning, Mia sat in the school cafeteria, staring at her reflection in the window. The girl staring back wasn’t her. She was different—stronger, wiser. She grabbed a pencil and wrote:

*"I see you."*

She folded the note and slipped it into her pocket. As she left the cafeteria, she smiled. For the first time in weeks, she felt like herself.

Leo was waiting for her outside. "You okay?"

Mia nodded. "Yeah."

Leo’s eyes were serious. "What happened?"

Mia hesitated. Then she pulled out the note. "I found something."

Leo took it, read it, and looked up. "You’re not alone."

Mia’s smile was real. "No, I’m not."

Leo grinned. "Good. Because I don’t want to be the only one who sees."
