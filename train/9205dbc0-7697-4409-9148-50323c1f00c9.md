**BREAKING:** Mayor of Riverdale Announces New "Green Corridor Initiative" to Combat Urban Heat Island Effect
Riverdale’s mayor, Eleanor Hart, today unveiled a sweeping plan to transform the city’s downtown along the Old Riverwalk into a climate-resilient "Green Corridor," integrating 15 acres of native vegetation, solar-paneled walkways, and water-retention ponds to combat extreme heat and improve air quality. The initiative, funded by a $12 million grant from the Federal Environmental Justice Office and a $8 million city bond, will replace asphalt with porous pavements and introduce 300 shade trees, including rare species like the riverbank willow. Public feedback sessions began yesterday; Hart emphasized, "This isn’t just infrastructure—it’s a commitment to equity, where low-income neighborhoods will receive priority access to cooling stations and job training for local workers."

**Critics warn of delays:** While supporters see it as a model for other cities, the Riverdale United Chamber of Commerce called the timeline "ambitious" and urged faster approval of the land-use zoning changes. Meanwhile, construction is set to begin next month, with phased deployment of "cool canopies" over parking lots by summer. The city will monitor temperature reductions via IoT sensors, with results shared annually.

**Transportation note:** The Green Corridor will include a new bike lane network, but road closures may disrupt rush-hour traffic. Residents are advised to use the temporary shuttle routes (routes 72 and 74) until permanent pathways are installed. Full details: [Riverdale City Council website](http://riverdale.gov/heatmap).
