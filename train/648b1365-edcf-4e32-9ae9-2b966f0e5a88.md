A tiny compass needle, once humbly fixed in a wooden box, began to spin erratically at dawn. Its magnetic core, forged in forgotten laboratories, now pointed not at magnetic north but toward an unseen current—one that pulsed through the veins of forgotten cities. The box’s lid was etched with symbols no one recognized: spirals, fractals, and a single eye that glowed faintly in the dark.

Children found it in the attic, and soon, whispers spread. The needle’s tip traced strange paths on the floor, marking routes to abandoned libraries or bridges that didn’t exist until it pointed to them. Adults called it a relic, a curse, or a trick of the light. But the needle did not care. It was a thread, invisible to all but those who followed its pull—until the world itself began to hum in its wake.
