In the dim glow of a crackling fireplace, an old leather-bound journal lay open on a wooden table, its pages yellowed with time. The last entry read: *"Tomorrow, I’ll return the borrowed knife."* Beneath it, a faded pencil sketch of a crumbling village church, a single handwritten note beside it: *"Don’t forget."* Rain hammered against the window, a steady rhythm that matched the pulse of a memory the woman clutched in her hands—the scent of damp earth and woodsmoke, the weight of a promise made to a stranger who had once saved her from a wolf. The ink on the page blurred as she traced the words again, her fingers trembling. Outside, a clock struck midnight, and the village’s lanterns flickered, casting long shadows across the floor. The promise was not just a word; it was a chain she carried, one that linked her to the past and a future where the knife might still be needed.
