A dimly lit café at dawn, steam rising from empty cups. The barista, mid-30s, hums along to a radio that plays a song from 1999. Across the counter, an older man with a cigarette dangling from his lips studies a newspaper dated 2002. The café’s owner, a woman in her 60s, adjusts the thermostat, her fingers brushing a clock that reads 3:17 AM.

A child’s laughter echoes from the alley behind them—too high-pitched, too sharp for the time. The older man’s face twists. *"You shouldn’t have come back,"* he whispers to the café’s empty floor, his voice carrying a tone from another decade. The barista freezes, then turns, her eyes reflecting a memory no longer hers. The newspaper’s headline—*"Local Man Found Dead in River"*—feels like yesterday’s news. The café’s door creaks open again, and a woman in a dress from the 1970s steps inside, her hand resting on a camera with a strap that hasn’t been used in decades. The radio cuts to static. The café’s owner exhales, her breath fogging the glass. The past has already arrived.
