A **Midsummer’s Unremembered Rite** (*Förglädje* in Swedish folklore) was once a pagan celebration of the summer solstice, marking the longest day. Traditionally, it included torchlit processions, bonfires, and the crowning of a **May Queen**—a woman chosen for her beauty and fertility to symbolize the sun’s rebirth. Over time, the festival merged with Christian traditions, and its original nature faded from memory.

In rural communities, people still gathered to sing, dance, and share food, unaware that their customs once honored an ancient belief in the sun’s eternal cycle. The forgotten rituals—like burning effigies of winter or planting wheat in a circle—were replaced by modern festivities, erasing the original meaning.

By the late 20th century, only oral stories and faded records remained, preserving fragments of a lost tradition. The name *Förglädje* (from *förglädja*, "to forget") became synonymous with the event, though its true origins were lost to time.
