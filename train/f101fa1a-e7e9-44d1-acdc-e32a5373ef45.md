The garden at dusk was quiet, save for the rustle of leaves in the autumn breeze. Old Mr. Whitmore, his hands gnarled with age, knelt beside his prized peach tree, pruning with a knife that had seen better days. He hummed an old tune under his breath, the sound blending seamlessly with the distant chorus of crickets.

His youngest granddaughter, Mia, stood beside him, her fingers brushing the fruit’s faint blush of ripeness. She whispered, *"When do we eat?"* but her voice carried no urgency, only wonder.

Mr. Whitmore turned, his eyes soft. *"After the last star falls,"* he murmured, pressing a smooth peach into her palm.

Mia’s laughter echoed as she bit into the fruit, the juice spilling onto her chin—golden, perfect, and just a little *sour*. She had never tasted anything like it before.
