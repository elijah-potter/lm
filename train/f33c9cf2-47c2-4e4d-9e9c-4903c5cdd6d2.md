## Project Chimera: A Cooperative Research Game

**A Game for 2-4 Players | Ages 14+ | Playtime: 60-90 Minutes**

**Introduction**

Project Chimera casts you as a team of leading scientists and specialists tasked with containing a rapidly evolving, genetically engineered pathogen known as “Chimera.” Developed in a clandestine laboratory, Chimera’s unpredictable mutations pose an existential threat to global stability. You must work together, allocating resources, conducting research, and deploying containment measures to prevent a catastrophic pandemic. Success hinges on collaboration, strategic prioritization, and the acceptance of difficult choices under immense pressure.



### Components

*   **Game Board:** Represents a global map divided into Regions, each with varying levels of vulnerability and resource availability.
*   **Chimera Cards (60):** Detail the pathogen’s mutations, escalating threat levels, and associated global effects.
*   **Research Cards (45):** Provide options for analyzing Chimera, developing countermeasures, and improving containment protocols.
*   **Resource Tokens (120):** Represent Funding, Personnel, and Equipment.
*   **Containment Markers (30):** Used to reduce Chimera’s spread in affected Regions.
*   **Event Cards (30):** Introduce unexpected challenges and opportunities.
*   **Scientist Pawns (4):** Represent the players' individual roles. Each pawn has a unique starting ability.
*   **Region Markers (20):** Indicate the severity of Chimera’s presence in each Region (Infected, Critical).
*   **Threat Tracker:** Measures the overall global threat level.
*   **Round Tracker:** Indicates the current game round.
*   **First Player Token:** Passes clockwise each round.



### Setup

1.  Place the Game Board in the center of the playing area.
2.  Shuffle the Chimera Cards and place them face down to form the Chimera Deck. Reveal the top three Chimera Cards and place them face up next to the deck; these represent the current mutations.
3.  Shuffle the Research Cards and place them face down to form the Research Deck. Reveal the top three Research Cards and place them face up next to the deck.
4.  Place the Threat Tracker at its starting position (level 3).
5.  Place the Round Tracker at round 1.
6.  Distribute Resource Tokens: Each player receives 3 Funding, 2 Personnel, and 1 Equipment.
7.  Each player chooses a Scientist Pawn and places it on the designated starting Region on the Game Board.
8.  Place one Region Marker (Infected) on three randomly selected Regions.
9.  Determine the first player and give them the First Player Token.



### Turn Structure

Each round consists of the following phases, completed in order:

**1. Chimera Mutation Phase:** Draw one Chimera Card and place it face up next to the Chimera Deck. Immediately resolve its effects. These effects typically increase the Threat Level, spread Chimera to new Regions, or introduce negative global consequences.

**2. Player Action Phase:** Starting with the first player and proceeding clockwise, each player takes two actions. Available actions include:

    *   **Move:** Move your Scientist Pawn to an adjacent Region.
    *   **Research:** Spend 2 Funding to draw two Research Cards, choose one to keep, and discard the other.
    *   **Analyze Chimera:** Spend 1 Personnel and 1 Funding to discard one of the face-up Chimera Cards and draw a new one. *Trade-off Mechanic 1: This action can be crucial for mitigating escalating threats, but it delays addressing existing Chimera mutations.*
    *   **Containment:** Spend 1 Personnel and 1 Equipment to place a Containment Marker in your current Region. This reduces Chimera’s spread in that Region.
    *   **Develop Countermeasure:** Spend Funding and Personnel as indicated on a Research Card to acquire its benefits. Some Countermeasures require specific Chimera mutations to be present.
    *   **Resource Allocation:** Transfer one Resource Token (Funding, Personnel, or Equipment) to another player in the same Region.

**3. Event Phase:** Draw one Event Card and resolve its effects. These cards introduce unpredictable challenges and opportunities, often requiring immediate player decisions.

**4. Round End Phase:** Pass the First Player Token clockwise. Advance the Round Tracker.



### Win and Loss Conditions

**Winning the Game:** You win the game if you successfully develop a permanent cure for Chimera (as indicated on a specific Research Card) before the Threat Tracker reaches its maximum level.

**Losing the Game:** You lose the game if the Threat Tracker reaches its maximum level at any point during the game.



### Advanced Variants

**1. Cascading Failures:** Introduce a "Cascade Die." After each Chimera Mutation Phase, roll the Cascade Die. On a result of 1 or 2, an additional Chimera effect is triggered based on the current Threat Level. This mechanic increases the game's volatility and demands more reactive strategies. *Trade-off Mechanic 2: This increases the difficulty and requires players to prioritize immediate responses over long-term research.*

**2. Limited Communication:** Restrict verbal communication between players during the Player Action Phase. Players can only indicate actions using pre-determined gestures or written notes. This simulates the pressure of crisis management and forces players to rely on non-verbal cues.

**3. Specialized Roles:** Assign each player a unique, asymmetric role with specific starting resources and abilities. This encourages deeper strategic planning and reinforces the importance of team interdependence. *Trade-off Mechanic 3: Players must weigh their individual strengths against the overall team needs, potentially foregoing personal advantages for the greater good.*



### Difficulty Scaling

*   **Two Players:** Begin the game with the Threat Tracker at level 2.
*   **Three Players:** Play the game as described in the core rules.
*   **Four Players:** Begin the game with the Threat Tracker at level 4 and use the Cascading Failures variant.



### Designer’s Note

Project Chimera was designed to be more than just a game. It aims to foster an appreciation for scientific collaboration, risk assessment, and the complex ethical considerations inherent in crisis response. The mechanics are intended to simulate the difficult choices scientists face when confronted with rapidly evolving threats, emphasizing the importance of communication, strategic prioritization, and the acceptance of imperfect solutions. The game’s educational goals include promoting critical thinking, teamwork, and a deeper understanding of the challenges involved in addressing global health emergencies.
