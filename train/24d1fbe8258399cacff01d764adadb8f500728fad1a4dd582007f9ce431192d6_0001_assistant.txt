Marguerite de la Rocque, a French noblewoman of the 16th century, endured a harrowing two-year ordeal of being marooned on an island off the coast of Quebec. Her misfortune began when she embarked on a voyage to establish a colony in Canada in 1542, accompanied by a relative. During the journey, Marguerite engaged in a scandalous affair with a fellow passenger, resulting in their banishment to the remote Isle of Demons near the Gulf of Saint Lawrence.

With limited resources, they constructed a small hut to shelter themselves from the harsh elements and relied on muskets and rocks to ward off wild animals. Marguerite faced the additional challenge of giving birth to a child on the island, miraculously surviving the ordeal. Tragically, her lover and servant succumbed to the hardships, and her young child soon followed. Despite the heartbreak and isolation, Marguerite persevered alone until her rescue in late 1544 by a group of fishermen who brought her back to Europe.
