Parmesan cheese, which is more accurately named Parmigiano Reggiano, was first made by monks in the  Parma, Reggio Emilia, Modena, Bologna, and Mantova provinces of Italy as these religious orders began to revitalize the land left abandoned by the Roman occupation. Eventually, these medieval monks had an excess of milk they needed to store before it spoiled. Thus, Parmesan cheese was invented around the mid-1200s. However, how people define parmesan cheese differs by geographical location. In Italy, Parmesan Reggiano must be aged for over a year, made with unprocessed milk, and grated before consumption. However, 'parmesan cheese' may refer to any hard, shredded Italian cheese in America.
