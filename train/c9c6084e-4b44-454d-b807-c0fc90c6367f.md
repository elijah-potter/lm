A dimly lit interrogation room. The fluorescent lights buzz harshly. The interrogator’s face is a shadowed mask behind a desk. The candidate sits straighter, fingers tightening around the edges of the chair, nails biting into palms.

"Explain yourself," the voice demands, low and deliberate.

The candidate clears their throat, voice steady but tight. "I—I was born in a small village near the coast. My father worked the fishing boats; my mother taught us to read by candlelight."

Pause. The interrogator leans forward, pen hovering over notepad.

"When I was fourteen, I joined the crew. The sea called. The storms were my teachers. I’ve seen things—men lost, ships foundering, the weight of the ocean that no one else can bear."

Another pause. The candidate’s hands tremble slightly.

"Then came the war. I didn’t fight. I watched. I carried supplies. I *remembered* what it was like to be hungry, to be afraid. When the war ended, I stayed. I learned to read maps, to navigate by the stars. Now I work the docks. Sometimes I help survivors. That’s why I’m here."

Silence. The interrogator doesn’t respond. The room feels heavy with unspoken questions.

Outside, waves crash against the pier. The tide rolls in.
