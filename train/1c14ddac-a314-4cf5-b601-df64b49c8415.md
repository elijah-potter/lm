In the quiet hum of the morning, as dust motes danced in the golden light filtering through the curtains, I noticed it again—the pattern. The way the shadows stretched, longer here, shorter there, always bending toward the east, like a silent promise.

The birds sang in unison, their notes a chorus of the same refrain, while the leaves rustled in a rhythm I’d memorized years ago. Each morning, the same: the first chirp at exactly seven minutes past seven, the cardinal’s trill a precise, unchanging thirty seconds before the robin’s.

I traced the pattern in my mind’s eye: the way my mother’s hands trembled just before she folded the last sheet of laundry, the exact moment she’d reach for the dishrag, her fingers brushing the edge of the counter where the salt was kept. The way her smile would flicker—just for a second—before she turned away, as if she were already thinking of the next thing.

Outside, the clock on the wall ticked like a metronome, and I counted the seconds until the pattern would repeat. The doorbell rang. The pattern was complete.
