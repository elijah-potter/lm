Revised draft of the scene:

---
The dim light from the overcast sky filtered through the window blinds, casting long shadows across the cluttered desk. **Ethan** sat hunched over a stack of yellowed pages, his fingers tracing the faded ink of the first draft of *The Hollow Crown*. A half-empty cup of black coffee sat beside him, its steam curling lazily.

A fresh draft lay spread across the desk: the skeleton of the story, now with the skeleton of a new twist—**the protagonist’s past betrayal unraveling in the present**. The villain’s motives had shifted subtly; the ending, once abrupt, now suggested a deeper, more tragic conclusion. Ethan’s jaw tightened as he re-read the lines aloud, his voice rough with the weight of each word.

His phone buzzed—a reminder for the deadline. He glanced at it, then back at the page. A pause. Then, a sigh.

He flipped to the last page, circled a passage, and began to underline. **"Not quite."** The words felt heavier now, more *real*. The story wasn’t just a plot anymore—it was a story about *fate*, about choices, about the way history bends you until you bend with it.

The door creaked open. His sister, **Lena**, stepped in, arms full of takeout. She set a tray of tacos on the counter. "You look like hell," she said, eyeing the coffee stain on his shirt. "Or like you’re finally done."

Ethan didn’t answer. He’d been staring at that last line for ten minutes. The story was done.
