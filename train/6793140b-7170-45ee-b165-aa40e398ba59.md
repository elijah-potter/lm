Politeness failed as the waiter’s fork clattered against the plate, sending a loud *clang* that echoed through the dimly lit restaurant. "Excuse me," the guest muttered, adjusting their tie, but the noise had already drowned out their apology. The server, mid-step, paused to glance over, their expression unreadable—blended between irritation and professional detachment.

The guest’s attempt at conciliation had been a miscalculation. Their polite phrase, *"I’m so sorry about that—it’s just the fork was really sharp!"*, was drowned out by the clatter, and the server’s response was a clipped, *"We’ll replace it,"* delivered with the precision of a surgeon, not a diplomat. The guest’s smile faltered mid-cheek, a performance they’d only learned to execute in high school plays. The awkward silence that followed was a silent judgment, a performance of their own failure to master the art of polite failure.
