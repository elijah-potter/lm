After milking a cow, the cream must be separated from the milk before the milk is used in cheesemaking. Once the milk is obtained, the process of making cheese can begin. First, the milk is taken, and based on the kind of cheese being made, a starter culture of bacteria is added in order to convert the lactose sugars in the cheese into lactic acid. After the bacteria has mixed with the milk, a curdling agent called rennet is added in order to cause the casein to curdle and separate from the water liquid known as whey. The curds are then separated and salt is added to help drain more whey before it is processed into its final form, which can range from being stretched and kneaded, to being pressed into a mold and aging. Cheddar, for example, is made by keeping no more than 39% moisture in it and aging it depending on the level of sharpness and hardness desired. To make mozzarella cheese, after the curds are formed, they are heated and kneaded together until it forms their signature elastic texture.
