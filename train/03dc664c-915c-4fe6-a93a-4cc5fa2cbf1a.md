A sudden knock at the door echoes through the quiet house, jolting the curtains to sway. The interior light flickers as the figure at the peephole—leaning slightly, arms crossed—steps back, revealing a worn leather jacket and the faint scent of rain. The doorknob turns with deliberate precision, and the door creaks open just enough to reveal a narrow gap.

From the threshold, a lantern casts long shadows, illuminating a man in a long coat, his face half-hidden by a knit cap. He adjusts his gloves, then steps inside, the door closing with a soft click behind him. The air inside is thick with the scent of old wood and something metallic, like old keys or a forgotten cigar. He doesn’t speak immediately, but his gaze lingers on the half-empty whiskey glass on the coffee table before him, as if the glass itself holds secrets.

“You’re not expecting anyone,” he observes, voice rough but measured.

A woman in an oversized sweater—her hands wrapped around a mug of tea—turns slowly, eyes narrowing. “I don’t *know* who you are.”

The lantern’s glow flickers again, casting his silhouette against the wall. He doesn’t move, doesn’t lower himself to the couch. The silence stretches, heavy with unspoken questions.

Then, the lantern snuffs out.
