The café was quiet except for the hum of the espresso machine, a low, steady rhythm like the pulse of a city that never slept. Outside, the rain tapped against the windows in a rhythm almost too familiar to ignore. I had come here for solitude, for the kind of quiet that lets the mind wander without fear of interruption. But today, the solitude had been too much.

I sat at the same table, the one with the cracked plate and the half-empty cup of tea I’d left there last time. My fingers traced the rim, remembering how it had felt in my grip, how the warmth of the liquid had seeped into my palm before it had cooled. I should have stayed. The book I’d brought—*The Weight of the World*—had been waiting for me, its pages heavy with thoughts I’d rather not face. But then the bell above the door chimed, and there stood her, the barista, her dark curls pulled into a messy bun, her hands busy with another order.

"You’re back," she said, not unkindly, but the words still stung. I nodded, turning my attention back to the book, but my mind wandered. The rain outside had stopped. The street below was slick with water, reflections of the neon signs dancing on the pavement. I should have left. The world was too bright, too loud, too alive. But then she came over with a coffee, the steam curling up like a question mark in the air.

I took it. The heat of the cup seeped into my fingers, and for a moment, I let myself forget. The pages turned, the words blurred, and suddenly, I knew what I wanted. Not just a book, not just a quiet place, but something else entirely. A change. Not in the way I thought, but in the way I felt.

I closed the book with a quiet click. The barista watched, her eyes soft. "You sure?" she asked.

I smiled. "Yes."
