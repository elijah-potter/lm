**Quantum Dot Cluster in a Semiconductor Nanowire**

At a wavelength of ~532 nm, the nanowire exhibits **coherent photon emission** with **spontaneous emission rates** inversely proportional to the **local density of states (LDOS)** near the surface. Fluorescence lifetime decays non-exponentially due to **non-Markovian environment-induced decoherence**, showing **sub-ps jitter** in emitted photons under high excitation. **Fano interference** causes intensity fluctuations with **statistical correlations** between photons, detectable via **second-order autocorrelation (g²(0))**, yielding **g²(0) ≈ 0.1** under low excitation, dropping to **g²(0) ≈ 0.2** at saturation. **Localized surface plasmon resonance (LSPR)** enhancement (~2.3×) at 780 nm shifts absorption peaks and modifies **photon emission spectra** via **radiative coupling**, producing **sideband structures** in photoluminescence. Temperature-dependent shifts in emission maxima (~0.5 nm/°C) reflect **bandgap renormalization** in the semiconductor core. **Photon antibunching** (g²(0) ≈ 0.3) confirms single-photon emission, while **stimulated emission suppression** reveals **population inversion** under pulsed excitation. **Nonlinear optical responses** show **two-photon absorption (TPA) cross-section** > 10⁻⁵ cm/GW, enabling **harmonic generation** at 1.06 µm with ~5% efficiency. **Thermal noise** in detectors induces **false single-photon events** (g²(0) ≈ 0.8) at high excitation, requiring **correlated detection** for accurate analysis.
