Lost to the sands of time, once stood a village where the wind sang through the skeletal remains of its past. The **Blackthorn Hollow** stood as a testament to forgotten legends, its stone walls carved with faded runes that whispered secrets to those who dared listen.

The **River of Echoes**, once wide and clear, now meanders through the ruins, its waters reflecting neither sky nor self. Fishermen used to cast their lines here, but the nets return empty—only the bones of forgotten fish and the ghosts of the past.

At its heart lies the **Hollow’s Heart**, a circular stone chamber where the elders gathered to share tales of the **Moonbound Tribe**, who once danced to the stars and spoke in languages that no longer exist. The chamber’s ceiling is cracked, the ceiling stones missing, revealing the void where the tribe’s last hearth once burned.

Around the hollow, the fields grow wild, overrun with thorned vines that twist like the fingers of the dead. The old well, cracked and moss-covered, still stands—though the water is no longer drinkable. Some say it still holds the last tears of the villagers who chose to drown their grief there.

The **Hollow’s Gate**, a massive oak door, lies half-buried in the earth. It was said to open only for those who carried a memory of the lost. Now, it is a hollow shell, its wood rotted, its hinges long since rusted away.

Visitors who wander here speak of figures in the mist—a woman with silver hair, a child with eyes like polished river stones, or a man who never ages, forever watching from the shadows. They vanish without a trace, leaving only whispers behind.

Some say the Hollow is not truly lost. That if you listen closely on a quiet night, you can hear the faint sound of children laughing, the murmur of voices, and the distant hum of a fire that has never gone out. Others claim it is a warning: that some things, once forgotten, never truly die—they only wait, hidden beneath the surface, ready to rise again.
