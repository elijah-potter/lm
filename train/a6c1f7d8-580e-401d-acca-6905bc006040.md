The room’s air hummed with an eerie stillness as the clock on the wall crept forward—each second a deliberate, dragging stroke. The air itself seemed to thicken, every breath a struggle, every step a deliberate, measured pause. The scent of old books and damp stone lingered, as if the world itself had paused to breathe. A child’s laughter echoed—too loud, too slow, like a recording played backward—before dissolving into a chorus of distant whispers.

Outside, the city seemed frozen. Cars’ headlights flickered, casting long, trembling shadows that stretched unnaturally across the pavement. A pigeon’s feather drifted lazily to the ground, suspended midair, as if time had coiled around it. The sun hung low, its light a dull, sickly orange, stretching the shadows into grotesque, elongated shapes.

The woman at the window pressed her forehead against the glass, her pulse a rapid, erratic thump against her skin. The world outside was no longer just a blur—it was a *memory*, every detail rendered in excruciating detail. The sound of a distant train whistle became a groan, the crunch of leaves beneath her boots a slow, deliberate crunch. The trees bent as if bowing under unseen weight, their branches twisting into shapes that defied geometry.

Her hands trembled. She could almost *feel* the seconds stretching like rubber, each moment stretched into something unnatural, something *wrong*. The world was not just slow—it was *stretched*, every inch of it, every breath, every heartbeat, a silent, aching drag.

Then—suddenly—the distortion snapped. The world snapped back, and she was gasping, her heart pounding as the world rushed back into its usual chaos. The clock ticked normally. The pigeon fluttered away. The city erupted into motion again, a blur of noise and color. She collapsed to her knees, her breath coming in ragged gasps, her mind still reeling from the horror of what she had almost felt.
