The clock struck midnight in the old observatory, its chimes ringing out like the last breath of a dying star. Dr. Elias Voss stood at the edge of the telescope’s field, his fingers tracing the cold metal rim. The sky above was a canvas of swirling purples and golds, where constellations shifted like liquid ink.

“Another year,” he muttered, though no one else was there to hear. He adjusted the lens, zooming in on a distant nebula, its edges flickering like embers. Then—nothing. Just static.

A whisper slithered from the void.

Elias turned. The door behind him stood slightly ajar, the hinges groaning as if in agreement. Inside, the shelves of his library were gone. Replaced by a single, gleaming orb, humming faintly. Its surface mirrored the last image he had seen: the nebula, but now it pulsed, alive.

He reached out. The moment his fingertips brushed its surface, the world dissolved.

---

The orb was warm. Inside, the observatory stood again, but the clock had stopped at noon. The telescope pointed toward the same nebula, but this time, it was *his*. Elias knelt, his bones aching as if he’d been asleep for decades. The orb glowed brighter, and the sky split open—not with stars, but with *letters*, swirling like wind through a field of wheat.

*Begin again.*

He exhaled. The orb pulsed. The letters rearranged.

**Ending as a beginning:**

**Begin at the edge of the universe, where light bends and time dissolves.** The first word you’ll read is the one you’ll need to carve into the bone of your next story: *"Static."* The second is *"Orb."* The third? *"Echo."* The nebula isn’t a place—it’s a door. And the key is what you *see* when the world ends. (But don’t look directly. The first line of your next chapter is already written in the void. Just find it.)
