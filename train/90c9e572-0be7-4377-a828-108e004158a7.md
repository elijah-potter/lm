When Maria first walked into the café, she was determined to stay away from coffee. Her doctor had advised her to cut back on caffeine, and she was determined to follow his advice. She had just finished her morning run, and she was feeling great—energized, focused, and ready to tackle her day.

But as she sat down at the counter, the aroma of freshly brewed coffee wafted through the air. It was so strong that her eyes watered. The barista, sensing her struggle, handed her a tiny, unsweetened coffee with just a hint of cinnamon. It was small, but it was enough to make her stomach growl.

Maria took a sip. The first few drops were strong, but the warmth and the subtle sweetness of the cinnamon made her stomach flutter. She took another sip, this time letting the steam rise to her face. It was rich, comforting, and unlike anything she’d tasted before. Her mind wandered to the days when she’d been a coffee addict, how she’d wake up feeling like she’d been running a marathon, and how she’d spend the rest of the day in a daze, only to crash hard at night.

She took another sip and realized she was smiling. She had been so focused on avoiding coffee, but this moment—this simple, warm cup of coffee—was exactly what she needed. She took another sip, then another, and slowly, the fear of caffeine withdrawal faded. The coffee wasn’t the thing she’d been avoiding; it was the habit, the routine, the way she’d trained herself to reject something that actually brought her comfort.

She ordered another one. Maybe this time, she’d treat herself to a proper coffee, with milk and sugar, and see how she felt. Maybe it wasn’t the caffeine she needed to reset; maybe it was the ritual, the pause, the chance to just *be* in the moment.

By the time she left the café, she wasn’t fighting the coffee anymore. She was embracing it, one sip at a time.
