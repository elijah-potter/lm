A dimly lit storage closet, half-hidden behind a curtain of dust and cobwebs. The air hums with the faint scent of old paper and mildew. Inside, the last box sits open, its contents spilling onto the floor: yellowed newspapers, a rusted key, and a single, battered photograph. A technician bends to pick up the photograph—a faded portrait of a young woman, her smile frozen in time. As they turn it over, the key catches the light, catching her eyes—*they’re the same*.

The technician’s phone buzzes. A notification flashes: *"Item found in storage: 'The Lost Letter.'"* Their hands tremble as they swipe to open it—an envelope, sealed with a wax stamp bearing the same initials as the photograph’s edge. The ink is smudged, but the words inside are clear: *"They’re coming for you. Find the clock."* The clock they’ve been searching for—one that doesn’t tick. The one buried beneath the old house on Blackwood Lane, where the woman vanished thirty years ago.
