The flickering emergency lights cast long shadows across the cramped apartment as the hum of the generator fades into a distant roar. Dust motes swirl in the dim glow of the single bulb above the kitchen counter, where a half-eaten sandwich sits forgotten. The air smells of burnt coffee from the espresso machine still warm on the counter.

Across the room, Maria clutches a laptop, its screen backlit by a single candle in a jar of water. Her fingers tremble as she taps the keyboard, the keys clicking erratically. The phone on the table buzzes intermittently, its signal flickering between static and a single, desperate call.

Outside, the streetlights flicker on and off like dying stars, the distant wail of sirens punctuating the silence. A storm rages—thunder crackles, and a sudden gust of wind rattles the windows, sending a loose picture frame crashing to the floor.

A knock at the door. Maria freezes. It’s not the neighbor who always knocks at midnight. The door creaks open, revealing the silhouette of a stranger in a soaked raincoat. They step inside, a flashlight in hand, its beam cutting through the gloom. Their voice is rough with exhaustion. "We need to leave. The storm’s coming."
