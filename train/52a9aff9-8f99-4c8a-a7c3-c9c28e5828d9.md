In the quiet of an autumn afternoon, when the world seems to hold its breath, the old clock tower stood sentinel over the town. Its face, once cracked with age, now displayed only the hour—twelve—and the hands had stopped moving, frozen in time like a painting left in the sun.

Beneath it, a small child with hands that trembled with curiosity picked up a smooth, silver coin from the cobblestones. The town’s silversmith, a man who had been dead for twenty years, had once given this child a similar coin, a gift meant to last a lifetime. The coin was warm in her palm, its surface etched with strange, spiraling designs that seemed to whisper secrets.

She turned it over, and the back was blank—not entirely, for a faint, glowing mark pulsed faintly beneath the surface, like a heartbeat. The mark was the same as the one on the clock’s face, though dimmer, as if it had been buried too long. The child realized then that time was not just passing; it was also *staring back*.

She pocketed the coin and ran, the streets echoing behind her. The town’s shadows stretched long, and for the first time, she noticed the clock tower’s shadow *moved*—slowly, deliberately—as if watching her approach. When she reached the edge of the village, she dropped the coin into the river, where it sank without a sound.

The river’s current carried it downstream, toward the place where the town’s wells fed into the river. There, the coin was absorbed by the earth, and the mark on its back faded into the stone. The clock tower’s hands, once still, began to move again—slowly at first, then faster, as if finally remembering what it was meant to do.

And somewhere, in the depths of the river, a single, silver coin glinted in the dark, waiting for someone to find it.
