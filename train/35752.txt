Researchers say the best way to deal with aggressive children is to be firm but loving, set clear limits and stick to them.
05 03 2008
It's easy to be the mum or dad of an angel. But consistent bad behaviour in a child, especially involving physical aggression, can bring out the worst, with you ending up ranting and raving.
But the evidence is that harsh discipline confirms in the child's mind that aggression is what adults do so it must be acceptable.
The parenting style which tends to work is firm but loving, setting clear limits and sticking to them, and when confronted with tantrums and aggression, distracting the child and praising them when they behave well.
A lot of the research in this area has been done in Australia and it's been tempting to teach parents this style. And in fact, such programs work when given to parents who are at high risk of having children with behaviour problems teenage single mothers for instance.
But what about giving a parenting program to all parents? Would that be of benefit?
Well, a trial by Melbourne researchers has found few benefits despite high participation rates. They think the real value may be in using these parenting programs to capture a high proportion of the population and allow a rational assessment of children, and then offer parents at risk more intensive assistance.
Author: Hiscock H, Bayer JK, Price A, Ukoumunne OC, Rogers S, Wake M. Universal parenting programme to prevent early childhood behavioural problems: cluster randomised trial.
2008 Feb 9;336(7639):318-21. Epub 2008 Jan 31
