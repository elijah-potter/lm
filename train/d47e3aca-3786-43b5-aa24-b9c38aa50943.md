Time stretched into a taut, unbroken continuum as the coffee brewed, its aroma thick and slow to diffuse. The morning sunlight, a lazy orb, spilled through the half-drawn blinds, painting the room in a golden blur—each second a half-second, the clock’s hands moving in reverse. The first sip tasted like a memory, bitter and deliberate, each drop lingering longer than it should, as if the body itself was resisting the rush of energy. Outside, the world buzzed in short, disjointed bursts: a car horn, a child laughing, the distant hum of a subway—each sound a fragment of a story unfolding, none of them quite making sense until later.

A half-smile tugged at the corners of her mouth. The morning had been a trick of perspective—every minute felt like an hour, every hour like a day. The moment dissolved like sugar in tea, leaving only the quiet certainty that time had been stretched, not passed.
