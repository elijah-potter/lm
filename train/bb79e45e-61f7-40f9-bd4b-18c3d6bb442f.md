The rain had turned the cobblestones into slick mirrors, reflecting the flickering neon glow of the city’s forgotten corners. Elias wiped his brow with the back of his sleeve, the fabric damp against his skin. His breath came in shallow gasps, each one carrying the scent of ozone and old paper. He had come here for the same reason every Tuesday: to find the last copy of *The Last Chronicle of the Hollow Earth*, a tome said to contain the secrets of a forgotten civilization buried beneath the city’s underbelly.

The door to the abandoned bookshop was slightly ajar, the hinges groaning like an old man’s cough. Inside, the air was thick with dust and the faintest hint of ink. The shelves, once full, now leaned precariously under the weight of time, their spines cracked and the pages of some books curled like dry leaves. Elias stepped inside, his boots crunching on shattered glass.

At the back of the shop, beneath a flickering lantern, he found it—a single, leather-bound volume, its cover stained with what looked like old blood. He exhaled sharply, the weight of the find pressing against his chest. The book was heavy, as if it had been waiting for him. He pulled it out, the pages whispering against his fingers like a secret whispered in the dark.

A sound. Not from the book. From the street outside.

Footsteps.

Elias froze, the lantern’s flame dancing in his peripheral vision. The sound grew louder, closer. Someone was coming. He had to move—now.

He grabbed the book, tucked it under his arm, and bolted for the door. The steps behind him were relentless, the rain slick underfoot. He didn’t look back. Didn’t dare. The door slammed shut behind him with a finality that echoed like a gunshot. The shop’s lantern flickered out, plunging him into darkness.

Outside, the city’s rhythm resumed—siren wails, distant laughter, the clatter of a street vendor’s cart. Elias pressed his back against the building, the book clutched to his chest like a shield. He had found what he came for, but now he had to decide: keep it, or let it go?

The steps stopped. He held his breath.

Then—nothing.

Silence.

Elias exhaled slowly, the weight of the book easing just a little. He turned away, the city stretching before him like an endless map. His fingers traced the spine of the book one last time before tucking it into his coat pocket.

The rain kept falling.
