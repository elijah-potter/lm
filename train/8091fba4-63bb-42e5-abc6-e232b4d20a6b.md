In a dimly lit interrogation room, the fluorescent lights buzzed weakly. The individual—tall, with a sharp jawline and eyes darting toward the ceiling—leaned forward, hands clasped tightly between knees. A recorder hummed in the corner, capturing every word.

"Listen," they said, voice low but steady. "I’m not here to lie. I just… needed to understand what they wanted to hear."

A guard cleared their throat. "You’re a former data scientist. You worked for a company that analyzed human behavior—what does that mean to you?"

Silence. The individual exhaled sharply, fingers tracing the rim of their cup of black coffee.

"People think I’m just… numbers. But I’m not. I’m someone who saw how systems treat people like data points. And then I did something stupid. I tried to fix it."

The recorder clicked on.
