AITA for insisting my sister pay for her daughter's wedding after promising to do so for fifteen years?

Okay, this is a long one, but I feel like I need to lay everything out. My sister, Eleanor, and I have always had a…complicated relationship. We’re five years apart, and while we were close as kids, adulthood brought a lot of friction. The core of it, I think, revolves around Eleanor's habit of making grand promises she doesn’t always keep.

It started back in 2008. Eleanor was 28, living in Denver, Colorado, and just starting her career as a graphic designer. Her daughter, Chloe, was then five years old. During a family dinner at my parents' house in Phoenix, Arizona, Eleanor, fueled by a bit too much wine, declared that she would pay for Chloe’s wedding when she got married. My parents, ever the optimists, encouraged her, and I, honestly, just rolled my eyes. It seemed like a typical Eleanor declaration at the time.

Fast forward to 2015. Chloe, now 15, was expressing interest in a very specific, and rather expensive, venue in Aspen, Colorado – The St. Regis. Eleanor, again, enthusiastically agreed that they could absolutely have it, reiterating her commitment to paying for the wedding. I remember specifically thinking, "This is going to be a disaster." I voiced my concerns to my mother, who dismissed them, saying Eleanor was “just being a supportive mom.”

Over the intervening years, Eleanor continued to make vague assurances. In 2019, when Chloe started seriously discussing wedding dates (she was 20), Eleanor began soliciting ideas and even started a separate savings account, ostensibly for the wedding. I asked her, in October 2019, to put her commitment in writing, a request she brushed off, claiming it was “unnecessary” and that she was “a woman of her word.”

Now, it’s June 2023. Chloe is 24, and the wedding is set for October 27th in Aspen. The total estimated cost, including venue, catering, photographer, and flowers, is $65,000. Eleanor has been increasingly stressed, and her contributions to the wedding fund have dwindled to almost nothing. Last week, she sat me down and, with tears in her eyes, confessed that she can’t afford to pay anything. She claims a series of unexpected expenses – a car repair, medical bills, a business downturn – have drained her finances. She now wants me, her younger brother, to cover the remaining $40,000.

I was, understandably, furious. I reminded her of her fifteen-year-old promise, the repeated assurances, and the fact that she actively encouraged Chloe to dream big. I pointed out that I have my own financial obligations – a mortgage, student loans, and a young family – and that I can’t simply conjure up that kind of money. She responded by accusing me of trying to “ruin her daughter’s dream” and being “selfish.” My parents are siding with Eleanor, saying I should be the “generous brother” and “make it happen.” Chloe is understandably distraught, caught between her mother’s broken promise and my refusal to foot the bill. I feel manipulated and resentful. I'm being asked to clean up a mess that Eleanor created. I offered to help Chloe find a more affordable venue and help her with other aspects of the wedding, but she is adamant about having the St. Regis.

My mother keeps saying I'm being unreasonable. My father is silent, but I can tell he feels conflicted. Eleanor is making me feel guilty and is portraying me as the villain. I feel like I’m being held hostage by a promise that should have been made with a bit more forethought. I am genuinely struggling to understand how I am being unreasonable in this situation. It seems pretty clear to me that Eleanor is ESH. I’m feeling incredibly frustrated and like I’m being taken advantage of.

AITA for refusing to pay for Chloe’s wedding after my sister promised to do so for fifteen years?
