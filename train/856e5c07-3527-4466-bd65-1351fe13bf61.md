The assembly line hums endlessly at the factory, each worker’s task a loop of repetitive motion: pick, place, repeat. The conveyor belt never stops, though the workers do—each cycle another item, another "perfect" product, another iteration of the same endless cycle. The foreman marks timecards with the same rhythm, counting hours as if they were a metronome, while the machines whisper promises of "eventually" completion, though "eventually" never arrives.

The warehouse holds stacks of unsorted boxes, each one marked with a label that reads **"UNFINISHED"**—yet the shelves are never cleared. The shipping department’s spreadsheet fills with the same line: **"TENTATIVE:** [Item Name] - [Due Date]". The due dates shift like waves, but the items remain.

A team of auditors reviews the same documentation over and over, cross-checking against the same flawed criteria, while the system logs the same errors: **"INCOMPLETE DATA"** or **"DEPENDENCY NOT MET"**. The cycle begins again, the same questions asked, the same answers returned—though no one ever finds the one thing missing.

The deadline looms, a fixed date on the calendar, but the work never quite reaches it. The team debates whether to call it "work in progress" or "unfinished business," but the truth is simpler: the task is a loop—endless, unbreakable, and always deferred to the next iteration. The clock ticks, the screens flicker, and the same question persists, unsolved: *When does it ever stop?*
