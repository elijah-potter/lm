A dimly lit office lobby hums with the soft drip of condensation from a ceiling fixture. The air smells faintly of stale coffee and old paper. A single lamp casts long shadows across the polished wooden floor. A lone figure stands in the center, arms crossed, their posture rigid—shoulders square, jaw clenched. The humming of the AC fades into static as the phone rings. No answer. Another ring. No response. The call ends abruptly.

Outside, a train whistle echoes down the tracks, a long, mournful wail that fades into the night. A clock ticks slowly, its hands moving in slow motion. The figure does not move. The weight of the silence presses down, thick and suffocating. A single door, slightly ajar, reveals a hallway that stretches into darkness. The only sound is the whisper of footsteps—no one there. Just the silence.
