**Mayor’s Office Announces New Sustainability Initiative in Eldermere**
Eldermere, 2024 – Mayor Elena Voss today unveiled a zero-waste pilot program in partnership with local businesses, targeting 80% food waste diversion by 2026. The initiative includes mandatory composting for all residential and commercial sectors, while a mobile food recovery truck will redistribute surplus produce to community gardens. Critics argue the plan lacks enforcement, but supporters praise its alignment with the city’s 2030 climate goals. Meanwhile, a new solar farm on the outskirts of downtown is expected to supply 15% of the city’s energy needs by next summer, with construction slated to begin next month.

**Protests Over Parking Metrics Spark Debate**
A new city ordinance mandating real-time parking data via mobile apps has sparked unrest in downtown Eldermere. Drivers claim the system unfairly targets high-income neighborhoods, while city officials insist it reduces congestion by 12%. Demonstrators blocked Broadway last week, demanding an audit of algorithm fairness. Mayor Voss has called for public hearings to address concerns, though some activists accuse the city of prioritizing data-driven efficiency over human equity.

**Local Tech Startup Secures $5M for AI-Powered Water Management**
Eldermere-based **HydroSense AI** has closed a $5 million round led by the city’s economic development fund. The startup’s platform uses machine learning to predict water usage spikes in real-time, aiming to cut waste by 30% within two years. The company plans to expand to neighboring towns if pilot results prove successful. Critics warn the deal may stifle competition, but HydroSense CEO Dr. Priya Kapoor insists the investment will position Eldermere as a leader in smart infrastructure.

**Archaeologists Unearth Pre-Colonial Burial Site**
Excavations near the old mill district have uncovered a 400-year-old burial mound with artifacts dating back to the 17th century. Local historians identify the site as part of the **Eldermere Confederacy**, though exact affiliations remain disputed. The city has agreed to fund further research while respecting Indigenous consultation protocols. A temporary fence has been erected around the site to prevent unauthorized access.
