The city glows at dawn like a fractal of neon—streets crisscrossing with golden veins of streetlights, pulsing like slow-burning embers. Bridges arch over the river, their lights refracting water into shimmering, fractured mirrors. High-rises cast long, jagged shadows, their windows a swath of static, flickering between sleep and wakefulness. Sidewalks are lined with flickering lanterns, their light bleeding into the pavement like slow oil. The sky, a vast, unlit canvas, contrasts with the artificial glow, its edges blurred by smog and the faintest hint of aurora from the north.
