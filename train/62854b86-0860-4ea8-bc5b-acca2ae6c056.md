The first time Liam saw the glowing orbs, he thought he was dreaming. They floated in the dark alley behind his house, pulsing like fireflies trapped in a glass bubble. His little brother, Jake, had just told him about his new science project—a glowing liquid he’d mixed from old batteries and water—but Liam had never seen anything like it before.

That night, Jake didn’t sleep. He sat on the kitchen counter, watching the orbs as they swirled together, their light turning the room into a strange, shifting dream. The next morning, Jake woke up early and vanished without a word. The orbs were gone, but Liam found a note taped to the fridge: *"They’re not mine. They’re ours."* Beneath it, a single word scrawled in messy handwriting: **ECHOES.**

Liam searched the house. The orbs were back, now in the attic, clustered near Jake’s old science supplies. His parents were at work, but Liam had always been curious about the attic anyway—it was where Jake kept his secrets. He climbed the ladder, his heart pounding. The orbs pulsed louder here, like a heartbeat.

Then he heard it—a whisper, faint but unmistakable. *"They’re not just light. They’re voices."* Liam spun around, but the orbs were gone. The attic was empty. He grabbed his flashlight and descended the ladder, only to find Jake’s journal open on the floor. The page he’d torn out was missing, but the rest read:

*"They’re not just echoes. They’re memories. And they’re not letting us forget."*

Liam’s phone buzzed. A text from Jake: *"Meet me at the old factory. Bring the orbs."* He hesitated. What if it was a trap? But Jake was always reckless like this. He grabbed his backpack and left.

The factory was abandoned, its rusted doors creaking in the wind. Liam followed the orbs, which now guided him like invisible hands. The air smelled of old paint and something electric. Inside, the walls were covered in strange symbols—maps, maybe? A figure stood at the center, wrapped in tattered fabric, their face obscured by the same glowing orbs.

*"You shouldn’t have come,"* the figure whispered. *"They don’t belong to us anymore."*

Liam’s hands trembled. *"Who are you?"*

*"We’re the last of the Echoes,"* the figure said. *"They’re trapped here, waiting for someone to free them. But you’ll never understand what you’re missing."*

The orbs faded. The factory doors groaned open, revealing a vast, empty space beyond. Liam turned to run, but the ground beneath him was shifting. The factory was collapsing. He stumbled, falling, but the orbs were still there—pulsing in his hands.

Jake was waiting for him at the edge of the ruins, holding a box of old cameras. *"They’re not just light,"* Jake said, pressing a camera into Liam’s hands. *"They’re pictures. And they’re not from yesterday."*

Liam looked inside. The film was clear—pictures of people he didn’t know, places he’d never seen. A girl in a black dress, a boy with wild hair, a factory that looked like the one they were in. The film was labeled: *"Before the Echoes."*

Jake smiled. *"They’re not just echoes, Liam. They’re stories. And they’re telling us to remember."*

The orbs in Liam’s hands glowed brighter. The world around them warped—colors bleeding into each other, memories bleeding into the present. The factory was no longer a ruin. It was a library, infinite and endless, filled with books that weren’t books at all, but stories—stories of people who had been here before, and others who would be here after.

Liam woke up in his bed, sweating. His phone was in his pocket—Jake’s name was still on the screen, but the text was gone. The only thing he had was the camera. He took a photo of the orbs, glowing faintly in the dark. The film inside was still clear.

That night, Liam didn’t sleep. He sat on the kitchen table, the camera in his hands. The orbs were gone, but the film was still warm. He opened the box, one by one, and started to tell the stories Jake had left behind.

The first was about a girl named Mira, who lived in a city that didn’t exist anymore. She found the orbs in a garden, and they showed her a world she’d never known. She didn’t want to leave, but she had to. *"Some memories,"* the orbs whispered, *"are not meant to stay."*

The second was about a boy named Elias, who discovered the Echoes in a museum. They showed him a future where the world was different—no more factories, no more cities, just endless forests. He chose to stay, to help shape the world he’d never seen. *"The Echoes,"* the camera read, *"are not just echoes. They are the threads of time itself."*

Liam’s fingers trembled as he played the last story. It was about a boy named Liam, who found the Echoes in a factory, and they showed him a future where the world was gone. *"You must remember,"* the orbs had said. *"You must choose."*

Liam looked at the film. The boy in the picture was him. He was holding a camera, just like the one he had now. The world around him was different—no more orbs, no more stories, just silence.

Liam closed the box. He didn’t understand it all. Some things were too big for words. But he knew one thing for sure: the Echoes weren’t just light. They were stories. And stories were the only thing that could save the world.

He took one last photo of the orbs, glowing softly in the dark. Then he put the camera away. Tomorrow, he’d start telling the stories. One by one. Because some memories weren’t meant to stay. But some stories were meant to be told.
