Abandoning a failing project in favor of a more promising opportunity was the correct decision—though the path to realizing it was fraught with self-doubt. The early signs of neglect were subtle: missed deadlines, unanswered emails, and the quiet exhaustion that follows burnout. Yet, the data remained clear: the project’s ROI was eroding faster than anticipated, and resources were being squandered on incremental fixes. By the time the decision was made, the opportunity’s potential had crystallized into tangible advantages—scalability, market alignment, and a team whose expertise aligned with the new direction. Hindsight painted the original approach as a misstep, but the challenge wasn’t the choice itself—it was the paralysis of hesitation. The lesson? Clarity in intent doesn’t always translate to clarity in execution until the alternative is undeniable.
