In the quiet village of Eldermere, the old clock tower stood sentinel over the cobblestone streets, its hands frozen at **3:17**. The villagers whispered that the clock had stopped at midnight the day the last lantern burned out in the church.

One evening, young Liora, carrying a lantern made from an old oilskin bag, passed by the tower. The lantern’s flame flickered as she approached, casting long shadows on the weathered stone. The clock’s hands had never moved since that night, but the lantern’s light danced like it had.

At the church, she found the lantern left behind, its wick still damp with oil. She lit it, and the flame caught the air, curling upward as if responding to something unseen. The church doors creaked open, revealing a hidden chamber where a second lantern sat, identical to the first.

The lanterns glowed brighter, their light weaving into a pattern that traced the clock’s face. The hands began to spin—slowly at first, then faster, until they reached **3:17** again. The villagers, startled, found the lanterns extinguished, and the clock tower silent.

As Liora walked home, she realized the lanterns had not just burned out—they had **restarted the clock**. The village’s lanterns, now lit anew, cast their light on the tower, and the hands turned once more, repeating the cycle.
