**"The Quiet Hour"**

The city hummed beneath her, a thousand whispered conversations—voices, laughter, the distant clatter of a thousand feet. She sat at her window, fingers tracing the rim of her coffee cup, the steam curling into the crisp autumn air like a ghost. The glass was cold, the city warm, and for once, she didn’t need to fill the silence.

A book fell open on her lap, its pages yellowed with time. She flipped through it, the weight of the words settling like dust. Somewhere, a train rumbled, but the sound was distant, a pulse beneath the city’s relentless heartbeat. She exhaled slowly, watching the smoke spiral into the dark.

Outside, the streetlights flickered to life, casting long shadows across the pavement. A single car passed, its headlights cutting through the dusk like a blade through still water. She closed her eyes, listening to the rhythm of her own breath—the steady, unbroken beat of something that had been waiting for this moment.

The evening air was cool, carrying the scent of rain promised for tomorrow. She leaned back in her chair, letting the weight of the day dissolve into the quiet like sugar in tea. Somewhere, a clock chimed—twelve. The city would wake again, but for now, she was home.
