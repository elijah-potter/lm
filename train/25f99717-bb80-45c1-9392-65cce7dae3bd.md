The neon hum of the city pulsed through the cracked glass of her apartment door. Inside, a half-eaten burrito sat untouched on the counter as her phone buzzed—another missed call from her ex. She grabbed the phone, fingers trembling, and tapped the screen.

*"We should talk."* The message was simple, just like everything else.

Outside, the rain drummed against the window, a relentless rhythm. She stood in the kitchen, staring at the clock. **5:37 PM**. The city’s heartbeat slowed to a whisper as she realized: one minute left.

Her breath hitched. The burrito crumbled in her hand.

She opened the door.

The street was empty—just the glow of a passing taxi and the distant wail of a siren. She stepped out, her shoes silent on the wet pavement, and walked toward the subway entrance.

The train arrived at **5:39 PM**, doors opening like a trap.

Inside, the air smelled of stale coffee and fear. She found an empty seat, her reflection in the glass beside her: eyes wide, lips parted. The doors closed.

The lights flickered. **5:40 PM**.

She reached for her phone again—no missed calls. No messages. Just silence.

The train pulled away from the station, and the city blurred through the window like smoke.

One minute. That’s all she had left.
