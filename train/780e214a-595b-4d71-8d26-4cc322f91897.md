In the quiet coastal town of **Port Haven**, residents have been left stunned by the sudden discovery of an ancient, weathered stone tablet found in the ruins of the abandoned **Blackthorn Lighthouse**. The artifact, uncovered beneath layers of salt and seaweed, bears a strange, unknown script that appears to be etched into its surface in concentric circles, resembling geometric patterns yet defying conventional interpretation.

Local historian **Dr. Elias Voss** of the **Port Haven Historical Society** confirmed the find after X-ray imaging revealed no signs of modern fabrication. "It's unlike anything we’ve seen," he said, his voice tight with both awe and unease. "The symbolism is cryptic, but the message seems deliberate." The tablet’s weight—estimated at nearly a hundred pounds—raises concerns about its stability, prompting officials to cordon off the lighthouse site until further analysis can determine its safety.

Archaeologists from the **East Coast Research Institute** have been dispatched, but their arrival has been met with skepticism. Some Port Haven residents, fearing a hoax or government overreach, have begun circulating rumors online, including claims that the tablet is a "false flag" meant to distract from local economic struggles. Police have issued a non-emergency advisory to discourage unauthorized access to the ruins.

Meanwhile, whispers of a **"Port Haven Protocol"** have surfaced in underground forums, suggesting that the tablet’s discovery coincides with a series of unexplained phenomena—strange light displays near the shore and a sudden spike in sea temperature just last month. The town’s mayor, **Catherine Marris**, has called for a town hall meeting tomorrow to address the situation, urging residents to remain vigilant but avoid panic. "We’re not in the Middle Ages," she stated, "but we *are* in Port Haven—and this isn’t a story to dismiss."

The Port Haven Chronicle, the town’s only newspaper, has published an investigative piece on the tablet’s potential historical significance, citing possible ties to lost maritime civilizations. The article ends with a cryptic question: *"What was buried here, and why?"* The answer, it seems, may soon be revealed—or buried again.
