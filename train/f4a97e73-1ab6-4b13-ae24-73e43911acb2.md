The rain fell—not loudly, just steadily, like a quiet promise. The house, once warm, now seemed to hold the weight of the sky’s sighs. Outside, the world moved on: children laughing without knowing the weight of the world, a dog napping in the sun, oblivious to the slow unraveling of time.

The garden was a memory now, its soil softer than the edges of forgotten things. The coffee tasted bitter, not because it was weak, but because it had been sitting too long, collecting dust like old secrets. The clock on the wall was accurate—nearly an hour had passed since the last time anyone checked it.

They had meant to leave something behind, something tangible, but the words dissolved like sugar in water. The only evidence left were the half-finished sentences on the page, the half-eaten sandwich, the way the light caught the rim of the coffee cup, turning it gold for a single, fleeting moment.
