A dimly lit office desk glows faintly under the fluorescent light. **Daniel** leans back in his chair, fingers tracing the edge of a crumpled spreadsheet. The numbers—**3, 7, 11, 15**—suddenly click into place. His eyes narrow as he realizes the hidden rule: each number is **4 more than the previous square number (1²=1, 2²=4, 3²=9, 4²=16 → 1+4=5, 2+4=6, 3+4=7, 4+4=8, but wait—no, 3²=9, 9+4=13—no, it’s 3²=9, but 3*4=12. Wait, no, it’s **4*(n+1) - 3n+1 → simplified to n+1**. So the sequence is **n+1 where n starts at 2**—so **3,7,11,15** fits: 3(2+1), 7(3+1), 11(4+1), 15(5+1)**. He mutters to himself: *"This is why I should’ve stopped at the 10th row."* His phone buzzes—a reminder for the next client. He sighs, grabs his notes, and heads to the door. The pattern isn’t just in the numbers; it’s in how the universe *tests* us before it lets us forget.
