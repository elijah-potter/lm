**Exterior: The Old Mill**

The rain fell in sheets, thick and relentless, turning the cobblestones slick beneath the flickering lamplight. Thunder rumbled ominously, its echoes bouncing off the ancient wooden beams of the mill, where the last few workers—tired, damp, and resigned—poured grain into the hopper, their movements slow and deliberate. Inside, the air was heavy with dust and the scent of mildew, the only light filtering through the stained glass in the arched windows.

**Interior: The Mill’s Common Room**

Outside, a storm howled, its fury matched by the fire’s dying embers in the hearth. The wind howled through the cracks in the walls, carrying whispers of the past. Inside, the room felt suffocating, the air thick with tension. A single candle burned low on the table, casting long shadows across the faces of the gathered—some weary, some furious, all of them trapped by circumstance. The rain pounded relentlessly against the roof, a metronome of despair.

A figure—tall, gaunt—stood at the far end of the room, arms crossed. Their gaze was fixed on the door, which stood ajar, letting in a gust of cold air that carried the scent of rain and something older, something like regret. The storm outside was a storm of their own making, a storm they had let fester. The candle’s flame flickered, then snuffed out in a gust. The room plunged into darkness.

Silence.

Then, a single tear—cold and sharp—cut through the stillness. Outside, the rain stopped. The wind died. The storm had passed, leaving behind only silence, and the hollow ache of what had been.
