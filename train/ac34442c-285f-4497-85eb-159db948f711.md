Sand beneath barefoot feet—crunching, warm, and shifting like liquid gold underfoot; embedded with flecks of ochre, quartz, and crushed shells. The air hums with the scent of salt and crushed cacti, thick with the weight of a thousand sunbaked days. Moss clings to weathered stone like a living blanket, soft yet stubborn, patching cracks with a mossy green that never quite fades. The wind carries whispers of rustling palm fronds against ancient, gnarled trunks—textures rough as a calloused hand, their bark peeling in thin, sun-bleached layers. Raindrops strike the surface of stagnant, tar-black water like tiny needles, reflecting a fractured sky in fractured shards. Dust motes dance in the midday light, suspended between layers of grit and shadow.
