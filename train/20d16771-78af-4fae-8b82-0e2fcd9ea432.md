In the quietest corner of the world where time seemed to stand still, there stood an old lighthouse, its beam a steady pulse against the horizon. It wasn’t built for grandeur, nor for the glory of men, but for the whisper of the sea, the rhythm of the waves, and the eternal dance of light and shadow.

The keeper, a man named Elias, had spent decades tending to this forgotten sentinel. His hands, gnarled with age, carved names into the wood—a list of ships lost, of storms survived, of sailors who had found solace in its glow. One evening, as the sun bled into the ocean, he noticed something strange: the beam wasn’t just cutting through the dark. It was *breathing*.

Elias followed the light down to the lowest level of the lighthouse, where the air was thick with the scent of salt and old copper. Beneath the rotten planks of the floor, he found it—a small, circular hole, like a wound in the earth. Inside, half-buried in the sand, was a mirror, its surface worn smooth by time and tide. The reflection in it wasn’t his own.

It was *blue*.
