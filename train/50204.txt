Premature termination codons, or early stop codons that lead to the production of truncated proteins or nonsense mediated decay of the mRNA, underlie several cancer syndromes as well as many sporadic tumors. The use of readthrough-promoting therapies initially developed to treat inherited genetic disorders such as Duchenne muscular dystrophy may be therapeutically useful in cancers that arise from the loss of protein activity. In this issue, two reviews discuss these therapies in detail – from the activity of the compounds available to the syndromes most likely to be improved by readthrough therapies. On pages 679–688, Laure Bidou, Valérie Allamand, Jean-Pierre Rousset, and Olivier Namy review the structures and mechanisms underlying the actions of currently available small molecules, and on pages 667–678 Renata Bordeira-Carriço, Ana Paula Pêgo, Manuel Santos, and Carla Oliveira discuss the cancers most affected by this class of mutations.
