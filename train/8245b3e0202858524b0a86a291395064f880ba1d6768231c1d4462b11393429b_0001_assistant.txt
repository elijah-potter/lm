Jellyfish are common in Hampton Roads coastal waters during the summer months. They are not aggressive, but they can sting people who accidentally bump into them. Jellyfish stings can be painful, but most are not emergencies. If you are stung, try to remain calm and seek medical attention if necessary.

Jellyfish are not fish. They are invertebrate animals that do not have a backbone. The most common jellyfish in Hampton Roads is the sea nettle. Sea nettle tentacles can extend several feet and contain thousands of stingers. When touched, a sea nettle's stingers pierce a person's skin and release venom that can cause pain, nausea, and vomiting.

Even a dead jellyfish can sting. If you see a jellyfish on the beach, do not touch it. Even a detached tentacle can release venom.

To treat a jellyfish sting, rinse the affected area with seawater. Do not use fresh water, alcohol, or ammonia. Apply a cold compress to the area to help reduce pain and swelling. Over-the-counter pain relievers can also help. If you have a severe allergic reaction to jellyfish stings, seek medical attention immediately.

There are a few things you can do to prevent jellyfish stings:

1. Check with local health departments and lifeguards about water conditions before swimming.
2. Avoid swimming in areas where jellyfish have been reported.
3. Wear protective clothing, such as a wetsuit or a stinger suit, when swimming in areas where jellyfish are present.
4. Apply an anti-jellyfish sting lotion before swimming.
