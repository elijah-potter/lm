The air hums faintly with residual static from a recently extinguished bulb hanging from the exposed beam above. Dust motes spiral lazily in the dim, uneven light filtering through the stained glass panels on the east wall—one cracked, its red tint peeking through a jagged seam like a half-remembered scar. The wooden floorboards beneath the cluttered desk—its surface littered with half-empty coffee cups, a half-dissolved teabag, and the occasional crumpled napkin—shift slightly underfoot, as if resisting a recent weight. The desk’s drawers are half-open, revealing a tangle of papers with yellowed edges and a half-empty bottle of ink that has spilled onto the edge, pooling in a dark circle.

A half-finished sketch of a distorted figure in black marker lies atop a pile of crumpled receipts and a half-eaten sandwich wrapper, its edges smudged with what looks like lipstick. The window, draped in faded, patched-up curtains, lets in a cold draft that tugs at the sleeves of the discarded jacket hanging from a hook near the door, its collar turned up despite the room’s lack of apparent warmth.

A single, unlit cigarette glows weakly in the ashtray, its embers still warm, though no one is near to finish it. The scent of old leather and damp wool lingers—perhaps from the armchair tucked under the desk, its upholstery slightly creased, as if someone recently sat there. The floorboard under the chair groans softly, as if whispering the same unspoken secret as the room itself.
