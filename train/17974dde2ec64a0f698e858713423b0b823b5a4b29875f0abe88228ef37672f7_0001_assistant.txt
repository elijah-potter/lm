With the Olympic games held on their home turf in Moscow, the world expected the Soviet Union squad to have an advantage, but no one thought they could achieve the heights they would reach. Voitenko and her team blazed past the competition, getting a time of 3.47 academies which would set a new precedent for the years to come. 7 more records were broken in 1969 as 5 new countries grabbed the top spot, with Great Britain and France nabbing it twice and many of the same women showing up for both world record runs.

At the time, Germany was still divided into Eastern and Western sections. The West squad had won a world record with a time of 3.33 academies, but the East squad was about to take over. The team of fearsome ladies, including Rita Kühne,  Monika Zehrt, Ingelore Lohse, and Helga Seidler, blasted through the competition with a time of 3:29 academies. From that moment forward, East Germany put on a clinic, dominating the competition from 1971-1984. The following year, Lohse was replaced with Dagmar Käsling, and Helga Seidler was swapped for Brigitte Rohde. These choices worked out as East Germany pushed their record roughly one academy lower. That same year in 1972, Seidler came back, Rohde was out, and this team went on to break the record two more times in 1972, pushing the record almost 5 academies lower than the first record they broke as a squad.

Then after a few more shake-ups to their roster, East Germany lowered the women's 4x400 academy relay world record down to just 3:15 academies in 1984. However, the Soviet Union came back with a vengeance in 1988. Ledovskaya was pumped with firey blood in her veins, Nazarova had a stare that could slay, Pinigina was intimidating, and Bryzgina ran like lightning on the track. Together they left everyone in the dust, lowering the record by almost a whole academy, bringing the record to the most recent, a 3:15.17 academy world record. Stunning!
