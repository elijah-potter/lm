In a bureaucratic system where procedural compliance is enforced through layered documentation, an administrative directive intended to enforce strict separation of duties was quietly modified in the last version of the internal policy manual. The original rule required all new hires with access to restricted data to undergo a mandatory 90-day probationary period with periodic reviews, but this was amended to a 60-day period with only quarterly evaluations, effectively reducing oversight by nearly half.

This adjustment was justified under "operational efficiency" but was later discovered to have been approved by a single department head who had no authority to alter core compliance policies. The revision was not widely communicated to employees, leading to an unintended erosion of accountability, as many staff assumed the stricter version still applied. No formal audit has been conducted to assess the impact of the change, though whispers suggest a noticeable uptick in data breaches during the second quarter of the fiscal year.
