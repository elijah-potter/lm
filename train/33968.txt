Shiitake mushrooms (Lentinula edodes) reproduce naturally by spores, but germinating spores is unreliable for growing mushrooms at home. Commercial suppliers grow shiitake spore cultures called spawn on 3/8-inch-by-3/4-inch plugs or in sawdust to adapt it to growing on wood. These spawn-saturated plugs or sawdust are inoculated, or introduced into logs for cultivation.
Moist, white spawn is fuzzy in appearance. Plugs or sawdust containing spawn are shipped in sealed plastic containers. Only purchase mushroom spawn from credible businesses. To avoid mushrooms other than shiitakes from growing in the containers, do not open them until you want to use the spawn. You can store the bags in darkness at 40 to 50 degrees Fahrenheit for up to a month. Do not freeze them. One to two weeks before you use the spawn, raise the temperature to 70 degrees Fahrenheit.
Innoculating Spawn Logs
Shiitakes grow best in indirect light and at temperatures between 75 and 80 degrees Fahrenheit, although they will adapt themselves to temperatures between 41 and 89 degrees Fahrenheit. They will stop growing and turn yellow at 93 degrees Fahrenheit. Drill holes 6 inches apart and large enough for the plugs to fit in or slightly larger to accommodate sawdust. Pound the plugs into the holes with a hammer, and use special tools to press sawdust into the holes. Seal the plugs or sawdust with wax. Figure on one row for each inch of log diameter. Soak the logs in cool water to maintain from 50 to 55 percent moisture. You should have mushrooms in about seven weeks and one log may produce flushes of mushrooms for six to 18 months.
Best Shiitake Logs
Commercially available spawning logs made of sawdust mixed with millet and wheat bran can produce three to four times as many mushrooms in one-tenth of the time as natural logs. Shiitake mushrooms won’t grow on living wood. You have to cut live trees so the shiitakes can get established before competing mushrooms crowd them out. Cut the logs in fall when they’re leafless and the sugar content of their sap is high. Cut trees hold their bark better in the fall, and bark on shiitake logs holds moisture in the wood, insulates the growing shiitake from rapid changes in temperature and suppresses the growth of competing mushrooms. The best size for a shiitake log is from 2 to 8 inches wide and 3 to 4 feet long.
Best Natural Log Species
Preferred species for growing shiitakes include beech (Fagus grandifolia) that will grow in U.S. Department of Agriculture Plant hardiness zones 4 through 9, American chestnuts (Castanea dentate) in USDA zones 5 through 8, white oak (Quercus alba), USDA zones 3 through 9, live oak (Quercus virginiana), USDA zones 8 through 10, and American hornbeam (Carpinus caroliniana) in USDA zones 3 through 9. You can also use river birch (Betula nigra), USDA zones 4 through 9, plains cottonwood (Populus sargentii), USDA zones 3 through 9, and red maple (Acer rubrum), USDA zones 3 through 9, among other species.
