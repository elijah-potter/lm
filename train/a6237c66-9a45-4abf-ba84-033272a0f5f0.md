The skeletal remains of a half-built tower rise from cracked concrete, ribs of exposed rebar jutting from the foundation like skeletal fingers. The upper levels are a chaotic mosaic of misaligned steel frames, some twisted, others still partially sheathed in rusted sheets. Painted slogans in faded graffiti—*"Finish Strong"* and *"Build Better"*—bleed into the peeling plaster, half-erased by time and neglect. Windows are either shattered or missing entirely, some framed in stolen glass that has been replaced with splintered plastic or jury-rigged metal bars. A rusted crane arm, half-suspended, casts long shadows over the site. The ground is littered with discarded materials: bent pipes, broken bricks, and the occasional half-buried bucket of cement. Dust swirls in the air, settling on every surface. A single, abandoned hard hat sits on a concrete slab, its brim dented and its paint chipped. The air smells of damp earth, mildew, and the faint metallic tang of rust. Nearby, a makeshift fence of twisted barbed wire marks the perimeter, though it sags under its own weight. Somewhere beyond the perimeter, the hum of machinery—still running, though silent now—fades into the distance.
