In the quiet hum of a café, the rain tapping against the windows, **Ethan** hesitated before opening his mouth. His fingers tightened around the coffee cup, the steam curling upward like a ghost of his own doubts. Across the table, **Mira**, her dark eyes sharp with unspoken tension, waited. The lie they’d been sharing for months—how her brother’s accident was an accident, not his own reckless driving—had been a weight between them, a silent contract neither wanted to break.

He exhaled slowly, the breath steady. *"You know how I’ve been saying you’re too stubborn to admit it?"* His voice was low, deliberate. *"Well, today’s the day I’m done playing games. Your brother’s not just ‘accident-prone’—he’s reckless. He knew the risks, and he took them anyway."* A beat of silence. Then, Mira’s hand shot out, gripping his wrist. Her knuckles were white. *"You don’t understand."* She stood abruptly, knocking over her chair. *"This isn’t about him. It’s about *us*."*

The words hung between them, heavy with the weight of what came next. Ethan stared at his hands, the coffee now cold. Mira’s phone buzzed. A text from her brother’s name: *"Why’d you lie?"* The screen was the first crack in the illusion they’d built. The café door swung open. Outside, the rain never stopped.

Truth, like a knife, cut through the illusion.
