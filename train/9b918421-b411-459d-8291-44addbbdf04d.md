The old leather satchel sat atop the cluttered desk in the dimly lit storage room, half-buried beneath dust and forgotten papers. It had been there for years—until the door creaked open, and Elias entered.

His fingers brushed the corner of the satchel, and for the first time in decades, something inside stirred. The weight was familiar, the scent faint but unmistakable.

Elias pulled it out carefully, revealing a small, tarnished pocket watch—its chain still coiled inside. The hands had stopped at 3:17 AM, the time he’d last seen it before the accident. The watch was damaged, the face cracked, but the mechanism still ticked, as if alive.

His breath caught. The last time he’d worn it was the day he’d lost his memory. The night before, he’d been a man named Daniel Carter, running from a man who had his family. The watch was his only link to the past.

Elias turned it over in his hands, the weight of the truth settling in his chest. The satchel was more than dust and memories—it was a key. And keys, he knew, were meant to be opened.
