Cliffs of Blackthorn Bay, where jagged basalt spires pierce a storm-wracked sky. The wind carries salt and the scent of ozone from the abyss below—a hidden river plunges into a cavern known only to the old sailors. The tide pools are shallow, teeming with bioluminescent algae that glow faintly against the obsidian rocks. Beyond the horizon, the mist rolls like a living thing, obscuring the line where earth meets sea. The air hums with the distant call of a bird that doesn’t exist on maps. The ground is soft with moss and the remains of things left behind—tools, bones, a child’s sandcastle half-eroded into the stone. At dusk, the fog lifts just enough to see the flicker of fire in the ruins of the last settlement, its walls half-collapsed, doors gaping like mouths. The ocean here doesn’t just wash against the shore; it *laughs*, a wet, guttural sound that makes the hair on your neck stand up.
