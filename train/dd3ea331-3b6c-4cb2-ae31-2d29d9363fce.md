The athlete follows a structured routine with **warm-up** (dynamic stretching, light cardio) to activate muscles and reduce injury risk. **Strength training** (e.g., compound lifts like squats, deadlifts, bench press) with progressive overload is performed with controlled reps and intensity, targeting major muscle groups (legs, core, back, shoulders, arms). **Conditioning** (HIIT, sprints, circuit training) is integrated for endurance, often paired with high-rep movements (e.g., push-ups, pull-ups) or plyometrics.

Cool-down includes **static stretching** (hamstrings, hip flexors, shoulders) and **breathwork** to lower heart rate. Recovery phases (e.g., mobility drills, foam rolling) maintain flexibility and reduce soreness. **Nutrition** focuses on high-protein, balanced macros, and hydration, with timed meals for energy. **Sleep** (7–9 hours) supports adaptation and performance.

In competitions, routines may include **technical drills** (e.g., agility ladder, medicine ball throws), **mental conditioning** (visualization, focus drills), and **specific game scenarios** (e.g., defensive positioning in sports). Adaptive adjustments (e.g., periodization) optimize performance peaks.
