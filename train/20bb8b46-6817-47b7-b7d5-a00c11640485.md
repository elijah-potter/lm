The air smelled faintly of rain before it even hit the pavement—like the first notes of a song you’d recognize if you tried hard enough. She sat on the bench outside the café, her fingers tracing the edge of the empty coffee cup, as though tracing the lines of someone else’s life. The window behind her was half-drawn, the glass reflecting the muted glow of streetlights, but not *her*—not the way she appeared in the café’s photos, the way her name was spelled on the menu, the way the barista had called her name, *Sarah*, the way she’d smiled at the waiter who remembered her order from last week.

She wasn’t there anymore, but the chair was. The café’s name—*Morning Blend*—was engraved on the table, the same way her name had been on the receipts for the past three months. She’d come here every Tuesday, the same time, the same seat, the same ritual of caffeine and nostalgia. But this Tuesday, the table was empty, and the chair was warm. The barista’s hands, calloused and practiced, hovered over the keyboard, but the screen read *No orders today*.

She stood abruptly, her pulse quickening, and turned to face the door. The café’s exit was a narrow gap, just wide enough for a shoulder, the kind of space where people stepped through without hesitation. She stepped through, and the world blurred—first the blur of the doorframe, then the sharp intake of breath as she stepped into the alley. The pavement was slick with rain, the kind that never quite stopped, the kind that made the city feel like it was breathing in slow motion. She pressed her palms against the wall, her fingers digging into the texture of the brick, and the weight of something pressed against her back.

She turned slowly. The figure was closer than she’d thought—too close. Tall, lean, dressed in a coat that didn’t match the rain, the sleeves rolled up to reveal forearms that looked like they’d been carved from stone. The eyes were dark, unreadable, but there was something in them—familiarity, maybe? A recognition that wasn’t quite a memory. The figure exhaled sharply, and the scent of something bitter and metallic hit her. She recognized it. *Him.*

His voice was low, rough, and he said, *"You came back for me."* The words were a knife twist. She should have run. She should have screamed. But she stayed still, her breath coming in shallow gasps, the way it did when she was younger and her mother had left, or when the doctor told her she had a tumor, or when the bank called to say her accounts were frozen. The words were a lie, but they were the kind of lie that settled into your bones.

He reached out, and she flinched. His touch was cold, but not from the rain. It was the kind of cold that settled in the hollows of your teeth, the kind that made your skin prickle even as it burned. He traced the curve of her jaw, his thumb brushing her cheekbone, and she could *feel* the past in the way he moved. The way he *looked* at her. The way he made her remember. And then he said, *"You belong here."* Not to her. To *him*. To the version of her that had never been hers.

The alley stretched long and silent, the kind of place where secrets fester and echoes linger. She could hear her own heartbeat, a drumbeat in her ears, and the distant hum of the city, like a second heartbeat, slower, steady. She thought about the past—about the name she’d been given, the faces that had been hers, the lives she’d lived, the ones she’d left behind. And then she thought about the future—about the name she’d been given, the faces that would be hers, the lives she’d build.

She stepped back, away from him, away from the alley, away from the past. The café’s door was still there, the same gap, the same exit. She stepped through, and the world sharpened—first the clarity of the doorframe, then the sudden rush of air, the scent of coffee and woodsmoke, the hum of the café’s regulars, the way the barista’s eyes flicked to her, then away, as if she’d never been there at all. She sat back down, her hands trembling, and the weight of what had happened pressed against her ribs like a second skin.

She picked up her cup, the one with the coffee that had been half-finished, the one that had been her alone. She sipped it slowly, the bitter, sweet taste filling her mouth, and the café’s name—*Morning Blend*—was engraved on the table in a way that wasn’t just ink and wood. It was a promise. A reminder. A *borrowed* moment, stolen from the past and pressed into the present, to be held until it was time to return it.
