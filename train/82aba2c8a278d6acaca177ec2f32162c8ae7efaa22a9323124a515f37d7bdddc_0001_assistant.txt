Check all fluids. This means oil, coolant in the radiator, brake fluid, and even transmission fluid if it is not sealed. Your vehicle user guide should have the relevant information as far as how much fluids need to be added and at what interval. You can also ask your favorite auto mechanic to check these items out for you, but be diligent and be prepared to pay more to have someone else do it.
Check your tire pressure. Keeping your tires inflated to their recommended pressure will help ensure a safe and smooth ride. Most tires have a max pressure number printed on the sidewall of the tire. Do NOT inflate to this number, it is the MAXIMUM amount of air the tire can take. Instead, there should be a label on the inside of your vehicle door that has the recommended tire pressure listed on it.
Clean your mirrors and windows. You might want to do this just for aesthetic reasons, but having clean windows and mirrors can also keep you safe on the road. Use a streak-free cleaner and lint-free cloth. Non-glossy newspaper works very well for cleaning windows without leaving streak lines. 
Have a road safety kit. It should include a flashlight, jumper cables, a cone or reflective triangle, and some gloves in case of poor weather. 
Check your spare tire and jack if so equipped. If you don’t have one, get a can or two of Fix-A-Flat. It may get you somewhere safe in case of a flat.
