The lost don't always want to be found. It's possible to lose yourself, to find grey contentment in your new hopeless aimlessness and never find yourself or your place in the world again. This new void is unexpectedly comfortable. It's not comforting, no, but you drift along regardless. You essentially start over, but this new beginning is murky and filled with doubt and shadows.
Being lost doesn't always involve being in an unfamiliar place with no understanding of how to return to what is recognizable. You can lose yourself without ever stepping foot somewhere new. It's a gradual process, and you don't realize it is happening until one day you discover that you have no idea who you are and struggle to remember who you used to be. You try to remember yourself. Bits and pieces come to mind but the rest is unfocused, obscured by the slow shift that pushed you into your current existence.
Some try and successfully find themselves again. Some decide that the effort involved in rediscovery is just too taking and too bothersome. It's pointless, really. It's easier to remain where you are. Why bother backtracking? Perhaps this is who you are meant to be. Perhaps who you were is gone for a reason. Is being lost really that bad? Why not just continue down that path you've drifted along and allow what is to continue to be, letting the current of your life carry you forward?
