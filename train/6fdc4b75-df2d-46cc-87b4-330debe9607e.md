The once verdant valley now stretches with cracked, parched earth, its once-lush canopies reduced to skeletal remains of brittle, ashen foliage. Rivers that once sang through the terraced hills are dried to shallow, sunken beds, their waters long evaporated by relentless winds. The once-vibrant meadows have been swallowed by dust, a blanket of golden and grey covering what remains of the land. The air hums with the scent of scorched earth and distant, faint embers from smoldering ruins. The sky is a pallid, bruised hue—dissipated clouds cling to the horizon like bruises, threatening to spill at any moment. The land breathes a slow, heavy sigh, its bones exposed.
