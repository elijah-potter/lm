The flickering neon signs on the boardwalk flickered like dying stars. A distant siren wailed through the damp, salt-laden air, its echo swallowed by the wind. Around her, the crowd huddled in groups, lanterns casting long, trembling shadows across the sand. Clara gripped her coat tighter, the fabric damp with seawater. The power had failed hours ago, and the last of the emergency generators had sputtered to life, their roar now a distant, muffled drone.

A sharp *crack* split the night. A fire alarm blared, its piercing wail cutting through the haze. Clara turned to see a man in a torn uniform—likely a lifeguard—running toward the crowd, his face streaked with tears. He reached her in seconds, his hands clutching a half-empty bottle of whiskey.

"Another one?" he panted, his voice rough with exhaustion.

Clara nodded. "I’ve been waiting."

The man’s breath hitched. "You’re not here for the drinks, are you?"

She shook her head. "I’m here for the *beach*."

Outside, the ocean roared against the shore, its waves crashing in silent defiance. The tide pulled at the sand, leaving behind only the ghosts of discarded bottles and forgotten dreams. Somewhere in the distance, a car alarm wailed—a single, desperate wail before it died. The city slept, its pulse replaced by the slow, steady rhythm of the sea.

A child’s laughter cut through the silence, bright and sharp. Clara glanced toward the sound and saw a group of kids, their faces illuminated by the glow of a single lantern. They were playing tag, their laughter bouncing off the dark waves. For a moment, it was just them—their joy a small, warm island in the storm.

Then the wind howled, carrying the scent of salt and something older, something buried deep in the earth. Clara closed her eyes. This was home. This was *now*.
