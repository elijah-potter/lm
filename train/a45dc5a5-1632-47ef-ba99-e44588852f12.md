You stood on the edge of the crumbling dock, the wind howling through the skeletal remains of the old pier. Your reflection in the rusted metal railing was blurred—half a person, half something else—before it dissolved into the mist. The neon sign of *The Rusty Anchor* flickered weakly, casting long, jagged shadows. Inside, the bar was half-empty, the patrons murmuring in hushed tones. The bartender, a gaunt figure with hollow cheeks, turned slowly as you stepped inside, his eyes fixed on the door.

*"You shouldn’t be here,"* he said, voice like dry leaves scraping stone.

You reached for the whiskey bottle on the counter but hesitated. The liquid inside was black, thick, and bitter—almost alive. The bartender didn’t move. The air smelled of salt, old wood, and something metallic, like blood that had long since turned.

Outside, the pier groaned under the weight of unseen forces. A low, rhythmic *thumping* came from the water—like the heart of something vast and hungry. The dock’s planks were splitting, and the waves crashed against the rotting pilings with a sound like teeth grinding.

*"You’re not supposed to see this,"* the bartender whispered, his fingers tracing the rim of your glass. *"But you already have."* The whiskey sloshed over the sides, and for a moment, the world blurred again—then you were standing on solid ground, your boots crunching on fresh snow. The pier was gone. The bartender was just a memory. And somewhere, a voice whispered: *"Welcome home."*
