A well-kept secret in whispered conversations, the promise lingers like a faint scent in the evening air—unnoticed by all but those who hear it.

The promise was never spoken aloud but was written in the way someone holds a child’s hand too long, in the way they avoid looking at you when they say, *"I’ll call you tomorrow."* It was in the half-smile of the stranger who hands you coffee at 3 AM, the way the baker remembers your name and your favorite pastry without you ever ordering again.

No one sees the quiet work—the unpaid bills folded into an envelope, the hours spent fixing what you swore you’d never fix, the way the door stays open just a crack, letting in a little light that you refuse to let go.

The promise is in the small, unspoken things: the way you don’t ask for the impossible, the way you let someone else’s happiness slip through your fingers and close the door behind you. It is the weight of a debt paid in silence, the quiet relief of knowing you kept your word even when the world forgot.

The promise is the thing that grows in the dark.
