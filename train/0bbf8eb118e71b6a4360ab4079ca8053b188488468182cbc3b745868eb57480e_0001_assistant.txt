To answer your question, "In New Orleans, Lousiana, do they have distinct dialects or speak a different language other than English?" the answer is yes. Here is a list of the dialect and languages spoken in New Orleans and their origin:

1. Cajun English - The Cajun people are of Acadian heritage and immigrants to Nova Scotia. When the first batch of Cajuns settled in Louisiana, they traded with English-speaking traders in the area using a language of commerce and trade called Cajun English.
2. Yat - This is more of a dialect than a language but significantly differs from regular spoken English.  Yat is spoken only in the New Orleans metro area and can vary considerably based on the accent of Italian and Irish immigrants who reside around the port city.
3. French and other varieties - French was spoken in Louisiana for 80 years before the state adapted to many different varieties of cultures. The French spoke Cajun French, and Creole was from a mix of French Creole. This eventually became known as Cajun French. Very few still speak Cajun French, but it is still used in some areas of southern Louisiana.
