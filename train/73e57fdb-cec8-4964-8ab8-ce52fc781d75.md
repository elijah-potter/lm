The kitchen smelled of coffee, the steam curling from the mugs left to cool on the counter. Across the room, Elias sat at the wooden table, fingers tracing the rim of his glass, watching as his mother adjusted the thermostat with deliberate slowness. She didn’t speak the words, but the air between them shifted—like a thread unraveling. The silence wasn’t empty; it was the weight of what couldn’t be put into words, the kind of understanding that didn’t require a voice.

Outside, the city hummed. Somewhere, a child laughed, their voice swallowed by the traffic. Elias’ phone buzzed—a notification he’d ignored for days. His mother’s eyes flickered toward it, then away. She didn’t need to say it: *You should’ve checked that.* The unspoken question hung between them, a question older than the last fight they’d had.
