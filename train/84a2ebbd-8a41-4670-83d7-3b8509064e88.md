A city at twilight, neon veins pulsing like veins in a sleeping beast, their blue and gold hues refracting off wet pavement. Streetlights cast elongated shadows that dance in the wind, bending toward the horizon where the sky bleeds into the first ghostly dawn. Overhead, the glow of distant traffic fades into static, a static hum of the unseen, while flickering street signs cast eerie, distorted reflections on glass. The air hums with warmth and cold, the warmth of the sun’s dying breath, the cold of the unfeeling concrete. In the distance, a solitary streetlamp flickers—its light dimmed by smog, yet stubbornly steady. The world is bathed in the spectral glow of memory and anticipation.
