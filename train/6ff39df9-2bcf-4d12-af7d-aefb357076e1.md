The room smelled of damp wool and old books, the kind that hadn’t been read in decades. Outside, the city pulsed with unseen energy, but here, in the quiet corner of this abandoned library, the weight of doubt settled like the first snowfall.

I should have left years ago. The letter in my pocket was the last thing I wrote before the train doors slammed shut behind me. The ink was still wet when I folded it, but the words had already begun to blur—*What if I’m wrong? What if I’m not enough?* The pen slipped between my fingers, and for a moment, I thought it would write itself, filling the page with something else entirely.

The mirror above the desk reflected not my face, but a stranger’s—one who stared back with hollow eyes and a smile that didn’t reach them. I reached out, touched the glass, and my fingers passed through it. The glass shattered.

Now, the room is empty. The books still stand, their spines cracked with age, their pages whispering secrets I never asked for. The city outside is still there, but I’ve stopped listening to it. Some things are too loud to hear. Some things are too quiet.

The narrator’s voice is a ghost in the room, a whisper that lingers after the last breath. They don’t know if they’re relieved or terrified. Maybe they never will.
