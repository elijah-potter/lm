A fractured clock tower in a forgotten village, its gears rusted and misaligned, ticks backward every third hour. The streets hum with echoes from a parallel timeline—footsteps, laughter, conversations that repeat but never match. The sky above is a shifting canvas of colors that don’t belong to any season, and the air smells faintly of ozone and old paper. Locals whisper about the "Glitch" where time doesn’t flow straight—sometimes it loops, sometimes it accelerates, sometimes it stands still. Those who linger too long often find their reflections moving in reverse.
