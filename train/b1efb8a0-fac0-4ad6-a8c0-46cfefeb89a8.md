## Proposal: The Willow Creek Pocket Park Revitalization – A Climate-Resilient Community Oasis

**Executive Summary:** The Willow Creek Pocket Park, currently a neglected and underutilized space within the Elmwood neighborhood, presents a unique opportunity to create a vital, climate-resilient community asset. This proposal outlines a comprehensive revitalization plan transforming the park into a biodiverse, flood-resilient green space that provides recreational opportunities, mitigates stormwater runoff, and fosters a stronger sense of community. The project, spearheaded by the Elmwood Neighborhood Association in partnership with the City Parks Department and local environmental educators, directly addresses the city’s commitment to urban greening, stormwater management, and equitable access to public spaces. We request $75,000 in municipal funding, matched by $25,000 in volunteer labor, in-kind donations, and fundraising efforts, to realize this transformative project. The Willow Creek Pocket Park Revitalization promises a tangible and lasting benefit to the Elmwood community and serves as a replicable model for future urban resilience initiatives.

**Project Plan & Timeline:**

The Willow Creek Pocket Park Revitalization will proceed through four distinct phases, each with designated responsibilities and deliverables.

**Phase 1: Assessment & Design (Months 1-2) – Responsible Partner: City Parks Department & Elmwood Neighborhood Association.** This phase focuses on detailed site assessment and community-informed design. The City Parks Department’s landscape architects will conduct a hydrological study to assess stormwater patterns and soil composition. Simultaneously, the Elmwood Neighborhood Association will facilitate two community workshops and an online survey to gather input on desired park features and aesthetic preferences. **Deliverables:** Completed hydrological report, finalized park design plans incorporating community feedback, and a detailed planting plan prioritizing native, drought-tolerant species.

**Phase 2: Site Preparation & Stormwater Infrastructure (Months 3-5) – Responsible Partner: City Parks Department & Volunteer Corps.** This phase focuses on necessary ground work. The City Parks Department will oversee the removal of existing debris, grading the site to optimize stormwater infiltration, and installing a bio-swale system designed to capture and filter runoff.  A dedicated volunteer corps, coordinated by the Elmwood Neighborhood Association, will contribute to site clearing and initial soil amendment. **Deliverables:**  Cleared and graded site, installed bio-swale system with appropriate filtration media, amended soil prepared for planting.

**Phase 3: Planting & Amenity Installation (Months 6-8) – Responsible Partner: Elmwood Neighborhood Association & Local Environmental Educators.** This phase focuses on the core planting and installation of park amenities.  Local environmental educators will lead volunteer planting days, focusing on the installation of native trees, shrubs, and pollinator-friendly plants as outlined in the planting plan.  The Elmwood Neighborhood Association will manage the installation of a small, accessible boardwalk constructed from recycled materials and a designated area for community gardening. **Deliverables:** Fully planted park with established native vegetation, constructed boardwalk, and operational community garden plot.

**Phase 4: Educational Signage & Community Programming (Months 9-12) – Responsible Partner: Local Environmental Educators & Elmwood Neighborhood Association.** This phase focuses on long-term engagement and education. Local environmental educators will design and install interpretive signage detailing the park’s ecological features and stormwater management system. The Elmwood Neighborhood Association will organize a series of workshops and events, including guided nature walks, composting demonstrations, and children's gardening activities, to promote park usage and environmental stewardship. **Deliverables:** Installed interpretive signage, schedule of community programming, and initial evaluation report on park usage and community satisfaction.

**Budget:**

The total project cost is estimated at $100,000. We are requesting $75,000 in municipal funding, with the remaining $25,000 secured through matching funds.  The municipal funding will be allocated as follows: $30,000 for professional services (landscape architect, hydrological study), $25,000 for materials (native plants, boardwalk materials, bio-swale filtration media, signage), and $20,000 for equipment rental and volunteer support.  The $25,000 in matching funds will be comprised of $10,000 in in-kind donations from local nurseries, $7,500 raised through community fundraising events, and $7,500 in volunteer labor valued at $15/hour. A contingency fund of 5% ($5,000) has been incorporated to address unforeseen expenses, such as unexpected material price increases or weather-related delays.  The detailed breakdown of costs is itemized within the attached budget spreadsheet (Appendix A).

**Community Engagement & Equity Impacts:**

Community engagement is central to the success of this project. The initial design phase actively solicited input from a diverse range of residents through public workshops and online surveys. Special efforts were made to reach historically marginalized groups within the Elmwood neighborhood, including Spanish-speaking families and senior citizens, through targeted outreach and accessible communication methods. The park’s design incorporates universal accessibility features, ensuring that all residents can enjoy the space. The community garden plot is specifically designated for use by low-income families and provides access to fresh, healthy produce. The project’s equity impact assessment (Appendix B) details the strategies employed to ensure equitable access and benefits for all community members.

**Risk Mitigation:**

Potential risks to the project’s success have been identified and mitigation strategies developed. These include potential weather-related delays, fluctuating material costs, and volunteer recruitment challenges. To mitigate these risks, a flexible timeline has been incorporated, allowing for adjustments based on weather conditions.  Contracts with suppliers include price protection clauses to minimize the impact of material price increases. A robust volunteer recruitment plan, leveraging the Elmwood Neighborhood Association’s established network, is in place to ensure adequate volunteer support.  A detailed risk management plan (Appendix C) outlines these and other potential risks, along with corresponding mitigation strategies.

**Post-Grant Sustainability:**

Long-term sustainability is a key consideration. The planting plan prioritizes native, drought-tolerant species, minimizing the need for ongoing irrigation and maintenance. The Elmwood Neighborhood Association has committed to ongoing stewardship of the park, including regular maintenance, volunteer coordination, and community programming.  A “Friends of Willow Creek Pocket Park” group will be established to foster continued community ownership and support. The City Parks Department will provide ongoing maintenance support, including trash removal and seasonal upkeep. A detailed sustainability plan (Appendix D) outlines these strategies and outlines key performance indicators to track progress.

**Call to Action:**

The Willow Creek Pocket Park Revitalization represents a transformative opportunity to create a vital community asset, enhance climate resilience, and foster a stronger sense of community within the Elmwood neighborhood. This project aligns directly with the city’s strategic priorities and offers a tangible return on investment. We urge the municipal funders to approve our request for $75,000 in funding, enabling us to realize this vision and create a lasting legacy for the Elmwood community. We are confident that this project will serve as a model for future urban greening initiatives throughout the city.
