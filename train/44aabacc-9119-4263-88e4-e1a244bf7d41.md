4. *The Last Transmission*
5. A man in a room with a single door.
6. The door has no handle, only a faint line where it might be pulled.
7. Outside, the wind howls like a ghost whispering secrets.
8. Inside, the clock ticks backward, each second a reverse of time’s usual march.
9. On the walls, the words *"You were here"* are carved in what looks like salt, dissolving into mist.
10. His fingers trace the words as if memorizing them, but the ink is already fading.
11. The air smells like ozone and old paper, the scent of a library that’s been closed too long.
12. At the center of the room, a chair is tipped backward, its legs now part of the floor like a bridge to another dimension.
13. He sits on it anyway, weightless, as if he’s already crossing.
14. The last line of a poem he wrote in his youth appears in his mind’s eye: *"The future is a place we don’t belong—yet."*
15. The door creaks open—not by wind, but by his breath, heavy with the weight of all the things he didn’t say.
16. Beyond it, the sky is a bruise of purple and gold, and the trees are skeletal, their branches reaching like fingers toward something unseen.
17. He steps through without hesitation.
18. The moment his foot crosses the threshold, the world in the room dissolves into static. The air freezes. The words on the walls curl like smoke.
19. When he returns, the chair is still tipped, but the poem is gone. The door remains closed, its line of pull now a straight line, no longer inviting.
20. He kneels beside it, touches the doorframe, and whispers: *"Tell them I was here."* The wind answers, but only with a sigh.
