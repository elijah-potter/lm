**Announcement:** *The Midnight Hour Review*

---

The city hummed beneath a sky choked with neon ghosts, their flickering faces painting the alleyways in hues of forgotten dreams. A single streetlamp, its bulb dimmed to a sickly yellow, cast long shadows across the pavement where the last of the night’s wanderers lingered. The air smelled of damp concrete and stale espresso, the kind that made your jaw clench just thinking about it.

Inside the dimly lit café, *The Rusty Hourglass*, a barista with hands that had seen too many cups of bitter brew poured themself into the tap water, stirred a fresh pot. The machine hissed, filling the room with the scent of something warm and dark, like the first sip of a memory you’d rather forget. The coffee was strong, the kind that didn’t care about your sugar tolerance or your existential dread—it just wanted you to sit, to stay, to let the caffeine do its work.

Across the table, an old man with a face lined like parchment from too many winters read from a worn newspaper, his voice a raspy whisper. *"Another day, another dollar lost to the grind. The system chews us up and spits out hollow men with hollow lives."* His fingers traced the edges of the page, as if he could peel back the layers of the world with just his mind.

Outside, the first light of dawn crept over the rooftops, but it was already too late. The city had won. The streets were empty, save for the occasional figure—some lost in their own thoughts, others just passing through, not stopping, not staying. The night had won, and the world would carry on, indifferent as ever.

The barista poured the coffee. The steam curled into the air, carrying with it the promise of something greater—something dangerous, something worth drinking for. The newspaper clattered to the table. The old man exhaled, a slow, satisfied sound. And then, the clock struck twelve.
