A soft rain dripped from the overhanging branches of an ancient oak, tapping gently against the moss-covered pavement. The last light of dusk bleached the edges of the lamplight, casting long, shifting shadows across the empty street. The scent of damp earth and distant blooming jasmine lingered in the still air.

A woman in a faded wool coat sat on a park bench, her fingers tracing idle patterns in the cool night. She had come here every evening for months, the rhythm of her life now tied to this quiet corner. The streetlamp flickered weakly, its light dimmed by the weight of the day’s exhaustion.

A single crumpled newspaper lay abandoned near her feet, its headline from a decade past still legible: *"Last Flight Home."* She had read it once, long ago, but never saved it. Now, the paper rustled softly as she picked it up, turning it over in her hands.

She had not cried today. Not even when the news came. The phone call had been brief, the voice on the other end quiet and final. The house was empty now. The garden, overgrown but still alive, whispered to itself. The refrigerator hummed, its contents untouched for days.

The train station was empty except for a lone figure, bundled in a heavy coat, stepping off the platform. They carried a small, worn suitcase, its contents carefully packed. The conductor nodded, and the train pulled away, its doors closing with a sigh of metal on metal.

A child, no older than five, ran barefoot through a field of wildflowers, kicking up dust as they chased a butterfly. They had no one to chase with, no one to tell them it was okay to stop. The world felt vast and empty, but for them, it was simply a place to run until they were too tired to keep going.

The last light in the city turned off, one by one, like a slow, deliberate breath. The streetlights dimmed, the neon signs flickered, and then—silence. Not the heavy silence of loss, but the quiet hum of a place that had stopped moving.

Somewhere in the distance, a clock struck twelve. The night swallowed the sound, and for a moment, everything held its breath.
