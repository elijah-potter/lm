The **threshold** is a hollowed-out oak door, its brass knocker worn smooth by generations of footsteps. Beneath it lies the **cave of forgotten names**, where the air hums with static—echoes of whispers that were never meant to be heard. The walls pulse faintly, like veins carrying the last breath of a dying world. The floor is a tapestry of half-buried bones and cracked mirrors, reflecting not faces but **fragments of the self**, each one a door that could be opened. The light here is neither day nor night but a slow, sickly glow from a forgotten lantern, casting everything in hues of ashen silver. The threshold is not a boundary, but a **gateway to the space between**—where the past lingers like a ghost and the future is written in the cracks of the earth.
