Mars was once a mystery to ancient astronomers. They were puzzled by its unstable movement across the sky. Sometimes, it would move directly - in the same direction as the Sun and other celestial objects. Sometimes, it would move in the opposite direction, called "retrograde". In 1609, the German astronomer Johannes Kepler used the superior naked-eye observations of Mars by his Danish colleague Tycho Brahe to figure out the planet's laws of motion. This paved the way for the modern theory of gravity in our solar system. Kepler found that the orbit of Mars was an ellipse shape. He found that it moved with nonuniform but predictable motion. Before this, astronomers had followed the Ptolemaic idea of hierarchies of circular orbits and uniform motion.
