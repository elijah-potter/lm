**The Hollow Hour**

The city slept beneath a sky that had forgotten to shine.
Neon whispers curled like smoke through the cracked windows of abandoned buildings, where the air smelled of rust and old coffee from forgotten cafés. A stray dog barked at nothing, its voice a broken record playing the same notes endlessly, while the rain fell in lazy, silver threads, tracing patterns on the pavement like the fingers of a ghost.

Then the shift.

The clock hands spun past midnight, but the hands of time had lost their rhythm. A single streetlight flickered to life, casting long, trembling shadows that danced like restless spirits across the alleyways. A woman in a coat too warm for the damp air stepped into the light, her silhouette sharp against the blur of the city’s forgotten life. She hummed, the sound low and smooth, a melody that didn’t belong to the hour.

The buildings stood silent, their windows like hollow eyes watching. The dog stopped barking. The rain slowed, then stilled. The world exhaled.

And then—

The hum became a scream.

A car horn blared, sharp and sudden, cutting through the stillness like a blade. A crowd erupted from the shadows—laughter, shouts, the clatter of boots on pavement—no longer the ghostly echo of a dying city, but the roar of life, hungry and unapologetic. The streetlight flared, then died, leaving only the sickly glow of flickering shop signs as the world shifted back, but not the same.

The woman in the coat turned, her face half-lit by the last remnants of the light, and walked away, her shadow stretching long behind her, a ghost in the making.

The city breathed again.
