The streets of downtown hum with quiet energy. Neon signs flicker in muted hues, casting long shadows on cracked pavement. The air smells like rain-soaked concrete and the faint, sweet scent of coffee from an old café that’s been here since before I left. A bench sits empty near the fountain where children used to splash water—now just a puddle, reflecting the muted glow of streetlamps. The library’s doors are slightly ajar, dust motes swirling in the afternoon light filtering through the stained-glass windows. Someone’s guitar plays softly from a balcony, the notes drifting over the hum of distant traffic. The scent of fresh bread from a bakery window lingers, mixed with the scent of old books and the faint metallic tang of a rusted bike frame leaning against a lamppost. The café’s old sign, scratched with time, still reads: *"Morning Coffee – Since 1978."* A new sign beside it advertises the latest local spot, but the energy feels the same—familiar, alive.
