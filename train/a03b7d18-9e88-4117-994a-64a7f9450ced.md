The last light of dusk bleeds through the broken skylight, casting long shadows across the crumbling floorboards. The air smells of damp earth and rust—old iron, melted paint, and something faintly metallic from the corroded pipes beneath. The walls, once painted a faded ochre, are now peeling at the edges, revealing layers of past life: graffiti in faded ink, fingerprints where hands once touched the surface, and the occasional splatter of dried blood.

The windows are shattered in places, but others remain intact, their glass etched with condensation that mirrors the slow, creeping decay of everything around. The furniture is a skeleton of its former self—wood warped by time and moisture, upholstery torn, cushions hollowed out. A lone chair leans against the wall, its armrest splintered, as if someone sat here just yesterday and then never came back.

The floorboards groan underfoot, some sagging where water has seeped through the floor above. A half-empty ashtray glints with embers, still warm. The last thing you see before you turn to leave is the reflection of your own face in the cracked mirror on the wall—your eyes wide, your grip too tight on the door handle.

You walk out into the night, the door clicking shut behind you. The wind carries the sound of distant laughter, but it’s not your own. The place is gone.
