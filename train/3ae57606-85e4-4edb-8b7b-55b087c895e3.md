Silent hum of distant traffic—low rumble, like a sleeping beast. Steamy pavement glistens, reflecting the sickly yellow light of a streetlamp flickering like a dying ember. The air thick with asphalt fumes, sharp tang of gasoline, mixed with damp earth and the stale breath of pavement. Metal clatters under sneakers, voices muffled—whispers of laughter, the occasional scream. Far off, a siren’s wail cuts through the noise, high and metallic, then fades into static. The scent of fried food drifts from a nearby eatery, grease slick with salt, spiced with garlic and smoke. Close by, the faintest hint of wet concrete and rain-soaked leaves. The wind carries the smell of rubber tires on pavement, tires that never stop spinning, never stop screaming.
