The space between breaths hums with what isn’t there—
a hollow ache that lingers like a ghost’s echo,
soft but insistent, pressing against the ribs
of a body that hasn’t stopped moving, though it’s
not *there* anymore.
The hands that once held the world now clutch
the edges of silence, trembling.
Light reflects off the walls like broken glass,
each shard catching the ghost of a face that’s
gone, but still stands in the corner,
watching, unblinking.
Time dissolves into a slow, unnatural crawl—
days stretch into nothing, months blur into
a single, endless moment where absence
feels like presence because it’s all that’s left.
The air tastes like dust from a room
that was never cleaned, where the furniture
has settled into the shape of sorrow,
and the floorboards groan under the weight
of what no longer belongs. The voice that used
to fill the house now only whispers,
a voice that’s been hollowed out, left to
linger like a song that’s been played once
and never finished. The body remembers
the way it used to move, but the motion
is now a question mark, a half-remembered
gesture that doesn’t quite fit anymore.
The absence isn’t a thing—it’s the space
between the last goodbye and the next
silence, the quiet that follows the
last note, the pause that feels like a
forever. It’s the way the world keeps
turning, but only for others, and the
heart, though still beating, doesn’t know
how to beat anymore. It’s the weight of
everything that’s been left behind,
a burden carried in the hollow of the chest,
a memory that’s still there, still aching,
still waiting to be told.
