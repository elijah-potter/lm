The sun set behind the ancient oak, its gnarled roots twisting into the earth like forgotten fingers. Beneath the canopy, the first child emerged from the mist—small, pale, and clutching a hollowed acorn in their tiny hands. The acorn glowed faintly, pulsing with an unnatural light.

The village elder whispered, *"The oak remembers. It remembers all."* The child did not understand, but the acorn’s glow intensified, casting shadows that danced like spirits.

Years passed. The village grew, children were born, and the oak remained silent. Then, one evening, a storm rolled in. Lightning split the sky, and the acorn’s light flared to life, revealing a figure wrapped in rags at the oak’s roots—older, weary, but not the child. The child stood before them, now a man, his face lined with time.

*"You came back,"* the elder said. The man reached into his coat and pulled out the same hollowed acorn, its light now steady. The oak groaned, its branches arching toward the sky. The child’s eyes widened.

The acorn pulsed brighter, and the earth trembled. The man’s voice was a whisper. *"The cycle begins again."* The village elder knelt, gripping the acorn between his palms. The mist swirled, and the child vanished—only to reappear as a child once more, clutching the glowing acorn as the oak’s roots unfurled beneath them.

The light returned to the world.
