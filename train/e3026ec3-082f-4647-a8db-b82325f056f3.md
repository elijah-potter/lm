The ruins of an ancient stone bridge, half-sunk in mist-laden riverbanks, its archways cracked by centuries. Cobwebs drift like spider-webs across the crumbling planks, where the air hums with the faintest echo of distant laughter. Beyond, a forest of skeletal trees clings to the cliffs, their gnarled roots twisted like ancient hands grasping the earth. The path ahead splits: one fork leads to a crumbling watchtower, its bell long silent, and the other to a hidden clearing where lanterns flicker in glass bottles, their light flickering like dying stars. The threshold here is not just between worlds but between time itself—where the past bleeds into the present, and the unseen watches from the shadows.
