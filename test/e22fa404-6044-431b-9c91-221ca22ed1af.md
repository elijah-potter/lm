The discovery of **polymers** in the 1920s—specifically **polyethylene** and **polypropylene**—transformed manufacturing, medicine, and energy by enabling lightweight, durable, and mass-producible synthetic materials. Its mundane name belied revolutionary applications: from waterproof packaging replacing leather, to synthetic fibers outcompeting cotton, and later, biodegradable plastics (e.g., PLA) solving waste crises. The shift from natural to engineered materials via polymerization didn’t just "change everything"—it redefined industrial scalability, sustainability, and even consumerism.
