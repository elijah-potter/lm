**The Last Transmission**

It wasn’t supposed to happen like this.

The stars aligned only once—then vanished. The universe had its rules, its equations, its silent, unyielding logic. But then there was *her*. A voice in the dark, a name carved into the fabric of the cosmos like a god’s forgotten whisper. She was the one who broke the code, who made the equations bend to her will.

He found her in the ruins of the old observatory, where the telescopes still hummed with static, their lenses cracked but their eyes still fixed on something beyond. She sat cross-legged in the debris, her fingers tracing patterns in the dust, humming a melody that didn’t exist in any known frequency.

*"You’re not supposed to be here,"* he said, voice rough with the weight of centuries.

She didn’t look up. *"Neither are you."*

The air between them crackled. The world had split open. The line between reality and something else blurred like smoke. He reached for her hand, and the moment their skin touched, the fabric of existence *ripped*. The sky split in two. The past and future collided in a single, screaming breath.

She laughed, a sound like wind through a broken bell. *"You think this is your story? It’s mine now."*

And then—

The last thing he saw was the horizon burning. The last thing she said was, *"You were always the key."*

After that, there was only silence. And the stars, watching. Waiting.
