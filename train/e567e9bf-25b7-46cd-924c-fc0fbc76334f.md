The rain-soaked road curled through the hollow at dusk, its surface slick with the weight of unseen storms. **Elias** gripped the reins tighter as the old gelding’s hooves splashed through puddles, the lantern’s flickering glow casting long shadows across the barn’s rotting planks. Inside, the scent of damp hay and old wood clung to the air, but the real stench was the fear coiled tight in his chest. His father stood at the hearth, arms crossed, face unreadable beneath the firelight. *"A man’s word is his bond,"* his father had said that morning, his voice low but steady. *"But bonds are made of iron, Elias. And iron bends."* Now, the question hung between them: *Could he break it?* The contract lay unopened on the table—a promise sealed with blood and oaths. The wind howled through the eaves as the first drop of sweat trickled down his temple. The gelding stamped once, its breath fogging the air. **What would it cost to keep his name intact?**
