The lab technician’s autoclave malfunctioned mid-cycle, releasing a vapor that dissolved the last of Dr. Elias Voss’s research notes on *the Seventh Error*. The lab’s security protocols, set to detect anomalous gas levels, triggered the emergency shutdown, but not before the fumes seeped into the hallway. By the time the alarms sounded, the elevator doors had already opened, revealing the hallway’s fluorescent lighting flickering erratically—like the static before a signal cuts out.

Voss’s assistant, Mara, stepped into the hallway, her breath fogging in the cold air. The lights stabilized, but the walls pulsed faintly, as if breathing. A low, rhythmic hum filled the space, and from the ventilation shaft, a single, perfect black spider emerged, its legs clicking against the tiles like a metronome.

“It’s not a mistake,” Mara whispered, her voice catching. “It’s… a *step*.”

The spider’s head swiveled toward them, its eyes reflecting the dim glow of the corridor. Then, the ventilation cover above them *split open*—not as a door, but as a *mouth*, and from it, something darker and faster than the spider emerged. The air turned thick, heavy with the scent of ozone and something older, something that made Mara’s teeth ache. The last thing she saw before the lights went out was Voss’s reflection in the hallway mirror—his eyes already wide, his mouth moving in silent words she couldn’t hear.

The next morning, the lab’s security footage showed only the elevator doors closing on an empty hallway. The next day, the city’s power grid flickered. By the third, the blackout spread. And when the last transmission from Voss’s encrypted terminal crackled through the static—

*“It’s not a mistake. It’s a correction.”*—

the static itself *answered*.
