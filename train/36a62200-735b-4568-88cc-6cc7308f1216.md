In the hollowed-out heart of the old mill, the last candle burned low, casting flickering shadows on the stone walls. The air smelled of damp earth and forgotten time. The floorboards groaned under the weight of Elias’s boots, each step echoing like a drumbeat in the silent room.

Outside, the wind howled through the broken windows, rattling the glass like a chorus of restless spirits. Through the narrow window slit, Elias could see the distant glow of a streetlamp, its light struggling against the storm clouds. The mill’s doors stood slightly ajar, a gaping mouth of wood and rust.

A figure stepped into the dim light—tall, clad in a coat that seemed to swallow the cold. Their face was obscured by a hood, but their boots, worn smooth from years of travel, left no doubt: they had come.

Elias exhaled sharply, pressing a hand to the cold stone. The stranger did not speak. The silence stretched, thick with unspoken words. Then, the door creaked shut behind them, sealing the moment in silence.
