Calculating the cfm (Cubic Feet per Minute) of an air compressor is just one of the steps you need to take before making a purchase. The cfm rating of an air compressor suggests how much air volume the device can generate in a set period, usually minutes.
This rating is crucial when deciding on an appropriate size for your business or workspace, as it will show you how powerful and efficient the tool is in comparison to other models available.
Cubic feet per minute (CFM) is a measurement of how much air a compressor can handle. It is used to determine the power and size of an air compressor. Understanding CFM is important when deciding which air compressor is best suited for your needs.
What is CFM?
CFM stands for “cubic feet per minute” and measures the rate at which air is moved into and out of space. This is important to take into consideration when you need an air compressor for your project. The size of the compressor will depend upon how much air volume, or CFM, is needed.
The basic equation to determine the required CFM for a particular project is:
CFM = (time in minutes x volume of space) / 60
To better understand what this means, let’s look at an example. Let’s say you have a workshop that is 40 feet long by 20 feet wide by 10 feet high and you need to replace the entire volume of air in five minutes:
CFM = (5(minutes) x 8,000 (cubic ft))/60
CFM = 1,333 CFM
This calculation tells us that to get a complete changeover of this area would require an air compressor with 1,333 cubic feet per minute of capacity (CFM). Keep in mind that other factors could influence the size of the compressor necessary such as openings or machines running inside the space. Make sure to adjust the equation accordingly if necessary.
What is the importance of CFM in an air compressor?
Cubic feet per minute (CFM) is an important measurement to consider when purchasing an air compressor. CFM determines how much air the compressor can output per minute, and subsequently how quickly it can run air tools or fill air tanks.
For smaller jobs, such as inflating a tire or powering a nailer for light work, low CFM tools can be fine. But for heavier-duty applications like sandblasting, operating a die grinder, and using spray guns, higher CFM tools may be required to perform the job efficiently.

Determine the volume of the air tank
CFM is an acronym for Cubic Feet per Minute and it is the measurement used to calculate the capacity of an air compressor tank.
To accurately determine the CFM of a compressor, you must first determine the volume of the air tank. This can be done by measuring the length, width, and height of the tank in feet and multiplying them together.
For example, if your air tank is 12 feet long, 3 feet wide, and 6 feet tall, its volume would be 216 cubic feet (12x3x6=216). Once you know your air tank’s volume, you can use this number to calculate CFM by dividing it by 60 (the number of seconds in a minute).
So for our example above 216/60 = 3.6 CFM. It’s important to note that this calculation does not take into consideration any losses due to friction or restriction from hoses or other components; it only provides a baseline calculation when determining your system’s capacity.
Calculate the pressure (PSI) of the air tank
Calculating the CFM (cubic feet per minute) of airflow from a compressed air tank requires knowing the volume of the tank and the pressure reading. Obtaining an accurate pressure reading can be challenging because, depending on the type of compressor, this may require connecting additional equipment to provide an accurate measurement.
For compressors with a manual pressure gauge connected, reading the PSI is fairly straightforward simply read and record the value. However, tanks with added gauges that display percentage readings need to have their max PSI setting calibrated in order to get an accurate reading.
Once you’ve obtained your accurate pressure (PSI) value for your air tank, you can then use it in conjunction with your known tank volume to calculate your total CFM flow of air from the compressor.
Calculate the flow rate of the air compressor
Calculating the flow rate of your air compressor is important for proper sizing and for system efficiency. Knowing the exact volume of air moving through your compressors helps to ensure that no capacity is wasted in distributing or using the compressed air.
The rate of airflow, also known as cubic feet per minute (CFM), can be calculated by measuring the fan speed, the pressure, and the fan blade size.
To calculate CFM, you must first determine the fan RPM (revolutions per minute). The largest fans will have slower RPMs compared to smaller fans with higher RPMs. If possible, find a spec sheet or data label on the fan that will provide this number. Otherwise use a basic tachometer to measure its speed.
Once RPM is determined, calculate the pressure in pounds per square inch (psi). This can be done with a basic psi gauge connected to a test port on an air compressor tank. For an accurate reading, allow adequate time for pressure levels to normalize prior to taking measurements.
