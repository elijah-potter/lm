The clock struck midnight, and the alleyway hummed with the ghostly whispers of forgotten dreams. Old Mr. Holloway’s lantern flickered, casting long shadows that danced like skeletal hands across the cobblestones. He adjusted his spectacles, his breath fogging the glass, and whispered, *"They’re not dead, just sleeping."*

A rusted key jingled from his pocket. It had belonged to his late wife—her last gift before she vanished into the mist of the river that night. He reached for it, his fingers trembling. The key fit into the lock of the attic door, the one they never opened.

Inside, the air was thick with the scent of old books and dust. The walls were lined with ledgers, their pages yellowed with time, each entry a secret buried in ink. Mr. Holloway traced the edges of a ledger titled *"The Holloway Ledger: Transactions of the Unseen."* His pulse quickened. *What would she have written here?*

He pulled out a yellowed letter, its edges singed. The handwriting was familiar—his wife’s. *"Dearest,"* it read, *"they’ve been watching. The ones who wear masks but carry chains. Don’t let them find you first."* The letter fell into his lap. The key turned again, the door creaking open.

The attic was empty.

But the ledger lay open, its final entry scrawled in hasty script: *"They’re waking. And they’re hungry."*
