The grand doors of the old library stand open, their brass handles rusted but still firm. The scent of aged paper and damp wood lingers—someone’s favorite book, perhaps, left behind on a shelf. The marble floor creaks underfoot, a familiar sound that feels like a welcome home. Bookshelves line the walls, though some shelves sag slightly, their spines cracked from years of use. The high ceiling, once towering, now seems slightly lower, as if the space itself has absorbed the passage of time.

Over there, the reading room glows softly under the same lamplight you remember. The worn leather chairs are still there, but the cushions are thinner, and a single, half-read novel sits on the desk—your own, untouched since you last stepped inside. The scent of coffee from the café below lingers in the air, mingling with the faint smell of aged leather and old paper.

The library feels like a living thing, breathing softly between the cracks in the walls. You almost forget how long you’ve been gone.
