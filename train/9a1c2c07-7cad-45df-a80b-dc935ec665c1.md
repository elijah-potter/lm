**The Whispered Secret**

The alley smelled of damp stone and forgotten dreams, the kind of place where time moved slower than a child’s breath. Raindrops clung to the cobblestones like tears, each one carrying a story no one would listen to. I crouched beside the rusted gate, my fingers tracing the edge of a half-finished sketch I’d been carrying for weeks—something abstract, a half-formed idea of a world that didn’t belong to this one.

My phone buzzed in my pocket. A text from her: *"You shouldn’t be here."* The message was simple, but the way it was written made my stomach twist. She knew. She *had* to know.

I exhaled, the breath curling into the damp air. This was what I came for—proof. Not just of what I feared, but of what she *wanted* me to see.

The sketch wasn’t finished. The lines were jagged, the colors too bright, too real. It wasn’t art. It was a map. A map of something that didn’t exist in the real world, but pulsed beneath the skin of this one. And if I looked close enough, I could almost see it—like a shadow moving just beyond the light.

I stood, the sketch clutched in my hand, and turned toward her. She stood in the flickering glow of the streetlamp, her back to me, her hands wrapped around a glass of something amber and cold. The way she moved—slow, deliberate—told me she knew. She had been waiting.

*"You shouldn’t be here,"* she said, her voice smooth, like silk over glass. *"But you always do."*

I didn’t answer. Instead, I held up the sketch. The lines blurred, and for a second, I swear I saw it—the thing beneath it all, the thing that had been whispering in the dark for years.

Then she laughed. A sound like breaking glass. *"You’re too late."*

The air turned cold. The rain stopped.

And the world changed.

**The Truth**

The sketch wasn’t a map. It was a warning.

Her name was **Liora**. She’d been my teacher, my friend, my greatest mistake. I’d known for months what she was—something between a goddess and a monster, something that fed on dreams and memories, turning them into something *hers*. But I didn’t know how much of it was her choice.

She had been testing me. Not for answers, but for *compliance*. For the way I’d stayed, for the way I *wanted* to believe.

*"You’re too late,"* she said, and it wasn’t a lie.

Because the thing that lived inside her wasn’t just hunger. It was *hungry*. And it had found a way into the world. Into *us*.

She reached into her coat pocket and pulled out a small, silver box. Inside was a single, perfect teardrop—a fragment of the thing that was growing in her veins. It shimmered, pulsing like a heartbeat.

*"You always do,"* she whispered. *"You always think you’re the only one who sees."*

I don’t know what I did next. I don’t know what I *am* now.

But I know this: the world isn’t broken. It’s *starving*.

And something is coming to eat it.
