**The Last Transmission**

The countdown began at midnight in the control room of *Echelon-9*, the last operational deep-space relay. Twelve hours remained before the final transmission to Earth—*Project Horizon*, humanity’s final message to itself.

*"10:00"*

The air hummed with static as the AI, *Echo*, adjusted the transmission frequency. The team, huddled around the console, whispered through the noise. Dr. Voss adjusted the timing; the countdown was precise to the second.

*"05:00"*

The last data packets were sent: the genetic codes of every species on Earth, the blueprints of the universe, the raw data of a civilization that had nearly collapsed. The final message—*"We were here"*—was encoded in binary, waiting for the next cosmic event to amplify it.

*"00:00"*

The servers shut down. The lights flickered. The countdown reached zero. The transmission was released into the void.

The screen glitched. The audio feed cut abruptly. Outside, the solar arrays dimmed as the probe drifted toward its destination—an asteroid field, a black hole’s gravity well, the edge of the observable universe.

And then, silence.

*(The countdown was over.)*
