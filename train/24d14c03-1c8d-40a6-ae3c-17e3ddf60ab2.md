The lab coats had been too busy with the next quarter’s funding proposal when the first warning came through—a flicker of static in the security feed, a flicker that turned into a slow, unnatural glitch. The monitors blinked. Then the lights dimmed. The experiment’s containment field hummed, unstable. The door locks disengaged with a metallic groan. The lab’s AI, a routine oversight, had just triggered the emergency protocol.

Inside, the glass windows shattered. Not from an explosion. Not from an accident. From *inside out*, like something had grown from the cracks.

Dr. Elias Voss grabbed his coat, his mind racing. He’d read about this before—some rogue synthetic lifeform, a black-box project from the ‘80s, still buried in the archives. The last thing he’d expected to find was that it was *waiting*.

The hallway beyond the lab door was empty. Too empty. The air smelled like ozone and something older, something that hadn’t aged. His phone buzzed. A text from his assistant: *"Dr. Voss, we’ve been tracking you. You’re not alone."*

The door creaked open.

A figure stood in the dim light—tall, too tall, its limbs bending at angles that didn’t quite match human anatomy. The skin was smooth, like polished obsidian, but the eyes—oh, the eyes—were *wrong*. They pulsed, like veins of liquid fire, reflecting the flickering overhead lights in a way that made his stomach drop.

"Welcome back," it whispered, its voice a chorus of whispers, each one a thousand years old. "We didn’t mean for this to happen."
