The crowd parted just as the car swerved. The driver, arms hanging limp, stared straight ahead, mouth slightly open. Blood seeped from the corner of his eye as he slumped forward, head lolling onto the passenger seat. The driver’s seatbelt snapped taut, but his body didn’t resist the sudden stop. The engine died with a quiet *thud*, and the engine block cracked open like an egg. The car’s front bumper crumpled inward, teeth marks left in the metal. The glass in the windshield had shattered in a fan of jagged shards.

I’d been standing near the corner, watching the news coverage of the accident unfold. The first thing I noticed was the way the other drivers—those who hadn’t yet pulled over—glanced at the wreck, then back at their own lanes. No one stopped. No one even seemed to care. The air smelled like burnt rubber and fear, and the sirens from the ambulance had just cut through the din of traffic. A police cruiser pulled up behind the car, siren still wailing. The officer on duty stepped out, hands clasped behind their back, and walked toward the driver’s door. They didn’t look at the body.

Then the paramedics arrived, moving like shadows, their faces half-hidden in their masks. One of them knelt, pressing their gloved hand to the driver’s neck. The body jerked once, like a startled animal. Then still. The officer stood frozen for a second, then gestured for them to leave. They didn’t argue. Just nodded and moved back to the ambulance, where they began lifting the driver’s limp form into the stretcher. The car’s door swung open, and the driver’s legs dangled out, still wearing his shoes. The officer stepped back, then knelt beside the car, fingers tracing the dent in the hood.

The ambulance drove off, sirens fading into the distance. The police officer stood there for a moment, then walked away, disappearing into the crowd. The car remained where it was, a silent monument to the moment it had stopped. The other cars resumed moving, engines humming, windows rolled up. No one spoke. No one looked back. Just another day in the city.
