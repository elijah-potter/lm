The rain hammered against the rusted roof of an abandoned car as the GPS beeped erratically. The neon sign of a long-closed diner flickered—*"CLOSED"* in faded letters—while the streetlights cast long, shifting shadows. The person stepped out, boots splashing through puddles, the headlight of a distant truck cutting through the downpour. A faint, unfamiliar sound of distant traffic echoed from the highway, while the familiar hum of their phone’s battery drain grew louder. A signpost, half-buried in weeds, read **"LOST LANDS"** in crumbling letters. Their phone’s battery died. The map on their wristband flickered one last time before the screen went black. They stood there, staring at the road that led neither to home nor the nearest town, the cold rain turning the pavement slick underfoot. The wind carried the scent of damp earth and old metal, and the weight of an unanswered question settled over them: *Where was I supposed to be?*
