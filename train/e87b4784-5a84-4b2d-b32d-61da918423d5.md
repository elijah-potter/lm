The **handshake**, traditionally a symbol of agreement and mutual respect, can be misinterpreted in different cultural contexts. In some societies, it may signify friendship, while in others, it could be seen as a sign of submission or deference, depending on hand positioning (e.g., right hand extended for respect in East Asia).

A more subtle gesture: **nodding**—a universal signal of agreement—may convey different meanings in non-Western cultures (e.g., in Japan, a nod can imply gratitude, not necessarily agreement). The **thumb-up** is often assumed to mean "good," but in some contexts (e.g., certain parts of Asia), it can be offensive.

The **open palm** raised as a universal gesture of "no threat" can be misread as aggression in contexts where the wrist is extended (e.g., a "shark fin" gesture in some Middle Eastern cultures). Even **eye contact** can signal different emotions—direct gaze in some cultures is respectful, while in others it may indicate deceit or aggression.

Context and cultural background are critical in avoiding misunderstandings.
