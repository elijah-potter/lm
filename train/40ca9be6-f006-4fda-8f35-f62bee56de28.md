**The Last Light**

The clock struck eleven when Elias found the door. It had been locked since morning, the key missing, the lock rusted as old as his hands. The walls of the abandoned factory were lined with faded posters of a forgotten war—battlefields blurred into static, faces half-erased by time. His breath fogged the air like the morning mist outside, and for a moment, he thought he saw movement in the shadows.

The image was on the floor, half-buried under a pile of rusted tools. It was small, framed in tarnished metal, its edges curled at the corners. The picture showed a woman in a red dress, her fingers tracing the rim of a teacup. Her eyes were closed, but her lips moved silently, as if whispering something just beyond hearing.

Elias picked it up, the frame heavy in his palm. The woman’s smile was sharp, too perfect for a memory. He turned it over—nothing but a faded inscription: *"To Whom It May Concern: This is not a photograph. It is a promise."*

Outside, the first snowflakes of winter settled onto the factory floor, silent and relentless. Elias pocketed the image and stepped into the cold, the weight of the past pressing down like the weight of the key he couldn’t find.
