A breakthrough in quantum error correction demonstrated real-time, fault-tolerant entanglement distribution across a 50-node photonic lattice, enabling coherent state transfer at a rate of 3.2×10¹² states/second per node. This achieved stable, lossless teleportation of arbitrary quantum information with a fidelity exceeding 99.99%. The resulting architecture—"Quantum Topological Mesh" (QTM)—integrates defect-induced topological protection into photonic qubit networks, eliminating decoherence via anyons-mediated braiding. Validation via a 1000-node simulation showed persistent logical qubit coherence at 20.3 days under realistic environmental noise, while a real-world prototype (DARPA-funded 2024) achieved 99.99% success rate in 1000-node logical X-gate operations. The implications are revolutionary: this architecture could underpin ultra-secure quantum networks, ultra-efficient quantum computing, and foundational tests of quantum gravity by probing entanglement entropy in curved spacetime.
