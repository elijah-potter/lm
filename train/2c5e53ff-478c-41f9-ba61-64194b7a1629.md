In the dim glow of an old desk lamp, Elias traced the edges of his half-finished sketches with a finger dusted in ash. His memory was a tangled web of half-remembered conversations, half-forgotten promises—each thread a whisper of a person who had once mattered. The memory of his childhood teacher, Mrs. Hargrove, flickered like a candle in the wind. She had once told him, *"The map is not the territory, but it’s the first step."* That line had stuck with him, though he’d never told anyone why.

Outside, the city’s hum grew louder, a relentless pulse of cars and sirens. Elias adjusted his glasses, the lenses fogging with the effort of focusing. His plan was a list scribbled on the back of an envelope—*find Mrs. Hargrove’s old classroom, ask about the missing map, secure a lead on the dealer before the next raid*. But the list was incomplete. The memory of her voice—warm, sharp-edged—had been the only thing that kept him from quitting. The map wasn’t a treasure; it was proof. Proof that the city’s corruption wasn’t just a story, but a living thing.

He exhaled slowly, the scent of old paper and cigarette smoke thick in the air. The plan was clear now: tomorrow, he’d start with the archives at St. Mark’s. But first, he needed to remember. The desk creaked as he leaned in, the lamp’s flicker casting long shadows across the pages he hadn’t read in years. The map wasn’t just a clue—it was a key. And keys, he’d learned, were often hidden where no one expected them.
