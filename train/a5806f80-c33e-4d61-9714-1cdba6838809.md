After the seismic shift, the valley lay buried under a perpetual twilight, the sky now a fractured canvas of bruised purples and sickly golds, stretched thin over the horizon like a wounded membrane. The ancient riverbed, once a ribbon of emerald, now a cracked, parched riverbed of exposed clay and rusted iron—its banks lined with skeletal trees that had not yet succumbed to the weight of the earth. The forest, once a dense, whispering emerald thicket, had been scoured clean, leaving only the gnarled roots and the hollowed-out trunks of giants, their canopies collapsed like the ribs of some dying beast. The air hummed with the low, persistent drone of unseen machinery, the scent of ozone and dampened metal clinging to everything, a metallic tang that made the throat ache. Far off, the ruins of the old bridges stood—half-sunk in the earth, their timbers bent and warped, the once-grand arches now twisted, like the mouth of some sleeping monster. The land itself seemed to breathe, rising and falling in slow, uneven waves, as if the earth had been given a new pulse, a slow, deliberate heartbeat. The sun, if it still existed, was a distant, bloodless orb, its light filtered through the ruins of the old world, casting long, jagged shadows that moved unnaturally, as if alive. The air was thick with the scent of decaying earth and something metallic, the faintest echo of a forgotten industry. The horizon was a blur of static and distortion, as though the world itself was fighting against the edges of perception. The landscape was no longer a place of harmony, but a wound—one that pulsed, and in the quiet moments, seemed to sing.
