A quiet evening in a bustling café, where the hum of conversation and clinking chairs drowned out the faint whisper of their own thoughts. They sat at a corner table, the hum of the city outside blurring into static, their reflection in the window a stranger staring back. A group of friends laughed over coffee, their laughter echoing like a chord they didn’t recognize—too familiar, yet foreign.

The scarf they’d chosen, the hemline of their sweater, even the way they held their cup—all felt like borrowed pieces. A stranger’s jacket lay casually over the back of the chair, its fabric too warm, too *theirs*. They reached for it, fingers brushing the fabric, and for the first time in years, they felt the weight of it in their chest—not as a gift, but as a burden.

A waitress approached, offering a menu, and they hesitated, their eyes scanning the words like they were a foreign language. The words *chocolate mousse* and *vegan* blurred together. A child’s laughter spilled into the café, but it wasn’t laughter—they recognized the sound of their own voice, the one that had once belonged to them.

They looked down at their hands, fingers tangled in their own skin, and realized they’d stopped moving. The café faded into silence, the city outside a blur. They had arrived too late to the party they’d always been missing. The scarf felt heavier now, the jacket too familiar. The café was a mirror, but they were the reflection, and they couldn’t decide if it was them or someone else entirely.
