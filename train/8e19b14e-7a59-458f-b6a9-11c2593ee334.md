The memory of that evening remains fragmented—fragments of laughter, half-finished thoughts, and a room that smelled like old books and something sweet, like vanilla and damp stone.

She mentioned she’d been reading *The Book of Lost Things* again, but I’d only heard snatches—*"the boy who followed the map to the sea"*—before she cut off mid-sentence, her voice catching. I remember her hands trembling slightly, fingers brushing the edge of her coffee cup as she said, *"I don’t know if I can do this anymore."*

He—*he*—had been quiet most of the time, just nodding, his dark eyes fixed on the fire. Once, he reached for her sleeve, and she pulled away, but not before I saw the way his jaw tightened. The fire crackled. A plate of cookies was passed. We all ate in silence.

She left after the third cookie, her coat still on, her purse slung over one shoulder. I asked if she’d be back, but she just shook her head, her voice hollow. *"No. Not tonight."*

The night was cold. Outside, the wind howled like a ghost. He lit a cigarette, his fingers still trembling. He didn’t say much after that, just stared into the flames, watching them dance like they knew something we didn’t.

By morning, the memory was already dissolving—one of those things that fades with time, like a dream you half-remember the next day. But sometimes, when the wind picks up just right, I swear I hear it again: the faint sound of a child’s laughter, the crinkle of a page turning, and the ghost of a question whispered just out of reach.
