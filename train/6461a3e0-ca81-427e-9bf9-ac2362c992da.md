The abandoned plan involved deploying a high-altitude solar array network in the Arctic Circle to harness perpetual daylight for clean energy. Reasons included:

1. **Technical Feasibility Challenges**: Extreme cold and harsh winds made structural integrity and maintenance difficult.
2. **Economic Viability**: Higher operational costs offset potential savings, leading to a break-even analysis favoring traditional energy sources.
3. **Environmental Concerns**: Disruption to local ecosystems and potential long-term ecological impacts outweighed energy benefits.
4. **Policy Shifts**: Changing global energy policies and subsidies favored decentralized renewables over centralized Arctic projects.
5. **Geopolitical Risks**: Tensions with neighboring nations over resource access and territorial claims made large-scale deployment untenable.
6. **Public Opposition**: Indigenous communities and local residents opposed the project, citing cultural and environmental harm.
7. **Market Volatility**: Fluctuations in solar technology costs and grid integration complexities made long-term planning unreliable.

**Decision**: Shift focus to modular, low-altitude solar farms in temperate zones with better regulatory support and lower maintenance costs.
