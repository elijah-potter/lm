A wandering cart rolled through mist-laden valleys, its wheels turning toward a distant marker: a crumbling tower in the foothills. The driver swore it stood at the crossroads of every path he’d taken.

That evening, the tower’s shadow stretched unnaturally, then vanished. The next morning, it had replaced the blacksmith’s forge at the village square, its spire now a chimney. The villagers muttered of "the shifting hand" but moved on, leaving the cart’s crew to trace its trail.

By the third day, the marker was a riverbank where the current carved a new shore every dawn. The crew rowed, but the water swallowed the path before they reached it. A map unfolded from the captain’s coat—pages rewriting themselves, ink bleeding into new routes. The compass spun wildly, then pointed north, south, east, and west simultaneously.

A third day passed. The cart found itself in a forest where every tree bore a different fruit, and the fruits changed when plucked. The guide, a gaunt figure with eyes like polished river stones, told them: *"The destination isn’t where you’re going—it’s where you’ve been, and where you’ll be again."* The cart’s wheels creaked as it turned, but the road ahead was already erasing beneath its tires.

They stopped at a bridge of glass, suspended over a gulf. The other side was a mirror of the present, but the reflection flickered like static. The guide’s voice echoed: *"All roads lead to nowhere."* The cart’s wheels caught fire. The fire dissolved into smoke, which swirled into the sky, leaving no trace—only the faint scent of ozone and the hum of a voice in the wind: *"You are never here."* The cart sat still, a relic in a field of rusted nails, as the earth beneath it shifted. The marker? Gone. The journey? A loop.
