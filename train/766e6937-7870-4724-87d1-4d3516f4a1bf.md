A crumbling brick archway, half-hidden behind ivy, marks the entrance to a forgotten courtyard. The stones bear the weight of decades, their cracks filled with moss and forgotten time. Yet the space hums with something permanent beneath its surface: a network of roots, buried deep, weaving through the earth like veins. The air smells faintly of wet stone and old wood, carrying the scent of a garden that once bloomed—now reduced to brittle husks. Overhead, a single, stubborn oak leans over the arch, its branches gnarled but strong, as if resisting the pull of decay. This is a place where time bends, where the illusion of transience hides the quiet endurance of what was meant to last. The soil beneath the arch is rich, stubborn, holding memories of the past that refuse to be erased.
