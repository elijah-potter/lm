A flickering neon sign on a rusted storefront reads *"Last Chance"* in faded letters, its bulb dimmed to a sickly amber glow. Behind it, a cramped alley buzzes with the hum of old machinery and distant voices—some in a foreign language, others muffled by the roar of a dying refrigerator. The air smells like ozone and something metallic, the kind that lingers after a storm. A single, half-melted ice cream cone sits abandoned in the gutter, its cone twisted like a question mark. The pavement beneath is slick with rain that hasn’t stopped, but the puddles reflect only the flickering light and the ghostly shapes of people who were here yesterday but are gone now—gone like the warmth in the abandoned café across the street, where the last customer left with a half-empty coffee cup and a half-empty soul.

A flickering streetlamp casts long shadows that stretch into the void between the buildings, where the pavement cracks like glass. A child’s toy, a plastic teapot with a snapped handle, sits in the middle of the road, forgotten. The teapot’s legs are bent at odd angles, as if someone once tried to right it and failed. A stray dog curls up in the shadows, its fur matted with dirt and something darker. It watches you with eyes that have seen too much.

The sky above is a bruised canvas of storm clouds, the kind that promise rain but never quite deliver. The wind carries the scent of damp earth and something metallic—blood, maybe, or rust. The air feels alive, like it’s holding its breath, waiting for something to happen. The only constant is the slow, creeping sense that this place isn’t *here* anymore. It’s a moment, a pause, a thing that will dissolve into memory—or worse, into nothing.
