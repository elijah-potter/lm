The rain outside blurred the neon glow of the city, reflecting in the puddles like shattered glass. Elias stood at the window of the abandoned bookstore, his fingers tracing the spine of a book he hadn’t touched in months—*The Weight of the World*. The scent of old paper and damp wood clung to the air, thick and suffocating.

He had come here for the same reason every night: to forget. But the city didn’t forget. It pulsed, indifferent, its rhythm a drumbeat of sirens and distant laughter. His phone buzzed in his pocket—another missed call, another message he’d never reply to. He pocketed it again, the weight of it heavy.

The door creaked open. A figure in a raincoat stepped inside, their silhouette sharp against the dim light. They set down a small, wrapped bundle on the counter. "Left it for you," they said, their voice low, rough. "You were here last week, too."

Elias didn’t move. "What is this?"

The stranger hesitated, then pulled back the paper. Inside was a photograph—a young woman, her hair tangled, her eyes wide with something like terror. "She was waiting for you," they said, voice breaking. "She was trying to tell you something."

Elias exhaled sharply, the air hissing between his teeth. The stranger didn’t wait for his response. They turned and walked toward the door, but paused. "You shouldn’t leave," they murmured. "Not like this."

The door slammed shut behind them.

Elias stood for a long moment, the photograph clutched in his hand. Outside, the rain fell harder. He turned, his boots splashing through the puddles, and walked out into the storm. The city swallowed him whole.
