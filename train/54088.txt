The Gardenia genus, known mostly for plants that have fragrant, creamy white blooms, contains more than 200 species. The plants' hardiness depends on the variety. For example, Gardenia augusta (Gardenia jasminoides) is hardy in U.S. Department of Agriculture zones 7b through 10. Gardenias prefer heat, and cold wind may cause leaf burn, bud drop and other kinds of damage. If your climate has prolonged freezing weather, protect your gardenia by taking it indoors for winter. Inside, you can give the gardenia the care it needs to stay healthy.
Place the potted gardenia in front of a south-facing window that receives direct sunlight for at least one-half of the day. If such a position isn't an option, place a fluorescent light or plant light a few inches above the gardenia. The plant will survive with less light, but it will not flower, and its leaves may turn yellow.
Remove faded blooms, crowded shoots and dead or diseased material.
Keep the temperature of the gardenia's room at 70 to 75 degrees Fahrenheit during the day and 60 degrees Fahrenheit at night. Temperature differences of more than 15 degrees between day and night will stress the plant.
Water the gardenia as you normally would if it were outdoors. Keep its soil slightly damp but never soggy. Ideally, the top 2 inches of the soil should always feel moist to the touch. Allowing the soil to dry out causes root shrinkage and cracking.
Operate a humidifier in the plant's room to boost the humidity level, which is usually low in winter. Alternatively, set the gardenia's pot on a tray filled with pebbles and water. Misting the plant's leaves with water provides only a temporary increase in humidity.
Inspect your gardenia regularly for spider mites. Spider mites, which commonly attack gardenias, hide on the bottom of leaves and are very tiny; use a magnifying glass to find them. Spray the leaves with a strong blast of water to knock off spider mites, or control the pests by applying an insecticidal oil on the leaves as directed by the product's label.
Things You Will Need
- Fluorescent light or plant light (optional)
- Scissors or pruning shears
- Room thermometer
- Watering can
- Magnifying glass
- Insecticidal oil (optional)
- Put your gardenia back outdoors in spring after the danger of frost has passed.
- Setting the gardenia beneath or above air vents or heaters can cause it to dry out.
- Fertilizing a gardenia is not necessary during winter. Resume regular fertilizing in spring after you put the gardenia back outside.
- Your gardenia probably will not flower indoors. That is normal and does not mean anything is wrong with the plant.
