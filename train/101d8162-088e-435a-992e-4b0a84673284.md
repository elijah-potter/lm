The morning coffee ritual started as a simple habit: one cup to boost focus before work. Over time, the habit expanded to two cups—then three—each with increasing caffeine content. The transition from one to three became inevitable as stress levels rose with deadlines. Initially, the habit was intentional; later, it became automatic, consuming time that could have been spent on deeper work. Friends noted the coffee dependency, but the habit felt ingrained, almost essential. The problem emerged when productivity dipped, social life suffered, and sleep quality worsened due to late-night caffeine. The routine had evolved from a tool into a dependency, requiring adjustments to maintain balance without breaking the cycle.
