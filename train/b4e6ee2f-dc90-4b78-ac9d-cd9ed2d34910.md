A hastily scribbled map, half-eaten sandwich, and a half-empty coffee cup at 3 AM. The map was a hastily drawn approximation of the "right" path, but the half-eaten sandwich was the only thing that tasted like food after the first three hours. The half-empty coffee cup glowed warm in the cold air of the abandoned subway tunnel, its steam curling like a ghostly outline of the original plan. They had stopped at a diner where the waitress called them "morons" for leaving their dishes, but they didn’t care—time had bent, and the plan had become something else: a series of detours, a chase through alleys, and a stolen glance at the neon sign of a bar that wasn’t on the map.

They told the story later, embellishing the details until the map was just a starting point, the sandwich a metaphor for survival, and the coffee cup the moment everything changed. The story wasn’t about the destination but the way the path unfolded, the choices made in the dark, the laughter stolen from the chaos. The plan had dissolved into a narrative of its own making—one where the detours were characters, the mistakes twists, and the half-eaten meal the first bite of something new.
