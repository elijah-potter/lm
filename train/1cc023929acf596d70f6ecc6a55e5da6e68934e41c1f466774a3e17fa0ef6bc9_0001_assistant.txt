The ghost town of Calico got its name from four traveling prospectors in 1881 who left Grapevine Station in Barstow to go to a mountain peak to the northeast. These prospectors described the peak as "calico-colored", and the name of Calico stuck. The prospectors discovered silver in this mountain range and opened the Silver King Mine, which was named after John C. King, the uncle of Walter Knott, founder of Knott's Berry Farm. Calico established its post office in early 1882, and its local weekly newspaper called the Calico Print started publishing shortly after. Calico also benefitted from the discovery of borate mineral colemanite in its mountains and had a population of 3,500 in 1890. The Silver Purchase Act was enacted the same year, driving down the price of silver. The value of silver decreased to $0.57 per troy ounce by 1896, which made Calico's silver mines undesirable, leaving Calico a ghost town by the start of the 20th century.
