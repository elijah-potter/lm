The auditorium was bathed in eerie, flickering light as the final note of the orchestra faded. A single chandelier hung above the stage, its ornate design casting long shadows. The crowd had dispersed, leaving only the empty seats, but the silence was thick enough to cut with a knife. No applause, no whispered conversations, no sudden movement—only the hollow hum of the air.

A lone figure stood in the center of the stage, their posture rigid, shoulders hunched. They held a piece of paper, its edges slightly singed, the ink smudged. The words on it were barely legible, but the message was clear: *"I am waiting."* The clock ticked slowly, its hands moving in a deliberate, unnatural rhythm. The silence stretched on, endless, suffocating.

Outside, the city’s distant hum—car horns, distant laughter—faded into the background, swallowed by the weight of what had been said. The figure didn’t move. They didn’t speak. They didn’t blink. Only the silence remained.
