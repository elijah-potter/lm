*The wind howled through the skeletal branches of Blackthorn Hollow, carrying whispers of the old ones. The villagers had long since learned to listen.*

*"The roots are waking,"* the old miller muttered, his fingers tracing the grain of his knife. *"The ones beneath the riverbank—they’re digging deeper."*

The mayor stepped forward, his cloak damp with the evening mist. *"Then we move the children before dawn. The old road—it’s no longer safe."*

The baker’s wife nodded, her eyes sharp with fear. *"I’ve seen it before. The trees lean toward the river now, their branches twisting like hands reaching for water. The last time, the river ran black for three days."*

The old seer, who had lived through the last great storm, exhaled slowly. *"The river remembers. And it’s hungry."*

The fire in the hearth crackled as the children were loaded onto wagons, their faces pressed against the cold. The last child, a girl with braids the color of autumn leaves, clutched her doll too tightly. *"Where are we going?"*

The mayor’s voice was grim. *"To the east. Away from the hollow. The old ones… they’ve learned to listen."*
