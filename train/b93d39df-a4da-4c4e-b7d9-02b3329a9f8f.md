The clock struck midnight in the old mill, its gears whispering secrets older than the paper they ground. Elias adjusted the worn leather strap of his quill, the inkwell trembling with the last drops of midnight ink. The letter before him was half-finished, the last paragraph missing entirely—just a scrap of parchment with a single line:

*"If you find this, know I tried."*

Outside, the wind howled through the rafters, carrying whispers of the past. He signed, tracing the words with his fingers. The mill had burned down three nights ago, and the only thing left was this. He should have burned it too, but curiosity was a stubborn beast. He had already lost everything else—his sister’s laughter, the last of his coin, and the name of the woman who had once called him *"son."*

Now, all that remained was this. The letter. The ghosts of the words he never wrote.

He folded the scrap carefully, tucking it into his coat pocket like a secret he dared not keep. The town’s blacksmith’s wife had warned him: *"Burn the rest. The rest is dangerous."* But Elias had always been a man who listened more than he spoke. So he kept it. And maybe, just maybe, he would write it all out tomorrow.

Or not.

The mill’s lantern flickered. Somewhere, a rat scurried across the floor. Elias exhaled, his breath fogging the glass. He had a choice to make: burn the letter and live like the rest of them, or keep it and risk the things that came with unfinished stories.

He didn’t know yet.

But the mill’s clock struck two.

And the first draft of the next chapter was already written in the dark."*
