The term "OK" originated in the United States, and its first recorded use was in Boston in the late 1830s. It started as a comical abbreviation during a fad for humorous abbreviations and stood for "oll korrect," a deliberate misspelling of "all correct." The term gained widespread popularity when it was used in the 1840 U.S. presidential election campaign by the Democratic O.K. Club, which supported candidate Martin Van Buren, who was also nicknamed "Old Kinderhook" (from his birthplace in Kinderhook, New York). Today, "OK" is one of the most universally recognized words in the world, indicating agreement, understanding, or acknowledgment.
