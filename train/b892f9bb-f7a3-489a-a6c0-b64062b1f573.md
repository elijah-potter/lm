The gradual erosion of a stone wall begins with the slightest discoloration at its base, caused by mineral deposits from nearby streams. Over time, moss and lichen take root, expanding their tendrils upward, turning patches of the wall a dull green. Small cracks form, widening as weather conditions shift—rain, frost, and wind gradually exacerbate them. Windblown sand and grit gradually wear away edges, exposing the rough texture beneath. Eventually, sections collapse, revealing deeper fissures and layers of ancient mortar. The wall, once solid, now shows signs of age, its once-clean lines now jagged and uneven.
