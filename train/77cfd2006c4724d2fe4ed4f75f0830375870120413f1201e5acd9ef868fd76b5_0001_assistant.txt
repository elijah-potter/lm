Steve Paul Jobs, born on February 24, 1955, was an American business magnate, inventor, and investor. Known for his role in the founding of Apple, Jobs contributed much to the personal computer revolution of the 70s and 80s. In 2003, Jobs was diagnosed with a rare form of pancreatic cancer, which he tried to treat with alternative medicine despite medical advice from his doctors to seek surgery as soon as possible. In 2004, he underwent a major reconstructive surgery known as the Whipple operation. Only 56 years old, Mr. Jobs's cancer took a toll on him, and he succumbed to the disease on October 5, 2011, just two months after resigning from his role as the CEO of Apple.
