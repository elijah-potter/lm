The last words spoken in the dim office were:
*"We should’ve—"* (his voice cracking).
She blinked. A half-finished coffee sat untouched, steam curling in the air. The screen flickered, displaying a half-edited draft titled *"The Last Light"*—his unfinished novel, the one he’d mentioned only weeks ago.

*"You know…"* His fingers hovered over the keyboard. *"I don’t think I can do this anymore."*

She didn’t reply. Outside, rain tapped against the windows like a metronome counting down time. The elevator doors hissed open. The hallway was empty. Somewhere, a printer hummed, the sound of a forgotten past.

Later, in the kitchen, she opened the fridge, found the coffee, and poured it into a mug. The label on the jar read *"Liquid Courage."* She drank it black, no sugar. No memory of the argument, the words, the way his voice had been raw with exhaustion.

The text file on her desk was still open, the cursor blinking at the first line:
*"The last light was a promise..."*

She closed it.
