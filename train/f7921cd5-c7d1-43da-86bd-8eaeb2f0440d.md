An abandoned church, now repurposed as a "haunted disco," hosts an event called *"The Last Dance."* Decorated with flickering LED balloons and mismatched vinyl records draped over pews, the crowd—mostly tech bro wannabes, goth millennials, and a few confused elderly—swirls in a makeshift dance floor. The DJ, a former underground rave operator, plays a mix of industrial synthwave and forgotten 90s techno.

Midway through, a neon sign flickers on: **"No Refunds"** in bold, glitchy letters. A stray dog, half-starved, curls around a discarded iPhone charging cable, its collar a patched-up leather belt. The DJ’s bass cuts out mid-set, replaced by a sudden, eerie silence—until the church organ, buried beneath years of dust, starts playing a discordant rendition of *"I Will Survive."* The crowd freezes.

A man in a tattered tuxedo steps forward, holding a single, bloodstained disco ball. *"This is the last show,"* he whispers. *"The lights are out."* The disco ball crashes to the floor, shattering into a thousand tiny fragments. The music fades into static.
