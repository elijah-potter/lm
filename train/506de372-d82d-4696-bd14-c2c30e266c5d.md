A tiny brass key, worn smooth by years of use, now bears the mark of an unspoken promise. Its chain, once thick with rust, has thinned to a thread—only the lock of a forgotten door remains, yet the key still fits. The symbolism lingers in the hollow where a once-gleaming tooth once sat: a lost tooth that wasn’t lost, just *given up*. The tooth is now a pendant, etched with names of those who passed before, their whispers faint but persistent. The key and the pendant together are a silent contract—one opens doors, the other holds secrets. The object isn’t just a key or a tooth; it’s a bridge between the tangible and the unseen, a physical manifestation of what can’t be said.
