## Explain Like I'm Five: Why the Sky is Blue

Okay, imagine you have a big box of crayons, right? And those crayons are all different colors: red, yellow, blue, green, purple – all sorts of wonderful colors! Now, imagine the sun is like a giant lightbulb, sending out all those colors at once! It's like the sun is holding all the crayons and throwing them all at the same time.

Now, the air all around us, the stuff we breathe, is made up of tiny, tiny bits – so small you can’t even see them! Think of them like little bouncy balls floating everywhere.

When the sun’s light (all those crayon colors!) comes to Earth, it bumps into those tiny bouncy balls in the air. Now, blue and violet colors are a little bit *extra* bouncy. They really like to bounce off those little bouncy balls in the air! They bounce all over the place – up, down, sideways – spreading blue and violet everywhere.

Red and yellow colors are a little less bouncy. They mostly go straight through the air. That’s why when the sun is setting, and the light has to travel through *lots* of air to reach us, we see red and orange colors. They finally get a chance to show off!

So, we see the sky as blue because the blue color from the sun's light is bouncing around the air more than any other color. It's like a big game of bouncy ball, and blue is the winner! Isn't that neat?
