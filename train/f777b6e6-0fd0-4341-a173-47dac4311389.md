The workshop smelled of oil and damp wood. Old tools rusted on shelves, their names long forgotten. Daniel wiped his brow with a rag, his fingers tracing the worn edge of a rusted wrench. His father’s favorite—engraved with a small owl.

A knock at the door made him jump. His sister, Mara, peeked in. "You’re not supposed to be here, Dad. The landlord’s coming."

Daniel ignored her. He pulled the wrench free, turning it over. The owl face glinted in the dim light. He pocketed it.

Later, the landlord’s warning bell tolled. Daniel’s phone buzzed—an unknown number. The screen lit up with a single message: *"You have the key."* The text was scrawled in what looked like his own handwriting.

Mara’s voice cracked over the line. "Dad… what’s happening?"

He didn’t answer. Just pressed play on his phone.

A video loaded—his father, alive, standing in a dimly lit room. "The owl," he whispered. "It’s the only thing that can unlock the door to the past."

The workshop door burst open. The landlord stood there, his face twisted in fury. Behind him, a group of men in dark suits stared. One held a device that flickered with strange symbols.

Daniel’s fingers tightened around the wrench. The owl’s eyes burned brighter.

The video ended. The call disconnected.

Mara grabbed his arm. "What the hell was that?"

Daniel exhaled sharply, gripping the wrench tighter. "I don’t know."

The room spun. Then, the door behind them clicked open. The men stepped forward, the device humming.

His father’s voice echoed in his mind: *"The past is not dead. It’s just waiting."*
