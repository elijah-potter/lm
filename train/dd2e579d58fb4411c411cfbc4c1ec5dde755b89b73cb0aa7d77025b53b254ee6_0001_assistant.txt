Typically when a house goes on the market, most states require you to fill out a disclosure form that lays out any known issues with the house. No states require hauntings or paranormal activity in the disclosures, so potential ghostly activity does not legally need to be disclosed. Minnesota is the only state that mentions hauntings in its disclosure law, and that is to relieve sellers from having to disclose any non-material facts, including any perceived paranormal activity. If you are specifically asked if a house is haunted, you may choose to answer that truthfully. There are also cases where hauntings are open and notorious. This is when hauntings are well-known and the home may be stigmatized, which could affect the material value of the home. A home that was the location of a high-profile murder, for instance, could fall into this category. If your home just has a “spooky vibe,” that is not something that legally needs to be disclosed.
