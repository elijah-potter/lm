A cramped, makeshift space in a post-collapse city—once a high-rise office, now a series of stacked shipping containers and salvaged furniture arranged in a chaotic, half-built grid. Rain drips through a patchy tarp over a makeshift porch, reflecting the flickering glow of a jury-rigged solar panel. The air smells of damp wood, burnt wiring, and the faint metallic tang of old solder. A single, flickering fluorescent light casts long shadows over a table cluttered with scavenged tools, a half-eaten can of beans, and a half-drawn sketch of a map. The walls are covered in hastily pinned-up notices in faded ink—wanted posters, barter agreements, and warnings about the "Rust Boys" from down the block. A child’s laughter echoes from behind a curtain of tattered fabric, though the door is closed, and no one can be sure if it’s real. The floorboards groan underfoot, revealing the hollows of the concrete foundation beneath. A clock on the wall ticks painfully slow, its hands stuck at 3:47 AM. The wind howls through gaps in the structure, carrying the distant wail of sirens and the occasional scream of a fire truck. This is not a home—it’s a holding place, a pause in the storm, a moment before the next move.
