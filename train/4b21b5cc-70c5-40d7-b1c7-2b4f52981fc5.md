In the dim glow of a flickering lamp, Elias sat cross-legged on the cold wooden floor, his fingers tracing the worn edges of an old book. The room was silent except for the distant hum of the city beyond the window. His breath formed small clouds in the cold air, unnoticed by anyone else. He had spent years listening to the voices around him—his parents, his siblings, the teachers, the strangers—each one offering their own version of truth, their own need to be heard.

Outside, a street musician played a melody that drifted through the open window, but Elias barely heard it. His mind had long since tuned out noise, not because it was unpleasant, but because it was unnecessary. He had chosen silence as a shield, a way to filter the world into something more manageable.

The book lay open between his hands, its pages filled with words he had read a thousand times but never truly understood. He closed his eyes and let the silence take him. For the first time in years, he did not need to fill the space between his thoughts with answers, with explanations, with the weight of others’ expectations. The silence was warm, the absence of sound a comfort he had not realized he craved. He opened his eyes slowly, his gaze meeting the reflection in the cracked glass of the lamp.

No one spoke. No one moved. The moment stretched, heavy with the weight of what could have been said but was not. Elias exhaled softly, the breath carrying no sound. The silence was his answer.
