The old mill stood abandoned on the edge of Black Hollow, its rusted gears silent under the overhanging oaks. Elias had spent his childhood sneaking inside, chasing ghosts of what might have been. Tonight, he didn’t come for the stories—he came for the hinge.

The mill’s central shaft was a labyrinth of iron and wood, its last operational mechanism buried beneath layers of dust and time. The hinge he sought was hidden behind a loose panel in the ceiling, where the old clockwork mechanism had been removed long ago. Elias pried it open with a crowbar, his fingers trembling with the weight of what he was about to do.

Inside, the mechanism was a tangle of gears and springs, its rusted teeth grinding faintly as he brushed away the dust. At the heart of it, a single, ornate hinge—blackened with age but unbroken—held the key to the mill’s forgotten function.

He had read the manuals. He knew the sequence: rotate the shaft counterclockwise, align the weights, and release the tension. But the moment he turned the first gear, the mill groaned in protest. A deep, resonant vibration ran through his bones, and the air itself seemed to thicken. The clockwork began to move on its own, the gears whispering like a chorus of the dead.

Elias’s breath hitched. The hinge had been the weak point—not just in the mechanism, but in the mill’s purpose. The mechanism had been designed to fail at this exact moment, ensuring the weights would never drop too far. But now, the mill was awake.

The shaft began to spin backward, pulling him toward the center. His vision blurred as the world tilted, the floor of the mill no longer a solid surface but a shifting, unstable plane. The gears screamed as they ground against each other, their noise drowning out all other sound.

Then, silence.

Elias fell.

He hit the ground hard, his body lurching forward as the mill’s core destabilized. The hinge had done its work—not in breaking, but in *reversing*. The mill’s rotation had flipped, and now it was spinning in the opposite direction, pulling him into the heart of the mechanism.

The last thing he saw before the darkness took him was the clockwork’s final, perfect gear—one that had been waiting for him all along.
