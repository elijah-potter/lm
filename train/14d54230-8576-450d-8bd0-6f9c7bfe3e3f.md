A ceramic teacup, delicate and ornate, glides across the polished granite countertop of a cozy café. The first hand, a woman with calloused fingers from years of gardening, lifts it carefully, its handle wrapped in a faded lace doily. She pours golden liquid from a matching pot into a teapot, the steam curling lazily into the air.

A child, clutching a stuffed rabbit, watches with wide eyes. Their mother, passing the teacup to them, murmurs, *"Drink, darling—it’s warm."* The child takes, their small fingers trembling slightly as they sip, eyes closing in contentment.

The teacup is returned to the mother, who sets it beside her coffee. A few moments later, an elderly man, his hands slow with age, picks it up, his breath visible in the cold café. He swirls the liquid, savoring the aroma, before passing it to his neighbor—a woman with a scar running down her cheek, her grip firm. She holds it longer, studying the pattern of the glaze before returning it to the table.

The teacup rests there now, unbroken, between them all.
