A quiet evening in a café where the hum of conversation and the clink of glasses feel like echoes from another time. The coffee tastes like it was brewed yesterday, yet the mug feels too familiar—slightly worn, like a well-loved book passed down through generations. The same table, the same shadows stretching across the floor, the same way the light from the window flickers like a half-remembered dream.

I sit here, watching the world move around me, and suddenly, I realize how much of life is borrowed. The music playing in the background—another artist’s melody, stripped down to its essence. The way the air smells, a scent from a different season. Even my own thoughts sometimes feel borrowed, echoes of someone else’s voice, half-expressed, half-understood. The moment is a gift, a fleeting ownership of something that doesn’t belong to me. It’s the quiet beauty of being both the observer and the observed.
