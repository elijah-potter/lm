If you decide to check out the top popular songs on any given week these days, on a platform such as Spotify, and you are an older person who is familiar with the pop music of decades ago, you're going to notice something right away. Modern pop music is very repetitive, one-dimensional, and shallow in both musical sophistication and lyrical content. Now, you could counter this observation and say that every generation of music gets that same critique. There's a hint of truth in that. But here are my arguments that the pop music of modern times truly does pale in comparison to the sophistication and variety offered in the 1960s through the 1980s.

Those three decades introduced such bands as The Beatles, The Rolling Stones, Led Zeppelin, The Doors, R.E.M, The Police... and a much longer list of iconic bands. The Who, Jimi Hendrix, The Eagles, Fleetwood Mac... Again, we're just scraping the surface. Not to mention all the Motown acts of classic R&B like Marvin Gaye or Al Green, and scores of similarly iconic black artists. All of these artists are on regular rotation on the radio to this day. On 'classic' stations, of course, but that's a testament to their staying power.

Musicianship and lyrical content were highly respected in those eras. There were a wide variety of genres, ranging from disco to progressive rock. And people bought full albums from these artists, collections that were consistent in quality for their entirety.

Today, with a few exceptions here and there, it's all about how an artist looks, not their talent. K-pop features artists who lip-sync, but none of their fans care, because it's purely about how attractive they are, and their dance moves. Electronica? People fill stadiums to watch someone twist knobs on a stage full of high-tech light shows. Some might say that I'm just an old curmudgeon. Well, that's fair enough. But let's see if artists like Drake or Rihanna or Taylor Swift are considered 'iconic' a few decades in the future.
