Shadows of a crumbling stone bridge arch over a river’s dark, rippling current, their edges frayed by time. The water beneath glints with faint, eerie reflections of a thousand sunsets, their hues bleeding into the moss-encrusted planks where the bridge once held sturdy.

Dusk paints the far bank in ochre and violet as the shadows stretch like fingers of smoke toward the skeletal remains of a church spire, its stained glass long shattered. The wind carries the scent of damp earth and old iron, where rusted lanterns still cast flickering light across the cobblestones.

A lone figure stands at the bridge’s edge, their silhouette too vague to define. The shadows dance around them, shifting like living things—sometimes merging, sometimes splitting—before vanishing into the mist that rolls in from the horizon, swallowing the world in twilight.

No names, only the weight of forgotten places.
