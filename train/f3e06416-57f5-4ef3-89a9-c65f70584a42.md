A dimly lit, angular chamber with walls lined by flickering, outdated holographic displays—each projecting static loops of fading data. The air hums with residual electromagnetic noise from defunct systems, making the silence itself dense. Furniture, half-buried under dust, shifts slightly when no one is looking, as if breathing. The floor is a cracked, reflective surface where shadows stretch unnaturally long, merging with the edges of the room. A single, cold air vent at the center pulses intermittently, sucking in the stillness like a wound. The walls bleed a muted, sickly glow from malfunctioning emergency lights, casting long, unnatural shadows that seem to *move* when you’re not watching. The ceiling—if it could be called that—is a patchwork of broken panels, some dangling like skeletal ribs. The space *remembers* too much.
