**Sunny skies, a gentle breeze**—a café in a quiet town, raincoats draped over chairs, patrons sipping black coffee. A child’s laughter fades into the distance as their mother glances at the sky, fingers tracing the condensation on her mug. Outside, clouds roll in, thick and unyielding, blotting out the afternoon light. The café’s lights flicker as the power cuts. A single candle burns low, casting long shadows across the room. A woman in a worn coat stands, holding a half-empty glass, staring at her reflection in the puddles outside. The rain turns to sleet, tapping against the window like disappointed fingers. Inside, the hum of conversation dies as everyone stares at the storm—each face a different kind of storm. A knock at the door. The storm outside is answered by the doorbell, and the café’s lights go out again. The last candle snuffs. Outside, the wind howls through the trees, whispering something no one can hear. The rain stops. Silence. A single raindrop freezes on the window. The sun peeks through, hesitant, as if unsure if it dare return. It does.
