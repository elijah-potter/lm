**BREAKING:** Mayor’s Unconventional Approach Sparks Debate in Havenport

Residents of Havenport woke to an unexpected morning after Mayor Elias Voss announced a bold initiative to transform the city’s abandoned industrial zones into a "greenway corridor" by the end of summer. The plan, dubbed the "Riverfront Revival," includes repurposing the old textile mills along the Blackthorn River, converting them into a mix of community gardens, artist studios, and a floating market. Critics argue it risks displacing small businesses, while supporters praise it as a long-overdue economic reboot. "We’ve been stuck in the past," Voss said, adding, "Havenport’s future starts with its roots." Meanwhile, local unions have called for a citywide strike to demand fair compensation for displaced workers.

**Downtown Havenport:** Traffic jams expected as construction crews reroute streets for the new riverfront access. Protests near the old mill district could disrupt early morning commutes. Police have advised residents to prepare for increased foot traffic in the coming weeks.

**Public Reaction:**
- *Pro-growth supporters* see it as a chance to revitalize the city’s economy.
- *Traditionalists* fear it will erode the historic charm of the downtown core.
- *Environmentalists* welcome the push toward sustainability, but warn of overdevelopment risks.

City officials urge residents to attend the public hearings on June 12 to share concerns or ideas. The project’s first phase is set for completion by Labor Day, with plans to expand beyond the riverfront by 2025.

**Note:** Officials confirm that while some businesses will relocate, new ones will replace them. Details on funding and zoning changes will be announced in the next city council meeting.
