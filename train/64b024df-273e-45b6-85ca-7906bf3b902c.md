**Scene 1 – The Library – Past**
A cracked floorboard groaned under the weight of Elias as he slipped into the shadows behind the stacks. The air smelled of aged paper and old books. His breath fogged in the cold, but his focus was sharp—he was searching for something his father never found: the book that proved the alchemy of their family was real.

He pressed his palm against the spine of a leather-bound tome, its cover worn smooth with time. The words on the title page glowed faintly, though no light illuminated them. A single candle flickered on the table beside him, casting long shadows. Elias exhaled, his fingers tracing the edge of the forbidden text.

*"The Secret of the Parallel"* was scrawled in ink that didn’t fade. His father had whispered warnings—*"Burn it, Elias. It’s cursed."* But Elias had only ever seen the world in two dimensions: his own, and the one his father claimed existed in the margins. The book was the key to seeing them both at once.

He reached for it, but the moment his fingers brushed the cover, the pages flipped backward. The candle extinguished. The library was empty—except for a single, glowing figure standing at the far end of the aisle, watching.

**Scene 2 – The Present – Waiting Room**
The fluorescent hum of the waiting room drowned out the distant murmur of the clinic. A sign above the door read **DO NOT ENTER**, but the glass was fogged with rain. Outside, the city’s neon lights reflected in the puddles like fractured mirrors.

Lena pressed her palm to the window, her breath fogging the glass. She had been here for hours, waiting for the call she never expected. Her phone buzzed—a text from her sister, *"They’re calling you. Meet me at the bridge."* The bridge. The same place she had stood every night for the last year, watching the same two reflections in the water.

One was her—pale, hollow-eyed, her hair pulled into a messy bun. The other was her younger self, laughing as she skipped toward the edge, arms wide. Lena had tried to stop her, but the girl had only smiled and said, *"It’s not the same if you don’t jump."*

She swallowed. The text from her sister was a lie. The call was from the clinic. Her father was dead.

Lena’s fingers tightened around the phone. The screen lit up with a new message:

**DO NOT ENTER.**

---

**Scene 3 – The Mirror’s Edge – Between Worlds**
The bridge was silent. The water below churned like a storm in slow motion. Lena stood at the edge, her breath misting in the cold air. Below her, the younger girl—her sister, now dead—skipped toward the abyss, her laughter echoing off the stone walls.

*"You’re not ready,"* Lena’s voice whispered in her ear.

The girl turned. Her eyes were the same—blue, but different. *"But I’m always ready."*

A figure stepped from the mist between them. Elias. His coat was damp, his face pale. He held a book in his hands, the pages glowing like embers. *"You’ve been waiting for this,"* he said, his voice a whisper in the wind. *"The world isn’t just here. It’s everywhere."*

Lena stumbled back. The younger girl’s reflection rippled, splitting into two—one her, one Elias. *"You’re not alone,"* the girl whispered. *"The other one is inside you."*

The bridge trembled. The water below roared.

---
**Scene 4 – The Clinic – Aftermath**
The door burst open. A nurse in a white coat stood frozen, her hands hovering over the IV drip in Lena’s arm. Behind her, a doctor in a sharp suit looked at her like she was a ghost.

*"You’ve been in a coma for three days,"* the doctor said, voice flat. *"But you’re awake now."*

Lena’s vision swam. The room blurred—until she saw it. The clinic was just a reflection. The real world was outside, where the bridge stood, where the younger girl laughed, where Elias waited.

The nurse’s hands moved. The IV dripped. Lena’s fingers twitched.

*"The other one,"* she whispered.

The room dissolved. The clinic was gone. The bridge was real.

---
**Scene 5 – The Choice – Parallel Endings**
Elias stood at the edge of the bridge, the younger girl in his arms. The water below was still, like glass. Lena watched from the shore, her reflection splitting into infinite versions of herself—some running, some crying, some laughing.

*"You can jump,"* Elias said. *"Or you can stay."*

Lena’s breath hitched. The clinic, the waiting room, the library—it was all the same. The only difference was how she saw it.

A voice called from the shore: *"Elias."*

She turned.

Her sister was there. Her younger self. Her *future* self.

*"You’re not ready,"* the older Lena said. *"But you’ll be."*

Lena looked at the bridge. The water. The endless drop.

She stepped onto the edge.

The younger girl smiled. The older Lena nodded. The bridge split.

And the world refracted.

---
**Scene 6 – The Code – The Key**
The book was in Elias’s hands. The pages burned with equations—mathematical proofs that the universe wasn’t linear, but a series of infinite threads. One line stood out:

**Σₐᵢₐᵤ = Cₒₑᵣₑₓₑₗₛₑₐₗ (∑ₐᵢₐᵤ)**

Lena read it. The symbols meant nothing. But the way it felt—like a promise—did.

Elias held up the book. *"This isn’t just a story,"* he said. *"It’s a way out."*

Lena reached for it. The bridge dissolved into static. The clinic, the waiting room—they were all just echoes.

The real world was here. And it was waiting.

She took the book.

The pages glowed brighter.

And the universe split again.
