I stood frozen, my breath fogging in the cold air as sirens wailed in the distance. The crowd around me had thinned—some had fled, others lingered, eyes darting between the flickering emergency lights and the chaos unfolding beyond the barricades. A man in a torn jacket stumbled past me, clutching his stomach, his voice barely audible over the chaos: *"They’re not supposed to be here."* Then he was gone, swallowed by the throng.

The front of the building groaned, splintering wood and shattered glass spilling onto the pavement. Smoke curled from the windows, thick and acrid. A woman screamed as a man in a black hooded jacket pulled her into a doorway, his voice low and urgent: *"Stay down."* The explosion rocked the street—shards of glass rained down, slicing through the air like knives. I ducked instinctively, my heart hammering as the ground trembled.

Then, the sirens stopped. The crowd scattered in panic, but not before I saw the body of a child—small, unarmed—lying motionless at the curb. The street was a graveyard of shattered dreams. The only sound was the distant hum of police radios. I didn’t move. I couldn’t. The world had just become too real.
