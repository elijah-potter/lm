A dim hallway flickered with the glow of a single dying bulb. The air was thick with the scent of old paper and damp stone. Elias sat on the edge of a narrow wooden desk, fingers tracing the grain of the wood, his knuckles white. His reflection in the cracked glass wasn’t quite right—hollow eyes, lips pressed into a thin line that didn’t quite match the smirk playing on his face.

He exhaled slowly, the sound barely audible. *"I told you I’d never do this."* The words hung in the silence like a curse. His mother’s voice, sharp with betrayal, echoed in his memory. *"You’re not a man who can lose."*

The door creaked open. His father stood framed in the dim light, a glass of whiskey in hand. His expression was unreadable—neither pity nor judgment, just quiet acceptance. "You’re shaking," he said.

Elias didn’t look up. "I know." The lie tasted bitter. "But it’s over. I’m done."

His father’s fingers tightened around the glass. "You’ve been doing this for years." His voice was low, a blade between them. "And every time, you’re the one who breaks."

The desk scraped as Elias stood, the movement jerky. He didn’t meet his father’s eyes. "I’m not the one who’s supposed to be the strong one, am I?"

Silence. Then, from the other room, a child’s giggle. A sound that cut through the tension like a knife. Elias’s chest tightened. He had forgotten. He had forgotten that someone else existed—someone who needed him, who counted on him.

His father’s voice dropped, almost a whisper. "You’re not just a man, Elias. You’re a son."

The words struck like a punch. He swallowed hard. "And I’m not sure if I want to be."

Outside, the wind howled through the empty streets. Somewhere, a clock struck midnight.
