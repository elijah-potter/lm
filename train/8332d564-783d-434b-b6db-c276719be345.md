The kitchen was too quiet after the dishes, the hum of the fridge the only sound cutting through the silence. My hands trembled as I reached for the phone, my fingers numb from too many nights spent staring at the same wall. I dialed the number again, my voice raw with exhaustion.

*"I don’t know what to do,"* I whispered into the receiver. *"It’s like I’m drowning in my own thoughts, and I don’t even know how to swim."*

A pause. Then, a voice—steady, warm, like sunlight after a storm.

*"Tell me what’s drowning you."*

I exhaled sharply, the words clawing their way out. *"The weight. It feels like a rock tied to my feet. I can’t move. I can’t breathe. I keep thinking about how much I love her, but how much she loves *him*. And the more I think about it, the more it feels like I’m being erased."* The phone went silent again.

*"You’re not being erased,"* the voice said softly. *"You’re being given a choice."*

I sat frozen, the weight of the words pressing down like a physical force. *"What choice?"*

*"To let her go, or to fight for something that’s already slipping through your fingers."*

Silence. Then, a single, broken laugh escaped me. *"I don’t know if I can fight anymore."*

The line was cut. The phone lay in my hand, warm and useless. I didn’t call back.

Outside, the rain began to fall. Slow. Steady. Like the slow, steady unraveling of something I’d spent years weaving together.

I stared at the phone in my palm. The screen glowed faintly, unreadable.

And for the first time in months, I felt the weight of my own decision.
