The neon glow of the alley flickered as rain hammered against the pavement. I stood frozen, my coat damp with splashes from the storm, watching two figures clash near the dumpster. One was a woman in a torn leather jacket, her hands gripping the back of a man’s shirt—he was trying to pull away. His face was twisted in pain, but his eyes locked onto mine before he crumpled. The woman didn’t notice me at first, but then she turned, her breath shallow, and screamed something unintelligible. The crowd around me—drunk college kids, a couple of teens with skateboards—stared, mouths open, but no one moved. The cops were already there in seconds, shoving the woman toward a squad car, her arm still wrapped around the man’s body as if he were still there. The paramedics rushed over, but it was too late. The woman’s head lolled back, her jaw slack. The cops cuffed her, her fingers twitching around the man’s limp wrist. The rain kept falling. No one said a word. I turned away, my boots sinking into the mud, and walked toward the streetlight. The sirens were already wailing in the distance.
