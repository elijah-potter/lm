In the quiet hours before dawn, the old oak’s roots tangled beneath a moss-stained earth, whispering secrets to the damp soil. The morning dew clung to the cobwebs in the barn’s creaking attic, where a rusted nail still held the half-finished birdhouse—a child’s dream frozen in time. The kitchen clock, its hands stuck in the shape of a crescent moon, chimed one lone strike.

A woman moved through the half-light, her coat collar turned up against the chill. She carried a thermos of black coffee, the steam curling like a ghost into the stale air of the kitchen. Outside, the garden slept—tomato plants bent their heads against the frost, their leaves whispering of summer’s promise. The door creaked open, and the world beyond was a canvas of frost-kissed grass and shadows stretching long and silent.

Then—

The sky was a bruise of violet, bleeding through the edges of the horizon like ink spilled on parchment. Above, a single gull wheeled, its wings a blur of silver against the swirling, misty dawn. The air smelled of damp earth and the faint, sweet tang of frost on the wind. The sun would rise, but for now, the world was still. The old oak stood sentinel, its branches heavy with the weight of forgotten days, and the dawn was only a whisper in the wind.
