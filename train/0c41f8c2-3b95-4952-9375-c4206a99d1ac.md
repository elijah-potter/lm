The rain blurred the streetlights as they walked side by side, their boots splashing through puddles. Her fingers brushed his sleeve unconsciously—just a touch, a shared moment in the dark. His breath hitched; he didn’t know why.

*"You always forget your keys,"* she said, voice low, eyes reflecting the glow of the passing neon.

He froze. *"I do?"* The words hung between them, heavy with the weight of years.

She tilted her head, smirking. *"Every time."* The pause stretched. Then, softer: *"I should’ve said something sooner."*

A laugh—bitter, relieved—escaped him. *"You didn’t have to."* His hand found hers, gripping tight. The world around them dissolved into silence, just the two of them, finally *seen*.
