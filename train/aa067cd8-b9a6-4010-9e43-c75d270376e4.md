## A Counseling Session: Navigating Uncertainty

**Setting:** A warmly lit, comfortably furnished therapist’s office.

**Characters:**

*   **Eleanor Vance (Client):** A woman in her late twenties, visibly anxious and somewhat hesitant.
*   **Dr. Alistair Finch (Therapist):** A seasoned therapist with a calm and reassuring demeanor.

---

**Dr. Finch:** Welcome, Eleanor. Please, make yourself comfortable. It’s good to see you again. How have things been since our last session?

**Eleanor:** (Fidgeting slightly) Not…not great, to be honest. I’m still feeling quite overwhelmed. The promotion at work, while positive, has brought with it a lot of new responsibilities, and I'm finding it difficult to manage.

**Dr. Finch:** I understand. It sounds like a significant shift in your role. It’s perfectly natural to feel overwhelmed when facing such a change. Can you tell me a little more about what specifically feels challenging?

**Eleanor:** It’s just…everything. The expectations are higher, and I constantly worry about making mistakes. I second-guess every decision, and I feel like I'm always on edge. I used to enjoy my work, but now it just feels like a source of constant stress.

**Dr. Finch:** That sounds incredibly draining, Eleanor. It’s clear you’re putting a lot of pressure on yourself. It’s admirable that you’re striving for excellence, but it seems that pressure is impacting your overall well-being. You mentioned second-guessing your decisions. Could you give me an example of a recent situation where you experienced that?

**Eleanor:** Just yesterday, I had to present a proposal to the executive team. I spent hours preparing, but as soon as I started speaking, my mind went blank. I stumbled over my words, and I felt like everyone was judging me. Afterwards, I replayed the entire presentation in my head, focusing only on what I perceived as flaws.

**Dr. Finch:** That sounds like a very uncomfortable experience. It’s common to be self-critical after a presentation, especially when you're feeling anxious. But it sounds like you're amplifying those perceived flaws. You said you replayed the presentation focusing *only* on the negatives.  What, if anything, went well?

**Eleanor:** (Hesitates) I… I suppose the data was solid. The numbers were all accurate. And a couple of people afterwards said they thought the core idea was strong. I just… I didn’t focus on those comments.

**Dr. Finch:** It's understandable that the negative feelings were more prominent in your immediate recollection. But it’s a pattern we can address. It’s like your mind is selectively highlighting the perceived shortcomings. We can explore techniques to help you recognize and acknowledge the positives, even when anxiety is present. Have you ever considered journaling, perhaps to record both the successes and the challenges?

**Eleanor:** I’m not much of a journaler, but I’m willing to try anything at this point.

**Dr. Finch:** It doesn't have to be elaborate. Even a few sentences each day, noting what you accomplished and what you learned, can shift your perspective.  We can also work on cognitive restructuring – examining those automatic negative thoughts and challenging their validity.  The goal isn't to eliminate anxiety entirely – that’s unrealistic – but to develop strategies to manage it and prevent it from controlling your actions.  How does that sound?

**Eleanor:** It sounds…hopeful, actually. I feel like I’ve been drowning in this feeling for so long, just acknowledging that it *can* be managed is a relief.

**Dr. Finch:** That’s a very valid feeling, Eleanor. And it’s a starting point. Remember, progress isn’t always linear. There will be days when you feel better than others.  For this week, I’d like you to focus on that journaling. Even just five minutes each evening. We can discuss how it’s going next time. Does that feel manageable?

**Eleanor:** Yes, it does. Thank you, Dr. Finch. I appreciate you listening.

**Dr. Finch:** You’re very welcome, Eleanor. I’m glad you feel heard.  Remember, you’re not alone in this. I’m here to support you through this process.
