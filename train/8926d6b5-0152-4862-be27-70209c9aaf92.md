The abandoned plan involved constructing a floating solar farm on Lake Erie by 2025 to address energy shortages and reduce carbon emissions. Key components included modular solar panels, advanced hydrodynamic stabilization systems, and integrated battery storage to ensure reliability. Financial backers, primarily local corporations, secured $45 million in initial funding, with projections of $2 billion in total investment by 2030. Environmental concerns, such as potential impact on aquatic ecosystems, were mitigated through strict impact assessments and adaptive management strategies.

However, the project was abandoned due to:
1. **Regulatory Delays**: Complex approvals from the EPA and NOAA extended beyond 18 months, delaying permits.
2. **Economic Shifts**: Global energy transition accelerated post-2023, making solar farms less competitive with falling costs of wind and battery storage.
3. **Technical Challenges**: Unforeseen weather patterns (e.g., extreme storms) and corrosion risks from lake water damaged prototypes, raising safety concerns.
4. **Corporate Shift**: Major backers pivoted to offshore wind projects in the Great Lakes region, citing higher ROI and lower maintenance costs.
5. **Public Opposition**: Local fishermen and environmental groups lobbied against the project, citing threats to fishing grounds and displacement of marine life.

Alternatives were pursued, including a hybrid wind-solar hub in Ohio’s Cuyahoga Valley, leveraging existing infrastructure. The abandoned plan’s lessons emphasized the need for agile risk assessment and diversified energy portfolios.
