Definitive Jux (formerly known as Def Jux) was an independent hip-hop label founded by rapper El-P and his manager Amechi Uzoigwe. Disillusioned with his experiences at Rawkus Records, El-P formed Def Jux in 1999 with Amechi.  The label sought to cover overheads on projects and offer 50% of royalty earnings to the artists. In 2001, the label released a compilation project called Def Jux Presents, which was followed by the critically acclaimed Cannibal Ox album The Cold Vein later that same year. The label was forced to change its name that same year when Def Jam records sued over the similarities in name. After an out-of-court settlement, the label changed its name to Definitive Jux records. The label continued to release a string of albums to critical acclaim and modest commercial success for most of the mid-2000s. The death of artist Camu Tao in 2008 created tension between artists. At the same time, El-P had become unhappy with the development of Def Jux records, feeling that the output of the label had become homogeneous. This culminated in El-P announcing that he would be focusing more on his career as an artist and put the label on a permanent hiatus in 2010. 

Five prominent albums released on the label are:
1. Cannibal Ox - The Cold Vein (2001)
2. Aesop Rock - Labor Days (2001)
3. El-P - Fantastic Damage (2002)
4. RJ-D2 - Deadringer (2002)
5. Cage - Hell's Winter (2005)
