CEO Raj Patel interrupted, *"That’s impressive, but how does this solve our supply chain delays?"* He opened a new tab on his laptop to pull up the real-time inventory system.

Elena tapped her tablet. *"The efficiency data wasn’t just about speed—it revealed hidden inefficiencies in the current system. The blockchain’s audit trail flagged duplicate payments and manual reconciliation errors, costing $12K monthly. Our current system has no real-time visibility."*

Raj’s eyes widened. *"Wait—you’re saying this was already happening, but we didn’t know?"* He clicked on the inventory dashboard. The system showed pending payments for 24 suppliers, with 42% of orders stuck in "pending approval" status.

Mark, the logistics lead, leaned forward. *"The problem isn’t just the blockchain—it’s the lack of a unified system. Suppliers use different payment gateways, and we manually reconcile 70% of transactions."*

Elena nodded. *"The blockchain’s immutable ledger can auto-match payments once we integrate the existing system’s data. We’ll reroute 60% of transactions through it, cutting reconciliation time by 45%."*

The room erupted. Raj stood up, *"This isn’t just cost savings—it’s a strategic pivot. We’re replacing our entire payment and reconciliation system overnight. Let’s draft the proposal for Q2."*

Within 30 minutes, they had a draft memo to the board. The unexpected solution? The blockchain wasn’t just a tool—it was the missing piece in an already broken system. The team’s efficiency metrics had been a red flag pointing to a larger problem.
