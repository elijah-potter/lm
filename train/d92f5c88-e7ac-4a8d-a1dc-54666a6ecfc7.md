The alley smelled of damp concrete and old pizza boxes. A car door slammed hard, startling me from my usual spot by the fire escape. The man inside was a shadow—no face, just a hood pulled low over a scarred jaw. He tossed something onto the curb: a folded piece of paper, the edges singed, like it had been dropped in a fire. The car pulled away, tires crunching gravel. I picked up the paper, fingers trembling. It was a receipt for a dozen beers, a bar’s logo burned into the top. Below it, in messy script: *"Meet me at the old mill. Midnight. Bring the knife."* My breath fogged in the cold night air. I should’ve left it. But curiosity won. I folded the paper neatly and slipped it into my pocket. The mill stood abandoned behind the river, its rusted gates sagging. The wind howled like a warning. I didn’t know why I was here. But the receipt had a name I recognized: *"Kyle."* And for some reason, I knew he’d be waiting.
