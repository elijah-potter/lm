The first mistake was a misplaced pipe: *|* instead of a semicolon in the script for the night shift at the old mill. It wasn’t just a typo—it was a signal. The alarms didn’t ring; the lights flickered blue instead of red, the usual sign of an emergency. By the time the foreman noticed, the first worker had already turned off his machine, leaving the gears spinning in slow motion, their teeth interlocking like broken teeth.

The second mistake was the one who called it in: a young apprentice who’d been too tired to double-check their own notes. The supervisor, a man who’d spent thirty years listening to warnings dismissed as "the mill’s old ways," laughed it off. *"Another ghost story,"* he said, but the machine’s last report had been a scream in binary: **10101010101010101010**.

They found the body in the basement, where the pipes had been buried under years of rust and forgotten parts. The apprentice was the one who’d found it, kneeling over the machine’s heart, a pen still gripped in their frozen fingers, writing *|* in the margins of the logbook. The foreman’s last words were whispered in the dark: *"You’re going to regret this."* The mill’s clock stopped at 3:17 AM.
