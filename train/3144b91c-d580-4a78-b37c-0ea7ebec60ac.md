The last light of dusk bled through the skeletal trees, their branches clawing at the fading sky like skeletal hands. The air smelled of damp earth and rusted iron, thick with the scent of pine and something older—something that had been here before the last storm.

The path ahead was slick with rain, the cobblestones glistening like polished obsidian. The bridge, once sturdy, now groaned under its own weight, the planks warping, the iron hinges groaning as if whispering secrets. The water below was a dark, churning mass, reflecting nothing but the dying embers of the city’s forgotten glow.

Your breath fogged in the cold, but you didn’t care. The weight of the past pressed down on you—memories tangled in the rusted wire of lampposts, the laughter of children lost to time, the weight of promises unkept in the hollows of abandoned shops. The wind carried the sound of a distant train, a ghostly echo of something that had been here before you.

The last thing you saw before you turned your back for good was the silhouette of the old watchmaker’s shop, its windows broken, its clock stopped at 11:59. The hands of time had run out.
