In dimly lit alleyways, a lone figure moves with deliberate, cautious steps, head bowed against a misty drizzle. The flickering glow of a flickering phone screen casts long shadows on the damp pavement, illuminating faint footprints leading toward a crumbling brick wall where the scent of damp earth and rust lingers. The person’s breath forms thin, ghostly plumes in the cold air, hands gripping a worn notebook, each page filled with sketches and words—notes of what they’re searching for.

A distant, echoing laughter cuts through the silence, but the figure doesn’t turn. The notebook flutters open, revealing a single, half-remembered phrase: *"The thing you can’t hold but always lose."* The wind howls through the alley, carrying fragments of a voice—*maybe yours, maybe someone else’s*—a whisper that was never meant to be heard. The phone buzzes again; a notification flashes: *Unread message from [name]*. The person exhales, fingers tracing the worn edges of the notebook. The search continues, though the target feels like smoke in the dark.
