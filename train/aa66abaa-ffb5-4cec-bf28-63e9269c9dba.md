The silence settled like a damp cloak,
softening the edges of a world too loud.
A single breath—steady, unhurried—
dissolved the echoes of the past hour.

Distant hum of a refrigerator
pulsed through the wall, a metronome
counting down to the next storm.
A door creaked open, then shut,
and the air smelled of rain on pavement,
of copper and ozone, of time
stretched thin as a spider’s web.

No words. No noise. Just the weight
of what isn’t said, the way
the light caught the dust motes
and turned them into a silent choir.
