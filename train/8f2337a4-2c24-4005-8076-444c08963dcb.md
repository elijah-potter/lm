The vast, silent void of an abandoned orbital station hums with the faint echo of a thousand forgotten voices. Metal groans under the weight of its own age, cracked plating reflecting the dim, sickly glow of dying emergency lights. Dust motes swirl in the airless corridors, suspended in time—somewhere between the last human and the next mechanical repair. A single, rusted vent emits a slow, rhythmic hiss, like a dying breath. The air smells of ozone and old oil, thick with the weight of forgotten meals, unwashed dishes, and the occasional rusted nail from a long-dead toolbox. The walls are lined with faded graffiti: *"Stay strong"* in faded blue paint, *"I love you"* in a smudged white marker, and a single, jagged symbol that could be anything—a warning, a curse, or just static. The station’s central hub is a cavernous chamber where the ceiling arches like a collapsed dome, its structural supports sagging. A lone, flickering holographic display flickers to life: *"WARNING: UNMANNED STATION—DO NOT ENTER."* The shadows stretch impossibly long, pooling in depressions where walls once were. In the distance, the faint glow of a dying star casts a cold, distant light over the ruins, and the silence is only broken by the occasional metallic groan of a forgotten mechanism. The space feels like a tomb waiting to be opened.
