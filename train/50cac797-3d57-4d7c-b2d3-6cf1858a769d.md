A silent pulse of tension coiled through the old oak tree at the edge of the forest, its branches bending like a question mark in the wind. The squirrels had stopped gathering acorns, and the birds had paused their morning chorus—no sound, just the whisper of dry leaves shifting underfoot. The moon, a pale coin, had dipped behind the ridge, casting long shadows that seemed to stretch themselves toward the sky like fingers waiting.

The river below was still, its surface a mirror of the afterglow, reflecting nothing but its own reflection. The current carried no debris, no noise—only the slow, deliberate rhythm of water sliding over stone.

She watched the way the light caught the edge of the path, turning it gold for a single, fleeting moment before fading. The decision wasn’t made in words, but in the way the air held its breath, in the way the earth itself seemed to pause. The choice was written in the absence of movement, in the stillness that followed the first, unspoken silence. No one spoke. No one needed to. The world had already answered.
