The wind carried whispers through the hollows of the old oak, its gnarled roots twisting into forgotten stories. The cobblestones beneath my boots were slick with dew, each one a cracked mirror reflecting the muted glow of streetlamps that flickered like dying embers. I sat on the edge of a crumbling bridge, my fingers tracing the worn planks, the city sprawling beneath like a vast, restless beast.

A woman in a faded coat stood near the railing, her breath visible in the cold morning air. She turned, and for a moment, her eyes held mine—two windows into a world I dared not enter. The sky above was a canvas of fractured light, streaked with the pale, ghostly hues of dawn. The sun, a reluctant giant, peeked over the horizon, its reflection shivering on the river’s surface like a trapped thing.

The city exhaled—steam from the pipes, the distant hum of a subway train, the low murmur of a café below. Somewhere, a clock struck noon. I stood, my shadow stretching long against the stone, and walked away, the weight of the world pressing down like a second skin. The river, still, waited.
