AAA put out a list of the six most common car seat mistakes parents make as part of National Child Passenger Safety Week, which starts Sept. 12.
Any of them seem familiar? Check out the following list. For more information visit AAA's site at AAA.com/carseat or the American Academy of Pediatrics' site here or the Alliance for Community Traffic Safety here.
According to the AAA, motor vehicle crashes are the leading cause of death for children.
• One mistake? Not using a car seat in the first place. Any child under age 8 or a height of 4 foot 9 inches needs some kind of safety seat in addition to the vehicle's seat belt.
• Not reading the vehicle or car seat instructions. Four out of 5 car seats are installed incorrectly. You can also get yours checked by professionals at various sites throughout the state. Go here to the ACTS car seat clinic calendar for more information on free checks.
• Switching the set-up to soon. Infants should stay rear-facing until they approach the weight limit of the rear-facing car seat, which is usually around 30 to 35 pounds. At the very least, keep your child rear-facing until he or she is at least 1 year old and weighs 20 pounds. All kinds under 13 should ride in the back seat.
• Installing safety seats too loosely. When a child safety seat is properly installed, it should not move more than one inch in any direction. Children should use a booster seat until an adult seat belt fits them properly--typically around age 8 or when the child is 4 feet 9 inches tall.
• Adjusting seat harnesses incorrectly. Safety seat harnesses should always be snug and lie flat without twists. Harnesses should be at or below the child's shoulders when rear-facing and at or above the shoulders when forward-facing in order to hold the child's body upright and against the seat. The chest clip should be positioned at armpit level.
• Keeping loose items in vehicle. Any loose items -- handbags, DVD players, laptops, umbrellas and other items can become dangerous projectiles in a crash or sudden stop, and can cause severe injury to a child, other passengers or the driver. Make sure to secure loose items and provide children with only soft toys to play with in a vehicle.
