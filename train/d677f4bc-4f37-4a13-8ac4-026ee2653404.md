The room smelled of damp wool and old coffee, the kind that lingers long after the mug is discarded. Outside, the city pulsed with a rhythm neither understood nor desired—streetlights flickering like dying stars, the distant wail of sirens dissolving into static.

I sit here, fingers tracing the edge of a half-empty glass. The ink on the page bleeds unevenly, as if the words themselves are afraid. What do I write? The past is a weight I carry too heavy to ignore, and the future—well, it’s just a promise I’ve never trusted. The mirror above the desk reflects not my eyes, but the ghost of a face that once believed it knew itself.

The pen hovers over the paper, a silent judge. Should I start with the things I know? Or the things I fear? The line between certainty and uncertainty blurs here, where the only truth is that I don’t know what I don’t know. The clock ticks. The city breathes. And I—

I am just a story waiting to be told, one that refuses to choose its ending.
