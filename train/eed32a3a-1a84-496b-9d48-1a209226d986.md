The air smelled of old books and damp wool, the kind of scent that lingers like a half-remembered dream. The café’s fluorescent lights flickered, casting long shadows on the walls where someone else’s life had once been played out. The mug in my hands was warm, its surface etched with the pattern of a stranger’s fingers. I traced it absently, fingers numb with the weight of the borrowed weight I carried.

I had come here to escape the echo of my own voice—the one that had already been spoken, the one that was still being recorded, the one that might never be my own. Outside, the city pulsed with a rhythm I didn’t recognize. The hum of conversation, the clatter of dishes, the distant laughter—all of it was someone else’s, a chorus of voices I didn’t belong to.

The waitress stopped beside me, her smile sharp with familiarity. *"Another one?"* she asked, voice low, like she already knew the answer. I nodded, but my hands shook—not with fear, but with the quiet terror of being caught in the act of being someone else’s story.

The window above me was open, and through it, I saw the reflection of a woman who looked like me, but older, her hair silver, her hands lined with the weight of years. She was reading, her fingers pausing over the page. For a moment, I thought I saw me there, watching myself watch her. Then she turned away, and the reflection blurred like smoke.

I paid the bill in silence. The receipt slipped into my pocket, a physical reminder of how little I had left to claim. The door closed behind me, sealing me in the borrowed moment—temporary, fleeting, and utterly my own.
