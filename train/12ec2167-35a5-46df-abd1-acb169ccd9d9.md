The first time rain kissed the city streets at dusk, turning the pavement into a mirror of light and memory. A child’s laughter, sharp and golden, echoed off the glass of an open café window where I sat, the last of the evening’s warmth still clinging to my fingers. The scent of blooming jasmine from a nearby garden drifted through the open door, mingling with the faint metallic tang of the city’s pulse. I reached out, touched the rim of my coffee cup—cold now, but not yet forgotten—and wondered how many times I’d stand here, watching the world breathe, before the moment slipped through my fingers like smoke.

The moment was the way the streetlights flickered to life, painting everything in a soft, uneven glow, as if the city itself had turned its head to watch me. The moment was the way the café’s neon sign flickered, casting a blue-green reflection on the floor, and how, for a single, perfect second, the world felt quiet—too quiet to be real. The moment was the way the child’s shadow stretched long and thin across the sidewalk, moving just out of reach, as if time itself had paused to let me see it. The moment was the first time I understood that some things are not meant to be repeated, not even in memory, because they are only ever held in the space between what was and what will be.
