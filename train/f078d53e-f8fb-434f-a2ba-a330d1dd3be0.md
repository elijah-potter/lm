The path before me was jagged: the whispers of doubt coiled around my ribs, the weight of tradition pressing like a second skin. Yet, in the quiet before the storm, a voice—my own, maybe—had whispered: *"What if you don’t look back?"*

I hesitated. The familiar was a shield, the known a crutch. But the unknown was a door, slightly ajar, and the wind outside whispered promises of what might be. The leap wasn’t just about the jump; it was the act of choosing to trust the fall before you even took the first step.

The hands of the clock ticked slower than my heartbeat. The stakes felt infinite. And yet, for the first time, the fear wasn’t the enemy—it was the fuel. I exhaled, bracing myself, and let go.

*"This time,"* I thought, *"I’m not running."*
