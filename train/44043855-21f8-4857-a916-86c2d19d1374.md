The cliffs of **Vermilion Peninsula** at dawn, where the Atlantic crashes against jagged rocks, mist curling between the jagged teeth of the earth. A solitary lighthouse stands sentinel, its beam cutting through the fog as it marks the last land before the endless, whispering ocean. The air hums with the call of gulls and the distant cry of a seal, the scent of salt and decay clinging to the damp stone. Below, the waves lap at the base of the rocks, a rhythmic, hypnotic sound that feels like a heartbeat. The horizon is a thin, bleeding line where the sky meets the water, a place where the world seems to pause—just for a moment—before the next storm.
