The neon hum of the abandoned subway station pulsed through the cracked concrete walls, casting jagged shadows across the flickering streetlamps. Raindrops plastered against the raincoat you’d borrowed from the corner deli, your breath fogging in the cold air. The sign above the door—*Exit: Only Emergency Personnel*—was splattered with graffiti, but the words *You shouldn’t be here* had been scratched into the metal in fresh, jagged lines.

A rusted handrail scraped against your elbow as you leaned forward, fingers brushing the edge of a rusted metal box half-buried in the dirt near the platform. Inside, something glinted—a rusted key, still warm to the touch. The station had been empty for years, but the air smelled of damp earth and something older, something like old blood. You’d followed the sound of distant voices, whispers that didn’t belong to anyone you knew.

The key turned in the lock with a groan, the door groaning back as it opened. Inside, the fluorescent lights buzzed like dying insects, illuminating a hallway that stretched on forever. The walls were lined with discarded items—half-eaten sandwich wrappers, a crumpled newspaper, and something worse: a child’s drawing of a family, but one of the parents had been stabbed through the chest. The ink had faded, but the bloodstains were still fresh.

A door at the end of the hallway stood slightly ajar. Your pulse quickened. The voices had stopped. The key felt heavy in your pocket, too heavy to ignore.

You stepped inside. The room was a mess—clothes strewn across the floor, a desk piled with yellowed papers, and a figure curled in the corner, barely moving. They were a child, no older than ten, their face pale with exhaustion. They opened their mouth to scream, but the sound was choked off by a small, bloodied handprint on the back of their throat.

Then, the lights flickered. The child’s eyes locked onto yours before they went still.

The key in your pocket turned cold.
