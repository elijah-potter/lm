## TIL the “Wow Signal” Remains Unexplained

Today I learned about the “Wow! signal,” a strong narrowband radio signal detected on August 15, 1977, by the Big Ear radio telescope at Ohio State University. It was so unusual that astronomer Jerry Ehman, who was reviewing the data, circled the printout and wrote “Wow!” next to it – hence the name.

The signal originated from the direction of the constellation Sagittarius and lasted for 72 seconds before disappearing. Its frequency, 1420.455 MHz, is particularly intriguing as it corresponds closely to the hydrogen line, a frequency naturally emitted by hydrogen atoms and often considered a universal marker for potential extraterrestrial communication.

What makes the Wow! signal so compelling is that it has never been detected again despite numerous attempts to relocate it. Theories abound, ranging from a terrestrial source of interference (though none have been definitively identified) to a brief, focused transmission from an extraterrestrial civilization. While it’s possible the signal was a fluke or a misinterpretation of natural phenomena, its characteristics remain stubbornly anomalous, fueling ongoing speculation and captivating the imagination of scientists and enthusiasts alike.
