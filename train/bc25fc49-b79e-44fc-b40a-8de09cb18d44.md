The eerie glow of the half-lit streetlamp flickered against the cobblestones, casting elongated shadows that stretched like skeletal fingers toward the alleyway. The air hummed with the faint, rhythmic *thump-thump* of distant machinery, muffled through the thick, decaying brick walls. A lone figure stood at the edge of the ruins—a half-roofed archway where the last remnants of a collapsed church tower jutted upward like a jagged tooth. The person’s posture was rigid, arms crossed, shoulders hunched slightly, as if bracing against unseen weight. Their breath came slow, visible in the cold dampness, and their gaze was fixed not at the figure ahead, but on the way the light danced across the moss-stained pavement, the way the stone cracks in the floorboards glowed faintly with age. The only sound was the occasional whisper of wind through the skeletal remains of what had once been a cathedral’s stained glass, now reduced to jagged fragments suspended in the dim glow. The tension wasn’t in movement but in the *absence* of it—the way silence pressed like a physical weight, heavier than any sword drawn or battle raged. The ruins themselves spoke, not with words, but with the slow, deliberate decay of their own story.
