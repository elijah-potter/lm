A dimly lit alleyway, fog thick enough to obscure streetlights, cast long shadows over the cracked pavement. Neon signs flicker weakly, their blue and red hues bleeding into the murky air like a dying star. The air hums with the distant wail of a siren, but no sirens answer. A half-eaten sandwich lies abandoned on a rusted metal table, its plastic wrapper peeling back to reveal a soggy patty. The alley’s walls, lined with peeling graffiti in faded, layered colors, seem to whisper secrets no one can hear. A single, abandoned bicycle leans against a dumpster, its chain rusted but still intact, its reflection warped by the rain-slicked ground. The only movement is the slow, deliberate tread of a figure—clad in a hoodie with the sleeves rolled up—who walks past, shoulders hunched, eyes scanning the dark corners. The background isn’t just scenery; it’s the *clue* of what’s coming.
