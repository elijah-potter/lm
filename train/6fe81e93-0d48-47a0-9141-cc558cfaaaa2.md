A rusted key rolled into the cracked sidewalk near the abandoned alleyway, its tarnished surface catching the dim light filtering through the broken window of the boarded-up storefront. A hand brushed against it, fingers closing around the cold metal as the wind picked up, sending a gust of autumn leaves swirling around the corner.

The key felt familiar—too familiar, like it belonged to a pocket no longer worn. The owner paused, scanning the shadows before following the sound of the chain ringing, the familiar *click* of a lock turning. Inside, a small, half-empty jar sat on the worn counter, its contents spilling onto the floor—a handful of loose change, a single crumpled note, and a folded receipt from the diner across the street. The key fit perfectly into the lock, and the door creaked open, revealing a cluttered desk, a half-empty coffee mug, and a single, folded photograph with a name in faded ink: *"Lena, 1998."* The scene was a quiet, unexpected reunion.
