Most people fondly remember the days of Blockbuster. The once prominent film rental company had a stranglehold on the industry for many years. Many people made a habit of going to Blockbuster once a week to pick a movie. It became a true part of people's routines. In the late 90's, Blockbuster had over 9,000 rental stores and 65 million customers. Flashforward to today though and things could not be more different. Blockbuster filed for bankruptcy with over $900 million in debt. There are no more Blockbuster stores. What happened? The easy answer is the emergence of streaming services like Netflix and Hulu. But Blockbuster was struggling even before Netflix moved to a streaming service. And the issues that caused the downfall of Blockbuster are bigger and more complex than the rise of streaming services. Along with the emergence of sites like Netflix, Blockbuster struggled to stay up-to-date with the demands of the times. Blockbuster's stores required a level of commitment and effort from their customers, and there was an issue with their payment structure. This article will explore the downfall of Blockbuster and the elements that led up to it.
