The last light of dusk bleeds through the cracked glass of the window, painting the room in crimson and gold. Dust motes swirl in the dying air, catching the faint glow of the streetlamp outside. The air smells of damp earth, old wood, and something metallic—like rusted iron and the faint tang of rainwater on copper.

The floorboards groan underfoot, bearing silent witness to decades of footsteps. A half-eaten sandwich sits on the kitchen counter, its crust crisped and the edges browned by time. The refrigerator hums, its contents half-concealed by a layer of dust, some items long forgotten. A single, half-empty bottle of wine lies on the counter, its label faded to a ghostly print.

The walls are lined with framed photographs—faces blurred, memories faded. One corner of a room holds a child’s drawing of a house with a garden, its colors washed out by age. A grandfather clock ticks slowly, its pendulum swaying like a pendulum of time, the hands moving forward despite the weight of everything else.

The last breath of the day lingers here, thick with the weight of what was and what will be. The last time I come, I’ll take this with me—not just the things, but the quiet, the silence, the way the light catches the edges of things. The last time I stay.
