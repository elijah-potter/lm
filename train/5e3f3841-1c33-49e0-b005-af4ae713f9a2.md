The last time she thought of him, it was in the dim glow of her bedside lamp, her fingers tracing the rim of an empty wineglass. The plan had been simple: forget him, move on, let the past drown like a memory in the ocean’s depths. But memory, stubborn as ever, clung to the edges of her thoughts like a shadow refusing to fade.

She had written it down on the back of an old postcard—*Paris, 2018*—the one with the cracked frame where the ink had blurred over time. *"If you’re reading this, he’s already forgotten."* The words were half-hearted, the penmanship shaky with the weight of half-truths. She’d slipped it into her bag, sealed it with a rubber band, and left it behind when she moved. But memory doesn’t respect distance or time; it finds its way back like a ghost returning home.

Now, standing on the balcony of her new apartment, she traced the same path she had in Paris, the one where he’d once pressed his lips to the glass of her favorite red wine. The plan had shifted—she’d add a lock to the door, a new name for the coffee she’d always ordered the same way, a habit that would outlast his memory. But memory, it turns out, is a thief who steals not just what you forget, but what you never truly let go.

She exhaled, the breath curling into the cold night air. The plan was still there, waiting to be rewritten, but memory was already rewriting it in ink that refused to fade.
