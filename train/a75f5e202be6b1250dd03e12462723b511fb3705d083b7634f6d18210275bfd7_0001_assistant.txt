The crooked old man's study was a very unusual place. It was dark and old-fashioned. Coated in spider's webs and coated in dust, the room held several bookcases. The lower levels of these bookcases held ancient leatherbound books, some rumoured to be magical. In the central bookcase was a bust of Shakespeare, cast in copper and worn and weathered with verdigris. The professor was known by some to hold discussions with the bust, puzzling over a variety of academic and ethical conundra. In the far corner of the room, stood a tall and dark mahogany closet, within which, peering from the shadows, was a skeleton. On the western wall hung a tall mirror, gilded and ornamented, within which was said to live the spirits of long-dead philosophers. A statue of a raven perched on a plinth by the door posed mid-flight and mid-caw. The feathers were intricately carved and it was so lifelike that the observer might have expected it to take off and flutter around the room at any moment. A portrait of a young girl hung near the fireplace, richly decorated in a birch frame, the dark wood reflecting the firelight. The girl was beautiful, and it was said that she might step out at any moment to beckon an onlooker to join her in her painted world. Finally, across from the fireplace was a richly carved oak bar and benchtop. Sumptuous pillars and panelling framed the generously stained surfaces. On the rear bench was placed a crystal decanter, with a strange amber liquid sloshing about inside. The stopper of this decanter was formed in the shape of a wolf, teeth bared, little glass fangs covered in minute and frozen glass blood.
