A bustling, sun-drenched village square at noon, where elders and children alike gather under a shade of olive trees. Men in leather aprons and women in embroidered kaftans trade stories and spices at wooden stalls. A child tugs at the sleeve of a man who also once carried her to school, their laughter blending with the chatter of a dozen voices. The scent of freshly baked bread mingles with the salt of the sea, carried by a breeze from the docks where fishermen unload their catches. A single, well-worn path winds through a maze of alleys where every face is a known one, names whispered in hushed tones or shouted across the crowd. The air hums with familiarity, the rhythm of a place where everyone, no matter how young or old, has found their place.
