The café’s hum was a shared breath, thick with the scent of stale coffee and something older—cigarette smoke, perhaps, or the quiet despair of those who had given up on the day’s promises. The woman at the table beside mine was absorbed in her phone, fingers dancing over the screen like she was conducting an invisible orchestra of notifications. She never looked up.

Across the room, an older man sat alone, his knuckles white around his empty mug. He had been here for hours, and yet no one had noticed his fingers trembling. No one had noticed the way his posture sagged, how his jaw clenched as if fighting something he couldn’t name.

I watched him for a while.

He was the first to order. The first to leave. The first to disappear into the night without a word to anyone. No one remembered his name, his face, his existence beyond the briefest glance. They passed him like water over stones.

I noticed the way his coat sleeves were rolled up, how the sleeves of his shirt were rolled down, as if he were trying to hide something. Not from the world, but from himself. Not from the cold, but from the weight of it.

I noticed the way his breath came in shallow, uneven gasps, how his lips parted just enough to let out a sound that wasn’t quite a word, not quite a sigh. He was breathing in the air we all shared, but he wasn’t breathing it in. He was breathing it *through*, like he was already somewhere else.

I noticed the way his hands moved when he wasn’t looking, how they curled into fists or uncurled into something fragile, like he was trying to hold onto something that had already slipped away.

I noticed the way the café’s light caught in his dark hair, how it made him look like he was made of something older than the world—something that had seen more than the rest of us, and yet no one cared.

He was the kind of person who didn’t need to be seen. He was the kind of person who was seen but never *noticed*.

And that was the thing that made him disappear. Not the cold, not the darkness, but the way we all turned away when the light was just bright enough to make us see.
