The air smells faintly of damp earth and woodsmoke from the chimney, though the fire has long since died down. The cobblestones beneath my boots feel cool and uneven—some cracked, others worn smooth by decades of foot traffic. The walls of the old tavern are lined with faded posters of local legends, their colors faded by time. The wooden bench where I sat last time is still there, its weathered surface worn by countless hands, but now it’s empty except for the occasional stray cat prowling near the door. A single, half-empty mug sits abandoned on the counter, its glass etched with the name "Eliza" in someone else’s handwriting. The scent of roasted chestnuts and mulled wine lingers in the air, though the oven is cold. The jukebox hums a distant tune, its needle skipping over a record that’s been lost to time. Outside, the river’s murmur is steady, as it always has been. The bridge creaks underfoot, but the path is clear—no one to greet me, no one to remind me that I’ve come back. Just the familiar, aching quiet.
