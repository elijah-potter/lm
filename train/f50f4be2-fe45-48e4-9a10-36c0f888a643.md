The rain hammered against the rusted roof of the old barn, turning the afternoon into a muted, drizzly dusk. Inside, the air smelled of damp earth and old wood, thick with the weight of forgotten things. A single candle flickered on the counter, casting long shadows that danced across the walls like ghosts of something long past.

From the corner of the room, a figure stepped forward, their boots silent against the worn floor. They carried a satchel slung over one shoulder, the strap dusty with age. The lantern in their grip cast a narrow, golden beam over the space, revealing a face half-hidden in shadow—a woman, perhaps, though her expression was unreadable.

She didn’t speak at first. Instead, she pulled a folded note from her pocket, unfolding it slowly. The words were familiar, but not to her—just as they weren’t to anyone else. The paper had been pressed between two pieces of leather, the edges worn smooth.

The candle guttered. She placed the note on the counter, then turned to leave.

The door creaked shut behind her. Outside, the rain continued to fall, but no longer seemed so loud.
