The dim glow of the café’s fluorescent lights cast long shadows across the empty booth. She traced the rim of her coffee cup, the warmth of the liquid forgotten. Outside, a single bird chirped against the window—too loud, too abrupt. The silence inside the booth had become a comfort she didn’t realize she’d missed.

Her phone buzzed. A text from her mother: *"You coming home tonight?"*

She deleted it without reading. The silence in her head was louder than the hum of the AC. She leaned back, closing her eyes. The weight of words—her own, others’—had been a cage. Now, she was free.

The bell above the door chimed. A stranger sat across from her, fingers steepled. He didn’t speak. Neither did she. The pause stretched, not broken, not filled. Just. Empty.
