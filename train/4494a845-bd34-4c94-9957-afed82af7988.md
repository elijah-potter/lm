The hum of the city faded behind him as he sat on the park bench, knees pulled to his chest. Raindrops tapped against the glass of the café across the street, soft and insistent. His fingers traced the rim of the empty coffee cup, the heat of the liquid long gone. A single cigarette burned between his lips, the embers glowing like a dying star.

She sat across from him, arms crossed, jaw set. No words came between them, not since the argument that had turned into an argument about silence itself. Her eyes were closed, head tilted slightly, as if listening to something beyond the world around them. The silence wasn’t empty—it was thick with the weight of what had been left unsaid.

Outside, a car horn blared, sharp and sudden. He didn’t react. The noise dissolved into the background, another layer in the tapestry of sound that had become meaningless. He exhaled slowly, smoke curling from his mouth. The cigarette was out in seconds, but it didn’t matter. He had chosen silence, and it was his sanctuary now.
