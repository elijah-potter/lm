My Christmas cactus didn't bloom this year like it has for the past 10 years or so. Did I do something wrong and what can I do differently next year?
- James G. Winder
Christmas cacti are photoperiodic plants. They need to receive at least 12 hours of darkness each night or temperatures below 50 to 55 degrees to induce flower buds.
Maybe your house has been brighter or warmer than usual. Or, did you repot this year? These plants prefer to be root bound in their pots. A repotting will cause fewer or no flowers for the next year or so. The flower buds may also form and then drop off. This is typically caused by a change in temperature or the plant producing more buds than can be supported. In general, these plants prefer to stay relatively dry, so water when the top couple inches of the soil are dry. Fertilize the plants monthly with liquid fertilizer and keep the plants in bright indirect light, indoors or outdoors until frost. The cactus will get a bleached or sun-burned look if it receives too much sun.
After the plants have flowered, they can be pruned to promote better branching. The pruned sections can be rooted and given to a friend.
