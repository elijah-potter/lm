A vast, empty observatory dome, its glass walls reflecting the starlit void but hiding the silence between the unseen. The air hums faintly with the residual warmth of a single, long-dead laser, its beam now a ghostly arc suspended in the darkness. The floor is littered with discarded tools—rusted wrenches, frayed cables—each item a relic of human presence, though no one walks here anymore. The ceiling, a curved lattice of fiberglass, bears faint, irregular scratches where unseen fingers once touched it. The walls, lined with panels of dead sensors, glow with a cold, flickering blue light, their displays showing nothing but static. A single, abandoned control console stands in the center, its keyboard still sticky with the residue of a forgotten command. The weight of the silence presses down, thick as the absence of breath in a tomb. The only sound is the distant, rhythmic pulse of a malfunctioning deep-space beacon, its signal fading into the void like a dying ember.
