In a dimly lit café, a weary customer slides a half-eaten pastry toward the barista, then abruptly shifts focus to the coffee machine. *"So… how’s the new roast?"* they ask, while the barista adjusts the grinder. Outside, a street musician’s sax blends into the hum of conversation, but the customer’s eyes drift toward the window, then back—no longer on the drink, now on the passing crowd. A waitress slides a menu across the table, and the customer picks up a pen, scribbling a note on the back. *"Actually, I was wondering about that last delivery—was it on time?"* they ask, as if the barista hadn’t just been interrupted. The scene subtly shifts: the barista nods, then returns to stirring a pot. The musician’s sax fades into the background as the customer’s gaze lingers on the menu, now half-read.
