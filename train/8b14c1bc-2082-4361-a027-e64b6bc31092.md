In the quiet hum of a midnight city, where neon ghosts flickered like dying stars, a stranger sat beneath the flickering glow of a half-burned café sign. The scent of stale coffee and forgotten dreams clung to the air, thick as the silence between the cracks in the pavement. They had come for answers, but the café’s patrons were already ghosts—just whispers and half-remembered conversations from the past.

Their notebook lay open, pages filled with questions they had carried for years: *Why do we forget?* *What if the only truth is what we refuse to see?* The stranger traced a finger over the edges of the text, where ink had blurred with time, as if the words themselves were begging to be rewritten. A single line stood out, bold and unyielding: *"Do you believe in ghosts who remember?"*

Outside, the distant wail of a siren cut through the night, but the stranger only turned back to the café’s empty windows, where the reflection of their own eyes—blurred, like the edges of a dream—seemed to answer before they could ask again.
