The rain had turned the cobblestone streets into slick mirrors, reflecting the flickering gas lamps and the distant glow of the city’s neon signs. Elias adjusted the collar of his worn coat, the fabric damp against his skin. His breath came in shallow gasps, the cold biting at his lungs.

He had expected to find her here—her laughter still echoing through the alleys of the old quarter, the way she used to run her fingers through his hair before he would kiss her forehead. But the café was empty, save for a single figure hunched over a steaming cup, their back turned to the door.

His stomach twisted. He had written her a letter, one he had promised himself he’d never send. The words were raw, the sentiment desperate. He had never been good at this—keeping promises, holding back.

A rustle of fabric. Elias spun, heart pounding. There she was, standing in the doorway, her dark eyes reflecting the same shadowed light as the street outside. Her hair was damp, her lips pressed into a thin line.

"Took you long enough," she said, her voice soft but sharp with disappointment. She stepped into the dim light, her heels clicking against the worn floor.

Elias exhaled, relief and regret warring in his chest. "I—I was waiting for you."

She tilted her head, studying him with an expression that was both weary and familiar. "You always were."

He reached for her hand, but she pulled away. "Don’t."

A beat of silence. The weight of the unspoken between them pressed down like a stone.

"You should go," she said, her voice breaking. "Before it gets worse."

Elias watched as she turned, her coat billowing behind her, the rain still falling. He didn’t follow.

She walked away, her footsteps fading into the night, leaving only the sound of the distant laughter that once had been his.
