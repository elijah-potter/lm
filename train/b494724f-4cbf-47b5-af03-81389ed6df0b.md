The city’s neon glow blurred into static, a ghostly dance of light and shadow on the pavement. Outside, the hum of traffic was drowned by the distant wail of a siren, but inside, the café’s air smelled of stale coffee and something sweeter—old secrets, unspoken. The barista poured the second cup of tea with deliberate care, her hands trembling not from age but from the weight of a question she hadn’t dared ask herself in years.

She had come here to forget, to lose herself in the rhythm of someone else’s hands, the sound of laughter, the way the world felt smaller when you were surrounded by strangers who didn’t know your name. But the truth, like the coffee, was bitter. She had spent decades pretending she didn’t care. That she was content. That the ache in her chest was just the normal weariness of a life well-lived.

But now, as she watched the reflection of her own face in the steam rising from the cup, she saw the cracks—the ones she’d buried, the ones no one else would ever find. She had lied to herself for so long, she didn’t even recognize the person staring back anymore.

The admission came like a whisper, a half-hearted confession she didn’t believe. She had loved him once, yes, but love wasn’t the thing that had broken her. It was the way she had chosen silence over truth, comfort over honesty. The way she had let the world keep moving without her, even when her heart wanted to scream. She had been afraid of being found out—not just by him, but by herself.

And that, she realized, was the hardest part. Not the fear of loss, but the fear of *knowing*.
