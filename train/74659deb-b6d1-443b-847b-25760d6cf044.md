**Dawn’s Hush**
Frost clings to the windowpane like a whisper. The sky, a bruised canvas of violet and teal, thins to a whisper of gold as the first light bleeds through cracks in the clouds. Raindrops, slow and deliberate, trace jagged paths on the glass—each one a tear, a sigh. The air hums with the quiet pulse of damp earth, thick with the scent of damp earth and something older: the earth’s breath after a long sleep.

Inside, the fire crackles, its embers mirroring the faint, shifting hues of the sky outside. The room is a maze of shadows and half-lit corners, the curtains drawn tight against the early chill. A single lamp flickers, casting long, wavering fingers of light across the floor. The walls, painted a muted green, seem to hold the weight of the storm—each breath of wind outside feels like a whisper against the pane.

A chair creaks under the weight of a figure seated at the table, hands wrapped around a mug of something bitter and warm. The coffee is black, the steam curling like smoke from a distant chimney. Outside, the rain has turned to a steady, rhythmic patter—like the heartbeat of something vast and patient. The figure exhales slowly, watching as the light dances on the raindrops, each one a fleeting promise, a flicker of hope that dissolves as quickly as it forms.

The walls are warm, but the air is thick with tension. The fire’s glow is soft, almost melancholic, as if it knows the weight of what’s to come. The room is a sanctuary, but the storm outside is a storm of emotions—raw, unfiltered, and impossible to ignore. The fire’s light flickers again, and for a moment, the room seems to hold its breath, waiting. Then, the rain softens, the wind dies down, and the world outside breathes a sigh. Inside, the fire burns brighter, the coffee steams on the table, and the figure leans back, listening to the silence that follows the storm.
