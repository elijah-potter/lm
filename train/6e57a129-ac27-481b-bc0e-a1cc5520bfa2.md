The **broken glass** lay beneath the lamplight, a shattered symbol of the agreement they’d made. **"I’ll be back by midnight,"** Elias had said, his voice rough with the weight of his own exhaustion. But the clock had run out, and by dawn, his family found only the empty bottle and a note: *"Too late to fix this."*

For months, their house had been a battleground of silence and half-truths. **"I didn’t mean to,"** his mother said, her hands trembling as she wiped the kitchen table. **"I just… needed to think."** His father, a man who’d once been their rock, now avoided the dining room table like it was cursed. Their daughter, sixteen and sharp-eyed, had started keeping a journal under her bed, writing down every *"I’m sorry"* as if they were currency. The promise had been more than words—it was a contract, and neither side had fulfilled their end.

Now, the promise was just a name for the cracks in their lives. The glass didn’t shatter again. It just *stayed there*, a reminder that some debts were paid in blood, not time.
