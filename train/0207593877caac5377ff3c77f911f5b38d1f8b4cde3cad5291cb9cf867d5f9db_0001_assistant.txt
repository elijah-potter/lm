"Good luck Paul! I'll be sitting with some of the other parents behind first base and your father should be here by the time the game starts. We love you!" said Mom as I grabbed my baseball bag out of the trunk of the car.

"Thanks, Mom. Love you too," I said as I slammed the trunk closed and turned toward the field. I tossed the bag over my right shoulder and jogged toward the dugout. As I got close, I pulled the bag down and leaned it up against the wall. I grabbed my glove and my bat out of the bag and brought them into the dugout with me. I was pretty early for the game still. Only the coach and a couple of my teammates were in the dugout.

"Howdy, Paul," said Coach, "I've got you playing second base again today. You can go ahead and get some stretching and long toss in with James."

"Sweet, sounds good, Coach." I grabbed my glove and jogged quickly toward the outfield where James was already standing. I'll be honest. I was a little bit upset that Coach had me playing second base. I was hoping that he'd soon let me play shortstop again. Before I joined this team, I had only ever played shortstop. I was fast and had a really strong arm. On my old team, I was one of the best players. That was one reason we'd joined this new travel team.  My parents wanted me to play with a more competitive team so I could get better experience and coaching. I missed a lot of my old teammates. A lot of them were friends that I still hung out with sometimes. But I had to admit that it was probably good for me to play with this new team. We won a lot of games, and everything felt a little bit more serious. My newer teammates were still nice, but I hadn't really made any friends yet. I gave James a wave as I got closer and tossed my glove to the ground to start stretching.
