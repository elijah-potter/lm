**The makeshift news hub in downtown Cairo, Egypt, on the evening of October 28, 2019.**

A crowd of around 150 people—men, women, and children—sits on plastic chairs and improvised benches outside a closed government office, its windows boarded up with plywood. A few tents, scavenged from markets, provide shade. A single battery-powered radio, cracked and barely functional, broadcasts intermittent news bursts from Al Jazeera and local stations. The air smells of exhaust, damp concrete, and the faint tang of kerosene from the generators running in the street. A few phones, cracked screens and dead batteries, are passed around, their screens flickering with static. Some clap when a voice crackles over the radio—an update on protests, arrests, or government statements. The tension is palpable; some whisper, others shout over the radio’s weak signal. The news is slow to arrive, and the wait feels endless.
