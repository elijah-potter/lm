Ceremony took place in a dimly lit hall, illuminated by flickering torches and ancient tapestries depicting forgotten rites. The crowd—dressed in stiff, embroidered robes—seated in rigid rows, their faces blank with the weight of tradition. The high priest stood at the altar, hands clasped in prayer, his voice a monotone chant in a language no one understood. A procession of masked figures marched in, carrying censers and ceremonial daggers, their steps synchronized to an unseen rhythm.

The host, a gaunt man in a black cloak, delivered a speech: *"This is duty, not choice."* The crowd murmured, some nodding in silent assent. The priest raised a ceremonial knife, and the host knelt, blood pooling on the stone floor as the priest invoked an unseen deity. The torches flared higher, casting long shadows across faces that were already hollowed by obligation. The ritual ended with a single, deafening strike—something broken, something forgotten.
