The moon hung low over the abandoned factory, its silver glow bleeding through rusted windows like a slow, creeping infection. The air smelled of damp wood and old oil, the kind of scent that lingered in your bones long after you left. Inside, the hum of forgotten machinery drowned out the distant laughter of children playing in the alley below. They didn’t know the ghosts of these walls, the way the paint peeled to reveal hidden messages scratched into the plaster—*"This place is cursed,"* someone had once whispered, though no one believed it.

Then, the door creaked open.

A gust of wind howled through the empty space, carrying the scent of something sharper now—burning gasoline and the metallic tang of fear. The children’s voices faded into silence. The light from the streetlamps cast long, trembling shadows that seemed to twist and writhe as they moved.

A figure stepped into the dim light—a girl, no older than sixteen, her eyes wide with something that wasn’t just fear but something older, something that had seen too much. Her fingers trembled as she reached for the rusted latch, her breath coming in sharp, uneven gasps. The factory doors groaned open, and the night swallowed her whole.

She never came back.

The children found her the next morning, huddled against the broken window, her voice a whisper lost in the wind: *"It’s not just the walls… it’s the people who built them."* Then she was gone, just like the others. And the factory, left to its own devices, began to hum again—its secrets whispering back to those who dared listen.
