The café’s hummed like a quiet machine,
while others fidgeted with their screens,
each lost in a glow that never dimmed—
yet I saw the way the light cast shadows
on the walls where the old map still hung,
its edges worn by time and hands
that traced its paths with curious fingers.

I heard the cough of the elderly man
who sipped his tea, his breath slow,
while others rushed past, their steps
a blur of haste, their minds already
on the next thing they’d miss—
but I lingered, letting his words
seep into the cracks between the noise.

The child’s laughter, bright and untamed,
drowned by the parents’ voices,
their voices sharp with instructions
for a future they’d already lost sight of.
I watched the way the child’s eyes
shimmered with wonder—unfiltered,
unbound by the weight of tomorrow’s tasks.

The artist’s sketchbook, half-finished,
its pages filled with lines that whispered
of something deeper than what they saw—
what they chose to ignore, to overlook,
to bury beneath the noise of the world.

I saw the world as it was,
not as it was supposed to be.
And in that quiet moment, I realized:
the world didn’t need more noise.
It needed more of what others missed.
