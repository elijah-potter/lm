The morning sun peeked through the curtains of 14th Street House, painting the room in soft gold. Twelve-year-old Mateo sat at the cluttered kitchen table, his fingers tracing the edges of the half-eaten pancake. The recipe had been his grandmother’s—*panqueques de miel*—and though he’d dropped half into the syrup, he still felt the warmth of her voice in the way she’d hummed while stirring.

“You’re a mess,” his mother said, pushing a bowl of scrambled eggs toward him. Mateo groaned but picked it up anyway. Outside, the street was alive with the hum of school buses and the distant laughter of kids on the playground. He glanced at his reflection in the kitchen window: messy hair, a scuffed sneaker, and a backpack full of books he’d never finish.

“You know you’re not allowed to stay out past curfew,” his mom said, but her tone wasn’t scolding. It was the same one she used when she’d catch him reading *The Hobbit* at 10 PM instead of homework. Mateo exhaled. He’d been avoiding the library again.

That night, as he lay in bed, his phone buzzed. A text from his best friend, Javier: *Dude, the old warehouse on 15th is empty tonight. Think we should check it out?*

Mateo’s stomach dropped. He’d been there once before, during the summer, with his dad. They’d found old records, maybe even some of his grandma’s old letters. But his dad had called it a bad idea, and now his mom was suspicious of him hanging out with Javier. He should’ve said no. But Javier was always the one to find the weird stuff.

---

The warehouse smelled like rust and damp wood. The doors were locked, but Javier had his lockpick set. Mateo’s fingers trembled as he followed him inside, the fluorescent lights buzzing overhead. The walls were lined with shelves, half-empty boxes stacked like a labyrinth. Then they saw it: a half-buried crate in the corner, its lid pried open.

Inside were stacks of yellowed newspapers, dated from the ‘70s. Mateo’s breath caught. He’d never seen anything like this. Javier grabbed one, turning it over. The headline read: *“Local Teen Discovers Hidden Archive.”*

Mateo’s pulse pounded. His grandma had always talked about her family’s history—how her parents had fled during the revolution, how they’d kept secrets. If this was her work…

He flipped to the back, where a handwritten note was tucked in: *“For Mateo. When you’re ready, find the old attic. The letters will tell you more.”* His name. His grandma’s handwriting. His heart raced.

Javier handed him the paper. “You’re gonna tell your mom, right?”

Mateo’s throat tightened. He could tell her, or he could keep it a secret. He could run away to the attic tonight, or tomorrow, or never. But what if this was a trap? What if his grandma was dead?

He picked up the paper again, the words blurting out in his head: *Mateo. The attic is your key. The past is waiting.*

Javier’s voice cut through his thoughts. “Dude, you’re freezing.”

Mateo shoved the paper into his pocket. He’d think about it later. For now, he had to decide what he was willing to risk.
