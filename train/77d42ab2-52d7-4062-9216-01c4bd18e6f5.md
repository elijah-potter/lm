In the quiet hum of a dying neon sign flickering to life, a stray cat with eyes like polished obsidian pressed against the glass. It watched as a single, perfect raindrop—diamond-edged—plunged from the sky, turning the streetlamp’s glow into a prism. The cat’s tail twitched; the drop vanished in a pulse of light.

A man in a trench coat, clutching a half-empty flask, stumbled out of the alley, nearly colliding with the cat. His breath fogged the air as he muttered, *"Another one."* The cat hissed, but the raindrop had already split into three, now dancing on the pavement like fireflies.

The man caught one between his fingers, then dropped it. It shattered. He exhaled sharply, then picked up the coat pocket where the flask lay—empty. His reflection in the cracked mirror of a gas station didn’t move. The cat vanished into the shadows before the last drop of light in the sign guttered out.

Outside, the first snowflake landed. The streetlights died. The world held its breath for a single, perfect moment.
