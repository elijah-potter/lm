The rain pattered against the grimy windowpanes of the empty café. The neon sign flickered—*Café Noir*—glowing weakly in the dark. A lone figure sat at a round table, arms crossed over the worn wooden tabletop, legs dangling over the edge. The coffee mug sat half-empty, steam rising in silent circles. A half-eaten croissant crumbled at their feet, forgotten.

Outside, the city hummed—a distant siren, the occasional car horn, the murmur of a crowd far off. The air smelled of damp pavement and old books. The wait was endless. They didn’t know what waited, only that they had to endure it.
