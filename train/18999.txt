To celebrate Women's History Month, the Girl Scouts of the USA chose a group of young scouts to dress up as their female role models, from Hillary Clinton to Whoopi Goldberg. The future women then got to pose for photo shoots in character—and they all nailed their looks.
"Women’s History Month is always an important time for Girl Scouts to reflect on the amazing women who’ve come before us—blazing trails, setting records, breaking barriers, and generally wowing the world," reads a blog post on the Girl Scouts website. "Sharing their stories with girls today is important not only in providing phenomenal role models, but also in showing girls the power that each of us has within us to create a better world."
Young girls dressed up as Amelia Earhart, Hillary Clinton, Condoleezza Rice, Lucille Ball, Whoopi Goldberg, Celia Cruz (the Cuban-American "Queen of Salsa"), Mae Jemison (the first African-American woman to travel in space), Madam C.J. Walker (an entrepreneur and philanthropist), Juliette Gordon Low (the Girl Scouts' founder), and the three NASA mathematicians portrayed in Hidden Figures: Katherine G. Johnson, Dorothy Vaughan, and Mary Jackson. As explained in press materials sent to Self, many of the women the girls dressed up as "have revolutionized historically male-dominated industries like comedy, music, government, entrepreneurship, and the sciences."
Girl Scout Daisy Kennedy is one of the three girls in the Hidden Figures-inspired shot. "Without them, people might not be able to go to space," she said, about the three women in the film. "Girls can be anything when they get older. I want to run my own company when I grow up!"
The photos were taken by Tricia Messeroux of Toddlewood. "My hope is to not only enlighten young girls about who blazed the trails, but also to encourage girls of all shapes, sizes, and ethnic backgrounds to continue the legacy and perhaps make their own history" Messeroux said in an email to SELF.
