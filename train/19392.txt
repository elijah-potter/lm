What is Masik Shivratri? Masik Shivratri is a blessed Hindu holiday that is exclusively devoted to Lord Shiva. Meaning of Masik Shivratri – Shivratri signifies Lord Shiva’s night in Hindi, while masak means monthly. For Hindus, especially those who worship Lord Shiva, this event is of immense significance.
So, when is Masik Shivratri? Masik Shivratri starts on 24 September this year. Masik Shivaratri, also known as Masa Shivaratri, occurs on Chaturdashi Tithi during the waning lunar phase. Hindu worshippers fast on this day each month and worship Lord Shiva and Shakti in order to get enormous blessings. There are 12 Masik Shivaratri celebrations per year, and each month has special meaning.
Story Behind Masik Shivratri:
The stories surrounding Masik Shivratri are many. Numerous mythical stories are connected to the incident. According to Shiv Purana mythology it goes that, Brahma and Vishnu had a heated argument regarding their supremacy. A fiery Lingam and an endless Jyoti materialised from them all of a sudden. Since neither the beginning nor the end of the Lingam could be located, both agreed that the person who finds the end first will be recognised as the greatest God.
Lord Vishnu transformed into a boar to discover the button, while Lord Brahma flew high as a swan to observe the beginning of the flaming Lingam. Lord Brahma, however, lied and claimed to have seen the lingam at the top. Just then, Lord Shiva showed up and revealed his true nature as an infinite Jyotir Linga. As a punishment, Lord Shiva swore that no one would ever worship Lord Brahma on earth. Hence in the form of the divine Lingam known as the Shiva Lingam, Lord Shiva appeared on the day of Shivaratri.
So, on this day, Lord Shiva is worshipped by his followers in order to get his blessings and live a happy and tranquil life.
Masik Shivratri Significance:
According to the Skanda Purana, both Maha and Masik Shivratri are powerful days to worship the almighty Lord Shiva. It is Shiva Shambhu’s greatest day. Masik Shivratri Ritual:
For the duration of the day, one observes a fast as part of the traditional Masik Shivaratri ceremony. After performing Shiva Pooja, the next morning, some devotees break their fast. People can perform Shiva Pooja at home or go to local Shiva temples. By performing Abishekam (hydration Pooja) with holy water, milk, curd, ghee, honey, turmeric powder, Vibhuti (holy ash), and rosewater, one can worship an idol of Lord Shiva or a Shiva Lingam. The only surefire way to gain the Lord’s blessings is to chant “Om Nama Sivaya.”
