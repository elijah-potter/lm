A fleeting neon-lit alleyway in an abandoned district, its doors swinging open only for a few hours each night. The air smells of damp concrete and ozone, where a single flickering bulb casts long shadows. A street performer plays a distorted jazz tune on a battered violin, drawing a ragtag crowd of locals—some drink from empty cans, others sit in plastic chairs, lost in whispered conversations. The entrance to the hidden underground bar behind them is barely visible, its door slightly ajar, inviting but never quite reachable. Once the crowd disperses, the alley vanishes, swallowed by the city’s ever-changing facade.
