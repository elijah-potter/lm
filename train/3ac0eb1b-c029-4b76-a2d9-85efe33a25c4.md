A dimly lit room, half-lit by flickering fluorescent tubes, held a single chair near a cracked window. The air was thick with the scent of damp wool and stale coffee, the last remnants of someone else’s breakfast. A half-empty mug sat beside the chair, its surface scuffed with fingerprints, the condensation long dried.

The person—clad in a long coat, sleeves rolled up to their elbows—leaned forward, fingers tracing the rim of the mug absentmindedly. The hands were calloused, the nails worn down to nubs, each one a story of years spent gripping something tight, turning it over and over in their mind.

Outside, the city hummed—a distant, rhythmic pulse of traffic and voices, like a heartbeat that didn’t quite match the clock on the wall, which had stopped at 3:17 AM. The clock’s hands were frozen in time, a cruel joke against the seconds slipping through like sand.

A single candle, unlit, sat on the coffee table. Its wick was dead, its wax cool, but the glow of its ember still lingered in the shadows, a half-remembered promise. The person had tried to light it once, but the flame had guttered out, leaving only the ghost of warmth in their chest.

They had waited here before. The same chair, the same mug, the same cracked window. The same half-expectation of what might come next, though they couldn’t say what it was. Maybe a letter, maybe a knock at the door. Maybe nothing at all.

The silence was the only thing they could count on. Everything else was a question mark, a pause between breaths. They closed their eyes, but the world didn’t fade—it just stretched out, endless and uncertain.
