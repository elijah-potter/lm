Warm dampness clings to the stone walls like a whisper of mist, thick enough to press against the skin. The scent of damp earth and crushed leaves lingers—earthy, sharp with the tang of rainwater. A soft, rhythmic *plink-plink* from a distant fountain, the sound muffled by layers of time and moss. The air hums with the distant chorus of crickets, their voices layered with the low, steady pulse of water. The ground beneath bare feet is uneven, yielding first to cool pebbles, then softer moss, before giving way to the damp, yielding clay beneath. A faint, salty breeze carries the scent of the sea, carried inland by the wind. The temperature is cool but not cold, a comfortable equilibrium. The wind carries the scent of pine and something sweet, like honeyed rain. Shadows stretch long and mysterious, shifting like living things. The air smells faintly of decaying flowers and the earth’s slow, organic breath. The sound of dripping water and distant laughter, muffled but unmistakable, lingers in the humid silence.
