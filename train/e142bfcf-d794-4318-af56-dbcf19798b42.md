In the dim glow of fluorescent lights, their breath caught in the hollow of their throat as they stepped into the crowded room. The clatter of heels on polished wood echoed like a drumline of strangers, each one absorbed in their own world. Their eyes scanned the sea of faces, searching for a familiar face—something that would anchor them to something more. But the room was a maze of unfamiliar expressions, the hum of whispered conversations masking any hint of recognition.

They sat at the back of the room, where the noise was slightly quieter, where the air felt less thick with the weight of expectations. Their hands fidgeted with the fabric of their jacket, the sleeves too short, the fabric too stiff, a constant reminder of the mismatch between their clothes and the casual, carefree energy of the others. They wished they could blend in, but the way their knees touched as they leaned forward was too rigid, their posture too stiff, the way they held their notebooks under the table a habit from a different place.

The teacher’s voice cut through the silence, and they flinched, their ears still adjusting to the sudden change in volume. They wanted to disappear—just for a moment—into the hum of the classroom, but the world seemed to be holding its breath, waiting for them to say something, to fit in, to belong. They wished they could be invisible, or at least invisible to the world around them. They wanted to be seen, but not in the way they were being seen now.

A laugh from the table next to them made them flinch. It was a familiar sound, but the way it was delivered—loud, brash—felt foreign. They wanted to laugh too, but their throat tightened, and their voice came out strained, half-hearted. They wanted to be accepted, but the fear of rejection was a physical ache, a weight in their chest.

Outside, the world seemed to move in slow motion, each step, each sound, each glance a barrier between them and the rest of the world. They wanted to be part of it, but the fear of being different, of not fitting in, was a constant shadow, a silent reminder of the divide between them and the world around them. They wanted to belong, but the world felt like a place where they didn’t quite fit.
