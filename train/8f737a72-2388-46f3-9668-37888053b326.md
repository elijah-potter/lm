A soft rain patters against the overgrown windowpanes of an abandoned cottage, its eaves sagging under decades of moss. Inside, the last ember in the fireplace flickers to black, casting long shadows on the walls where spiderwebs stretch between jagged cracks. The air hums with the faint scent of old wood and damp earth, the remnants of a life that once lived here. A single, crumpled letter lies open on the worn table, its edges frayed—its message now unreadable, swallowed by silence. Outside, the wind stirs the last of the autumn leaves, settling them one by one onto the hearth like scattered confessions. The door creaks shut behind the last visitor, the latch holding it firm. No footsteps echo, no voices rise—only the slow, inevitable settling of the world into stillness.
