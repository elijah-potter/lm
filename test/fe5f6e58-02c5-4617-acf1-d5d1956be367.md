The discovery of **quantum tunneling** in superconducting materials (e.g., high-temperature cuprates) revealed that macroscopic systems could spontaneously transition into superconducting states by overcoming energy barriers through quantum probability—previously only observed in tiny particles. This implied that even in everyday matter, electrons could pair and move without resistance at near-room temperatures, revolutionizing energy, computing, and transportation by enabling frictionless energy transmission and ultra-efficient electronics.
