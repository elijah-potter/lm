A **chronometric anomaly** in the **Abyssal Clock Tide**—a submerged archipelago where the ocean floor is embedded with **lunar quartz crystals** that pulse in inverse relation to tidal cycles. Local fishermen use **pendulum-based "gravity wells"** (fixed to driftwood) to track "reverse hours": a "noon" when the tide is ebbing corresponds to the solar zenith in the sky, and "midnight" occurs when the high tide peaks. The anomaly is measured in **"tide-sprints"**—a 4-hour cycle of 12 "tide-marks" (each representing a phase of gravitational pull), making a full "day" last ~5 Earth-days. Navigators mark their passage on **blackened obsidian "time-slabs"** that record not minutes, but the **last 3 ebbs** before arrival. The clock is **not linear**; a day here is 12.5 Earth-days, and the "year" spans 12 tide-sprints (~60 Earth-days).
