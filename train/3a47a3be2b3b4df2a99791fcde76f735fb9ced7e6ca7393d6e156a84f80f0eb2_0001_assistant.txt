On 21 July 1969, American astronaut Neil Armstrong became the first person to walk on the Moon, after the successful landing of the spaceflight Apollo 11. Stepping out of the Apollo Lunar Module Eagle on the moon's surface at an area called the Sea of Tranquility, Mr. Armstrong, together with lunar module pilot Buzz Aldrin, explored the Moon's surface, collecting data and soil samples weighing 47.5 pounds (21.55 kg), before planting the US flag at 03:41 GMT. They unveiled a plaque that read, “Here men from the planet Earth first set foot upon the Moon, July 1969 AD. We came in peace for all mankind,” which had then President Richard Nixon's signature. The two stayed on the Moon's surface for 21 hours and 36 minutes before joining their other companion, Michael Collins, who successfully orbited the Moon in the mothership Columbia.
