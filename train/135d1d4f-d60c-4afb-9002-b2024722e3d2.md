The city streets hum with the same familiar rhythm after weeks away. The scent of rain on concrete lingers—bitter, sharp, the same as always. The café where I used to sit in the morning now has a new owner, but the same worn armchairs remain, their edges still slightly frayed. The barista’s uniform is the same color, the coffee machine’s steam curls up identically from the same old cup.

The subway tunnels feel narrower, the echoes louder. The same graffiti remains: faded but unmistakable—names, symbols, the same mix of rebellion and nostalgia. A local musician’s band played here last week, and the bassline from their set still lingers in the air.

A park bench by the river is exactly where I sat last summer. The trees have grown taller, but the shadow of the old oak tree still falls where it always has. Someone left a fresh pot of coffee on the bench—someone new, but the smell is the same.

I pass a storefront that used to sell the same vintage record I bought last time. The display case is empty now, but the label still hangs crookedly on the glass. The bell above the door rings as I walk past. The sound is the same, though.
