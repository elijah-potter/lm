In the hollowed-out core of an abandoned subway tunnel, a single flicker of neon light pulsed against the damp walls. A man—mid-30s, dressed in a patched-up raincoat—stood at the edge of a crumbling platform, his breath misting in the frigid air. His pocket watch, a relic from another era, chimed *twelve* as the seconds ticked.

A distant *clang* echoed from the depths. He turned. A rusted train door, half-swallowed by mold, groaned open. Inside, a single passenger—an elderly woman in a moth-eaten coat—stood motionless, her fingers tracing the edge of a half-empty coffee cup. The tunnel’s eerie silence was shattered by the *whoosh* of the train’s engine as it roared to life, its headlights cutting through the gloom.

The woman didn’t move. The train’s brakes screeched to a halt *a foot* from her. The man’s heart raced as the woman reached into her coat, pulling out a folded newspaper—*The Daily Grind*, its pages brittle with age. She unfolded it, her trembling hand pausing at the headline: **"MORNING IN THE TUNNEL."** The train’s door slid shut.

The man exhaled, watching the train’s taillights vanish into the tunnel’s endless void. The neon pulse dimmed. The clock on his wrist—now broken—stopped. The single minute had ended.
