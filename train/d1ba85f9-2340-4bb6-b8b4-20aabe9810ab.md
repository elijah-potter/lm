A neoliberal cityscape where skyscrapers with self-adjusting facades shift their glass panels to reflect current cultural trends—one moment reflecting a neon billboard for a viral TikTok dance, the next an abstract art installation that updates based on algorithmic sentiment analysis. Algorithms guide urban infrastructure: traffic signals cycle in real-time based on drone surveillance of commuter moods, while sidewalks expand or contract to match foot traffic density, creating a dynamic metropolis where infrastructure evolves faster than human adaptability.

Suburbs blur into corporate "living spaces" with AI-managed housing units, where residents’ preferences (color schemes, furniture layouts) are pre-configured via facial recognition and biometric data. Schools operate as micro-data centers, teaching children to optimize their cognitive performance for next-gen job markets. Meanwhile, older generations live in "legacy zones"—communities with rigid, outdated infrastructure, where residents cling to analog routines, unaware that the digital fabric they’re embedded in is rewriting the world around them faster than they can process change.
