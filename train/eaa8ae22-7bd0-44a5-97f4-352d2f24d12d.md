Two figures stood at the edge of the riverbank, their silhouettes stark against the dusk. The water rippled beneath them, reflecting the fading light like fractured glass. One held a half-empty bottle of whiskey, the other a folded piece of paper with a single name scrawled in ink. Their shoulders brushed as they leaned closer, the scent of damp earth and salt mingling in the air.

She reached out, fingers brushing his sleeve—not to touch, but to pause. His breath hitched. She had said nothing, but the words were there, written in the way she held her breath, the way her eyes lingered on the name on his paper, the one he’d never told anyone before. He didn’t move, didn’t speak, but his hands trembled as he slid the paper toward her. The moment the paper touched her palm, the weight of years dissolved like mist. A single, shared sigh. Then they turned toward each other, faces close, lips parting—nothing said, but understood. The river lapped softly, indifferent.
