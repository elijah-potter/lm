Potatoes an easy-to-grow veggie that's good for you
Did you know that potatoes are fat-free, cholesterol-free and sodium-free? They are high in vitamin C and a good source of potassium. Potatoes are a feel-good food. They also are one of the easier vegetables to grow in your garden.
Potato seed is actually just a potato from last summer. The potato is cut into pieces with each piece about the size of a golf ball. Each seed piece should have an eye. You will notice a tiny sprout growing out of the eye at planting time. Potatoes should be planted in early to mid-May. Plant the seed pieces about 10 inches apart and about 3 inches deep.
The seed piece under the soil is protected from frost, but once the stems emerge, they are susceptible to frost damage. You may need to protect them from late spring frosts.
When the plants are about a foot tall, you will need to hill your potatoes. Hilling is pulling in about 3 inches of soil around the plants as you move down the row. I use a hoe. Potato plants will only set the new potatoes in the soil between the seed piece and the soil surface.
Potatoes are susceptible to both early blight and late blight. These diseases often come late enough that the plant has already formed the new potatoes. Size and yield can be reduced by the blights. Fungicides can be used to protect potato plants from these diseases.
The major insect pest of potatoes is the Colorado potato beetle. If potato beetles appear, picking them off by hand works well. Be diligent in June to keep them picked off. If they are on the plants long enough to lay eggs you will have a problem. The young larvae that hatch are very hungry, and there are lots of them.
Finally, harvest your potatoes when the vines die off. Store them in a cool, dark place.
