The river hummed its slow, steady song beneath the overhanging willows, their leaves whispering secrets of the past. The air smelled of damp earth and crushed pine, a quiet perfume that settled into the bones like old wine. She sat cross-legged on the bank, fingers tracing the edge of a cracked pottery jar, its glaze still holding the memory of a forgotten season. The storm had passed—just as it always did, relentless but temporary—leaving behind the same calm as the first light of dawn, though the sky was still a bruise of twilight.

A fish broke the surface, its silver scales catching the last light. It vanished beneath the water’s surface, leaving only the ripple of its escape. The sound of her own breath was the only answer to the silence between her thoughts. She exhaled, slow and deliberate, like the tide receding, and for the first time in weeks, the weight of the world felt like a dress she had outgrown. The river lapped at her feet, unasking, unjudging. She closed her eyes and let the weight of the world dissolve into the rhythm of her own pulse.
