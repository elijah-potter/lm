The café’s dim glow flickered between them as the older man’s fingers traced the rim of his coffee cup, pausing just long enough to meet the younger woman’s gaze across the table. His voice, usually measured, carried a note of something raw—*hunger*—when he said, *"You always knew the way home, even when you didn’t remember the road."* Her breath hitched; the words hung between them like a question. The silence stretched, but then she exhaled, *"I remember everything now. Even the parts I thought I’d lost."* His jaw tightened. Outside, a single streetlight cast their shadows long and sharp. The moment between them dissolved into something unspoken, charged with years of unspoken things.
