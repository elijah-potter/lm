Rich with the weight of centuries, the stone floor hums underfoot, a whisper of moss and age. The air thickens with the scent of damp earth and old wood, mingled with the sharp tang of rain-streaked metal. A distant murmur—voices, laughter, the low hum of machinery—carves through the quiet like a blade through butter. The temperature is cool, damp, clinging to skin like a second layer of flesh. Shadows stretch long, shifting with unseen movement, the ghost of the sun’s past glow still clinging to surfaces. The silence between breaths is heavy, thick with the scent of something burning faintly, like the first hint of smoke before the fire takes hold.
