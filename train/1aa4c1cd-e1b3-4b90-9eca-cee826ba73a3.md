## Explain Like I'm Five: Why the Sky is Blue

Okay, imagine you have a big box of crayons, right? And in that box, you have *every* color you can think of: red, yellow, green, blue, purple, even orange! Now, imagine you shine a flashlight at those crayons.

The flashlight’s light is actually made of *all* those colors mixed together! It looks white to us, but it’s really a rainbow hiding inside.

Now, the air all around us, the air we breathe, is made of tiny, tiny bits – much smaller than even a grain of sand! These tiny bits are like little bouncy castles for light.

When the sunlight comes down to Earth, it bumps into those tiny bouncy castles in the air. Now, here’s the important part: blue light is like a really energetic little ball. It bounces *everywhere* when it hits those bouncy castles! It bounces up, down, sideways – all around!

Red light, and yellow light, and green light – they're more like bigger, heavier balls. They mostly go straight through the bouncy castles.

Because blue light is bouncing *everywhere*, it spreads out across the whole sky. That’s why when we look up, we see blue! It’s blue light bouncing all around us.

Think about it like this: if you threw a small, bouncy ball in a room full of pillows, it would bounce all over the place! But if you threw a big, heavy ball, it would mostly roll straight ahead. Blue light is the bouncy ball, and the air is full of pillows!

Sometimes, when the sun is setting, the sky looks red or orange. That’s because the sunlight has to travel through *more* air to reach us. All the blue light has already bounced away, so we see the red and orange light that's left.



Isn't that neat?
