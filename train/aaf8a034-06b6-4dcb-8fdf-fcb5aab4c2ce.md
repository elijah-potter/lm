A floating market in Hanoi’s old quarter, where vendors in traditional conical hats sell fresh herbs, sticky rice noodles, and lotus-flavored tea from bamboo stalls balanced on bicycles. The market’s alleyways curve like ancient rivers, lined with lantern-lit shops that blend colonial-era architecture with modern street cafés. The scent of grilled lemongrass and jasmine drifts between the stalls, while the sound of the Hoan Kiem River’s gentle flow and the clatter of wooden boats reflect the city’s layered history. The air hums with the rhythm of a thousand voices—some from decades past, others from the next block’s new condominiums.
