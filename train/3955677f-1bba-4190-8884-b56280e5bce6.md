The gradual erosion of the old bridge’s wooden planks exposed hidden cracks, revealing a decaying substructure beneath. Engineers documented each layer—rust-stained metal reinforcements, once solid, now crumbling like brittle bones. Water seeped through gaps, staining the deck a damp gray, while termites carved their tunnels into the timbers. The first replacement plank was nailed in place, but the joints sagged under the weight of the repaired section, testing the integrity of the design.

The next phase saw the removal of the corroded beams, their rusted remnants left to rust further. New steel braces were welded into the frame, their cold precision contrasting with the organic rot. A series of small adjustments—tightening bolts, smoothing rough edges—transformed the skeletal structure into something stronger, though still fragile. The final step was the gradual installation of the new decking, each board laid with deliberate precision, the final one snapped shut with a final, quiet *click*. The bridge, once a relic of time, now stood as a testament to incremental resilience.
