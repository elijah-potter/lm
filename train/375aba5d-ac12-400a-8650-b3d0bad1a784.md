The flickering neon sign of a closed diner casts a jagged glow on the cracked pavement where Elias kneels, fingers tracing the edge of a half-eroded map. His breath fogs the cold air of an autumn night, but the scent of damp earth and distant rain lingers in his lungs. The map’s ink has blurred with age, the lines for the "City of Lost Things" now smudged like a half-written dream. A pocketknife, cold and clutched too tightly, rests beside him—no sharp edge, just the weight of a promise. The wind howls through the alley, carrying whispers that aren’t there, echoes of voices that may never return. He counts steps backward, each one a step toward the promise of something that isn’t a door, a key, or a name. The only certainty is the ache in his ribs where the missing thing used to live.
