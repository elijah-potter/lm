She sat in silence, hot tea in hand, and felt the cool air move past her wrists and up her sleeves. Warm light streaming through leaves overhead gleamed as river water on the windowsill. The cat pawed it for a moment of study before taking his seat beside her. And a spring aroma enlivened the old room, brought it into the outdoors, and forgave the cold nights they spent tucked under the quilts. An eastern towhee, perched on the porch rail, sang his two-part love call; two sharp syllables and a trill. Two more sharp syllables and a trill. Two more and his three-count trill. He disappeared into the walnut tree but tried his song again. The cat tilted his chin to her and squinted his eyes. It's probably past 6:30, she thought. A late start to the busy day ahead. She stood up and undressed there beside the window, no human eyes to watch her, and grabbed yesterday's clothes hung on the stone of the smoke stack whose embers were dim and low. Putting on her wool socks, she slipped them into each boot, right toe exposed but secured, and pants tucked in at the shin. Her coat was a man's size and draped awkwardly over her small frame like a boy wearing his father's suit. Putting her hat on she said, "Alright Bingey, time to get to work," and the cat followed her out the front door and to the tool shed.
