A cavern lined with moss-covered stone pillars, their surfaces etched with faint, ancient runes that hum under dim bioluminescent fungi. The air is thick with the scent of damp earth and something older—like crushed petals and forgotten incense. A slow drip echoes from the ceiling, forming a cascade of silver droplets that refract light into prismatic shards. The walls pulse faintly, as if holding a collective breath. In the center, an open fire burns low, casting long shadows that move like living things when no one is looking. The floor is a patchwork of cracked tiles, some glowing faintly where the moisture seeps through. A lone bench sits half-buried in the shadows, its back carved with the same runes as the pillars. The silence isn’t absolute—it’s the quiet of something holding its breath, waiting for the next breath to fill it.
