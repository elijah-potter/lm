The **Old Town Square** at **Dusk**

Silent cobblestones reflect the last golden light of a setting sun. The air hums with the unspoken rhythm of the market—merchants’ whistles, the occasional clink of a coin, the murmur of bargains whispered, not shouted. The **clock tower’s hands** stop mid-minute, though the time is never written. Children run between the stalls, their laughter swallowed by the low murmur of adults who trade in favors, not currency.

A **stone bench** under the old well is where the elder always sits. They watch but say nothing. The **lanterns** flicker to life at twilight, their glow filtering through the **draped curtains** of the tavern, where the bartender pours the same drink—**black**—to all who ask. The **blacksmith’s hammer** never strikes, yet the anvil vibrates with an unspoken need.

A **dog**—black as the well’s water—lies at the square’s edge, tail between its legs. It doesn’t bark. It doesn’t beg. It simply **listens**. The rules are in the **unspoken glances**, the **half-finished sentences**, the way the **old woman in the red shawl** always steps aside when the **child with the silver coin** passes. The square breathes. It knows what’s expected. It *knows* what’s forbidden.

The next day, the same things happen. The same people. The same silence. The same unspoken laws.
