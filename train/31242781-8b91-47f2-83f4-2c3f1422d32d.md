The first mistake came when Elias forgot to close the door behind him. The cold draft seeped through the crack, making his breath fog in the dim hallway. He had been late—again—and his boss’s glare had been sharper than usual. As he reached for the knob, his fingers brushed the edge of the old, dented mailbox instead.

Inside, a single yellowed envelope waited. Its return address was stamped with the same faded ink as the rest of the city’s mail. Elias hesitated, then plucked it out. The paper was brittle, the seal cracked. He tore it open slowly, fingers trembling.

*"You’re next."*

The letter was brief, just a name and a date. But the words burned into his mind like acid. He had been warned before. The city’s officials had dismissed it as a hoax, a prank, a joke. But this time, the letter was addressed to *him*—Elias Carter, the man who had been the first to notice the cracks in the pavement beneath the city’s grandest square.

The next morning, his phone buzzed. A text from an unknown number: *"8:00 AM. Don’t be late."*

The clock on his wristwatch read 7:59. Elias exhaled, then grabbed his coat. The mistake had started with a forgotten door, but this time, it wasn’t just a draft. It was a countdown.
