Carpal tunnel syndrome (CTS) is a condition where a nerve gets trapped between two other things like ligaments and bones. People’s wrists have something called a carpal tunnel going through them and when the median nerve (the nerve that goes through the arm) gets trapped, it can cause pain, numbness or a tingling feeling. The median nerve is responsible for 90% of trapped nerves related to nerve damage. People usually get some of the symptoms of CTS in their thumbs, pointer finger, middle finger, and the side of the ring finger that faces the thumb. The symptoms will vary with each person. They can get pain that moves up their arm, too, and the worse the problem gets, the weaker the hand muscles can become. It can get bad enough to cause problems using the hand and make people clumsy.
When CTS first starts, people usually get symptoms at night when they’re lying down, and not so much during the day. As it gets worse, the symptoms start to happen even during the day, especially when they’re doing things like drawing, typing or playing games, since those things make people do things over and over. In the worst cases, people will have symptoms all the time.
People who are most likely to start having symptoms like tingling, numbness and pain are those who use computers for a long time each day and people who work with machines that have constant vibration. Any job where people have to do the same things over and over a lot can cause Carpal tunnel syndrome.
