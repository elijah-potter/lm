The last light of dusk bled through the city’s smog, painting the streets in sickly gold. A child’s laughter, sharp and fleeting, echoed off the brick walls before dissolving into the hum of distant traffic. The air smelled of rain promised but never quite came—just the faint metallic tang of pavement and the warm, cloying scent of the old bookstore down the block, its shelves whispering forgotten stories.

In the distance, the silhouette of the mountains rose like a sleeping giant, their peaks shrouded in mist. Above them, the sky was a canvas of shifting hues—purple where the sun bled into the horizon, streaked with the ghostly glow of a half-dissolved aurora, as if the heavens themselves were holding their breath. The stars, though few, burned brighter than usual, their cold light cutting through the twilight like the edge of a blade.
