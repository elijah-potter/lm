Once upon a time, in the annals of world lore, there lived a man of unrivaled valor named Genghis Khan. He was the creator of the grand Mongol Empire, an entity whose vastness was rivaled by none. His prowess in battle was stuff of legends, using mind games and innovative horse warrior tactics that left his enemies quaking in their boots. The lands he ruled stretched far and wide, from the eastern frontiers of Europe to the majestic Sea of Japan. His dominion reached as far north as the icy Arctic and as far south as the golden lands of Iran, India, and China. This empire, a contiguous mass of land, was of a magnitude the world had never seen before, its vastness overshadowing even the renowned Roman Empire.
