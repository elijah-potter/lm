In the hollow of an ancient oak, where time had etched its fingers into the bark, I found the book I had sought for years—a relic of forgotten voices, bound in leather cracked with age. The pages whispered secrets, though I could not always decipher them, for my heart, like the ink, had long since run dry.

I traced the words of a poet who had once loved with the same reckless abandon as I had dreamed. *"Love is not the fire that consumes, but the embers that linger."* Her words stung, and I thought of the love I had given away like confetti at a birthday that had no one left to celebrate with.

I turned to the final page, where a single line stood out: *"I am the ghost you never saw."* My pen hesitated, then trembled as I wrote beneath it:

*"I am the love I let go."*
