It wasn’t supposed to happen like this. The first time I saw her, I thought only of the way her laugh lit up the room—like sunlight through rain—until she slid into my peripheral vision at a bar in Portland, where we’d both been drinking too much whiskey and arguing about whether coffee was overrated. She was laughing at something I’d said, her dark curls escaping a messy bun, and suddenly, I was counting the seconds until she’d look at me.

She didn’t. Instead, she ordered another round, and I followed, because I’d already lost. That night, I wrote her a note, not knowing if she’d read it, and left it under her car door. The next morning, I found it folded in her jacket pocket, as if she’d meant to leave it there for me.

Now, we’re standing on the edge of a cliff in the middle of nowhere, the kind of place where the only things that matter are the stars and the way the wind shifts between silence and a whisper. She’s holding my hand, her fingers warm, and I’m trying to remember how to breathe. She says, *"We’re not supposed to be here,"* but her voice is quiet, like she’s already forgotten what she meant.

Outside, the sky splits in half—one half violet, the other bruised gold. Below us, the earth moves. Not in time, not in rhythm, but in a slow, deliberate pulse, like something old and patient waiting to be born. I reach for her, and she doesn’t pull away. The ground beneath us is soft, yielding, and for the first time, I think maybe it’s supposed to happen this way.
