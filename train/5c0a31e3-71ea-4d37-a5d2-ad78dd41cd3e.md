## A Counseling Session

**Setting:** A comfortably furnished therapy office. Soft lighting and calming artwork adorn the walls.

**Characters:**

*   **Dr. Eleanor Vance:** A seasoned therapist, known for her calm demeanor and insightful approach.
*   **Mr. Thomas Ashton:** A client experiencing significant stress related to his career.

---

**Dr. Vance:** Welcome, Thomas. Please, have a seat. It’s good to see you again. How have you been since our last session?

**Mr. Ashton:** (Sighs, settling into the chair) Honestly, Dr. Vance, not great. The pressure at work has just been relentless. It feels like it’s been building for months, and I'm struggling to keep up.

**Dr. Vance:** I understand. It sounds like you’re carrying a considerable weight. Can you tell me a little more about what’s contributing to this feeling of pressure?

**Mr. Ashton:** It’s a combination of things. We’re launching a new product line, and the deadlines are incredibly tight. My team is stretched thin, and I feel responsible for ensuring everything goes smoothly. I’m constantly checking emails, working late, and even thinking about work on weekends.

**Dr. Vance:** It sounds like you’re taking a significant amount of responsibility on your shoulders. It’s admirable that you’re so dedicated, but it also sounds exhausting. You mentioned feeling responsible – are you feeling that you are solely accountable for the success of this launch?

**Mr. Ashton:** (Pauses, considering) I suppose I do. I know, logically, that’s not entirely true. But I feel like if anything goes wrong, it will fall on me. There's this underlying fear of failure.

**Dr. Vance:** That fear is understandable, Thomas. It’s natural to want to ensure a positive outcome, especially when so much is at stake. Let’s explore that fear a bit more. When you imagine things going wrong, what specifically are you worried about?

**Mr. Ashton:** Mostly, I’m afraid of letting my team down. I don't want them to think I’m incompetent or that I’m setting them up for failure. And, honestly, I’m concerned about what my superiors will think.

**Dr. Vance:** So, the fear isn't solely about the project's success; it’s also tied to your professional reputation and the perception of your team. That’s a lot to carry. It sounds like you’re placing a significant amount of value on external validation.

**Mr. Ashton:** (Nods slowly) I think you’re right. I’m constantly seeking approval, and it’s become a source of a lot of stress.

**Dr. Vance:** It’s a common pattern, Thomas. We often tie our self-worth to our achievements and the opinions of others. It’s a habit that can be challenging to break, but it’s certainly possible. Let’s talk about some strategies. One technique we can explore is reframing your perspective. Instead of focusing on the potential for failure, could you try shifting your focus to the learning opportunities that arise, regardless of the outcome?

**Mr. Ashton:** That sounds… difficult. It’s hard to think about the positive when I'm feeling so overwhelmed.

**Dr. Vance:** I understand. It won't happen overnight. It's a conscious effort, like building a new muscle. We can also look at ways to delegate tasks within your team and establish clearer boundaries between work and your personal life. Are you able to identify any tasks you could realistically delegate?

**Mr. Ashton:** There are a few things, yes. I'm just hesitant to relinquish control.

**Dr. Vance:** That’s a very common feeling when we’re used to handling everything ourselves. But trust is a crucial element in any successful team. And sometimes, letting go allows others to shine and contribute their expertise. We can work on strategies to communicate your expectations clearly and build trust within your team. For our next session, I’d like you to make a list of three tasks you could potentially delegate and consider the reasons you’ve been holding onto them.

**Mr. Ashton:** Okay, I can do that. It’s good to have something concrete to focus on.

**Dr. Vance:** Excellent. Remember, Thomas, progress isn’t about eliminating stress entirely; it’s about developing the tools to manage it effectively and cultivate a more sustainable approach to your work and your well-being. Do you have any questions for me before we conclude today?

**Mr. Ashton:** No, I think that’s everything for now. Thank you, Dr. Vance.

**Dr. Vance:** You’re very welcome, Thomas. I look forward to seeing you next week.
