## A Counseling Session

**Setting:** A comfortably furnished therapy office. Soft lighting, a calming color palette.

**Characters:**

*   **Dr. Eleanor Vance:** A seasoned therapist, known for her thoughtful and empathetic approach.
*   **Mr. Thomas Ashton:** A client experiencing significant stress related to a career transition.

---

**Dr. Vance:** Welcome, Thomas. Please, have a seat. It's good to see you again. How have you been since our last session?

**Mr. Ashton:** Thank you, Eleanor. To be honest, it’s been… difficult. The job search continues to be a significant source of stress. I feel like I’m hitting brick walls everywhere I turn.

**Dr. Vance:** I understand. That sounds incredibly frustrating. It’s natural to feel discouraged when facing repeated setbacks. Can you tell me a little more about what specifically feels so challenging about this process?

**Mr. Ashton:** It's not just the rejections, although those are tough. It’s the feeling of inadequacy. I’m questioning my skills, my experience… everything I thought I knew about my career. I spent fifteen years in one field, and now I feel completely lost.

**Dr. Vance:** That's a profound shift, Thomas. Fifteen years is a substantial investment of time and energy. It’s understandable that you’re experiencing a sense of disorientation and questioning. It’s a significant loss of identity, in a way. Can you describe a recent situation where you felt particularly overwhelmed by these feelings?

**Mr. Ashton:** Just yesterday, I had an interview for a position I felt genuinely well-suited for. I prepared extensively, I thought I presented myself well, and then… silence. No response. It just reinforced this feeling that I'm not good enough, that I’m somehow failing.

**Dr. Vance:** That’s a painful experience, and it’s completely valid to feel disheartened. It sounds like you put a lot of yourself into that interview. I want to acknowledge the effort you invested and the disappointment you’re feeling.  It’s easy to internalize these setbacks, but it's important to recognize that rejection doesn’t necessarily reflect your inherent worth or capabilities. Often, it’s about factors outside of your control – company restructuring, a change in priorities, or simply a matter of fit.

**Mr. Ashton:** I *know* that logically, you’re right. But the feeling persists. It's hard to separate the objective reality from the subjective experience of failure.

**Dr. Vance:** Absolutely. And that’s where the challenge lies. Feelings aren’t always logical, and it’s okay to feel them. What I’m hearing is a significant amount of self-doubt creeping in. Let's explore that a little. When you say “failure,” what specifically does that word mean to you in this context?

**Mr. Ashton:** I suppose… it means that I’m not adaptable enough. That I’m not valuable. That I’m going to be stuck.

**Dr. Vance:** Those are powerful statements, Thomas.  Let’s unpack them. You mentioned adaptability. It takes considerable courage to transition careers, especially after a long tenure in one field. That, in itself, demonstrates a significant degree of adaptability.  Perhaps we can reframe the feeling of being "stuck" as a temporary phase, a period of adjustment.  And regarding feeling "not valuable," let's look back at your fifteen years of experience. What accomplishments are you most proud of? What contributions did you make?

**Mr. Ashton:** (Pauses, considering) Well, I led several successful projects… I mentored a lot of junior colleagues… I was always considered a reliable and dedicated employee.

**Dr. Vance:** Those are significant achievements, Thomas. They speak volumes about your capabilities and your value. It seems that your internal narrative is focusing on the negative, obscuring the positive aspects of your professional history. We can work on consciously challenging those negative thoughts and actively reminding yourself of your strengths. Would you be open to exploring some techniques for cognitive restructuring?

**Mr. Ashton:** Yes, I think that would be helpful. It’s just… it feels overwhelming right now.

**Dr. Vance:** It’s understandable that it feels overwhelming. We’re not going to solve this in one session. We're building tools and strategies to help you navigate these challenges. Remember, progress isn't always linear. There will be ups and downs. My role is to support you through those fluctuations. Let's focus on small, manageable steps this week. Perhaps identifying one positive accomplishment each day and consciously acknowledging it. How does that sound?

**Mr. Ashton:** That sounds… doable. A little less daunting, at least.

**Dr. Vance:** Excellent. That’s a wonderful starting point. And remember, Thomas, you’re not alone in this. Many people face career transitions and experience similar feelings of self-doubt. We’re here to work through it together.
