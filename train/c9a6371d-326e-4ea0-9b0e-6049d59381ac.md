A hollowed-out cathedral’s nave, its stained-glass windows faded to static hues of moss and rust, casting flickering, spectral light that dissolves into nothingness at the edges. The air hums with the weight of suspended time—echoes of a choir long gone, whispers of forgotten prayers, and the occasional drip of rainwater from a broken roof, turning to mist before it reaches the stone floor. The pews are empty, but the weight of their absence presses down like an unspoken presence. A single, half-remembered hymn lingers in the space between notes, drifting like smoke. The floor beneath feels like it’s sinking slowly, as if the ground itself is holding its breath. The walls breathe, too—every few minutes, they exhale a puff of dust that settles into patterns like forgotten names. At the altar, a single candle burns dimly, its flame flickering in sync with the slow, unsteady heartbeat of the space. No one is here, but the place *remembers* the ones who were.
