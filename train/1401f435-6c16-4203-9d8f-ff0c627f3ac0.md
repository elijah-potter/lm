## Proposal: The Willow Creek Pocket Park – A Climate-Resilient Community Oasis

**Executive Summary:** The Willow Creek neighborhood faces increasing challenges from urban heat island effect and localized flooding. This proposal outlines a transformative project to revitalize a currently underutilized and neglected public space – the vacant lot at the corner of Elm and Oak Streets – into a climate-resilient "Pocket Park." This park will incorporate native, drought-tolerant landscaping, rainwater harvesting infrastructure, and shaded community gathering spaces, providing tangible benefits including reduced temperatures, improved stormwater management, and enhanced social cohesion. The total project cost is $75,000, with $50,000 requested from the Municipal Community Development Fund and $25,000 secured through local fundraising and in-kind donations. This investment represents a strategic opportunity to address critical environmental and social needs while fostering a thriving and resilient community.

**1. Project Description & Need:**

The vacant lot at Elm and Oak Streets is a significant detriment to the Willow Creek neighborhood. It is frequently littered, lacks aesthetic appeal, and contributes to the urban heat island effect.  During periods of heavy rainfall, the lot exacerbates localized flooding issues, impacting adjacent properties.  This project addresses these issues by creating a thoughtfully designed Pocket Park that prioritizes climate resilience and community benefit. The park’s design emphasizes native plant species requiring minimal irrigation, a permeable paving system to reduce runoff, and a shaded pergola for community gatherings.  The park will serve as a demonstration site for sustainable landscaping practices and provide a safe, accessible green space for residents of all ages.

**2. Project Plan & Timeline:**

The project will be implemented in four distinct phases, each with designated responsible partners and deliverables.

* **Phase 1: Design & Permitting (Weeks 1-6) – Responsible Partner: GreenSpace Design Collaborative.** This phase focuses on finalizing the park design, securing all necessary municipal permits, and conducting a detailed site assessment. Deliverables include: a finalized park design blueprint (Weeks 2), a comprehensive site assessment report (Week 3), and all required permits approved by the City Planning Department (Week 6).
* **Phase 2: Site Preparation & Infrastructure Installation (Weeks 7-12) – Responsible Partner: Willow Creek Neighborhood Association, with contracted labor from Earthworks Landscaping.** This phase involves clearing the site, installing the permeable paving system, and establishing the rainwater harvesting infrastructure (including a 1,000-gallon storage tank). Deliverables include: a cleared and graded site (Week 8), installed permeable paving (Week 10), and a fully functional rainwater harvesting system (Week 12).
* **Phase 3: Landscaping & Construction (Weeks 13-18) – Responsible Partner: Earthworks Landscaping, guided by the Willow Creek Garden Club.** This phase focuses on planting native, drought-tolerant vegetation and constructing the pergola structure. Deliverables include: installation of all designated plant species (Week 15), completion of the pergola structure (Week 17), and initial irrigation system setup (Week 18).
* **Phase 4: Community Launch & Education (Weeks 19-22) – Responsible Partner: Willow Creek Neighborhood Association, with support from the City Parks and Recreation Department.** This phase involves a community launch event, signage installation, and ongoing educational workshops on sustainable gardening practices. Deliverables include: a successful community launch event (Week 19), installation of interpretive signage (Week 20), and three educational workshops completed (Weeks 6, 10, & 14 following launch).

**3. Budget:**

The total project cost is $75,000.  We are requesting $50,000 from the Municipal Community Development Fund. The remaining $25,000 is secured through a combination of local fundraising efforts and in-kind donations.

* **GreenSpace Design Collaborative (Design & Permitting):** $10,000 (paid in full upon design approval)
* **Earthworks Landscaping (Site Preparation & Landscaping):** $25,000 (paid in installments based on phase completion)
* **Permitting Fees (City Planning Department):** $2,000 (paid upon permit approval)
* **Materials (Paving, Rainwater Tank, Plants, Pergola Lumber):** $28,000 (paid upon delivery and inspection)
* **Community Launch Event & Signage:** $5,000 (paid prior to and immediately following the event)
* **Contingency (10%):** $5,000 – This fund is reserved to address unforeseen circumstances such as material price fluctuations or unexpected site conditions.

The in-kind donations represent a significant contribution to the project’s success. These include volunteer labor from the Willow Creek Neighborhood Association (estimated value: $5,000), donated plant materials from local nurseries (estimated value: $7,000), and professional guidance from the Willow Creek Garden Club (estimated value: $3,000).

**4. Community Engagement & Equity Impacts:**

Community engagement has been integral to this project's development.  Initial surveys and public forums were conducted to gather input on desired park features and design preferences.  The Willow Creek Neighborhood Association has actively championed the project and continues to serve as a vital communication channel. The park's design incorporates accessibility features, ensuring usability for individuals with disabilities. The selection of native plant species prioritizes biodiversity and supports local ecosystems. This project directly addresses environmental justice concerns by improving the quality of life for residents in a historically underserved neighborhood.  The educational workshops will be offered in multiple languages to ensure broad participation.

**5. Risk Mitigation:**

Potential risks have been identified and mitigation strategies developed.  Weather delays during construction are addressed through a flexible timeline and contingency planning.  Material price fluctuations are mitigated through early procurement and vendor negotiations.  Volunteer safety is prioritized through comprehensive training and supervision.  Community concerns are addressed through ongoing communication and responsiveness.  The contingency fund provides a financial buffer to address unforeseen challenges.

**6. Post-Grant Sustainability:**

Long-term sustainability is a key priority. The Willow Creek Neighborhood Association will assume responsibility for ongoing park maintenance, including watering, weeding, and litter removal.  The Willow Creek Garden Club will provide horticultural expertise and support.  The City Parks and Recreation Department will provide periodic inspections and assistance with larger maintenance tasks.  Educational signage will promote responsible park usage and encourage community stewardship.  The rainwater harvesting system will reduce water consumption and minimize reliance on municipal water resources.



**Call to Action:**

The Willow Creek Pocket Park represents a unique opportunity to invest in a vibrant, resilient, and equitable community.  We respectfully request the Municipal Community Development Fund’s approval of our $50,000 funding request. This investment will transform a neglected space into a valuable asset, providing tangible benefits for generations to come. We are confident that this project will serve as a model for future community development initiatives and contribute to a healthier, more sustainable city.
