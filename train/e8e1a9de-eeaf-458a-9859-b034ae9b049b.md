In a dimly lit server room, a single flicker of a distant LED marked a low-priority alert. The hum of cooling fans drowned out the whisper of a failing disk drive, its intermittent *click-click* sound ignored in favor of a more urgent system message. Engineers rushed past, their footsteps echoing over the metal floor, while the drive’s warning—*"Data integrity check failed"*—lingered unnoticed in the background, a ghostly notification in a sea of red.

A terminal screen glowed faintly in the corner, displaying a 404 error, its text barely readable. The network traffic spiked briefly, then stabilized, as if the system had decided to forget the error entirely. The network interface card’s status light, a dull amber, pulsed weakly, signaling a minor issue that would be fixed later, if ever. The room’s air smelled faintly of ozone and old electronics, a mix of neglect and the quiet desperation of systems that had long since given up on being heard.
