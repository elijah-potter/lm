Rich, deep earth beneath damp soles, heavy with minerals. Warm, sun-warmed stone cracks with the weight of centuries, releasing faint, crystalline whispers. The air hums with pollen—sweet, sticky—sticky with morning dew that clings to skin like a second layer of flesh. Raindrops, heavy and cold, pound the surface, turning to mist that lingers in the hollows between rocks. The wind carries salt from a distant shore, sharp and salty, mingling with the scent of damp moss and damp wood. A cool, damp breeze lingers, carrying the taste of dampness and earth.

Sounds: distant waves, their rhythm a steady pulse; the crunch of pebbles underfoot; the low, resonant growl of distant thunder. The absence of the wind’s roar, the quiet hum of unseen life—crickets, the occasional rustle of leaves. The taste of damp, the sound of dripping water, the weight of something unseen pressing against the skin.
