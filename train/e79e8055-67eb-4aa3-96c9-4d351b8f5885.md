A half-empty cup of tea sits on the kitchen counter, steam rising into the dim afternoon light. The mug is unfamiliar—one I didn’t make, though I poured it. The spoon I use to stir it is a family heirloom passed down from a grandparent who died before I was born. The recipe for the tea? A secret, whispered only among a few, though I’ve never asked. This moment, though it’s mine to drink, feels like it belongs to someone else. The warmth of the cup, the taste of the herbs, the way the light filters through the lace curtains—it’s all there, but it’s not *mine*. It’s borrowed, borrowed from the past, borrowed from a life I never lived. The moment lingers, heavy with the weight of what’s not mine to claim.
