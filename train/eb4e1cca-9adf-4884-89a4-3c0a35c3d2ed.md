A crumbling stone archway, half-buried in ivy, stands as a silent sentinel on a forgotten hill. The locals whisper of it—*the Bridge of Echoes*—a relic of a time when this valley was a crossroads of trade and legend. The road beneath it sags under centuries of moss and rust, its planks worn smooth by the passage of forgotten feet.

In the center, a small, circular stone sits, cracked but unbroken. Those who dare touch it hear whispers—the voices of the past, lost to time. Some say they hear a merchant’s haggling, a lover’s last words, or a child’s laughter. Others claim nothing at all, only the hollow echo of their own breath.

The village below no longer remembers the bridge’s name, but the old maps still mark it—*"Here, the river once flowed through stone."* The river is gone now, its banks overgrown, its waters long drained into the earth. But on the hill, the arch remains, a ghost in the dusk, waiting for the day when someone finally pays it homage.

Or never will.
