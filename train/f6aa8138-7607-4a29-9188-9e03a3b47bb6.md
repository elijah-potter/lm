The rain hammered against the old wooden door as Elias wiped the streaks of soot from his brow. His flashlight flickered weakly, casting jagged shadows along the walls of the abandoned factory. He had come here to retrieve the tools his father had left behind before moving to the city. The rusted iron boxes lined the shelves had been empty for years, but the promise of something useful kept him pushing forward.

His boot caught on a loose floorboard. He knelt, brushing dirt from the gap beneath the rotted plank. A small, flat box—blackened but intact—tumbled out. He scooped it up, heart pounding. Inside, wrapped in a faded piece of cloth, was a pocket watch. The chain was broken, but the face was still legible: a hand reaching for the second hand. His father’s watch.

Elias exhaled sharply, gripping the box tighter. The factory seemed quieter now, as if the weight of his discovery had settled over him. He turned to leave, but then—his flashlight beam caught something else on the floor. A single silver button, half-buried in dust. He picked it up, examining it. It was small, smooth, and cold. A piece of the past, perhaps from the factory’s old machinery.

He pocketed the button, then the watch, both now safe in his worn leather satchel. As he stepped back into the downpour, he wondered which treasure was worth more—a memory or a forgotten relic?
