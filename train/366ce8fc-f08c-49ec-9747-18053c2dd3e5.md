A mid-afternoon meeting in a dimly lit conference room, the hum of a dying projector mixing with the occasional chime of an unanswered phone. **Daniel** sat across from his boss, **Lisa**, whose expression was rigid, eyes scanning the screen where the proposal still flickered—half-finished, half-baked. "We’re dropping the project," she said, voice flat. Daniel’s fingers tightened around his pen. The numbers on the slide—*18% profit margin* under current estimates—had been his worst nightmare for weeks. But then his phone buzzed. A text from **Javier**, the intern: *"Hey, can you meet me at the café? I found a way to refactor the code to cut costs by 12%."* The numbers adjusted like they were alive. His stomach dropped. He exhaled. "Wait," he said. "Hold on." Lisa’s pen paused mid-air. The room’s tension cracked like a glass underfoot. Outside, a car horn—someone was already late to something else. Daniel’s mind raced: *What if this works? What if we salvage this?* He stood, the chair scraping, and grabbed his laptop. The café was three blocks away, but the way to the exit was now a tunnel of uncertainty. By the time he got there, Javier was already there, a half-drunk espresso in hand. The intern grinned. "You okay?" Daniel’s jaw clenched. "Yeah," he said. "Yeah, I think I’m gonna try this."
