The three layers of the skin are the epidermis, dermis, and subcutaneous layer.

1. Epidermis: The outside layer of the skin. New skin cells form at the bottom of this layer and then go to the top within two to four weeks. Each day, people naturally lose approximately 30,000 to 40,000 dead skin cells. This layer also has melanin, which gives skin its color. 
2. Dermis: The middle layer of the skin. This layer has nerve endings that send messages to the spinal cord or brain to tell people how things feel. This layer also has tiny blood vessels that bring nutrients and oxygen to skin cells and remove waste. 
3. Subcutaneous Layer: The bottom layer of the skin. This layer is mainly fat to help bodies stay warm and absorb shocks when hitting something. This layer also helps attach the skin to other tissues and has hair follicles to help body hair grow.
