A detective noticed a suspect’s alibi was off by two hours. The witness claimed they were at a café from 9:00 AM to 12:00 PM, but the suspect’s phone logs showed they were online between 8:45 AM and 11:30 AM. The café’s security footage showed the witness leaving at 10:45 AM, matching the suspect’s last recorded location.

The detective realized the truth: the suspect had lied about the entire alibi. They’d been watching the witness’s phone, tracking their movements, and then manipulated the time frame to make it seem plausible. The real crime happened earlier, and the witness was either unaware or coerced into providing an inconsistent story. The detective cross-referenced records and found the suspect had been seen near the crime scene at 8:30 AM. The misunderstanding had been deliberate—evidence was misplaced, and the witness was misled. The suspect confessed during a follow-up interview, admitting they’d staged the alibi to cover their tracks.
