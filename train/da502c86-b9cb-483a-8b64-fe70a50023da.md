The classroom hummed with the quiet clatter of pencils and the murmur of conversations, but I sat alone, my fingers tracing the worn edges of my notebook. The walls, painted in muted tones, felt like a cage—too narrow, too loud. Everyone else seemed to know what they were doing: whispering with friends, doodling in the margins, laughing at the same jokes. I was the one who sat stiffly, my hands clasped tightly around my cup of coffee, the steam rising unnoticed. The professor’s lecture drifted past, but my mind was elsewhere, replaying the way my reflection in the mirror had looked like a stranger’s face—eyes too wide, lips too straight. The hallway outside was a maze of doors, each one a potential escape, but I stayed put, the weight of being the odd one out pressing down harder than the backpack on my shoulders. The world had moved on, and I was stuck in the space between the past I’d left and the future I couldn’t claim.
