Smooth obsidian glints like shattered moonlight on a frozen lake, its surface etched with microscopic veins of polished lava. Beneath the ice, ancient moss clings to damp stone, its fronds curling against the weight of centuries, soft as a forgotten memory. The air hums with static from rusted wires embedded in crumbling brick, each thread whispering forgotten signals of a world that once hummed with life. Gravel underfoot shifts unpredictably, each pebble rough as a whisper, some smooth from age, others jagged like teeth. A damp cloth clings to exposed roots in a moss-covered crevice, their fibers tangled with the scent of damp earth and something metallic—old iron rusting in the dark. Warmth seeps through the cracks of a half-buried wooden door, its grain still visible where it warped in the damp, like a story half-told. Dust motes swirl in the afternoon light, suspended between layers of faded tapestries and the faint sheen of polished copper. The air smells of damp wool, old parchment, and the faint metallic tang of blood that never dried. A single feather lies abandoned on a table, its edges frayed, as if plucked from a long-dead bird. The walls breathe with the weight of time, their plaster cracking in patterns like spiderwebs, but beneath the cracks, the sound of distant water trickles—a river that once flowed through this place. The ceiling, if there is one, is a tangle of exposed beams, some warped, others still straight as a sword’s edge, their ends painted a faded red like dried blood. Shadows stretch long and thin, dancing like shadows of things that should not walk.
