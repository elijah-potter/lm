What is hidden in the hollowed-out heart of the obsidian cathedral where time is carved into the walls like a frozen scream.
The library’s shelves whisper secrets to the dead—books with no spines, their pages folded in half as if someone tried to read them backward.
The riverbank where the drowned men’s shoes are washed ashore, always the same pair, never the same size.
The garden where the flowers grow upside down, their roots tangled in the ceiling.
The blacksmith’s forge that never cools; the anvil hums with the sound of something being *unmade*.
The mirror that reflects the same face three times over—each reflection older than the last, their mouths stitched shut.
The door in the cellar that isn’t locked, but no one enters because the walls *breathe* in syncopation.
The café where the coffee tastes like it’s been brewed from the ashes of a thousand unspoken apologies.
The clock tower that ticks backward, counting down to a day that never comes.
The basement door marked with a single, shifting letter: **L**, always moving, never still.
The ocean where the waves form words—*YOU*, *I*, *WAS*—before dissolving into mist.
The graveyard where the headstones are buried under the bodies of those who were buried alive.
The room where the walls are lined with the remains of things that refused to be named: a half-eaten sandwich, a cigarette butts in a spiral, a child’s drawing of a place that wasn’t there.
