A crumbling stone archway, half-buried in ivy, stands at the edge of a skeletal courtyard. The plaster on the walls is peeling, revealing rough brick beneath, while exposed wooden beams sag in the wind. A rusted metal gate, its hinges broken, is propped against the wall, its lock missing. A half-drained fountain sits in the center, its basin cracked and filled with algae, the statue inside now toppled. The path leading to it is uneven, overgrown with wildflowers and brambles, and the floor tiles are missing in patches, exposing the uneven, damp earth. A lone, half-built scaffolding leans precariously against a wall, its planks warped, and tools scattered beneath it—some still usable, others rusted beyond repair. The air smells of damp stone, mildew, and old paint. The place feels abandoned, yet there’s a faint, eerie sense of unfinished business.
