The ancient citadel stands like a jagged fossil beneath a sky of endless, shifting blue—older than the whispers in the wind. Its spires pierce the heavens like the quill of a forgotten god, while the moat, a murky river of stagnant light, reflects neither sun nor moon. The streets within are narrow as cracked bones, lined with statues that move in unison when the shadows deepen, their faces carved from stone that breathes in slow, rhythmic sighs. The air hums with the chorus of a thousand unseen voices, and the scent of damp earth and something sweet, like crushed violets and old parchment, lingers like a half-remembered dream. The lanterns flicker like dying stars, their glow cast in patterns that shift with the wind—sometimes forming words, sometimes shapes that dissolve into the dark.
