I sit. I sit on my front step. I sit on my front step in a circle of chalk. I draw it close to me. Only me. My mind wanders its halls. It washes every mirror and smooths every stone. It polishes the floors and dusts the windows. It makes my mind a palace. A stronghold. But when I look out from the balcony, all I see is chalk.
I draw. A bit wider. I draw a bit wider to the sidewalk before my front step. I watch the space. I wait. Footsteps in. Another. Out. Shoes with mud on them. Worn shoes. Working shoes. When will I go back to work? My stronghold quakes. I take a breath and look up. A face flits through. Laughing on the phone. When will I make friends again? My stronghold cracks. Leans.
I draw the circle close to me. I make pillars and fill the base with concrete. I take down the mirror on my balcony and draw the circle with a more open mind. I am ready this time.
A dog trots past. An owner in fit clothing. I'm glad they're enjoying the nice day. My castle flings open its shutters. I draw the circle a little further. My neighbors, who I've never invited over, still smile at me. My sports teams that still want to be my friends wave. My community. I draw short. There is so much love that is needed. Child support, homelessness, and the environment. I return inside and walk down to the canal that runs through my castle. It is full. I have enough to give. I race back up the steps and draw a circle around the whole world.
I see everywhere I look the connectedness of humanity, the heartbreak of humanity. I check my reserves, and when they lessen, I take a moment in my castle to fill them up, but I never pick up my chalk again. My circle was never my safety. It kept me from being.
