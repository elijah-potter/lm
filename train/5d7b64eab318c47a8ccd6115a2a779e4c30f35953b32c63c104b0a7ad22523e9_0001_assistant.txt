Small holes or cracks in sheetrock are easy to repair. Using a putty knife, or just your finger, spread some spackle into the hole or along the crack. Allow that to dry for several hours, then
lightly sand the area. You may need to touch up the paint. One jar of spackle costs a few dollars and can last up to a year or more. Keep the cover on tightly to prevent it from drying out.

If you have a larger hole to cover, then it’s best to make a nice clean square-shaped cut using a hand saw. Next, cut out a piece of sheet rock the exact size of the hole. Gently place the sheetrock into the hole and tape and bed the area just as you normally would when hanging drywall. This involves applying tape and then texturing over it and sanding.

Sheetrock is purchased in 4’ X 8’ pieces and is available in various thicknesses. Try to match what you currently have in your walls when making repairs. A 4’ X 8’ sheet costs anywhere from $12 to $20 per piece. Drywall joint compound (or mud) is about $20 for a 5-pound bucket. Drywall tape comes in at around $5 for a 250-foot roll.

Though it may seem like a lot of trouble, once you buy these items, you’ll have them out in the garage or shed for any future sheetrocking repairs. These products can last for years when stored properly.
