The air smelled faintly of damp earth and old wood. A single, half-extinguished candle on the table cast long shadows across the worn Persian carpet, its wax pooling in a small puddle near the base. The walls, covered in peeling wallpaper with faded floral designs, were cracked along the baseboards, revealing a dark, unpolished plaster beneath. The wooden door, slightly ajar, leaned inward with the force of whatever had been pushed against it from the inside.

On the coffee table, a half-eaten sandwich with a single bite taken out of the bread, its crust crumbled at the edge. A half-empty glass of whiskey sat beside it, the glass rim still glistening with condensation. A single, crumpled business card lay next to it, the name on it blurred with age or condensation.

The floorboards creaked underfoot, their edges uneven and warped, one step slightly higher than the rest. A small, discarded jacket sleeve was draped over the back of a chair, the fabric stained near the cuffs. The scent of perfume lingered faintly—someone had been here recently, lingering just long enough to leave traces.
