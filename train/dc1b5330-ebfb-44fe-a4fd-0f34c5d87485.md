The elevator doors opened with a slow hiss, revealing a dimly lit hallway lined with flickering fluorescent lights. The late-night hum of distant chatter from the lobby faded into silence. Your shoes crunched against the polished marble as you pushed open the door to the conference room, finally stepping inside.

The room was already half-empty, a few scattered heads barely visible through the low-light glare of the single desk lamp. The door clicked shut behind you, sealing the relief in as you dropped into the chair across from the desk. Your phone buzzed—a notification from the morning’s meeting: *"Attendees: 3/5."*

A sigh escaped you as you leaned back, fingers drumming the desk. The weight of the day had been crushing, but this moment—just the space to breathe—felt like a small victory. Outside, the city pulsed with its own rhythm, oblivious. You were free.
