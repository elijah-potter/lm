**The Garden of Forgotten Thoughts**

I had planned to write of the old oak’s roots,
how they coiled like serpents deep beneath the earth,
how time had carved its bark into a thousand stories,
each petal a whisper from the wind’s slow breath.

But now, as I sit here, pen hovering over paper,
I realize—I had never *meant* to write this.

The oak was not the subject, not at all.
It was the *space* between my fingers,
the way my mind had wandered,
how a single thought—*how it felt to be still*—
had become the thing I clung to.

So let the pages remain blank,
let the ink stay in the bottle,
let the words I once wrote dissolve
into the air like smoke.

The garden exists. The wind still hums.
I did not need to name it.
