The rain pattered against the window like a thousand whispered secrets, each drop carrying the weight of time. Clara sat upright in her childhood bed, the quilt still damp from the night’s drizzle, fingers tracing the faded edges of the old pocket watch her father had given her—*"A promise is more than a timepiece; it’s a memory with teeth."* The clock had stopped at 3:17 AM when he left, the same moment he whispered, *"I’ll be back by midnight."* She had cried herself to sleep, but the clock still ticked, relentless, as if the universe itself was keeping count.

Years later, the watch lay in a shoebox in her attic, dust motes swirling in the afternoon light. She opened it now, her thumb brushing the brass face where the hands had frozen—*3:17 AM*—the moment her father had vanished without explanation. The air smelled of old paper and the faint metallic tang of regret. She pressed the watch to her chest, and for a heartbeat, the past pulsed through her like a second skin. Then the clock chimed, and the memory slipped away like smoke.
