The first time I realized how quiet the library was at dawn was when I left my book behind—an old, dog-eared copy of *The Count of Monte Cristo*—on the table where I’d been reading. No one else moved. Not even the librarian, who’d been gone for hours. I thought I’d been careful, but the corner of the page, where the spine curled, told me the book had been opened. I’d been too distracted to notice.

That’s the truth: I’ve been stealing books for years. Not for pleasure, not for love, but because I don’t trust anyone to return them. The ones I’ve kept are stacked in my closet, their spines worn smooth by the weight of secrets. The ones I’ve lost are the ones that mattered most.

The last one was *Moby-Dick*. I didn’t even finish it. I started it because my mother said it was a must-read, and I was too afraid to admit she was wrong. I took it home, curled up in my bed with the light on, and wrote in the margin every time the story made me think of her—how her hands trembled when she read aloud, how she always asked me if I’d read the end. I wrote: *"She’d say it was a metaphor for life. I’d say it was a lie."* The book slipped from my hands once. The library found it.

I didn’t tell them.

I’m still reading. The next one is *The Trial*. I’ll keep it until I’m sure no one’s watching.
