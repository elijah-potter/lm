A mismatched festival in an abandoned subway tunnel, where a group of strangers gathers for a rave with mismatched attire—some in neon, others in vintage 1980s flannel. The music is a distorted mix of synthwave and folk covers, played on a cracked boombox and a laptop running a cracked streaming service. Glowing candles and flickering LED lights cast eerie shadows. A makeshift stage has a DIY DJ set spinning vinyl and recorded sounds, while a few people dance in the cold, their movements jerky against the damp air. Drinks are shared from mismatched cups and vintage bottles, often spiked with whatever’s available. A lone street performer plays a broken accordion while others ignore them, too absorbed in a heated debate about quantum physics or conspiracy theories. The energy is chaotic, nostalgic, and strangely liberating—like a ghost party that never should have happened.
