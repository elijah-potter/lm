A dim morning stretches before the city’s waking pulse, the air thick with the scent of damp pavement and unshaken coffee. The clock on the kitchen wall reads 7:05 AM, but the alarm—if there was one—is long since forgotten. A half-eaten cereal box sits on the counter, its crumbs whispering tales of a half-hearted breakfast.

The shower is cold, the water running slower than usual, as if resisting the urgency of the day ahead. By the time the coffee brews, the neighborhood’s usual morning hum is already muted—loudspeakers silent, the streetlights dimmed by early dawn. A newspaper lies open on the couch, its headline about a delayed train service, the ink smudged by the morning’s haze.

The first commute is a blur of traffic jams and the occasional honk of a driver who clearly remembers the morning rush. By noon, the stomach gurgles loudly, and the only option is a drive-thru, the plastic bag heavy with greasy burgers and fries.

A meeting starts at 3 PM, but the schedule slips further, the projector screen flickering with missed slides. The boss’s email arrives at 4:17 PM, the subject line: *"Urgent: Late Update."*

The day ends with a late-night snack, the TV blaring static between channels, the city’s lights still casting long shadows over the streets. Tomorrow, another cycle begins—only this time, the clock may not be as cruel.
