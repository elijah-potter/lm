In the hollowed heart of the old oak, where time bends like a river’s curve, a poet once whispered secrets to the wind. The ink of forgotten verses still lingers in the cracks of the stone bench, though no one remembers who carved them. The moon, a coin with a thousand edges, rolls into the cup of the world’s eye, its reflection dancing in the puddles that never dry.

The air hums with the ghost of a summer’s breath, thick with the scent of crushed lavender and damp earth. The fireflies, tired of their fleeting light, have gathered in the center of the courtyard, pulsing like a slow, steady heartbeat. Their glow paints the twilight in shades of silver and emerald, a living tapestry that shifts with the breeze.

Above, the sky is not a canvas but a constellation of fractures—ripples of silver where the clouds part, revealing a thousand stars that have been waiting for the light to fade. The last embers of dusk cling to the horizon like a lover’s hesitation, their glow bleeding into the void where the night breathes. The stars are not distant points of light but threads in a grand, silent tapestry, each one a whisper from a universe that has been holding its breath since before time was named.

Dawn will come, but for now, the sky is a wound open to the infinite, its edges frayed with the promise of what comes next.
