The half-finished factory looms at the edge of town, its skeletal steel framework clinging to the skeletal earth. Rust-stained beams crisscross like skeletal fingers, the remnants of a once-great assembly line now crumbling under the weight of neglect. The floor slants unevenly—where concrete once supported machines now yields to dust and the occasional dent from forgotten tools.

A broken door stands open, its hinges swollen with age, inviting an unseen presence. Inside, the air hums with the ghost of machinery, the low whine of old engines still audible through the silence. Shelves line the walls, laden with parts that have long since rusted or vanished. A single yellow warning sign, its paint peeling, still flickers: **"DO NOT ENTER"**—though no one has dared.

The river outside mirrors the decay, its banks jagged and overgrown. Boats half-sunk in the shallows, their hulls split open like wounds. Children play here sometimes, their laughter too bright against the melancholy of the place. They chase shadows, whispering about the factory’s secrets—ghosts of workers who vanished, machines that still run on their own.

At dusk, the place feels heavier. The wind carries whispers from the unseen, and the skeletal structures seem to breathe. Some say the factory is haunted by those who never left. Others claim it’s just waiting to be built back up—though no one knows if anyone will ever finish it.
