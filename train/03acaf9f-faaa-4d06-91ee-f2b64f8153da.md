The fluorescent hum of the sterile office filled the air with a quiet ache. My chair’s wheels scraped against the tile as I sat frozen, eyes darting between the fluorescent lights and the half-eaten sandwich on my desk. The meeting’s agenda had been announced in the morning email—*"Collaborative Brainstorming: Redefining Team Culture"*—yet no one had mentioned the way my pulse quickened when I caught the CEO’s gaze lingering on me. The others had already begun—laughing about the new coworker’s awkwardness, nodding along as they debated the pros of "authenticity" in the workplace.

I was the only one who didn’t feel it. The only one who saw the way their hands fidgeted when they spoke, the way they adjusted their tie mid-sentence. The only one who wondered how many of them had been here longer than me, how many of them had learned to fake the smile until it hurt. I had read that feeling like an outsider was a kind of armor, but it wasn’t protecting me. It was making me invisible.

Outside the window, the city’s glow pulsed like a heartbeat, too bright to ignore. I exhaled, watching the condensation on my glass blur into shapes that weren’t there. Somewhere in the distance, a train whistle cut through the hum, and for a second, I thought I heard my name—just a whisper, but it wasn’t mine. The screen beside me flickered. The cursor blinked, waiting.
