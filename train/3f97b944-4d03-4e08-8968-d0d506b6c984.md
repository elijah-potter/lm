**The Weight of Silence**

I sat in the dim glow of the kitchen lamp, the coffee cup half-empty, the morning news still playing on mute. My fingers traced the rim of the mug, the familiar groove of it grounding me. Outside, the city breathed—traffic, distant chatter, the hum of a world that didn’t pause for anyone.

I had spent years chasing things: titles, validation, the quiet approval of others. And for a while, it worked. The world tilted, then righted, but the weight never lifted. It settled deeper, like sand in a jar, creeping in until I could no longer see the light.

I opened my laptop and stared at the blank screen. Words spilled from me like ink on paper—about the weight of silence, the cracks in the walls I’d built to keep the world out, the way some doors, once opened, refuse to close. I wrote because writing was the only thing left that didn’t lie.

But then the truth hit me: I hadn’t written *for* the admission. I wrote because I had to. Because the truth was already there, waiting. And if I couldn’t say it aloud, I’d drown in the same silence I’d spent so long running from.

The last paragraph I wrote was simple:
*"I admit I’m tired. Not of fighting, but of pretending. The world is too loud, and I’m too quiet to carry it all anymore."*

The screen saved itself. The rest was just noise.
