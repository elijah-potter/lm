Miscommunication in healthcare led to incorrect patient diagnosis. Medical records showed symptoms of allergic reaction but misinterpreted as mild flu. Physician’s notes omitted key patient history, causing delayed intervention. Blood test results initially flagged as inconclusive were dismissed due to rushed review. Patient’s family suspected something was wrong but dismissed warnings as stress-induced. Later, severe anaphylactic shock revealed the true cause: undetected penicillin allergy. The misdiagnosis stemmed from:
1. Lack of follow-up on patient’s prior allergy history.
2. Overlooking visual cues (e.g., rash) due to routine focus on lab results.
3. Time-sensitive error in emergency protocol adherence.

Systematic review of records later confirmed the original symptoms were consistent with severe allergic reaction, not flu. The incident prompted stricter protocols for allergy histories and real-time symptom tracking in patient records.
