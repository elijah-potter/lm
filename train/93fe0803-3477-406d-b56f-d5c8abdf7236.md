*Cables hum faintly against the rain-slicked glass of a futuristic conference room, where holographic projections flicker above a table of worn, high-tech desks. A half-dozen figures—some in sleek, augmented suits, others in retro-futuristic gear—lean over a central display, fingers tracing digital ink in the air. The air smells of ozone and recycled coffee.*

**"By 2047, they’ll have wiped out the last remnants of the Old World’s carbon footprint entirely—all by banning fossil fuels and replacing them with *something* synthetic."** The voice—smooth, synthetic, yet tinged with urgency—drips from a floating terminal. Shadows beneath the hologram’s glow pulse as a data storm rolls across the display: **quantum networks, geoengineering, and a single, ominous term: *The Great Rewilding Project.***

A woman in a **cybernetic eye**—the only visible augmentation—stares at the screen. Her fingers pause. **"They’re not talking about conservation. They’re talking about *control.*"** The room falls silent. Outside, the city’s skyline—once a maze of steel and glass—now glows with **bioluminescent forests** growing along the rooftops, their vines twisting like living art. The wind carries the scent of **fermented algae and ozone.**

A man in a **blackened trench coat**—a relic from the old world—grunts. **"So what’s the catch?"** His coat ripples as he exhales, smoke curling from a cigarette that doesn’t burn. **"They’re not giving us *choice*,"** he says, voice low. **"They’re giving us *compliance.*"** The hologram’s edges distort. The data stream fractures into a thousand fragmented images: **a child’s face, half-erased by a neural interface; a map of the world, now a grid of corporate zones; a single word—****"SYSTEM."**—spitting from a terminal’s speakers like a virus.**

The door hisses open. A figure in a **militarized exo-suit**, its armor etched with the logo of the **Global Regulatory Authority (GRA)**, steps inside. **"Protocol 9.7 is now active. Mandatory compliance begins in 48 hours."** The hologram’s voice cuts in again, but this time, the words are **static.** The screen flickers—**then goes black.**

Silence. Then, a single, slow drop of rain—**not from the sky, but from a single, glitching pixel** in the air.
