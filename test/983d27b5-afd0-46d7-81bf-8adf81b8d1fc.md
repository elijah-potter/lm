The first time Mia saw the old clock tower, she thought it was just another relic of her small town—until the hands stopped at 3:17. That was the day everything changed.

Her dad, a history teacher, always said time was a river that couldn’t be stopped, but Mia had never believed it until then. Now, every 3:17, the clock chimed differently, and the air smelled like old books and something metallic. One evening, she found a hidden book beneath the tower’s stone steps, its pages filled with names she recognized: hers, her dad’s, and a dozen others—all erased, but their voices whispered in her dreams.

At first, Mia thought it was just a nightmare. But then she met Elias, a boy who moved to town the same month the clock stopped. He claimed to have seen the same thing—faces flickering in the dark. Together, they discovered the tower wasn’t just a monument; it was a prison. The people inside weren’t lost in time but trapped, waiting for someone to free them.

Mia’s mom, a librarian, had always warned her about "the stories that don’t end well." But Mia ignored her. She didn’t know then that her mom was one of them. That the clock tower didn’t just tell time—it held the past, and the past had a way of catching up.

The library was the first place they searched for answers. Elias showed her a book titled *The Threads of Echoes*, its pages crumbling like dust. Inside, a single line repeated: *"Some doors shouldn’t be opened."* But Mia was determined. She followed the whispers, the way the clock’s hands moved backward when she wasn’t looking.

One night, they found the others—her dad, a girl named Lila, and a boy named Leo, all frozen in time. Their faces were smooth, their clothes tattered, as if they hadn’t aged a day. Mia realized then that the tower wasn’t just a clock; it was a bridge between worlds. And the only way to cross it was to let go of the past.

But the tower didn’t like that. Every time Mia tried to free someone, the clock would scream, and the faces would twist into something worse—grimaces of pain that didn’t belong to any of them. Her dad warned her, *"You can’t save them all."* But Mia had already broken the rules.

The final choice came on the night of the full moon. The tower pulsed like a heartbeat, and the people inside were screaming—not in fear, but in hunger. Mia had to decide: let them stay trapped, or risk everything to set them free. She chose free.

As she stepped into the light, the clock stopped. The faces vanished. The tower fell silent. But when she turned back, her hands were empty. And for the first time in years, she didn’t hear her name whispered in her sleep.

The morning after, her dad found her at the library, staring at an old ledger. The last entry read: *"The river runs deep, but the current doesn’t stop."* Below it, in her own handwriting, was a new line: *"I ran."*

Elias was waiting for her outside. "They’re not gone," he said softly. "They’re waiting." And beneath the clock tower, in the cracks of the stone, Mia saw them—her dad, her mom, Lila, Leo—all watching from the shadows, as if they’d never left.

The town didn’t talk about what happened. No one did. But sometimes, when the wind blew just right, Mia swore she heard the clock chime—twice. And then, a little later, her name.
