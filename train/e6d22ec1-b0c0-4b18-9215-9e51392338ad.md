Across the stage, the singer took a sip of water, the glass trembling slightly. The audience, a sea of faces half-turned toward the stage, didn’t react—not yet. But the pause was the only part of the performance they’d remember. It wasn’t the loudest or most technically flawless; it wasn’t even the most *beautiful*. It was the pause where the audience *felt* something—like a breath held too long, or the pause before a question is answered, or the moment after a lie is told and the truth lingers just beyond reach.

The pause was the crack in the wall, the gap where the real story began. The drummer struck again, and the music exploded forward, but it was already too late. The silence had been the proof.
