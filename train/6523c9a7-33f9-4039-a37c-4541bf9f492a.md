The first light of dawn crept through the cracks in the curtains, painting the room in soft, golden hues. Outside, the earth stirred, a slow and deliberate pulse of life waking from the night’s embrace. A single raindrop, caught in the windowpane, shattered like a fragile promise—one moment whole, the next scattered into a thousand silent whispers.

Inside, the kitchen smelled of coffee, bitter and rich, the first sip of something warm and familiar. The oven timer chimed, and the air hummed with the anticipation of something sweet being born. A spoonful of sugar, a pinch of spice, the delicate dance of flavors preparing to emerge.

The morning air carried the scent of damp earth and wildflowers, their petals unfurling against the stubbornness of the cold. Children’s laughter echoed from the backyard, a high, unfiltered melody that cut through the quiet like a blade. They chased each other across the grass, their shadows stretching long and jagged across the lawn before vanishing into the mist.

A car pulled up outside, its engine growling like a beast waking from slumber. The driver’s window rolled down, revealing a face half-hidden in the morning light, a hand clutching a coffee cup as if it were the only thing keeping them grounded. They leaned out, their breath fogging the glass, and said something—maybe a goodbye, maybe a promise—that felt like a secret only they could understand.

The sky, vast and endless, stretched above them, a canvas of endless possibilities. Streaks of cloud drifted lazily across the horizon, their edges soft and blurred by the light of dawn. The first true blue fingers of the day had yet to touch it, but already, it held the promise of something brighter than the rest.
