Warm, damp air clings to the crumbling stone steps of the old churchyard, moss thick as a blanket. The last light bleeds through stained-glass windows—still half-melted in places—casting jagged shadows over the half-buried tombstones. The scent of damp earth, rotting flowers, and something metallic lingers in the air, like rusted hinges. The graveyard is a maze of overgrown paths, where ivy climbs like a second skin over the iron crosses and fallen headstones. A single crow caws from a broken gargoyle, its wings catching the last fading sunlight.

The church itself stands as a silent sentinel, its tower half-collapsed, but the doors are still ajar. The air inside is thick with dust and the ghost of incense that never burned out. A faded banner—once green, now greenish-brown—hangs crookedly above the altar, its faded letters barely legible: *"Here lies...*" something that should have been inscribed but was never finished.

The last thing I see is the stained-glass window at the far end, where the colors have faded to muted blues and pinks, like a dying sunset trapped in glass. The glass cracks where it was shattered long ago, but the light still leaks through, golden and fleeting, as if the world itself is holding its breath.
