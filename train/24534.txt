Suffering from sun spots on skin? These unwelcome patches of darkened skin make your life harder in the cover-up department, that's for sure. But, instead of merely covering up your sun spots, take proactive measures in finding out what causes them and how to prevent more from popping up.
So how do sun spots start? Basically, it's a side-effect of staying in the sun too long, hence their name. But they're not quite like freckles. In some cases, it's a fungus that spawns a spot. The ones you get from staying in the sun too long also get called liver spots or, in some cases, age spots.
Sun spots on skin can also be a condition called actinic keratoses, which causes your skin to turn a brownish-red color in certain spots. If you have these spots, a dermatologist might tell you that it's from not applying enough sunscreen to your face when you were growing up. Obviously, there isn't anything you can do to go back and change that now, but you can take proactive measures to fix the spots that exist now.
Sun spots on skin don't just appear on your face. It can occur on your chest and neck and on your hands and arms. The more you go outside without sunscreen on, the more likely you are to aggravate the problem.
Sun spots on skin require strong medicine. Topical treatments made with hydroquinone work to bleach the spots and even out the skin tone; however, you can't really take measurable levels of hydroquinone without a doctor's prescription. It's very effective when used for a couple months at a time.
Doctors can treat sun spots on skin with lasers in some cases. Although they won't remove the spots, the lasers can prompt the skin to produce new melanin and exfoliate the outer layers, thus bringing the sun spots to the surface where the skin can shed them and allow new skin to emerge. Just be prepared to spend several hundred dollars for this type of treatment. You'll need a consultation with a doctor to receive permission for the procedure.
