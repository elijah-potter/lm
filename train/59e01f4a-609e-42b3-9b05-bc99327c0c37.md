In the hollowed-out core of the old mill, the last beam groaned under the weight of rust and time, its cracked surface whispering secrets to the damp wind. The clock on the wall—its hands frozen at 3:17—was a relic, its face etched with the scars of forgotten hands. The air smelled of damp wool and something older, something like old books and iron. The fire in the hearth had long since died, but embers still glowed faintly, casting long shadows that danced like restless spirits.

Elias crouched by the door, his fingers tracing the worn wood. The lock was broken, the hinges warped from age, but he didn’t care. He had come for the answer, and the mill had been waiting.

He stood, his boots crunching on broken glass and half-buried nails. The door swung inward with a sound like a sigh, the draft from outside carrying the scent of rain and something faintly metallic—blood, maybe, or the earth itself. The hallway stretched before him, its walls lined with shelves that had once held tools, now just dust and forgotten things.

Then—footsteps.

Slow. Deliberate. Too deliberate.

Elias turned just as the door behind him creaked open. A figure stepped into the light, tall and gaunt, their face half-hidden beneath a cloak that swayed like a veil. Their hands were bare, knuckles pale as bone, and when they spoke, their voice was a rasp, like dry leaves scraping stone.

*"You’ve been waiting long enough."*

The figure moved toward him, and Elias realized, with a jolt of something like fear, that this was no stranger. This was the mill itself. The thing that had been waiting for him all along.
