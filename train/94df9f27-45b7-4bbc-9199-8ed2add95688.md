The rain fell in quiet, unrelenting sheets against the old brick wall of the abandoned bookstore, each drop a whisper of forgotten stories. Inside, the scent of aged paper and damp leather clung to the air like a ghost’s embrace. Elias sat at the counter, fingers tracing the spine of a dust-covered novel, its cover torn at the corner. The book was titled *The Last Lightkeeper’s Journal*, its pages brittle with age, but the words within had seeped into his bones like ink on parchment.

He had come here for closure. For answers. The letters he’d received from the station master at the old lighthouse were nothing more than a string of numbers—coordinates, times, dates. No names. No explanations. Just a trail of coordinates leading to a place where the sea had swallowed the last lightkeeper whole. The last person to keep watch when the storms came.

Elias had been searching for this book for months. It was the only thing that might explain why he had started seeing them—figures standing at the edge of the cliffs, their faces blurred by the mist, calling his name in voices that weren’t his own. The station master had been one of them. Or so it seemed.

He turned the page, his breath shallow. The words were cryptic, the ink faded, but the meaning was clear: *The lighthouse does not keep the light. It is the light that keeps the lighthouse.* A chill ran down his spine. He had read this before, in a dream, in a fevered whisper from the sea.

A sharp knock at the door made him jump. The rain had stilled, replaced by the eerie silence of the storm’s passing. The door creaked open, and a figure stepped inside—tall, cloaked in a hood that hid their face. The air between them was thick with tension.

"You’ve been looking for this," the stranger said, voice low and gravelly. "It’s been waiting for you."

Elias didn’t answer. The book in his hands felt heavier than it should. The stranger stepped closer, their breath warm against the glass of the counter. "The lightkeeper isn’t gone," they murmured. "He’s still here. Waiting."

Elias exhaled sharply, the sound swallowed by the storm’s distant howl. He didn’t need to see their face to know what they were. Not really. He had seen enough of them already.

The stranger reached into their coat, pulling out a small, silver key. It was old, worn at the edges, but it gleamed faintly in the dim light. "You’re not supposed to have this," they said. "The key to the old lantern room."

Elias’ fingers tightened around the book. "Why?"

The stranger’s lips curled into something that wasn’t quite a smile. "Because you’re the only one who’s ever come back."

A beat of silence. Then, the stranger’s voice dropped to a whisper. "Come with me."

Elias stood slowly, the book slipping from his grip. The stranger extended a hand, and he hesitated—just for a second—before taking it. The key was cold, unyielding. He let it slip into his palm.

Outside, the storm had broken, but the wind still howled through the empty streets. The figure in the hood didn’t look back. They turned away, disappearing into the mist, leaving only the faint scent of salt and something older, something like old wood and forgotten prayers.

Elias walked out into the rain, the key warm in his hand. The book in his bag was heavy, but he didn’t care. He knew what he had to do. The lightkeeper was still waiting.

The first step was always the hardest. He walked away, toward the cliffs where the sea whispered his name.
