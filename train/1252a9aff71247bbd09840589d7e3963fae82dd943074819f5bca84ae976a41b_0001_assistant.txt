1. Introduction
     A. Brief explanation of Zero-Knowledge Proofs
     B. Why Zero-Knowledge Proofs are important
          i. Harder to trace transactions
          ii. Addresses concerns about privacy
2. Early research on Zero-Knowledge Proofs
    A. David Chaum
          i. Ecash
             a. An early effort at digital currency
             b. Based on the 1982 paper "Blind Signatures for Digital Payments"
                 i. Proposed revamp of banking system for greater user privacy
                 ii. Suggested use of cryptographically secured public and private keys for payment
                 iii. Not yet divorced from the mainstream financial system
             c. DigiCash
                 i. Created Ecash
                 ii. Featured in tech magazines
                     a. Wired
                     b. New York Times
                 iii. Failed to gain traction
         ii. "Zero-Knowledge Undeniable Signatures"
              a. Published in 1991
              b. Expands on 1989 presentation on undeniable signature protocols
              c. Describes undeniable signature scheme verifiable with a public key
      B. The Knowledge Complexity of Interactive Proof Systems
           i. Paper published in 1985
           ii. Authors
              a. Shafi Goldwasser
              b. Silvio Micali
              c. Charles Rackoff
           iii. Early mention of Zero-Knowledge Proofs
3. Zero-Knowledge Proofs and Bitcoin
    A. 2010 Bitcointalk discussion of Zero-Knowledge Proofs
         i. Satoshi Nakamoto
            a. Cited need to avoid double-spends
            b. Concerns about need to know all transaction history for a coin
            c. Queried about ways to establish Zero-Knowledge Proofs without introducing double-spend risk
         ii. Possible solution
             a. Add a "bit" that can denote whether a coin is "spent" or "unspent"
             b. Can use binary "1" (yes) or "0" (no) system
      B. No immediate solution found
           i. Nakamoto's departure in 2011
           ii. Viable zero-knowledge proof system not available yet
4. Zcash
    A. First effort to use Zero-Knowledge Proofs for a cryptocurrency
    B. ZK-SNARK
         i. Early usable Zero-Knowledge Proof System
         ii. Showed signs of trusted setup-related security flaws
       C. ZK-STARK
          i. Upgrade to ZK-SNARK
          ii. Fixed security flaw in trusted setups
5. Ethereum
    A. Continues work on Zero-Knowledge Proofs
    B. Recommends applications for Zero-Knowledge Proofs
         i. Decentralized identity
         ii. Online authentication
             a. Decentralized apps
                 i. Can connect to online Ethereum wallet
                 ii. Poses security risks
          iii. Verifiable computation
          iv. Online voting
               a. Reduces corruption
               b. Reduces collusion
