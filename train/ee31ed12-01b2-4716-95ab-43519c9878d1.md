An unfinished manuscript lies open on a cluttered desk, its pages filled with scribbled notes, abandoned drafts, and half-formed ideas. The final chapter’s title—once boldly marked—now fades into the margin, its boldness dulled by the weight of the blank spaces that follow. A half-eaten sandwich sits beside it, its crusty remnants crunching underfoot, a silent metaphor for the half-baked thoughts that linger.

The author’s hands, once steady, now tremble as they trace the edges of the last page, where a single sentence ends abruptly mid-paragraph. A stray pen mark circles the word *"conclusion"* in the margin, the ink smudged as if erased by hesitation. Outside, rain taps against the window like impatient fingers, each drop a metaphor for the stories that could have been told but never were.

Some unfinished projects are like this—half-baked, half-hearted, yet carrying the ghost of potential. The world moves on, but the unfinished remains, a haunting reminder of what was never finished.
