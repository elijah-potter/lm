While logic may tell you that the more support you have for a point, the more you can convince the other side that you are right, the BBC reminds us that most of us have fairly feeble attention spans.
Photo by Tony Webster.
In preparation for the UK's televised parliamentary debate earlier this month, the BBC shared the most important strategies one can use in a debate. Among many great tips, they noted that more information isn't always necessarily better:
Another important consideration, often overlooked, is how long to take to make a point. Maintaining the audience's attention can be tricky and Mr Vit suggests observing the length of TV ads, or the duration of a scene in soap opera, as useful guides in judging attention span.
Tips like this can be effective in presentations, too, or any other form of oral and written communication for that matter. Note that this doesn't mean talk fast—you need to be understandable—you just need to boil your point down to the most important pieces necessary to convince the other party (or the audience), so as not to lose their attention.
