The grand hall was dim, the air thick with the scent of aged wood and damp stone. At the center of the stage, a lone figure sat on a high stool, fingers tracing the rim of a cracked glass. The stage lights flickered to life, casting long shadows across the polished floor.

No one spoke. Not a whisper, not a breath, not a sound—only the occasional creak of the old floorboards. The audience, a mix of strangers and acquaintances, sat in silent rows, their faces blurred in the low light. Their gazes were fixed on the speaker, waiting.

The figure paused, then slowly stood, the movement deliberate, deliberate. The silence stretched, a palpable tension, before the words spilled out—not loudly, but with a weight that carried the room.

*"You expected something more,"* they said, voice barely audible over the hum of the distant machinery. *"But silence is the only answer we have."*
