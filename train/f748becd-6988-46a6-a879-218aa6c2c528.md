The flickering neon of the abandoned diner cast long shadows across the cracked linoleum as Maria wiped down the counter, her fingers tracing the same worn grooves as they had decades before. Her mother’s voice, sharp and weary, echoed from a tape recorder tucked under the counter: *"Maria, remember—always leave the coffee dirty."* She turned, eyes brimming, and the old woman in the corner—now just a shadow—stepped forward, her fingers brushing the table. The hum of the jukebox, *Dust in the Wind*, played on loop. A man in a 1970s suit leaned against the doorframe, his coat collar turned up against the cold, his face half-lit by the glow of a cigarette. The air smelled of burnt sugar and old coffee. He tapped his watch, then his fingers lingered on the pocket watch chain around Maria’s neck—a gift from her late father, now cracked at the wrist. A hand, calloused and familiar, reached across the table, gripping her wrist. The clock on the wall struck seven.
