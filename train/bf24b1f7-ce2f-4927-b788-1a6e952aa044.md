A dimly lit chamber, walls lined with cracked plaster and faded symbols of forgotten deities. The air hums with static, the scent of old ash and damp earth clinging to the air. A low fire burns in a hearth, its embers casting long shadows across a table draped in a single, worn cloth. The cloth is embroidered with a pattern of abstract, repeating shapes—what once may have been a map, a calendar, or a promise—but now resembles nothing in particular.

A lone figure kneels before the table, their fingers tracing the edges of a bowl filled with water that no longer runs. The bowl’s rim is etched with a name that has long since faded—only a whisper remains. The figure murmurs, but their voice sounds like a record playing backward, each word dissolving into static.

They reach into a pouch and pull out a knife, its blade dull and rusted. The ritual begins with the act of cutting the air—no blood, no purpose—just a slow, deliberate stroke that leaves no mark. The figure then picks up a small, cracked jar, its contents a mix of powdered minerals, dried herbs, and something black that might once have been ink or oil. They sprinkle the mixture over the water, watching it dissolve into a swirling, colorless mist that rises like smoke.

A final gesture: the figure claps their hands once, and the fire snuffs out, though no one has touched it. The room falls silent. The symbols on the walls flicker in the dark, as if responding to the absence of meaning. The table remains untouched. The ritual is done.
