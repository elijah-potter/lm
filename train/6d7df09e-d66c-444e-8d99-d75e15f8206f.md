The rain had been falling for hours, turning the cobblestones slick under the weight of my boots. I pressed my palm against the cold glass of the café’s window, watching as the last of the neon sign flickered out. *"Regrets are just echoes of what could’ve been."*

I turned to the barista, who was already wiping down the counter with a rag. "I’m sorry," I said. The apology was heavy, the words thick with the weight of the evening. She didn’t look up, just nodded. "It’s fine."

Outside, a car horn blared—too close, too late. The sound cut through the quiet, and for a moment, I thought of everything I’d left behind. The promises unkept, the choices made in haste, the things I’d said that I’d later regret.

The barista set down a fresh mug of coffee. "Here," she said. "Black. No sugar. Like the morning."

I took it, the heat of the cup seeping into my fingers. The apology hung between us, but she didn’t press. Instead, she turned back to the counter, her back to me.

The rain kept falling.
