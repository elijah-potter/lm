The clock ticked relentlessly against the wall, each second stretching like a thread of static, thick with the weight of indecision. The coffee cup—once warm, now lukewarm—sat untouched, its steam condensing into a slow, unblinking haze. Outside, the city pulsed with a rhythm too fast for quiet reflection, but here, in this corner of the room, time had paused. The silence wasn’t empty; it was the space between breaths, between the hesitation of a decision that had been made too quickly, or never at all. A single word, half-formed in the back of the throat, lingered like a ghost: *maybe*. The door creaked open, and in came the familiar scent of rain and pavement, but it barely registered. The moment had become a hollow echo, stretched thin by the ache of waiting. The phone buzzed. It was a text—just a single line, a question, a prompt to move on. The screen glowed, sharp and immediate, snapping the illusion of time. The moment had been erased, but the weight remained.
