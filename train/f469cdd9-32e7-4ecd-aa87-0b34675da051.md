A weathered wooden dock extends into a bay, its planks groaning under the weight of time. Beneath the rusted railings, the tide rolls in and out in a relentless rhythm, washing away footprints like sand in an ocean tide. The house beside the water is half-sunk into the earth, its windows cracked and curtains fluttering in the salty breeze. The sign above the door reads: *"Welcome"* in faded paint, though no one ever stays long enough to notice.

Children’s laughter drifts from the beach below—short, sharp, and impossible to trace back to a single family. The shells they leave behind are half-buried in the sand, each one a story that erases itself the next high tide. The pier, once a lifeline, now sways gently in the current, its ropes frayed, its purpose unclear. Yet here, in the space between waves, there is a kind of permanence: the quiet insistence of things that aren’t meant to last.
