The deepest part of the ocean is the Challenger Deep, located in the Mariana Trench. According to measurements taken in 2010 by the United States Center for Coastal & Ocean Mapping, the depth of the Challenger Deep is recorded at 10,994 meters (36,070 feet) below sea level, with an estimated vertical accuracy of ± 40 meters. This depth exceeds the height of Mount Everest, the highest mountain on Earth, by over one mile of water. The first depth measurements in the Mariana Trench were conducted by the British survey ship HMS Challenger in 1875, recording a depth of 8,184 meters (26,850 feet). Subsequent measurements in 1951 by another Royal Navy vessel named HMS Challenger discovered an even deeper location with a depth of 10,900 meters (35,760 feet).
