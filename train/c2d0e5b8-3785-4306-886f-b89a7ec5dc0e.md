A valley of long, tapered shadows stretching eastward like fingers in a damp, misted dawn. The western slopes of the canyon cast them as dark, jagged lines against the peach-scarred sky, where the sun rises just above the ridge. Shadows of twisted oak roots coil like serpents in the dry, cracked earth. At the valley’s heart, a temple’s stone walls cast a single, elongated shadow—longer than its own height—fanning out like a fan of death across the courtyard. The shadows of gnarled willows bend northward, their lower branches lost in the underbrush, while the tallest trees’ trunks cast vertical shadows, sharp and geometric, cutting through the haze like the blade of a knife. The shadows whisper—of forgotten rivers, of stones that shift when no one watches, of a place where time moves slower than the slowest breath.
