A fluorescent-lit office desk, cluttered with half-empty coffee cups and crumpled receipts, glows faintly under the fluorescent lights. A single, old-fashioned phone sits on a shelf above the desk, its cord stretched taut over a cracked floorboard. The screen flickers with static, displaying a distorted image of a city skyline at night—one that isn’t quite real, as if viewed through a warped lens.

Beside the phone, a small, rounded object rests: a smooth, obsidian-like stone, half-buried in a pile of old keys. One key, slightly larger than the others, has a strange, spiraling engraving that doesn’t match any lock. A faint, rhythmic *click-clack* comes from beneath the desk, like a machine counting in Morse code.

A window behind the desk is slightly ajar, revealing a view of the same city skyline—same buildings, same shadows—but from a different angle, as if the buildings themselves are slightly tilted. The street below glows with an eerie, pale blue light, and the occasional figure in a long coat moves too slowly, their movements suspended in time.
