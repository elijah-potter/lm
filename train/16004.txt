There’s a national park just South of Bangkok which was once known for a very spectacular and rare event, an enormous lotus bloom. Now, for the first time in a decade, the colourful explosion of flowers has returned.
The rare event, which in the past has brought a flood of tourists and local excitement, is unfolding in Khao Sam Roi Yot (a national park three hours south of Bangkok). Although the park remains open, the AFP reported park officials have closed the sections around the bloom.
Park rangers, who have been working to restore the park to its former health, have warned that huge numbers of visitors this year may disrupt the environment and put the flowers at risk. “The national park is in the process of restoring the lotuses. When it comes to the right time the park will open them up to the public,” head ranger Rungroj Aswakultarin said to AFP.
When it reopens, you can look forward to what looks from above like a green cosmos – a wide expanse of green speckled almost infinitely by tiny fuchsia dots. From below it’s like wading through an inverted forest, with green stalks but a brightly coloured canopy. Of course, it’s only accessible by canoe or boat as the flowers extend from water lilies.
The flowers are sacred in Thai culture and are used in Thai medicine, cooking and cultural celebrations.
The lack of blooms in previous years has an ambiguous cause. AFP reported that park officials have blamed drought, pollution from fertilisers and nearby seafood farms for disrupting the park’s ecology.
