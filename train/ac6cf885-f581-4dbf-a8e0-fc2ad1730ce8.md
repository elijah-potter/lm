A quiet suburban sidewalk at dusk, where the usual rush of evening commuters has thinned to a trickle. A lone figure—an elderly woman in a faded floral dress—walks past a rusted mailbox that hums faintly, as if powered by residual energy. Her cane taps a rhythm on the pavement that doesn’t sync with her steps. Behind her, a streetlamp flickers, casting long shadows that seem to shift slightly, as if the light itself is breathing. The air smells faintly of damp pavement and something metallic, like old blood or rusted metal. She pauses at a crosswalk, where the traffic light glows green—but the cars never move. The stop sign beside her has been replaced by a circular sticker bearing a single, unreadable symbol. The woman exhales slowly, her breath fogging in the cold.
