**Mayor Thompson Announces New Solar Initiative for Elmville’s Energy Future**

Elmville residents will soon benefit from the city’s newly adopted **Solar Share Program**, aimed at expanding renewable energy access across residential neighborhoods. The initiative, launched this morning, partners with local solar cooperatives to install community solar gardens on vacant lots and municipal buildings, with up to **50% discounts** for eligible households. Applications open tomorrow at Elmville’s Economic Development Hub, where participants can also receive incentives for energy efficiency upgrades.

While details on exact installation timelines remain pending, Mayor Thompson emphasized that the program reflects Elmville’s commitment to reducing carbon emissions by **2030**, aligning with state climate goals. "We’re not just powering up our future—we’re powering up our community," Thompson said during a press conference.

Opponents, including a group calling themselves **Elmville Stewards**, have raised concerns about land use conflicts and the potential for increased property values in solar-rich areas. The city has assured residents that pilot sites will be selected based on **existing shade structures** and minimal visual disruption. Further public hearings are scheduled for next month.

Meanwhile, construction crews are already preparing two demonstration projects: a **200-panel solar array** on the Elmville High School parking lot and a **community garden** on the former city lot near Maple Street. Residents are encouraged to submit feedback by emailing **solar@elmville.org**.
