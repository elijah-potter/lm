The empty chair at the dinner table hums with its own weight, a silence so dense it’s tangible—like the air after a storm when nothing moves but the faintest, lingering current of what could have been. The walls, usually alive with the murmur of voices, now hold the weight of unsaid things, the kind that press against your ribs like a ghost’s breath. The mirror still reflects you, but your reflection is a stranger, one who doesn’t remember the names of the faces that used to fill the room, who doesn’t know how to sit still when the absence is louder than any presence ever was.

Time is a thief here, stealing fragments of days, leaving only the echoes of routines that once anchored you. The clock ticks, but the hands move too fast, too slow—it’s as if the seconds are stretched thin, trying to hold back the inevitable. The absence isn’t just the person who left; it’s the space they carved out, the rhythm they set, the way the world stopped feeling like it should be moving. Now, the world moves on its own terms, indifferent, and the ache isn’t in the missing person but in the fact that you are still here, still *alive*, and that’s the cruelest part.

You learn to recognize the signs—the way the coffee tastes different, the way the door creaks differently when it’s not someone else’s weight pulling it shut. The absence is in the details, the quiet things that no one else notices because they’re too busy being present. It’s the way the bed feels different when you lie down, the way the sheets remember the shape of someone else’s body. It’s the absence of laughter, of the way they used to catch your eye across a crowded room and say something that made you forget where you were. Now, you’re just here, and the absence is the only thing that feels real.
