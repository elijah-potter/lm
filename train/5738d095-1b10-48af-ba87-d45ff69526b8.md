Returned to the old town center after months away. The scent of roasted coffee beans from the morning café lingers on the air, mingling with the faint sound of distant church bells. The cobblestone streets, once worn smooth by countless footsteps, now feel slightly rougher, as if time has carved deeper grooves. A single streetlamp casts a warm glow over the alleyway where the old bookstore’s window displays now show more faded spines than new releases. The park bench where I used to sit and read is still there, but the grass is taller, the birdsong quieter—every sound feels like a memory half-remembered. The market stalls have been rearranged, and the vendor who sold me those candied walnuts last summer is still there, though she smiles differently now, as if she’s just figured out how much I’ve changed too. The riverbank is still there, its banks lined with the same overgrown willows, though the water now runs slightly faster, carrying more debris than I remember. The library’s grand reading room, with its towering shelves and the scent of old paper, is exactly as I left it—except the stacks have a new layer of dust, and the librarian’s voice, when she greets me, carries the weight of years. I step onto the bridge, and the view is the same: the city sprawls below, a patchwork of buildings and lights, but the reflection in the water is slightly darker, as if the world itself has absorbed some of my absence.
