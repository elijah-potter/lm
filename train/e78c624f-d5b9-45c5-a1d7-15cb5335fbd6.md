## Explain Like I'm Five: Why the Sky is Blue

Okay, imagine you have a big box of crayons. Lots and lots of colors! Red, yellow, blue, green, purple – all sorts of beautiful colors. Now, imagine those colors are like sunshine. Sunshine isn't just yellow, it’s actually *all* those colors mixed together! It's like when you mix all your crayons together – you get a muddy brown, right?

Well, when sunshine comes down from the sky, it bumps into tiny bits of air all around us. These bits of air are so small you can’t even see them! Now, blue light is a bit like a bouncy ball. When it bumps into those tiny bits of air, it bounces *everywhere*! It bounces up, down, sideways – it just bounces all over the place.

Red light, and other colors like orange and yellow, are more like bowling balls. They mostly go straight. They don't bounce around as much. 

Because the blue light is bouncing all over, it spreads out across the whole sky. That’s why when you look up, you see blue! It’s not that the sky *is* blue, it’s that the blue light from the sunshine is bouncing around so much that it’s what we see. 

Sometimes, when the sun is setting, you see orange and red skies. That’s because the sun is lower in the sky, and the light has to travel through *more* air. All the blue light bounces away before it reaches us, and we see the red and orange light instead! 

So, the sky is blue because blue light is a bouncy ball, and it loves to bounce! Isn't that neat?
