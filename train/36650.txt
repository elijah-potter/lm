1 reason why Michigan's population is growing
More Michiganders are leaving the state for other parts of the country than the number of those moving to Michigan, new data from the U.S. Internal Revenue Service shows.
That's been the case for years. But the gap is shrinking.
About 127,925 people left Michigan for other states and 116,523 people moved to Michigan from other states — a loss of 11,402 people, according to 2015-2016 tax records. The last time Michigan saw a net loss of fewer people was in 1999-2000 with 7,780 residents leaving for other states, according to an analysis by Kurt Metzger, demographer and Mayor of Pleasant Ridge.
The IRS compares year-to-year address changes on matched tax returns to create the state migration files.
The greatest difference between the number of people moving to the state and residents leaving happened between 2007 and 2008 when the state lost 78,991 people.
The data also show an increase in the number of states new Michigan residents are moving from. Between 2014-2015 Michigan gained residents from nine states. Last year people moved to Michigan from 25 different states. Illinois and New York were the top two contributing states.
Metzger describes the latest IRS data as good news for the state. "Michigan's population fortunes are primarily driven by domestic migration trends. The small net loss in 2015-16 signals an expectation for another year of population growth when Census population estimates are released," he said. New population estimates are scheduled for release by the Census Bureau on Wednesday.
Last year, Michigan posted population gains for a fifth straight year at 0.1% or 10,585 residents, slower than the national growth rate of 0.7%.
