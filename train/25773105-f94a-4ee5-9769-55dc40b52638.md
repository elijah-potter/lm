A hollowed-out vow whispered in the dark,
half-truths wrapped in glittering lies.
The weight of it—*keep this*—
a chain of ink on parchment
stained by the scent of rain
and the slow unraveling of resolve.

They called it love when it was a cage.
*"You’ll be my sun,"* they promised,
but the sun bleeds first—
a promise of light
that’s already drowning in shadow.
Now the glass is cracked.
The fire in the hearth
burns the same,
but the air tastes like smoke
and the words, once smooth,
are now rough, frayed threads
stuck between the teeth
of something that refuses to break.

Some promises are just paper.
Some are bones.
Some are a single, trembling breath
held too long—
and then you learn
that the only way to keep it
is to let it go.
