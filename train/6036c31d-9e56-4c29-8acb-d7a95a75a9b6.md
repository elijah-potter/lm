A dimly lit office, lined with half-empty coffee cups and scattered notes, hums with the soft hum of a computer fan. The author, hunched over a laptop, presses the backspace key repeatedly, erasing lines of dialogue until a fresh draft appears: *"The door creaked open, revealing a stranger with hollow eyes and a coat that smelled of rain."* The previous version read: *"The door opened, a figure in a coat that smelled of rain."* The revision sharpens the scene—focus shifts from atmosphere to character tension. The pen skitters across a notebook, jotting new details: *"A shadow flickered behind the stranger’s gaze—was it fear or something else?"* The computer’s screen freezes on a single line, the cursor blinking, waiting for the next rewrite. Outside, rain taps against the window.
