Last evening’s descent through the crumbling stone archways of the abandoned cathedral, its stained-glass windows long shattered into jagged, iridescent shards that catch the dying light like scattered jewels. The scent of damp earth and old wood lingers, thick with the ghost of incense that never burned again. The grand nave’s vaulted ceilings groan under centuries of weight, their once-proud columns bent like old men’s knees. The pews, stained dark by centuries of kneeling, bear the marks of time—peeling paint, the ghostly outlines of hands that once pressed fingers to hymnbooks now cracked and crumbling.

The altar, once a beacon of fire and sacred light, is now a shrine of silence. The candles—if they were ever candles—have long been extinguished, but their wax pools still run slow, like tears of wax melting into the stone. The high altar’s gilded cross, once a symbol of hope, now rusts like a relic left to the elements.

The air hums with the sound of nothing—no voices, no prayers, no footsteps. The only movement is the slow, creaking of the floorboards, the distant drip of water from a leak in the ceiling, or the whisper of the wind through the broken arches, carrying no message, just the sound of nothing left to say.

I stand at the edge of the last light, watching the last shadows stretch across the floor. This is not the end of the cathedral. It is the end of something else—something that once filled its halls, that once believed in its stories, its miracles, its faith. Now, it is just stone and silence, waiting. Waiting for what? The wind answers: nothing.
