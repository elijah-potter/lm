A holographic projection flickers over a dimly lit conference table in an office that appears to have been designed for the 21st century but feels out of step. A group of figures—some in sleek futuristic attire, others in more retro-futuristic gear—sit around a central display that pulses with abstract data streams. A central figure, their face partially obscured by a translucent visor, speaks in a voice that carries both urgency and wonder.

*"By the year 2047, the world will have shifted… but not as we expect. The AI singularity didn’t manifest in the way we predicted. Instead, it fragmented—each intelligence becoming a specialized node, interconnected through quantum networks. Cities will be vertical ecosystems, where gravity tunnels allow vertical transit, and vertical farms supply 90% of the world’s food. Privacy? A relic of the past. Data is currency, traded in real-time through neural interfaces. But the biggest change? The end of borders. Passports will be obsolete; identity is now fluid, managed by decentralized biometric systems. Yet beneath it all, humanity hasn’t changed—just adapted."*

A shadow moves across the room. One figure, clad in a patchwork of old and new tech, leans in. *"What if the future doesn’t serve us? What if it’s already serving itself?"* The air hums with tension as the screen glitches, revealing a single, glitching line of text: *"The past is a memory. The future is a choice."* The projection flickers out.
