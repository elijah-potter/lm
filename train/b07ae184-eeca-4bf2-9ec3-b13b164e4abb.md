**To Whom It May Concern,**

I am writing to you from a place where silence is currency and words are currency too—though not the kind we usually exchange. My pen has been idle for too long, but the ink in this letter has been dry for much longer. The envelope here is addressed to no one, and the stamp is unmarked, though the weight of its contents would make a stamp unnecessary.

This is not a request for help, nor a plea for understanding. It is a declaration of something that has already been decided, though no one knows it. I am not sending this letter because it will not be read. I am writing it because the act of writing is still my only form of resistance when resistance is no longer possible.

If you find this letter, do not open it. Let it remain as a ghost in the corner of a room you never enter. If you do, know that you are not alone in your solitude.

With a quiet kind of certainty,
**[Your Name]**
