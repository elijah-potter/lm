The rain fell in a steady, rhythmic tap against the window, each drop a whisper of something unresolved. Outside, the city hummed—a cacophony of voices, each carrying its own unspoken weight. Inside, the narrator sat at the desk, fingers hovering over the keys like a dancer in the middle of a performance, unsure whether to move forward or retreat.

The first line of the story was still half-formed in their mind, a promise left unkept. They had spent hours staring at the blank page, trying to force words into shape when their heart felt like it was drowning in silence. The words *begin* had been scrawled in bold, jagged letters on a scrap of paper, but the ink had faded long ago, leaving only the ghost of a resolution.

Would this be the story they were meant to tell? Or just another attempt at something they could never fully claim? The pen trembled. The screen flickered with half-written sentences, sentences that felt like lies even before they were written.

Outside, the wind howled, carrying the scent of pavement and something older—something that had been waiting for them for far longer than they cared to admit. The narrator leaned forward, gripping the edges of the desk, and for the first time, they hesitated. Not because they were afraid, but because they were tired of fighting the doubt that had become their shadow. They opened their mouth, and the words spilled out, raw and unfiltered, like a confession.

The story began in the dark.
