The river whispered secrets beneath its silver skin,
howling against the rocks where time carved its names.
The moon, a coin tossed by the tides, left half-buried,
while the willow bent its branches in slow surrender,
watching the sky bleed gold and crimson at dawn’s break.

I pressed my palm to the cracked pavement—
the city’s breath, sharp and stale,
but softer than the earth’s embrace beneath my feet.
A child’s laughter, lost in the crowd,
danced like a thread through the static hum of the street.

Now, the wind carries only the scent of rain—
cool, steady, a promise wrapped in mist.
The world exhales.
