A young woman stood at the edge of a crowded city square during sunset, her hands clenched around a crumpled ticket stub for the bus home. She had spent the last two days working extra shifts at the diner, saving every penny for the train ticket to her hometown—only to realize the train had left without her. The bus station was packed, and the lines stretched endlessly. She hesitated, her breath shallow. A group of teens nearby laughed, their laughter sharp and dismissive.

Then she saw the man in the blue jacket. He stood near the ticket booth, his face half-lit by the flickering fluorescent lights. His eyes flicked toward her, then away, but not before she caught the flicker of recognition—something like pity or something sharper, like curiosity. She exhaled. The bus would take hours, and she knew she’d miss her train. But what if she took the bus instead? She could catch a later train tomorrow.

The choice wasn’t about the destination—it was about the moment. Would she let fear or inertia decide for her, or would she choose to act? She stepped forward, her feet carrying her toward the ticket booth, past the indifference of the crowd. The man’s gaze followed her, unblinking, as she handed over the crumpled stub and asked, *"Do you have any seats left?"*

The moment she stepped out into the night, she became someone else. Not just a woman with a choice, but a woman who had already made it.
