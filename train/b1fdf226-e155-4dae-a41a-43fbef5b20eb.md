The sensation of being overheard manifests as an unsettling awareness of being observed, often accompanied by:

1. **Psychological discomfort** – A vague, creeping sense of vulnerability or exposure, sometimes triggering paranoia or anxiety.
2. **Physical tension** – Unease in the chest, rapid heartbeat, or shallow breathing as the mind fixates on potential eavesdropping.
3. **Cognitive distraction** – The mind drifts, searching for signs of listening (e.g., shadows, whispers, or unintended sounds), even when none exist.
4. **Emotional unease** – A mix of embarrassment, fear, or a sense of being judged, especially if the overheard moment involves intimacy or personal vulnerability.
5. **Avoidance instinct** – Subconscious behaviors like lowering voice, glancing around, or seeking solitude to mitigate perceived exposure.

The experience is subjective, blending physical reactions with mental alertness, often leaving the observer feeling both *exposed* and *hyper-vigilant*.
