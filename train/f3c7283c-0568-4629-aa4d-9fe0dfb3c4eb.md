**The Library at Dusk**

The air smelled of old paper and damp wood as I found my place on the third shelf, where the spines curled like sleeping snakes. The light from the single bulb above flickered over the titles—*Moby-Dick*, *Ulysses*, a dusty copy of *Wuthering Heights*—each one a promise of worlds untold. I had come here to lose myself, to let the words swallow me whole, to forget the noise of the city outside, the way my fingers ached from typing for hours, the weight of unfinished drafts in my back pocket.

The first half of the piece was about the quiet, the way the city hummed beneath the library’s hush, the way my mind wandered like a stray cat through the stacks. I wrote of the way the ink on the page felt like a lover’s touch—warm, hesitant, then pulling away when I didn’t want to leave.

Then, the shift.

The second half became something else entirely. The library doors creaked open—not with the usual sigh of the wind, but with the sound of something *breathing*. The light in the bulb dimmed, not because of a bulb, but because the glass had been shattered. The shelves groaned as something heavy dragged itself across the floor, its shadow stretching long across the floor like a hand reaching for the ceiling.

I didn’t write about fear. I wrote about *power*. The way the words on the page, once soft and yielding, now felt like armor. The way the city’s noise, once a distraction, became a weapon, a chorus of voices demanding I *hear them*. The last line was a whisper, then a scream:

*"The words don’t need to be true to be true."*
