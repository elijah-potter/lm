In the dim glow of the old attic, I found the box half-buried beneath layers of dust and forgotten things. The letters within were brittle with age, their ink faded like the hands of a man who had written them long ago. *"They say I should have kept my promise,"* one read, *"but promises are like sand—what stays is what you choose to remember."*

I lifted the lid of the chest, its hinges groaning like a dying beast, and inside lay a single photograph: a child’s face, sharp and unguarded, pressed between two yellowed pages. The name at the bottom was scrawled in a hasty, trembling hand—*"Emily"*—but the date was crossed out. *"She was never mine to keep,"* the note inside whispered, though I knew better.

Outside, the wind howled through the broken window, carrying away any chance of privacy. I turned away from the photograph, let the cold air seep into my bones, and closed the lid with a sound that echoed like a secret left unspoken. The box stayed where it was, forgotten in the dark, its secrets buried with the lies we tell ourselves to survive.
