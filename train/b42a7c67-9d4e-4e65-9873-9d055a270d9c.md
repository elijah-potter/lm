The old library’s scent of aged paper clung to his fingers as Elias traced the worn spine of *The Alchemist’s Codex*. His breath hitched—not from the weight of the book, but the weight of the past pressing against the future.

Memory: The night he’d pressed his palm to the brass plaque outside the temple of the Obsidian Clock, whispering the same words his father had: *"Find the key beneath the river’s reflection."* The river had been black, the key—a smooth stone with a single black vein like a wound in the world. His father had laughed, saying it was a trick of light. But the stone was real. And now it was gone.

Plan: He would return to the river at dawn. The current had shifted since last year, the banks softer, the water deeper. If the key still waited, he’d find it. If not, he’d find the man who’d stolen it and ask why. The man had called himself *The Keeper*, though Elias didn’t know what that meant. The Keeper had taken everything else from him—the letters, the coins, even the name he’d been given. But the stone was the only thing left of his father. And that was worth more than all the rest.

He pocketed the book and stepped into the cold, the weight of what he’d lost pressing harder than the weight of the past. The river was calling.
