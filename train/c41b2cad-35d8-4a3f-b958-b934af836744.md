Exploring genetic markers associated with Alzheimer’s disease, researchers found a novel polymorphism in the *APOE* gene (APOE-ε3/ε4) linked to increased amyloid plaque accumulation in early-stage dementia. However, this correlation doesn’t fully explain the disease’s progression—why do some carriers with this marker develop symptoms decades later, while others remain asymptomatic? The variability suggests additional epigenetic or environmental factors may modulate risk. Additionally, the role of mitochondrial dysfunction in neurodegeneration remains unclear; how do these genetic findings intersect with cellular energy deficits? Further studies are needed to clarify whether the *APOE* variant is a primary driver or merely an early indicator of underlying systemic stress pathways.
