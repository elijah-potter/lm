In a dimly lit café, under the flickering glow of an old lamp, a young woman sat across from her estranged father. The air between them was thick with unspoken words. Her hands trembled as she cleared her throat, her voice barely above a whisper.

"Father, I’ve been hiding something from you."

His eyes narrowed, but he let her continue.

"For years, I’ve been lying to you about my relationship with Sarah. I couldn’t bear the thought of you disapproving, of the way you always seemed to see the worst in people. But I’ve been wrong."

A beat of silence. Then, quietly, he replied, "I know."

She exhaled shakely. "I’ve also been lying about the money. The bills, the expenses—every little thing that piled up. I’ve been working double shifts, saving every cent I could, just to keep the lights on. But I couldn’t tell you because I didn’t want to burden you."

His face softened. "I was wrong too, daughter. I judged without understanding."

She wiped a tear from her cheek. "It’s not about punishment. It’s about honesty. About you and me being real with each other again."

His voice was rough. "Then I guess we’re starting over."
