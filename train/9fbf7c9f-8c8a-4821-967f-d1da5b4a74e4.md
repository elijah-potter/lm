After the seismic shift, the valley lay exposed in fractured, sunbleached ruins of its former self. The once-lush canopies of the ancient forest had withered into skeletal stumps, their bark peeling like old skin, revealing the earth beneath in patchwork hues of ochre and mossy green. Rivers, once swift and deep, now meandered sluggishly through newly exposed bedrock, their banks lined with jagged, crystalline formations that glinted in the afternoon light. The sky, once a blanket of swirling storms, was now a vast, unbroken expanse of pale blue, unmarred by clouds—a serene, unnatural calm. The air hummed with an unfamiliar, static charge, thick with the scent of ozone and damp mineralized earth. The land itself seemed to hold its breath, waiting for the next tremor or the return of something forgotten.
