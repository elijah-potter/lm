The dim light of an empty office flickered against the walls as Elias sat at his desk, fingers tracing the rim of his cup. Outside, the city’s hum—subway trains, distant horns, the occasional scream—faded into the background like static. His phone buzzed; a text from his colleague: *"You up for lunch tomorrow? The usual spot?"*

He stared at the screen. No answer. The silence stretched, thick as the air, and for the first time in years, he didn’t feel the urge to fill it. The words *"I’m busy"* weren’t enough. He wanted to erase them, to leave no trace.

Instead, he closed the app. The phone lay silent beside him, its glow extinguished.

Outside, a train rumbled past, its engine a growl of iron on steel. Elias exhaled slowly, watching the sound disappear into the night. Somewhere, a clock chimed. The world kept turning. He didn’t have to say anything.
