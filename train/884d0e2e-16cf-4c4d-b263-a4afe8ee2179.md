A quiet promise kept between the pages of a worn journal, the ink faded by time yet legible in the margins where two hands once clasped. The recipient never knew—only the sender, who carved it in bone or whispered it into the wind, certain it would find its way. The act itself was less the words than the silence that followed, the absence of noise when the deed was done. No grand ceremony, no dramatic gesture; just the quiet hum of a life carried out as promised, unseen, unspoken. The world moved on, but the weight of the vow lingered like dust in the air, a whisper that only the few who knew its source would ever hear.
