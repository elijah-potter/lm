The artist stood at the threshold of their own canvas, fingers trembling over the brushes, the ink swirling like restless spirits. The world outside was a blur of muted colors—dust motes dancing in the afternoon light, the hum of traffic swallowed by the distance. They had spent years chasing the illusion of control, of mastery, of creating something permanent from fleeting moments. But the canvas was not theirs alone; it was a mirror, a voice that whispered secrets no one else could hear.

*"You are not the artist,"* the voice inside their head murmured, *"you are the story being told."*

The last stroke faded into the background, the edges blurred with uncertainty. The piece was raw, imperfect—like the first draft of a confession. The admission was already there, waiting in the cracks between the strokes: *"I did not build this. I only held the brush."*

The frame was still empty. The world, indifferent, would fill it with its own truth.
