**Tropical monsoon climate (30°C–40°C year-round, high humidity, frequent heavy rains, especially between May–October):**
Rising above the mist-shrouded jungles, the *Chitwan National Park* in Nepal glows under a perpetually low, golden sky. The air is thick with the scent of damp earth and wild orchids, while the distant roar of the Narayani River blends with the cry of the rare Bengal tiger. At dawn, the mist clings to the bamboo thickets, and the temperature is still warm, but the sweat on your brow cools into dew by midday. Dusk arrives as the sky erupts in fireworks of crimson and violet—**electroluminescent**—as the monsoon winds carry the scent of fermenting rice and fermenting *chaas* from the village stalls. The heat is oppressive yet intoxicating, a siren song of survival and splendor.

**Desert steppe (15°C–25°C, dry but with occasional sudden warmth spikes, cool nights, wind scouring sand):**
The *Atacama Desert* stretches endlessly, a cracked obsidian plain where the air is so still it seems to hold its breath. The sky is a vast, unblinking eye—**pale blue, then violet at dusk**, as the *huayra* winds howl through the *salares*, dragging dust into swirling storms. The wind is a knife; it slashes through your clothes, stealing the warmth from your bones. At night, the temperatures drop to **freezing**, and the stars—**galactic, unfiltered**—burn like embers in a thousand silent bonfires. In the distance, the *Salar de Uyuni* mirrors the sky in shimmering, fractured glass, reflecting the same bleak, endless beauty.

---
**Arctic tundra (–20°C to 5°C, frozen ground, perpetual twilight in summer, polar night in winter):**
The *Svalbard archipelago* is a frozen museum of time. Summer arrives with a whisper—**the sun never sets**, casting the land in an eternal, sickly green glow as the *svalbard moss* and *blueberries* burst into life. The wind is a living thing, biting through your fur-lined coat, carrying the scent of salt and thawing permafrost. In winter, the world is a **silent, white cathedral**: the aurora borealis dances in ribbons of emerald and crimson, painting the sky as if someone had dipped a brush in blood. The air is crisp, the silence so thick it hums. The *ice caves* of Longyearbyen glow with bioluminescent algae, their walls a living sculpture of time and cold.
