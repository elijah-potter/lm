The skeletal remains of a crumbling plaza, its stone tiles cracked by decades of neglect, form a jagged maze where shadows stretch unnaturally long at dusk. The air hums with the faintest echoes of laughter—once, a market bustled here, but now only dust swirls in the wind from a broken fountain’s hollowed-out basin. A rusted gate, its iron bars bent at sharp angles, stands half-open, its padlock stripped away, as if someone tried to escape but couldn’t finish the job. The overgrown gardens at the edges whisper of forgotten blooms, their petals crushed underfoot by unseen visitors. A lone streetlight flickers intermittently, casting flickering pools of light across the uneven pavement. At the center of the plaza, a single, discarded sculpture—a broken statue of a child’s face—rests upside down, its features melted by time and the weight of abandonment. The scent of damp earth and old wood lingers, thick and oppressive, like a memory half-remembered. No one dares to linger, but the place *remembers* its own stories.
