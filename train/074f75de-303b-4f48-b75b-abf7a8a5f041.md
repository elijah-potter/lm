The fire crackled in the hearth, casting long shadows across the rough-hewn wooden walls of the tavern. Old Man Harkin sat hunched over his cup, his fingers tracing the rim as he stared into the amber liquid. A hand clapped him on the shoulder.

"—Knees, you old fool, tell us a tale. What’s that you’ve got in your head?"

Harkin exhaled sharply, rubbing his jaw. "Aye, I’ll tell ye one. But first—" He leaned forward, voice dropping to a murmur. "This one’s not for the ears of those who’ll judge me for it."

A hush fell over the crowd. Harkin took a deep breath.

---
In the year of Our Lord 1847, the Blackthorn River ran red. Not from flood or fire, but from the blood of men who had come to claim the land. The settlers had come with promises, with god and gold, but the earth had other plans. The earth remembered.

I was one of the first to hear the call.

I was a scout, not a soldier, but the general would have none of that. "You’ll die out here," he said, slapping my shoulder. "But you’ll live to tell this tale." So I came.

The forest was alive with whispers. The trees bent toward the river, their roots coiled like serpents. The air smelled of damp earth and copper. And then—voices. Not men, but something else. Something that had once been men.

I found them in the ruins of an old chapel, half-buried in the mud. The walls were carved with symbols I didn’t recognize, but the faces were familiar. My uncle. My cousin. My brother.

They were still here. Waiting.

The general’s men came. They didn’t see the shadows at first. But when they did, they ran. And the river took them.

I told the general what I saw. He laughed. "You’re drunk, boy. The land’s cursed, but not like this." But he sent men to dig. And when they found the bones, they found the truth.

The river doesn’t run red because of men. It runs red because the men who came here are already dead. And the earth remembers.

Harkin’s voice trailed off. The tavern fell silent.

For a moment, no one moved. Then the man beside him whispered, "So it’s true."

Harkin looked at him, then back to the fire. "Aye. And it’s not over."

---
The fire burned lower. The crowd murmured, but no one dared ask more.
