The dim glow of flickering lanterns cast long shadows across the walls of the abandoned church. Elias sat hunched over his half-eaten meal, fingers tracing the worn leather of his journal. His stomach growled, but his mind was elsewhere, replaying the words of his brother, Daniel, over and over.

*"You’re not cut out for this, Elias. You’re too soft for war. Too soft for survival."*

Elias exhaled sharply, the breath fogging in the cold air. He had spent years training, learning to endure the brutality of battle, but every time he closed his eyes, Daniel’s voice echoed—*You’re too soft*.

The door creaked open. His sister, Mara, stepped inside, her eyes sharp with suspicion. "You’ve been avoiding me again," she said, setting down her own bowl.

Elias didn’t look up. "I’m fine."

Mara’s voice softened. "You’re not fine. You’re drowning in it."

A silence stretched between them, thick with unspoken words. Elias knew what she was saying—he *was* drowning. The weight of every lost friend, every battle won by sheer will, every moment he’d spent pretending to be strong while his heart cracked. Daniel’s words had become his own, a self-fulfilling prophecy.

Mara reached out, brushing his sleeve. "You can’t keep hiding. Not from yourself."

Elias swallowed hard. "I’m trying."

The lanterns burned low, their light barely reaching the corner of the room where Elias sat. Outside, the wind howled through the ruins, carrying the ghosts of the past. Somewhere in the dark, his brother waited, and Elias wondered if he could ever outrun himself.
