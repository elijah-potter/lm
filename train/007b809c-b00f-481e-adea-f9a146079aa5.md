The discovery of **Neutrino Oscillations** revealed that these ultra-light, neutral particles could transition between three distinct "flavors" (electron, muon, tau) as they traveled through space, defying early predictions that they remained stable. This phenomenon suggested an unexpected property of neutrinos—**mass**—contradicting the Standard Model’s assumption of massless neutrinos.

The new questions raised include:
1. **Why exactly do neutrinos oscillate?** The mechanism governing flavor transitions remains an open problem, with theories like the *Seesaw Mechanism* proposing heavy right-handed neutrinos as a potential explanation.
2. **What is the mass hierarchy?** Oscillation data favors either a *normal* (two light masses, one heavy) or *inverted* (one light, two heavy) pattern, but precise measurements are still unclear.
3. **Do neutrinos have CP violation?** If true, it could mirror (but differ from) the matter-antimatter asymmetry in the universe, though experimental evidence is still emerging.
4. **What are the implications for dark matter?** If neutrinos interact with other particles (e.g., via a new force), it could impact searches for dark matter candidates.
5. **Do other particles exhibit similar oscillations?** If neutrinos are not unique, could other exotic particles (e.g., axions, dark photons) also exhibit hidden flavor transitions?

Further experiments (e.g., **DUNE, Hyper-Kamiokande**) aim to probe these questions by refining oscillation parameters and searching for anomalies like CP violation or sterile neutrinos.
