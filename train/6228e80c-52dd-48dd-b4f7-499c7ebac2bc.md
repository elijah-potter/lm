The quiet hum of the city pulsed through the cracked windows of the abandoned bookstore, each note a whisper of forgotten stories. Clara sat at the desk, her fingers tracing the edge of a yellowed page from a book she’d been meaning to read for months. The scent of old paper and damp leather clung to the air like a ghost’s promise.

She turned the page. The words blurred—*admit it, Clara, you’ve been running*—but the ink had faded too, leaving only the ghost of a truth she refused to acknowledge. Outside, the rain began to fall, steady and relentless, as if the universe itself had decided to drown her in its own sorrow.

She exhaled sharply, the breath catching in her throat. The admission was inevitable. She had spent years pretending she wasn’t drowning, that the weight of her own loneliness was something she could outrun. But the books, the quiet corners, even the rain—all of them were calling her to the truth: she was tired. Exhausted. And the only thing she could do was finally let it in.

She closed her eyes, the weight of it pressing down like the first snowfall of winter. The admission had arrived. And now, she had to carry it.
