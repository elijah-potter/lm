Title: Recommended Reading: “A Study in Scarlet”

High school teachers like me are always discussing the next great book to kick off the school year with, and I couldn’t recommend Arthur Conan Doyle’s “A Study in Scarlet” enough this year. It fits the bill for a great introduction to one of literature’s most popular characters, Sherlock Holmes, and the mystery genre as a whole. 

This is the first book in the Sherlock Holmes series, in which Dr. Watson meets Holmes, and they embark on their first difficult (and deadly) case. You’ll be able to teach your students about the themes of enforcing justice, revenge, observation, and deduction. 

Of course, there’s enough suspense to keep everyone in class on the edge of their chairs. There’s no running out of topics to do activities on either: the late-Victorian England setting, for example, gives amazing insight into technological development and how modern-day practices differ.

All in all, “A Study in Scarlet” is a great book to introduce to your class, especially if you haven’t picked out a thrilling mystery in a long time. I encourage you to grab a copy, make a plan, and have fun!
