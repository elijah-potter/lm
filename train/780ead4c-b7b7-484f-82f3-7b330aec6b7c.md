The **Great Wall of China**, a series of fortifications built across northern China between the 7th century BCE and the 17th century CE, stretches over **13,000 miles** (21,000 km). It stretches from the Yalu River in the north to the Yellow River in the south, with key sections including the **Jiankou, Mutianyu, and Badaling** regions. Structurally, it consists of **earthen ramparts, stone walls, watchtowers, and fortresses**, designed to defend against invasions. The **Qin Dynasty (221–206 BCE)** originally built parts of it, later expanded by Ming Dynasty (1368–1644) emperors to deter Mongol threats. Modern sections, like the **Badaling section**, feature preserved historical architecture, while others remain in ruins. Its boundaries include **natural (rivers, mountains) and man-made (walls, gates) elements**, defining a cultural and historical frontier.
