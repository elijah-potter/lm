## Project Chimera: Rulebook

**A Cooperative Game of Scientific Crisis Management**

Welcome, Researchers. The world faces a rapidly evolving biological threat – Project Chimera. Genetically engineered organisms, initially designed for agricultural resilience, have escaped containment and are exhibiting unpredictable and increasingly dangerous mutations. Your team, a specialized research unit, must work together to understand the crisis, develop countermeasures, and prevent global catastrophe. Time is of the essence.

### 1. Components

*   **Game Board:** A world map depicting major research hubs and affected regions.
*   **Researcher Pawns (4):** Representing the specialized researchers within your team.
*   **Mutation Cards (60):** Detail the evolving characteristics and spread of the Chimera organisms.
*   **Research Cards (40):** Provide opportunities for scientific breakthroughs and mitigation efforts.
*   **Event Cards (30):** Introduce unexpected challenges and complications.
*   **Containment Tokens (30):** Used to limit the spread of the Chimera.
*   **Research Tokens (40):** Track progress on key research objectives.
*   **Crisis Track:** A track indicating the overall severity of the global situation.
*   **Dice (6):** Standard six-sided dice used for various tests and actions.
*   **Rulebook (this document).**

### 2. Setup

1.  Place the Game Board in the center of the playing area.
2.  Place the Crisis Track marker at the starting position (indicated on the board).
3.  Shuffle the Mutation, Research, and Event card decks separately and place them face down near the board.
4.  Each player selects a Researcher Pawn and places it on the designated Research Hub in Geneva.
5.  Deal each player three Research Cards. Players may look at their hands but keep them hidden from others.
6.  Draw the top three Mutation Cards and place them face up beside the Mutation deck. These represent the initial strains of the Chimera.
7.  Determine the Difficulty Level based on the number of players (see Section 7).

### 3. Turn Structure

Each turn consists of the following phases, performed in order:

1.  **Mutation Phase:** Draw one Mutation Card and add it to the face-up display. Resolve any immediate effects described on the card. If the number of face-up Mutation Cards exceeds five, discard the oldest card.
2.  **Researcher Action Phase:** Each player, in clockwise order, performs two actions. Available actions include:
    *   **Move:** Move your Researcher Pawn to an adjacent region on the Game Board.
    *   **Research:** Discard a Research Card and gain Research Tokens equal to the card's Research Value.
    *   **Contain:** Discard a Research Card and place a Containment Token in the current region.
    *   **Analyze Mutation:** Discard a Research Card and attempt to understand a face-up Mutation Card. Roll two dice; the result must exceed the Mutation Card's Analysis Difficulty to succeed. Success grants the team a permanent bonus related to that Mutation (as detailed on the card).
    *   **Share Research:** Give one Research Card to another player in the same region.
3.  **Event Phase:** Draw one Event Card and resolve its effects. These cards often introduce unexpected complications or opportunities.
4.  **Crisis Escalation Phase:** Advance the Crisis Track marker one space.

### 4. Win and Loss Conditions

**Win Condition:** The team wins the game if they complete all four Primary Research Objectives (detailed on separate cards drawn at the start of the game) before the Crisis Track reaches its maximum position.

**Loss Condition:** The team loses the game if the Crisis Track marker reaches its maximum position.

### 5. Mechanics: Forced Trade-offs & Uncertainty

Project Chimera incorporates several mechanics designed to create difficult choices and highlight the uncertainties inherent in crisis management.

1.  **Mutation Prioritization:** When a new Mutation Card is drawn, the team must collectively decide which existing face-up Mutation Card to discard if the display exceeds the limit. This represents the need to prioritize research efforts based on the most pressing threats. The decision must be unanimous.
2.  **Resource Allocation (Research Tokens):** Completing Primary Research Objectives requires spending Research Tokens. The amount required varies, and the team has a limited pool of tokens each turn. Players must discuss and agree on which objective to pursue, understanding that focusing on one might delay progress on another.
3.  **Containment Risk:** Placing Containment Tokens provides immediate relief in a region, but each Containment Token placed increases the "Containment Strain" score. When the Containment Strain score reaches a certain threshold (indicated on the board), a "Containment Breach" event is triggered, introducing a new, unpredictable Mutation Card with particularly devastating effects. This represents the risk of over-reliance on containment measures.

### 6. Advanced Variants

*   **Specialist Roles:** Assign each player a unique Researcher role with a special ability that modifies their actions. (e.g., "Virologist" gains a bonus to Mutation Analysis; "Logistics Expert" can move other players).
*   **Dynamic Objectives:** Introduce a deck of Secondary Research Objectives that become available throughout the game, offering alternative paths to victory but potentially diverting resources.
*   **Limited Communication:** Restrict verbal communication between players during the Researcher Action Phase, forcing them to rely on non-verbal cues and strategic card play.

### 7. Difficulty Scaling

The game's difficulty is adjusted based on the number of players.

*   **Two Players:** Start with the Crisis Track marker two spaces below the starting position.
*   **Three Players:** Play with the standard setup (Crisis Track starting position).
*   **Four Players:** Start with the Crisis Track marker one space above the starting position.



### Designer's Note

Project Chimera was designed to be more than just a game; it's a simulation. Our goal was to create an experience that encourages collaboration, strategic thinking, and a deeper understanding of the complexities of scientific crisis response. The forced trade-offs and uncertainty mechanics are intended to mirror the difficult choices faced by researchers in real-world scenarios, highlighting the importance of communication, prioritization, and adaptability. The game aims to subtly explore themes of scientific responsibility and the potential consequences of unchecked technological advancement.
