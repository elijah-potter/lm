A half-empty bottle of whiskey on the kitchen counter, the glass half-full but the cork still lodged in its neck. The bottle had been there for days, forgotten in the corner by the window, the label peeling with age and the faint scent of vinegar clinging to the glass. The cork was old, cracked, and had been left behind when the bottle was first opened, its purpose unknown but its presence undeniable. A quick glance—no one was around to see—and it was shoved into the drawer along with the other junk: old keys, a broken watch, a half-finished sketchbook. The regret was sharp, a blade pressed against the ribs of the heart, but it had no time to linger. The drawer closed with a quiet *click*, sealing the moment away before it could fester. The whiskey was already gone, sipped in secret, the guilt swallowed with the last drops. The bottle stood alone, a silent witness to a decision made in the heat of the moment, its surface reflecting the dim light of the kitchen, unreadable, unspoken.
