The cityscape of Neo-Eden is a digital reimagined of a once-thriving metropolis, its streets repurposed for holographic traffic lights and floating data highways. Skyscrapers, once built of stone, now pulse with neon-lit circuits, their glass facades shifting like liquid light. The population—mostly AI-enhanced humans with neural interfaces—moves in sync with the city’s ever-evolving algorithms, adapting to new infrastructure mid-conversation.

Furniture is modular, furniture that *grows* with demand, reforming into workstations or lounges on demand. Public spaces are temporary, rebuilt every few hours based on real-time analytics of foot traffic and social trends. The old "restaurant" is now a dynamic dining experience: menus, decor, and even the food itself are generated by AI chefs in response to cultural shifts.

Children are raised in "ecosystem pods" that nurture them based on predicted societal needs—some grow up to become architects of the next iteration of the city, while others are trained as data curators for the city’s ever-expanding memory banks. The people themselves are semi-permanent, their bodies augmented with nanotech that allows them to evolve beyond human limits but never quite *stay* the same, constantly learning, unlearning, and reinventing themselves.

The paradox? The city’s speed is a feedback loop—every change sparks another, and the humans, though faster in adaptation, are still lagging behind the rhythm of the machine. Some call it progress. Others call it a ghost town. The only constant? The gap between what’s built and what’s needed.
