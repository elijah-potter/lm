In a dimly lit warehouse, neon signs flicker from the ceiling, casting an eerie glow over a team of workers assembling drones. The lead engineer, adjusting a wiring harness, mutters under his breath, *"Just a few extra volts here—no harm done."* His team exchanges glances; the rulebook clearly states no modifications to power distribution units. But the drone prototype they’re testing is showing signs of instability, and the deadline looms.

The engineer taps a hidden switch on the console, triggering a controlled overclock. The drones hum slightly, their systems responding faster than specified. The lead techs nod in approval. *"Close enough,"* he says, though the safety margin is now dangerously tight. The warehouse door creaks open; an inspector’s footsteps echo down the hall. The engineer watches the drones’ displays one last time, then slips the console’s power button with a practiced motion.

The inspector enters, clipboard in hand. The team freezes. The drone’s display flickers—just enough to make the engineer’s eyes narrow. He doesn’t hesitate. *"Run the diagnostics now,"* he orders. The system auto-checks, reporting all systems nominal. The inspector nods, satisfied. *"All good here,"* he says, turning to leave. The engineer exhales, relief washing over him. The rule was bent, but not broken—not yet.
