A quiet afternoon in a dimly lit café, rain tapping against the window. The server placed a steaming cup of coffee in front of her, but she hesitated before taking a sip. Across the table, an old man sat, his hands shaking slightly as he adjusted his glasses. He spoke first, voice barely above a whisper: *"I can’t pay you what I owe you."*

Her eyes locked onto his, unblinking. She knew the debt wasn’t just money—it was the weight of years, the lies she’d told to protect him, to keep him safe. The truth was a blade she’d never used, but one moment, the fear of him finding out later made her choose the knife over the shield.

She reached across the table, her fingers brushing his. *"You don’t owe me anything."* The words were raw, but they were the first truth she’d told him in decades. His breath hitched. The café fell silent, the hum of the city swallowed by the weight of what was unsaid before. Outside, the rain stopped.
