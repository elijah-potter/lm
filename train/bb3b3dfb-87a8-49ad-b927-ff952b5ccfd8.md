The garden’s edges blur with time, though it still holds the shape of what was once a plot of stubborn soil and forgotten seeds. The rain comes in quiet, persistent drips, as if the sky itself has learned patience, while the earth, slow as a child learning to walk, absorbs the drops with the same unhurried deliberation.

There is a chair in the corner—old, with a crack that splits the wood like a split personality. It’s never been used, or perhaps it’s been used, but the weight of its absence lingers, heavier than any object. The walls are paper-thin, the kind that let the world seep in, even when the door is shut. The light here is the kind that doesn’t know how to call itself day; it’s just the absence of darkness, a vague, golden shadow stretching across the floor like a question no one is brave enough to ask aloud.

The books are dusted with the ghost of their own stories, their spines slightly bent where they were turned too many times. One is open to a page where the ink smudged to resemble a face, though no one has sat here in years. The coffee machine still hums, though the last cup was long ago, and the machine’s memory of what it was to brew is as faded as the taste of the last one.

Sometimes, when the wind picks up just right, the house feels like it’s holding its breath. Not because it’s afraid, but because it’s remembering. The memories here are the kind that don’t need to be loud to be felt—they’re the quiet hum of a world that stopped making noise a long time ago. The garden outside, though overgrown, still remembers what it was to be tended, though no one tends it anymore. The roots of the plants twist through the earth like fingers, reaching, but never quite finding what they’re looking for.
