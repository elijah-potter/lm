A transient flea market at the edge of a city’s abandoned railway yard. Boards labeled "SOLD" mark stalls with mismatched thrift finds, while a lone vendor in a patched-up denim jacket hawks "last-minute" eBay returns. Streetlights flicker intermittently over cracked asphalt, casting long shadows. A single, half-empty coffee cup sits on a discarded pallet, its lid stained with what could be coffee or blood. The wind carries the scent of diesel and damp earth. Nearby, a derelict bus—once white—now painted with faded graffiti—stands half-buried in gravel. The market closes at sunset, its temporary soul fading as the last customers scatter, leaving only the hum of cicadas and the distant siren of a passing train.
