Parents across the country have been shocked by the tragic death of schoolboy Theodore Silvester, who died while at school in Hull after choking during his lunch.
In 2015, the North East was saddened by the death of Hartlepool toddler Jacob Jenkins, who died after choking on a grape.
Here, safety experts offer a video guide on how to help children who might be choking.
Choking child - what you need to do
1. Cough it out
Encourage them to cough it out. If that doesn’t work, try to slap it out.
2. Slap it out
If coughing doesn’t work:
• Help the child bend forward and use the heel of your hand to give up to five sharp back blows between their shoulder blades.
• Check their mouth to see if there’s anything in there. If there is, get them to pick it out themselves.
3. Squeeze it out
If the back blows don’t work, try giving them up to five abdominal thrusts:
• To do this, stand behind the child, making sure they are bending well forward.
• Link your hands between their tummy button and the bottom of their chest, with your lower hand clenched in a fist.
• Then pull sharply inwards and upwards.
If they’re still choking
• Repeat steps 2 and 3 – back blows and abdominal thrusts – up to three times, or until you’ve dislodged what’s in there, or they can breathe again.
• If they’re still choking after you’ve repeated these steps three times, call 999 for an ambulance.
• Once you’ve called an ambulance, continue steps 2 ‘ Slap it out’ and 3 ‘Squeeze it out’ until what’s in there has cleared, help arrives or they become unconscious.
• If they lose consciousness at any stage, open their airway and check their breathing. If they’re not breathing, start CPR - cardiopulmonary resuscitation to try to release whatever’s stuck in there.
