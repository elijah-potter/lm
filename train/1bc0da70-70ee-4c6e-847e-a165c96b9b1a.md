The garden at dusk was quiet, save for the rustle of leaves and the distant hum of cicadas. Elias knelt beside the old oak, its roots twisted like skeletal fingers into the earth. He traced the grain of the bark, his fingers trembling—not with fear, but with the weight of what he had just done.

The vial in his hand glowed faintly under the fading light, its contents swirling like ink in water. He had stolen them from the apothecary’s locked chest, a risk he could not afford to take. The elixir was said to grant visions of the future, but he had only ever seen fragments—shadows of what might be, not the truth. Tonight, though, he had a different purpose. He had to know.

The vial shattered against the stone slab beneath him. The liquid spilled across the earth, seeping into the cracks like blood. A single drop caught the light, reflecting like a promise. Elias reached for it, his throat tight. What if it wasn’t enough? What if the vision was still incomplete, still tangled in the same ambiguities as always?

He closed his eyes. The world dissolved into static, then—

A voice, not his own, whispered in his mind: *The river will rise. The city will burn.*

Elias gasped. The vision was clear, but the ending was not. What did it mean? The river? The city? And why did it feel like a warning, not a prophecy?

He pressed his palm to the ground, feeling the damp earth beneath him. The answer was there, waiting. But he already knew the answer. The question was just another puzzle in a life that had become one endless, aching question.

The wind shifted. The last of the light vanished.
