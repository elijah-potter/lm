The old piano in the attic hummed a forgotten tune, its strings whispering secrets of a life that had long since faded. Elias sat cross-legged on the worn rug, fingers tracing the cracked surface of the keys, each one a memory half-remembered. The music was not his—it was the ghost of another’s hand, pressing notes into the wood before the world had forgotten why it mattered.

He had spent years chasing ghosts, not of music, but of himself. Names he had shed like skin, identities he had outgrown in the rush of ambition and the quiet despair of what he had left behind. There was the boy who had dreamed of being a poet, the man who had become a lawyer, and the ghost of the man who had let himself be erased by the weight of his own choices.

Now, as the last note faded into silence, Elias exhaled. The admission came not in words, but in the quiet realization that some truths were not meant to be spoken aloud. They were meant to be carried in the silence between the notes, in the spaces where the past lingered like the scent of old books in a forgotten attic. He had spent his life running, and in that moment, he realized running had been the only way he had ever known how to stay alive. But the piano did not care about the choices he had made. It only played what it knew, and that was all.
