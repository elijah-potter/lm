In English, the word for actor, “thespian”, comes from the name of the person credited with being the first actor, Thespis of Icaria. A famous playwright (none extant), stage director and producer of ancient Greece, Thespis' most important theatrical innovation was to step out from the chorus to play an individual role. Previously, productions had consisted of the chorus presenting in unison Greek myths by using voice and movement. Thespis retained the chorus’ role while switching masks to represent a single lead character. It would take an additional 50 years for playwright Aeschylus to introduce a second actor. We know with certainty that Thespis won first prize in the City of Dionysia in 534 BCE and was influential enough to be written about by Aristotle in his work, The Poetics.
