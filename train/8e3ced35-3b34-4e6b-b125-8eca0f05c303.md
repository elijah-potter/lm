The library door creaked open under the weight of a single candle, its flickering glow casting long shadows on the walls that had once been painted in deep emerald and crimson. The air smelled of aged parchment and something faintly metallic—like old blood. Inside, the shelves groaned under the weight of tomes, their spines cracked with age, their pages yellowed as if kissed by centuries of forgotten hands.

She found her father in the corner, his back to her, his fingers tracing the spine of a book that wasn’t his. The ink was smudged, as if he’d been reading in the dark. His voice was a whisper, rough with something unspoken. *"You should’ve listened. The warning was there, in the margins, but no one saw it."*

A gust of wind rattled the windows, and the candle snuffed out, plunging the room into silence. The last thing she heard before the door slammed shut behind her was his voice, now muffled: *"Why did you have to come?"*
