A tiny brass key, worn smooth by age and use, now holds the weight of forgotten promises.
Its teeth, once sharp, now chipped like a cracked promise—yet in its rusted grooves, the scent of rain lingers.
The lock it once opened has long since been replaced, but the key still turns in the hand that once knew its purpose.
Symbols don’t need to be grand to bear meaning; some are just the quiet scars of what was.
