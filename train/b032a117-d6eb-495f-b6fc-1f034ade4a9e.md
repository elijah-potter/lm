An awkward pause lingered as the caller hung up, the hum of static lingering on the line. The message had been delivered with a stiff, almost robotic tone—*"I apologize for the inconvenience, but we’ve made an error in processing your request."* The recipient’s fingers hovered over the keyboard, fingers twitching at the edge of the screen. The system’s automated response had been the only sound, a hollow echo of a half-apology, half-excuse.

A notification glowed at the corner of their screen: *"Request Denied: Insufficient Verification."* The words felt like a physical weight. They’d spent weeks preparing for this—extra documents, repeated calls, a desperate plea in an email. The system’s cold logic had dismissed it all. The apology, if it was meant to be, had been buried under layers of bureaucracy, where empathy was just another checkbox.
