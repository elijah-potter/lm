As the bird soared above, it observed the Mole's unease and his swift decision to abandon any mention of impending trouble, aware that it violated animal etiquette. The topic was dropped promptly.

Curiosity piqued the bird's interest as the Mole inquired about what lay beyond the Wild Wood. The Rat responded, describing a realm of indistinct blues and dimness, where hazy shapes resembling hills or mere illusions blended with what might be smoke from distant towns or simply wisps of cloud.

The Rat dismissed it, asserting that the Wide World beyond the Wild Wood held no significance for them. It was a place neither had ventured into nor desired to if they possessed any semblance of reason. The Rat implored the Mole to never bring it up again. With that, they arrived at their secluded backwater, their chosen spot for lunch.

Leaving the mainstream behind, they entered what initially appeared to be a small, enclosed lake. Verdant grass sloped down on either side, revealing glimpses of snakelike brown tree roots beneath the tranquil surface of the water. Up ahead, the silver cascade and frothy churn of a weir greeted them, accompanied by the incessant drip of a restless mill wheel. The mill wheel, in turn, supported a gray-gabled mill house that emanated a comforting, muffled hum, intermittently interrupted by cheerful, clear voices. The sight was so breathtaking that the Mole could only lift both forepaws in awe, gasping, "O my! O my! O my!"

The Rat skillfully guided the boat to the bank, securing it firmly. He assisted the still unsteady Mole in stepping ashore and retrieved the luncheon basket. As a favor, the Mole requested permission to unpack the contents himself. The Rat gladly obliged, sprawling on the grass, enjoying a moment of rest while his enthused companion laid out the tablecloth and arranged the mysterious packets one by one. With each new revelation, the Mole continued to gasp in amazement, exclaiming, "O my! O my!" At last, when everything was prepared, the Rat encouraged, "Now, dig in, my dear friend!" The Mole eagerly complied, grateful for the invitation, as he had been engrossed in spring cleaning since the early hours of the morning, forsaking both sustenance and rest. Since that distant time, which now felt like countless days ago, he had undergone a multitude of experiences.
