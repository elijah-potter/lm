The valley now glistens under a thin, persistent mist, its contours softened by the damp. Ancient stone walls, cracked and moss-covered, arch over what was once a thriving farm—now a skeletal ruin of overgrown fields and half-buried foundations. The river, once a vibrant green, flows sluggishly, its banks lined with gnarled willows and reeds that sway in the rare, cool breeze. A single, rusted tractor lies abandoned in the center, its wheels half-sunk into the earth, its once-bright paint faded to patina. The air hums with the distant call of migratory birds, their shadows flickering against the horizon where the sky, once clear, now stretches like a bruise, thick with the weight of the storm that passed last night. The land breathes, slow and deliberate, as if holding its breath after a long, forgotten exhale.
