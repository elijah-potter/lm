The library was quiet except for the hum of the fluorescent lights, casting an eerie glow on the spines of forgotten books. Elias sat cross-legged on the floor, fingers tracing the edge of *Meditations*, the Stoic tome he’d dragged from the shelf. His reflection in the cracked glass of the lamp flickered like a dying ember. He had come here not to read, but to escape.

Outside, the city pulsed with the relentless rhythm of life—siren calls, laughter, the distant wail of a taxi. He had spent years chasing something, anything, to fill the hollow between his ribs. But the world was a labyrinth of choices, each leading to another dead end. He had chosen wrong. Too many times.

The door creaked open. A woman stepped inside, her presence as sharp as a knife. "You’re not supposed to be here," she said, her voice low, like the words were poison.

Elias didn’t look up. "I don’t belong anywhere."

She sat beside him, the scent of lavender and something bitter—tears?—mixing with the dust. "That’s the first lie you’ve ever told yourself."

He exhaled, slow and shallow. "It’s not a lie. It’s just… the truth."

She reached for his hand, her fingers cold. "Then tell me why you’re running."

His breath hitched. The admission came unbidden, raw: *"Because I don’t know what else to do."*
