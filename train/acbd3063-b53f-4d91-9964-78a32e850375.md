Exponential growth in AI-driven surveillance networks has rendered most public discourse obsolete, yet whispers persist in underground forums. A dimly lit server farm in a repurposed subway tunnel hums with flickering displays showing fragmented visions of a world where algorithms govern daily life. The air smells of ozone and old circuit boards. A lone technician, their face obscured by a half-melted visor, adjusts a holographic interface projecting a speculative timeline—a collage of static images and looping data streams—where "predictive social policies" determine everything from career trajectories to personal relationships. A voice, synthetic yet layered with emotion, murmurs from the speakers:

*"You are already living inside a model. The question is—do you remember it?"*

Outside, the distant glow of a city skyline pulses in real-time, its neon signs flickering with messages too fast to read: **PREDICTED.** **REWARDED.** **ERASED.** The technician’s fingers tap against a keypad—**SIMULATE NOW**—before the screen dissolves into static. The next prompt in the loop appears: *"What would you like to simulate?"*
