What is the difference between a CPA and an accountant in Florida? This is a question that many business owners ask. While many of the duties are the same for a CPA and an accountant, there are some very distinct differences. A CPA or Certified Public Accountant can perform all the duties of an accountant but the reverse is not true. 

The requirements to become a CPA vary from state to state. Becoming a CPA in Florida requires 120 semester hours of undergraduate education including accounting courses and business courses. A candidate must also pass the Uniform Certified Public Accountant Examination and get a specific number of hours of on-the-job training.

While an accountant need not be a certified professional, many have taken a number of business courses and have a good working knowledge of balance sheets, cash flow, owner’s equity, and the chart of accounts. An accountant is responsible for completing financial reports and most are fully capable of handling quarterly IRS tax reports and filings, as well as the annual tax return for a small business.

The CPA profession is regulated by the state of Florida and it requires ongoing training each year. Though this is not true for accountants, many are eager to stay current with any tax law changes or advancements in business and financial matters. Both the CPA and accountant perform very detailed financial work and understand the importance of getting reports and other work done correctly.

In both professions, you will find highly skilled individuals with a passion for numbers. They are capable to provide a wide range of assistance to business owners from tax preparation and auditing to accounts payable and receivable.
