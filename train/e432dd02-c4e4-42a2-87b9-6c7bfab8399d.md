The rain pattered against the crumbling brick wall of an abandoned church as the figure—clad in a worn coat, boots splashing through shallow puddles—stumbled down the dimly lit alley. A flickering streetlamp cast jagged shadows on the wet pavement. The headlamp on their utility belt flickered, casting eerie light on a hand clutching a crumpled map, its edges singed from an unremembered fire.

A rusted sign, half-hidden by ivy, read *"Turn left at the bridge,"* but the bridge had long since collapsed into the swollen river below. The person froze, listening to the distant echo of a train whistle—once familiar, now a disorienting sound. The map’s faded ink traced an unfamiliar path: a dead-end lane leading toward a forest where no one lived.

A cold draft slithered from the cracks in the alley, carrying the scent of damp earth and something metallic. The person’s breath fogged the air, and for a moment, they forgot where they were. The city lights—once a beacon—now appeared as distant, unreadable stars. A decision was made: turn back or press forward into the unknown.
