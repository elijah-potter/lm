The clock struck eight when Mia pressed the buzzer outside her bedroom door. Her parents’ voices echoed from the living room, but she ignored them, her eyes glued to the screen of her tablet. The last chapter of *The Last Eclipse* was loading—something about a mysterious celestial event that could rewrite history.

She exhaled sharply. "Okay, Mia, get up," her dad called, but she was already halfway across the room, her sneakers barely touching the floor.

The hallway smelled like stale coffee and old books. She slipped into her backpack and grabbed her water bottle, her fingers brushing the small, polished stone tucked inside—a gift from her grandma, who had told her stories about the stars long before she could read.

At the door, she hesitated. The house was quiet except for the hum of the fridge. She opened it slowly, the cold air making her eyes water. On the counter, her mom’s laptop sat open, a half-eaten sandwich beside it. The screen was still warm. She snatched it, skimming through the emails—mostly bills, a reminder about her science fair project, and one from her best friend, Javier.

*"Mia, you’re late. The eclipse starts at 11:30. Come over and we’ll watch together."*

She groaned, typing a quick reply: *"Can’t. Got to finish this chapter. Later!"*

---

The library was packed by 11:20. Mia sat at a corner table, her tablet glowing under the fluorescent lights. The air smelled like old paper and coffee. A group of girls in identical hoodies—all from her homeroom—sat across from her, scrolling through their phones. Mia ignored them, her focus sharp.

The last paragraph loaded: *"The sky darkened as if a shadow had fallen over the world. The temperature dropped. The stars blinked out one by one, leaving only the faint glow of the sun’s corona. At that moment, the first of the Time Anchors activated—objects that could rewrite history if used correctly."*

A sharp gasp from the girls made her jump. She looked up to see one of them, Lila, pointing at her tablet. "You’re reading that again? It’s a myth!"

Mia’s fingers tightened around the device. "It’s not. My grandma used to say the same thing. She worked in an observatory."

Lila smirked. "Yeah, right. You’re just looking for an excuse to stay up late."

Mia didn’t answer. The screen flickered, and the chapter ended.

---

Javier’s apartment was dim, the curtains drawn tight against the night. He was already on the couch, a half-empty energy drink in hand. His phone buzzed—another notification from the science fair committee.

"Still not coming?" he muttered, not looking up.

She sat beside him, the weight of the tablet heavy in her hands. "I told you. I need to finish this."

He smirked. "You’re a liar."

She didn’t answer.

The screen flashed—11:30 PM. The eclipse had begun.

Javier’s phone buzzed again. He glanced at it. "Shit. We’re late."

Mia’s eyes locked onto the screen. The countdown was ticking. The girls outside would be waiting. But she couldn’t leave yet.

The last line of the chapter appeared: *"The Time Anchors were activated. The world was changing. And Mia was the key."*

She exhaled. The decision was made.

She stood, her fingers trembling slightly. "I’m coming."

Javier groaned but followed her out. The apartment door clicked shut behind them.

Outside, the streetlights flickered. The sky had turned an eerie, deep blue. The girls stood in a cluster, whispering.

Mia stepped forward. "I’m ready."

Lila’s eyes narrowed. "You’re not."

She didn’t care. The tablet was still warm in her hands. The last chapter was loaded. And the world was about to change.

They walked together, the cold air biting at their cheeks. The girls followed, murmuring.

Mia didn’t look back.

The sky above them was a curtain of stars, some blinking out. The temperature dropped. The wind howled through the streets.

And somewhere, in the darkness, the first Time Anchor pulsed to life.
