The kitchen light flickered as Emma stood in the center of the room, her fingers tracing the empty space where the coffee mug once rested on the counter. Her stomach twisted, remembering the warm brew she’d just poured. The ceramic rim, still slightly damp, lay abandoned on the floor near the sink, its saucer cracked. She glanced at the clock: 6:47 AM. Too early to start the day without her ritual morning cup.

Her phone buzzed from the table—another missed call from her boss. She ignored it, focused instead on the half-empty teacup still steaming on the windowsill. The teabag had dissolved into the brew, but the sugar had scattered, leaving a tiny, unmistakable trail in the corner. The scent of caramelized cinnamon lingered, a ghost of something sweet that was no longer there.

Emma exhaled sharply, rubbing her temples. She’d left it on the counter last night after dinner, intending to refill it later. The morning rush had stolen the chance. The hum of the fridge behind her suddenly felt too loud, like a reminder of the day ahead—another one of those mornings where she’d forgotten something important, another one of those mornings where the world kept moving without her.
