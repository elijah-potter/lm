A quiet morning in an unfamiliar house, the scent of damp wood and old books lingering in the air. The choice before you is a fork in the path—one leads to the locked door at the end of the hall, the other to the half-empty bottle on the kitchen counter. The first whispers of danger, the second of surrender. Neither feels right, but each is necessary. The test isn’t in the choice itself but in what you carry forward from it. The weight of the decision isn’t measured in time but in the silence afterward. The question isn’t *what* you choose, but *how* you walk away from it. The answers aren’t found in the options, but in the cracks between them.
