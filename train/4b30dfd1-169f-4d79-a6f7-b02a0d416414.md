The dim hallway flickered with unsteady lamplight, the air thick with dust and stale coffee. A cracked ceiling fan spun lazily, its blades scraping against the rusted metal frame. The scent of damp wood and old paint lingered, mingling with the faint metallic tang of blood—fresh, but already fading. A shattered glass bottle lay half-buried in the carpet near the door, its jagged edges catching the light like a thousand tiny stars.

The room itself was a relic of another era: high ceilings, ornate moldings, and a grand fireplace where the embers had long since died. A half-eaten sandwich sat atop a chipped teacup on the mahogany desk, its crust still slightly warm. The door was slightly ajar, the brass handle cold against the palm. Outside, the distant murmur of voices—too familiar—faded into the static of a broken radio. A single, damp footprint in the dust near the floorboards.
