A hidden alleyway in a city’s old quarter, where cobblestones shift underfoot in ways that aren’t marked but are well-known. The air hums with the sound of a single, ancient lantern flickering at the end—a light that bends slightly toward the center of the path. There are no signs, but the pavement curves imperceptibly into a spiral, leading to a door with a lock that doesn’t require a key. The walls whisper in a language no one writes down but everyone speaks: the rhythm of footsteps, the weight of shadows, the way the wind carries the scent of dried herbs from a hidden garden. Rules here are carved into the earth itself—step too close to the edge of the spiral, and the pavement gives way to a narrow, unmarked bridge over a stream that runs backward. The water reflects not your face, but the future you’ve already chosen.
