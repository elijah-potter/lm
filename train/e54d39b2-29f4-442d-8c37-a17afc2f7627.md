In a dimly lit warehouse, fluorescent tubes flickering overhead, **Javier** adjusted the hydraulic clamp on a stack of crates labeled **"Do Not Export"** with a slight wobble—just enough to slide one crate past the tamper-proof seal. The barcode on the box was already scratched, but the label itself remained intact, the black ink still legible despite the deliberate wear. His fingers traced the edge of the receipt, the one he’d signed with a **Javier Morales** stamp that wasn’t his—borrowed from the local customs office’s office supplies. The forklift operator, **Marta**, didn’t question the extra crate; she just nodded and tipped her head toward the loading dock. The forklift rumbled forward, and the crates rolled out into the night, their contents—rare medical supplies—directed north instead of south. The camera at the dock had been disabled since the last audit, but Javier’s eyes flicked to the security feed on his phone, just to be sure. The feed was still black, as expected. The rule wasn’t broken; it was just **adjusted**, and that was the difference.
