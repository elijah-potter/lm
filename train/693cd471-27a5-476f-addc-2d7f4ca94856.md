At first, the old farmer believed his son’s claims about the wild goose’s feather being a curse. He dismissed it as superstition, but upon seeing the hen clutching the feather and trembling, he feared it was a sign of death. His daughter, however, saw only a bird’s natural behavior—one of the geese always carried feathers from the marsh.

She explained that the feather wasn’t cursed; the goose had simply lost a wing. The farmer, realizing his son had been telling the truth all along—just wrong about the cause—realized the misunderstanding had been a test. The feather was no curse, but the truth had been there the whole time: his son’s honesty had been the real gift.
