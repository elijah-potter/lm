**Mayor’s Office Announces New "Neon Haven" Initiative in Skyport**
The City Council today unveiled a pilot project aimed at reviving the once-thriving industrial district of **Neon Haven**, a once-dying area once known for its fluorescent signboards and neon-lit streets. Under the initiative, the city will collaborate with local businesses and artists to transform abandoned warehouses into a vibrant cultural hub, complete with rotating neon art installations, a public "light tunnel" walkway, and a community festival celebrating the area’s retro-futuristic heritage. "This isn’t just about aesthetics—it’s about reclaiming identity," said Mayor Elias Voss. "Neon Haven will be a beacon for both residents and tourists, blending history with cutting-edge sustainability." The first phase, targeting completion by spring, will include solar-powered LED installations and partnerships with local tech startups to ensure long-term energy efficiency. Critics warn of high costs, while supporters highlight potential tourism boosts. The project will open to the public in phases, starting with a grand unveiling at the **Neon Haven Memorial** next month—a temporary structure honoring the area’s past luminaries.

The city has proposed a **"Retail Revival Fund"** to subsidize rent for struggling businesses, but opposition from property owners argues it incentivizes further vacancies. Meanwhile, **Black Friday sales** saw a **15% uptick** in online orders, with e-commerce platforms like **HavenMart** reporting a **28% increase** in orders for "mystery neon products." Authorities urge businesses to adapt by offering hybrid shopping experiences—such as curbside pickup for high-demand items—to compete.

Most incidents—**82%**—involve break-ins during late-night festivals, where security is reportedly lax. The most common targets are vintage signs repurposed for events, such as a **$12,000** stolen 1980s-era "Happy Hour" sign from **The Neon Lounge**. Authorities blame overcrowding and distracted staff during peak hours. The city is deploying **motion-activated LED deterrents** in high-risk areas, but residents warn it may attract more criminals. **Transportation Announcement: "SkyTrak" Test Run Begins**
The **Skyport Transit Authority** has launched a **one-month trial** of the **"SkyTrak"**—a pilot electric glider shuttle service connecting **Neon Haven to the University District**. The 15-minute route, operating daily from 7 AM to 10 PM, uses solar-powered propellers and is powered by a **hybrid battery system**. Passengers report a **40% faster** commute than traditional buses, though initial delays have been noted due to wind turbulence. The service costs **$3.50 per ride**, with discounts for students and early-bird bookings. Critics argue the route could disrupt local traffic, while supporters praise its eco-friendly appeal. The city plans to expand the fleet if the trial succeeds. The first incident—a minor crash into a **neon sign** during a storm—was minor but sparked safety discussions.
