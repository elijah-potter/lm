The clock struck eleven before the first rain of the season, and that was when I noticed it—the way the shadows stretched diagonally across the pavement, just a little longer than the others. Every evening, after the streetlights flickered to life, the shadows in front of the old bookstore on Maple Street would cast a faint, repeating arc, as if the building itself were bending backward. I didn’t know what to make of it until the third week, when the same shadow appeared *behind* the parked cars at the same angle, mirroring the ones in front. The sun was sinking, but the pattern stayed consistent. By then, I was watching the pavement like a hawk, counting the days until the next rain. Then came the night the pattern shifted. The shadows didn’t just bend—they *twisted*. The rain came early, and when it did, the water pooled in a perfect, irregular shape, as if something had pressed down on the earth. The next morning, the bookstore’s window display had a new glass—frosted, not just from the cold, but from something *inside*. That was when I realized: the pattern wasn’t just there. It was waiting.
