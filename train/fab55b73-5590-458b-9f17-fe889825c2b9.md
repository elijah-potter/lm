The train’s whistle shrieked past the platform’s edge as the last passenger boarded, the doors hissing shut behind them. Inside, the man in the overstuffed suit coat clenched his fists, his breath fogging the window. He had left the café at precisely 8:45, but the Metro had been delayed by a strike at the last station, and the train had been packed. His watch read 9:03. He exhaled sharply, relief flooding through him—just as the conductor waved him to the front. The ticket collector’s beeped a "late pass" as he stepped onto the platform, where the crowd parted like the Red Sea. He grabbed his bag, muttered a hurried apology to the woman who’d been standing with her phone out, and sprinted toward the exit. The car door slammed shut behind him as he turned—just in time to see the stationmaster’s clipboard slip from his hand.
