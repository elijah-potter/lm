**Scene: The Night Shift**

The alarm buzzed at 3:17 AM, but neither of them was awake. The neon sign flickered outside the diner’s window as the first customer, a man in a worn-out coat, sat at the counter. The bartender, a woman named Clara, wiped the counter with a rag, her eyes heavy. The regular, a burly man named Jake, had already ordered his usual—a double espresso with extra whipped cream—when the door creaked open.

**Jake:** *"You’re late. Again."*

**Clara:** *"I was up before you. Had to drive through the rain."*

**Jake:** *"You’re always late. Why bother?"

A pause. The machine hissed as it brewed coffee. The man in the coat ordered a black coffee, no sugar.

**Clara:** *"You’re always late. Why bother?"*

The man in the coat nodded slowly. Clara set down his cup. Jake crossed his arms.

**Jake:** *"You think I’m late because you’re always late? Or because I’m *always* late?"*

The diner hummed around them. The coffee machine dripped. Clara stared at her hands. The man in the coat drank his coffee, untouched.

**Jake:** *"It’s not about you. It’s about the way I show up."*

Clara exhaled sharply. The door swung shut behind the last customer. Outside, a car honked in the rain.

**Clara:** *"Then fix it."*

Jake turned. The neon sign pulsed again. The coffee machine went silent.

**Jake:** *"I don’t know how."*
