A forgotten village nestled between mist-shrouded hills,
its stone bridges sagging under decades of rain,
whispered names carved into driftwood by travelers long gone.

The church bell tolls at midnight, its echoes lost to silence,
while the well—once brimming with fresh spring water—now bubbles with stagnant memories.

Children’s laughter fades into the wind,
their laughter a ghost in the cracked tiles of the schoolhouse roof.
The postman arrives only in dreams,
his letters turned to dust on the windowsill.

Even the river remembers,
carving the same worn stones where fishermen used to stand,
while the current hums the same old song of the past.

No maps mark this place anymore—
only the occasional traveler, a lost soul, stumbles upon its ruins,
stops to stare at the half-eroded sign:

*"Here once stood… now only memory remains."*
