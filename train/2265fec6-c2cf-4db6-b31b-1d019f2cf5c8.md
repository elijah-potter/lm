In a conference room, a diverse group of professionals gathered around a table, all nodding in unison during a consensus meeting. The CEO, a seasoned executive, announced a new company-wide policy: "All employees will participate in a quarterly team-building retreat." The room erupted in applause and murmurs of agreement. Each person had their own rationale:

- **Mark**, the HR manager, saw it as a proactive measure to reduce turnover by fostering camaraderie.
- **Sophia**, the marketing director, believed it would enhance brand perception through authentic employee stories shared publicly.
- **Daniel**, a skeptical engineer, thought it was a well-intentioned distraction from deadlines, hoping it’d ease his workload.
- **Priya**, the finance lead, framed it as a cost-effective way to boost morale without increasing budgets.
- **Jamie**, the youngest intern, simply wanted to "do something fun after long workdays."

By the end, they signed off on the resolution, each convinced their own reason was the most valid—yet no one questioned the others’ motivations.
