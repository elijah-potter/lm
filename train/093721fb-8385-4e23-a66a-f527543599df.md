**The Hollow Light**

The morning sun spilled over the broken glass of my window, a golden stain on the damp pavement outside. I sat at my desk, fingers hovering over the keyboard, the weight of silence pressing down like a debt I could never repay. The city hummed outside—siren wails, distant laughter, the occasional clatter of a delivery truck—but none of it reached me. I was a ghost in my own home, my thoughts a collection of half-formed sentences and unanswered questions.

The door creaked open. My mother stood there, her apron damp with flour, her face a mask of exhaustion. She didn’t look at me. Instead, she tossed a loaf of bread onto the counter, the scent of yeast and warmth clashing with the cold air in the room. *"You’re late,"* she said, voice rough like sandpaper. *"The baker’s closing early."*

I didn’t answer. The bread was stale, the bread she’d made, the bread that had once been whole.

**The Weight of What Was**

I ran my fingers through my hair, the strands sticking to my skin like ink on parchment. Outside, the streets were alive—people rushing, cars honking, laughter bubbling up from the cafés where strangers drank coffee and forgot their names. But I was somewhere else. Somewhere where the air tasted like smoke and the walls were made of old papers, the kind that crumbled when you touched them.

She walked into the room, her boots scuffing against the floor, her eyes sharp as knives. *"You think this is over?"* she asked, voice low, the words cutting deeper than any knife. I didn’t flinch. *"No,"* I said. *"I know."*

Her laugh was bitter, like the taste of rotten fruit. *"Then why do you keep coming back?"* she demanded, her voice rising. *"Why do you let them win?"*

The words hit me like a punch. I’d spent years pretending I didn’t care. But the truth was simpler: I couldn’t stop. Not because I wanted to, but because I didn’t know what else to do. The world outside was bright, loud, and indifferent. Here, at least, I could pretend I mattered.

She reached out, her fingers brushing mine. *"You’re not worth it,"* she whispered. And for the first time, I believed her.
