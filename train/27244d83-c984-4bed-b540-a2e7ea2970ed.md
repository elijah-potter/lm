**The Last Light**

They found the old clock in the attic, its gears rusted but still turning—one last time. The hands stopped at 11:59 PM, the second chiming like a death knell. The clock was a relic from another world, one where time had no cycle, only a single, endless present.

Outside, the city slept. The streets were empty, save for the occasional figure—always the same, always moving in the same direction, toward the horizon. They were not people. They were echoes, hollow vessels carrying no names, no memories, only the weight of what had been.

The girl who found the clock was not supposed to exist. She had no past, no future—only this moment, this weightless nothingness where everything was possible. The clock’s hands were her eyes, spinning in slow motion, revealing nothing but the infinite.

She stepped out into the dark, following the figures. They led her to the edge of the world, where the sky was a mirror reflecting the city’s last dying embers. The clock stopped. The seconds dissolved into air.

And then—

She was standing in a forest. The trees were tall, their leaves whispering in a language she did not understand. Above her, the stars were different—so close, so bright, pulsing like a heartbeat. She reached out, and the world shifted.

The figures were no longer figures. They were hands, grasping at her, pulling her toward the center of the forest. The clock’s chime echoed in her ears, not as a sound, but as a question: *What comes after this?*

She looked down. Her fingers were no longer hers. They were claws, digging into the dirt, searching for something buried beneath the roots.

The forest exhaled. The trees bowed. And the first light of dawn crept between them, slow and deliberate, as if counting backward.

The clock had not stopped. It had only paused.
