The faction divided at the council hall’s round table, tensions simmering beneath the polished oak. The elder’s voice cracked as they spoke of the new mining rights—"The northern pass is our birthright," they said, gesturing to the map spread across the table, while the younger merchant’s fingers drummed impatiently against the tabletop, arguing that the land was already claimed by the northern alliance’s treaty. The historian, whose quill had paused mid-sentence, leaned forward, their eyes sharp with calculation: *"What we gain today may cost us tomorrow."* A murmur rippled through the crowd as the vote was called, and the first dissenting cry rose—*"No!"*—before the majority’s murmur of *"Yes"* drowned it out. The group split along the fault line of differing priorities: survival vs. legacy, power vs. purpose. The elder clutched their robe tighter, whispering to the guard at their side, while the merchant’s eyes flicked to the merchant guild’s representative, already pocketing their ledger. The historian’s pen hovered over the ledger, tracing the contours of the next betrayal.
