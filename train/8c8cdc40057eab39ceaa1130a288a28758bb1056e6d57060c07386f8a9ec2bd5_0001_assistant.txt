Q: Do dogs descend from the gray wolf, Canis Iupus?
A: Dogs did descend from a species of wolf, but not the gray wolf or Canis Iupus, as most people believe. 

Q: Do scientists know exactly what species gave rise to dogs?
A: While scientists are still working to understand exactly what species gave rise to dogs, DNA evidence points to now-extinct Eurasian wolves as the ancestors of modern dogs.

Q: When did dogs break off from their wild ancestors?
A: Although no one knows for sure, genetics suggest that dogs broke off from their wild ancestors between 15,000 and 30,000 years ago. 

Q: How long did it take for wolves to evolve into domesticated dogs?
A: It’s impossible to say exactly; however, the general thought is that it took thousands of years for wolves to evolve into domesticated dogs. 

Q: What do scientists believe the process was of wolves becoming domesticated dogs? 
A: While it’s still quite a mystery, most scientists believe that wolves may have started down the path of becoming domesticated dogs by eating human scraps, then being fed intentionally by humans, and then welcomed into human homes and bred for certain traits.
