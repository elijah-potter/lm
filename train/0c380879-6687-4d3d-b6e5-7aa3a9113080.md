The sudden realization hit me as I slammed the door behind me: *"I should’ve asked more questions."* The decision had been made in a blur of adrenaline and panic, the kind that turns logic into static.

That morning, the email from HR had been clear—*"Urgent: New project lead for Q4."* The urgency was palpable, the stakes high. I’d spent half the night drafting a response, typing frantically, then hitting **Send** without verifying the recipient. A mistake, I realized now, as the reply was addressed to the wrong department.

The next morning, my boss stood in my doorway, arms crossed. *"You missed the meeting. Why?"* My face burned. *"I was… just…"* The words stuck. Haste had turned a standard response into a crisis. Now, I’d have to explain why a project lead’s initial contact was sent to the wrong team, why a critical deadline was delayed, and why I’d failed to double-check the details before hitting **Send**.

The lesson was simple: haste is the enemy of precision. But by then, the damage was done. The only thing I could do now was make it right—slowly, carefully, one step at a time.
