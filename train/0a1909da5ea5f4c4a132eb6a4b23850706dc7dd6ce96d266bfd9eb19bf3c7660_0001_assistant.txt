The distinction between a cult and a religion is a complex and debated topic. There are a few characteristics that can be used to describe the belief system of what is commonly considered a cult relative to more widely accepted religions. These are generalizations and not absolute criteria. Classifying a group as a cult or a religion can vary depending on cultural and societal perspectives.

- Leadership and Authority: Cults often have a charismatic and authoritative leader who is considered the sole source of truth and guidance.

- Exclusivity and isolation: Cults tend to be highly exclusive and isolate their members.

- Manipulation and coercion: Cults often use manipulative techniques to control and influence their members.

- Unorthodox beliefs and practices: Cults often have beliefs that deviate from mainstream religious teachings by a great deal.

- Transparency and accountability: Cults tend to lack transparency and accountability.
