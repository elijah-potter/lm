The first recorded routine was likely part of ancient Babylonian administrative practices, documented on clay tablets around **2000 BCE**. The *Code of Hammurabi* (c. 1754 BCE) included early procedural laws, though not strictly labeled as "routines."

Later, the Roman legal system codified administrative processes, such as the *Lex Hortensia* (180 BCE), establishing fixed procedures for lawsuits and public decrees. These early systems laid foundational procedural norms.

In medieval Europe, guilds standardized trade routines (e.g., apprentice progression, inspection standards), documented in charters like the *Statutes of Westminster* (13th–14th centuries).

The Industrial Revolution (18th–19th centuries) formalized factory routines, introducing timekeeping (e.g., *time clocks*), assembly-line methods, and standardized quality checks. Taylorism (1911) and Fordism (1913) further codified labor processes.

Modern IT systems (1960s–present) automated routine tasks via workflows (e.g., ERP systems), leading to standardized IT procedures in ITIL frameworks.
