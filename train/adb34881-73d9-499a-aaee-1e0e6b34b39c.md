Waiting is often the quietest act of defiance. A stranger paused in the rain to let a child cross the street, not out of kindness alone, but because the child’s hesitation was the same as his own. The bus arrived at precisely 8:07, a number etched into his wristwatch like a promise. He sat in the café’s half-lit corner, staring at the steam rising from his coffee, until the sound of his phone buzzing—an email from the office—made him exhale.

Waiting isn’t just time passed; it’s the space between what is and what will be. The old clock on the wall at the train station still ticked, but the hands didn’t move. He watched the reflections of the streetlamps in the puddles, each flicker a ghost of the future he’d yet to claim. The door to the café creaked open, and the scent of fresh bread wafted in, a reminder that life, like waiting, was a process of being *in between*. He closed his eyes. The pause was the only thing that felt like victory.
