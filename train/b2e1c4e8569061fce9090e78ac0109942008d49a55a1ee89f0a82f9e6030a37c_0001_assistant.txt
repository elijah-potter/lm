

Ball lightning is a controversial and intriguing natural phenomenon that has captured the interest of scientists and laymen alike. At its core, ball lightning is a brightly lit orb of electricity, typically observed to be between several centimeters and a meter in size. Its mystery is partially due to the fact that it remains constantly in motion, and only exists for a short time, no more than a few minutes. Our understanding of this phenomenon is limited, despite persistent occurrences and observations. So limited, in fact, that the scientific community cannot even come to a consensus on how or why ball lightning exists. 

This paper provides an overview of all current knowledge and theories on the topic from various sources. Primarily, we will focus on ball lightning's mechanisms, characteristics, and possible applications. Several current hypotheses attempting to define the start of ball lightning will be covered, including electromagnetism, atmospheric chemicals, and plasma physics. We will discuss all observed properties, such as color, sound, and movement, and investigate whether any of these have a lasting effect on the environment or our own bodies. Lastly, we go into detail on ball lightning research and a few of its potential modern uses, including possible new energy sources, and even spacecraft propulsion. In its entirety, this paper will be a comprehensive look at all current knowledge of ball lightning and also highlight the primary areas for future investigation.
