A storm raged outside, black clouds rolling like a living thing, the wind howling through the bare branches of an ancient oak. Rain lashed against the window, each droplet a silent accusation, the glass smeared with condensation like a ghostly face. Inside, the flickering lamp cast long shadows that stretched unnaturally, their edges dissolving into mist. The air smelled of damp earth and something older—rotting fruit, damp wool, the scent of a room that had seen better days.

Inside, the room’s only light came from the single candle on the desk, its flame flickering erratically, casting jagged patterns on the walls. The walls themselves were marked with faded, half-erased words—some from a child’s scribbles, others from something long forgotten. The air was thick with the weight of unspoken things, each breath labored like the wind outside. The floorboards groaned under the weight of a figure hunched over a ledger, their fingers trembling as they traced numbers in the margins, each one a promise or a curse. The candle’s flame danced toward the ceiling, the room itself seeming to lean in.

Outside, the sky split open. Thunder rumbled, not the distant sound of a storm, but the immediate, grinding roar of something ancient waking up. The rain fell harder now, a relentless, drowning weight. The candle guttered out. In the darkness, the figure didn’t move. The storm raged on, indifferent.
