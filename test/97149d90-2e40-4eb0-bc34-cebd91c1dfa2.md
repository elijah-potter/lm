The first time Mia saw the strange light in her backyard, she thought it was just her imagination. It flickered at the edge of the woods, a soft glow that didn’t belong there, like a forgotten lantern in the middle of a dense forest. Her little brother, Leo, was running toward it, his eyes wide with excitement. "Mia, come quick! Look what I found!"

She followed him, her sneakers crunching on dead leaves. The light was pulsing faster now, drawing closer. When they reached the edge of the woods, Leo tripped and fell, his hand brushing against the ground. A sharp *crack* echoed, and the light flared violently. Leo’s skin turned pale, his eyes rolling back. Before Mia could react, he was gone—just like that.

The next morning, Mia found his backpack on the porch. Inside was a single, crumpled note: *"Find the key. The woods hold answers."* She didn’t believe in ghosts, but something about the note made her uneasy. That night, she kept her window open just a crack, listening for any sound in the woods. She swore she heard whispers—soft, like voices in the wind.

A week passed. Then another. Mia started noticing strange things: her reflection moved on its own in mirrors, shadows stretched unnaturally, and the trees seemed to lean toward her house. One evening, she found an old, rusted key in the garden. It was small, smooth, and oddly familiar. Her fingers traced its shape, and suddenly, she remembered a childhood dream where she and Leo stood in the woods, holding hands, and the key was the only thing they could find.

The next day, she brought the key to the woods at dusk. The air was thick with silence, broken only by the distant cry of an owl. She placed the key in the dirt where Leo had fallen and stepped back. The light from the woods flared again, brighter this time, and the trees seemed to sway in unison. When the glow faded, she was standing in the middle of a clearing, surrounded by a circle of strange, glowing flowers.

Inside, she found Leo sitting cross-legged, his eyes wide but calm. "You came," he said quietly. Behind them, a figure stood in the shadows—a tall, pale man with hollow eyes. "You were meant to find this," he said, holding up the key. "The woods protect what is buried here. The key unlocks the past, but it also calls something else."

Mia’s stomach twisted. "What do you mean?"

The man’s voice was like rusted metal. "The key doesn’t just open doors. It opens *minds*." He reached into his coat and pulled out a small, glowing orb. "This is the heart of the woods. It feeds on memories, on emotions. If you hold it, you’ll see what Leo saw before you—what you both saw."

Leo’s hands trembled. "It’s not safe," he whispered.

The man smiled. "No. But it’s necessary." He pressed the orb into Mia’s palm. The moment she touched it, the world blurred. She saw flashes—her childhood, Leo’s death, the whispers in the woods, the key’s origins. She saw her own fear, her own regret. And then, she saw the truth: the woods weren’t just protecting something. They were *waiting* for her to decide what to do with the power she held.

When her vision cleared, the man was gone. The orb was still warm in her hand. She dropped it to the ground. The trees around her began to glow, their leaves shifting like living things. Leo was gone too, replaced by a figure wrapped in tattered clothing—his body, but his face was different. His eyes were empty.

Mia ran. She didn’t stop until she reached her house, her breath coming in ragged gasps. She didn’t sleep that night. She stared at the key in her pocket, at the note Leo left her, and wondered if she’d made the right choice.

The next morning, she found Leo’s backpack on the porch again. This time, inside, there was a single, new note:

*"Find the answer before it finds you."*
