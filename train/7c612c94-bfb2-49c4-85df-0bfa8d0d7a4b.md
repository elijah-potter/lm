The air is thick and still, suspended in a perpetual pause, as if time itself has been coiled like a sleeping serpent. The sky above is a bruised, overcast canvas of muted grays and sickly purples, the clouds forming jagged, half-formed shapes that seem to dissolve into the horizon like mist. The earth beneath is a patchwork of skeletal trees—their branches bent and frozen mid-motion, as if caught in a slow, agonizing wind. The ground is damp, the soil clinging to the soles of bare feet, a whisper of moisture that feels like a slow, deliberate exhale.

Every sound is muffled, a hush so dense it’s almost tangible. The distant murmur of a city, once a cacophony, now reduced to a faint, rhythmic pulse, like the heartbeat of a dying machine. The water in a still pond ripples with ghostly reflections—faces of those who linger just beyond sight, their eyes wide with unspoken questions. The air hums with an electric tension, charged with the weight of something unsaid. Even the shadows cast by the few remaining human figures are elongated, stretched into shapes that don’t quite fit, as if they, too, are holding their breath.
