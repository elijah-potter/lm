A gardener noticed a single forgotten seedling in a neglected corner of the communal garden. Its thin stem bent under the weight of a stray leaf, but its stubborn roots clung to the soil. Over weeks, as others pruned the overgrown paths and watered the thirsty plants, this one persisted. When the first frost came, the gardener left it alone, watching as the cold turned its leaves gold. By spring, it had grown into a full tree, its branches sheltering birds and casting shade on the cracked pavement below. To the gardener, it became a metaphor—how seemingly insignificant acts, nurtured in quiet moments, bloom into something unexpected.
