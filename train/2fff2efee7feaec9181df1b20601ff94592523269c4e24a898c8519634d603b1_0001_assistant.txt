Tyre sat at the bar with his face pressed against his hands. It had been a long day of work in the office and he really needed a drink. The bartender slid a drink his way, and Tyre flipped him a coin.

"A goblin willing to part with his money? Now I've seen everything." The bartender laughed at his own cutting-edge humor and Tyre just flipped around, swilling his drink in his hand.

"The war's over, bub," he said in his gritty voice. 

"I ain't lookin' for trouble," the barkeep said, raising his hands in mock surrender. "We all know goblins can be vicious when they have a mind to." 

Tyre grunted and took a swig of his drink. Why did goblins have to hold such a bad reputation? Sure, they might have served a dark lord or two, and, sure, they might have maybe tried to bring about the destruction of mankind more than once, but this was a civil age. Elves were known for their pointy ears, wits, and—as propaganda would have it—gorgeous flowing locks. 

Goblins, by contrast, were remembered for being subservient, greedy, and bloodthirsty. Tyre worked in an office with lots of humans. He was a tax official, which didn't really help his reputation, but it was honest work. He worked hard, earned a good wage, and spent it wisely. His days of brandishing a club at travelers to relieve them of their gold were long since over, although, just sometimes, Tyre wished they weren't.
