The hollowed-out chest of the old oak, its bark split like cracked leather, whispered secrets to the wind. Inside, a fire burned low, embers dancing like restless spirits. The scent of damp earth and pine clung to the air, thick as memory.

A child sat cross-legged on the floor, their small hands clutching a half-eaten apple. The firelight flickered across their wild hair, turning it to silver in the glow. They watched the flames, their eyes wide with wonder.

Outside, the village slept beneath a sky that had learned to be quiet. The stars—those slow, unblinking sentinels—punched through the velvet canvas of night, their cold light cutting through the dark like the edges of a wound. The Milky Way, a river of silver dust, wound itself across the heavens, its path a whisper of forgotten constellations.

The wind carried the scent of rain, promising a world softened by moisture. The child stood, their bare feet pressing into the damp grass, and followed the sound, their breath visible in the cold air. The sky, vast and indifferent, watched them go, its endless depth a promise of more worlds to explore.
