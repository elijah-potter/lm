A weathered boardwalk stretches between two crumbling piers, its planks warped by decades of salt air and shifting tides. Beneath the rusted railings, the sand shifts like liquid—always moving, never still. This place is the shore, a place where the earth and sky meet in a fleeting embrace. The buildings along the waterfront, with their peeling paint and sagging awnings, seem to whisper of a past that has slipped away. Yet the wind carries the scent of old fish and the distant cry of gulls, the same sounds that have echoed through generations. Here, time is both eroded and preserved—dissolved in the sand, yet imprinted on every crack in the concrete. The waves crash relentlessly, but the waterline does not retreat forever. This is a place that feels like it could vanish in an instant—yet it endures.
