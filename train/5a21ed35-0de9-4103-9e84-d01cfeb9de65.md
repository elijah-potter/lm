The late-night drive home was silent except for the hum of the engine. The rearview mirror caught the reflection of a half-empty glass in the passenger seat—something he’d promised himself he wouldn’t touch again. He’d sworn off whiskey after his last argument with his father, but the bottle had found its way into his bag before leaving work, and now it sat untouched, waiting.

He turned onto the empty road, the headlights cutting through the twilight like a blade. The regret coiled in his throat, sharp and bitter, but he clenched his jaw, pretending it was nothing more than fatigue. The radio played static, and for a moment, the world outside was just the distant hum of traffic and the flicker of streetlights.

Then the text came—just a single word: *"Sorry."* From his sister. The message was short, the delivery automatic. He opened it, his fingers trembling slightly as he read, *"I was thinking of you."* His throat tightened. He deleted the message without reply. Better this way. It would fade.
