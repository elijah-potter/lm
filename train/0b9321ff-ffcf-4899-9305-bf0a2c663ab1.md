## Municipal Humming Initiative Sparks Unexpected Sonic Boom in Oakhaven

**By Bartholomew Finch, Metro Affairs Correspondent**

Oakhaven, a city previously known for its prize-winning petunias and remarkably punctual bus service, has embarked on a bold new initiative designed to combat urban anxiety: the Municipal Humming Initiative, or MHI. Beginning last Tuesday, strategically placed sonic emitters across the city began broadcasting a low-frequency, sustained hum, calibrated to a frequency purportedly proven to induce a state of “serene receptivity” in human subjects. The program, championed by Mayor Mildred Abernathy, is being hailed by some as a revolutionary approach to public wellbeing, while others are experiencing… unexpected effects.

The theory, as explained by Dr. Percival Quibble, Chief Resonance Officer, is simple. “The constant barrage of modern stimuli – traffic, construction, social media – creates a disharmony within the human psyche. Our hum acts as a counter-frequency, gently nudging individuals toward a more tranquil state.” Initial data, collected from a cohort of volunteer participants, did indeed suggest a reduction in reported stress levels. However, the city’s pigeon population, seemingly attuned to a different harmonic spectrum, has begun exhibiting synchronized aerial displays, forming elaborate, albeit temporary, geometric patterns above City Hall. "It's quite beautiful, really," remarked Agnes Plumtree, designated Pigeon Liaison, adjusting her binoculars. "Though the droppings are… concentrated."

The most startling development, however, has been the inexplicable rise in the production of artisanal cheese. Local dairy farmers, previously struggling to meet demand, are now reporting yields exceeding expectations by a factor of ten. The cheese, described by connoisseurs as possessing a “distinctly resonant tang,” is rapidly depleting the city’s refrigerated storage facilities. "I’ve never seen anything like it," stated Bartholomew Butterfield, Grand Fromage of Oakhaven County. "The cows… they just *know* what to do. It’s almost as if the hum is… guiding them.”  The city’s annual cheese festival, previously a modest affair, is now anticipated to draw record crowds and require significant logistical adjustments. 

Furthermore, the low-frequency hum has demonstrably affected the city’s infrastructure. While structural engineers initially dismissed concerns about “vibrational interference,” cracks have begun appearing in the foundations of several older buildings, and the city’s beloved clock tower, a landmark for over a century, is now swaying noticeably. “We’re monitoring the situation closely,” assured Penelope Pettigrew, Director of Temporal Stability. “We believe a slight recalibration of the hum’s amplitude will mitigate the issue.  It's a delicate process, mind you; we can’t simply *stop* the serenity.” The city council is currently debating a proposal to construct a series of subterranean dampeners to absorb the vibrational energy.

The experiment continues, with Oakhaven becoming a veritable laboratory for the study of sonic wellbeing and its unforeseen consequences. The city’s unusual prosperity, coupled with the unsettling synchronization of avian life and the structural quirks of its buildings, have drawn attention from federal agencies.
