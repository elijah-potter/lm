In the quiet village of Eldermere, where the rivers whispered secrets to the old stone bridges, a tale of forgotten courage was whispered by the firelight. Old Harkin, whose hands had carved more than a thousand spoons, often told stories of his youth, when the village stood tall against the encroaching dark.

One winter’s eve, as the snow fell like silent tears, he recounted how his brother, Tomas, had stood alone against the forces that sought to drown the light. The village had been warned, but the details of the betrayal—how the traitor had been chosen not by blood, but by a bargain struck in the dark—were lost in the gathering storm. Only the fire’s embers remembered the names of those who had been left behind.

The children laughed, but the truth lingered in the hollows of their eyes, as it had in the hollows of Eldermere itself. For some truths, like the old bridges, were meant to be crossed, not carried.
