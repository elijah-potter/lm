In the hollowed-out silence of a room that held too many memories, Clara sat cross-legged on the worn carpet, her fingers tracing the faded lines of an old journal. The pages were filled with sketches of lost loves, half-finished poems, and dreams that had long since crumbled like dust.

Outside, the city pulsed with a life she barely recognized. Neon signs flickered like dying stars, casting long shadows over the cobblestones where she had once walked hand-in-hand with someone who called her "my everything." Now, they were just echoes—names on a list, faces in a photograph.

She exhaled sharply, the breath curling into the air like smoke from a forgotten cigarette. The journal clattered to the floor as she stood abruptly, her bare feet silent against the cold tiles. The door creaked open, a draft of stale air whipping through the room, carrying the scent of rain and something older—something like regret.

Clara stepped into the hallway, her boots kicking up dust. The hallway stretched endlessly, a labyrinth of mirrors reflecting her tired face and the hollow ache in her chest. She didn’t look back.

The elevator doors hissed shut behind her as she stepped inside, the weight of her past pressing down like a second skin. The doors ascended, and for a moment, the city blurred into a smear of lights and motion, indistinguishable from the blur of her thoughts.

She walked out onto the street, the rain now a steady patter against her hood. The neon glow of the skyline pulsed, indifferent. She didn’t belong here anymore.

Her footsteps faded into the crowd, swallowed by the crowd. The air smelled of exhaust and old coffee, and for the first time in years, she didn’t feel like she was running from anything. She felt like she was finally learning to walk away.
