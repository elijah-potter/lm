It wasn’t supposed to happen like this. The morning had been quiet, the kind of quiet that only comes after too many sleepless nights, after the weight of too many half-finished tasks and the hum of unresolved conflicts. The coffee was cold on the saucer, the news still playing in the background, a cycle of headlines that had lost all meaning. Outside, the city slept, its windows dark, its streets empty—just another day in a life that had already decided to ignore the promise of it.

She sat at the desk, fingers tracing the rim of the empty coffee cup, the weight of her own exhaustion pressing down like a physical force. The door creaked open, and in stepped the one person she hadn’t invited, the one who always came when the walls felt too small.

“You’re not supposed to see me,” she said, but her voice cracked, and it was all she could do not to look away. His presence was a current she couldn’t swim against, a pull that had dragged her under before and always found her again. The air between them thickened, charged with something older than their memories, something that had festered in the cracks of their relationship like rust in a forgotten joint.

“It’s always this way,” he murmured, stepping closer, the way he always did when the words got too heavy. The space between them dissolved, and for a moment, the world narrowed to the scent of his cologne—something expensive, something that made her think of him as a man who had already won, who had always been the one. The phone buzzed. She didn’t look at it. She didn’t need to. The message on the screen would tell her everything.

*They’re here.*
