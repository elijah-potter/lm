The clock struck midnight in the old, creaking church, its bell tolling with a sound like distant thunder. Elias stood at the altar, his breath shallow, his hands trembling around the cracked glass of the holy water.

The last candle flickered, casting long shadows across the stained-glass windows. The congregation murmured prayers, but Elias barely heard them. His mind raced with the vision he had seen—his wife, Clara, standing at the edge of a cliff, her arms outstretched as if embracing the wind. She was alive, but she was already falling.

A whisper slipped past his lips, *"Clara..."* The words were a prayer, a plea, a lie. The priest’s voice boomed from the pulpit, but Elias was lost in the weight of his own guilt. He had let her go—again.

The church doors burst open. A figure in a long, tattered coat stepped inside, her face obscured by a scarf. She moved with the deliberate precision of someone who had walked this path before. *"You’re late,"* she said, her voice like gravel underfoot. *"The choice is yours, Elias. You can walk away, or you can push open this door and face what’s coming."*

The hinge in the door creaked, and the moment hung between them, thick with fate. The priest’s sermon dissolved into silence. The congregation stood still, waiting. The air shimmered, and for a heartbeat, time itself seemed to slow.

Then—*the choice was made.*
