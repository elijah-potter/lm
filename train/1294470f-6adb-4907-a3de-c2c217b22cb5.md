An elderly woman sat at a cracked wooden table in a dimly lit room, her fingers tracing the rim of a half-empty teacup. Rain pattered against the half-open window, tapping out a slow, irregular rhythm. A single candle flickered on the shelf above, casting long shadows that stretched like skeletal hands across the floor. She wore a worn coat, sleeves rolled up to her elbows, and her hands trembled slightly, though she refused to let it show.

Outside, a distant siren wavered, then faded. A clock on the wall ticked, its hands moving sluggishly, as if time itself had grown heavy. She didn’t know what she waited for—only that the air smelled of damp earth and something faintly metallic, like old blood. A knock at the door came too quickly, too uncertain. When she answered, the stranger who stepped inside was wrapped in a coat too large for them, their face half-lit by the flickering candle. They held a folded piece of paper in their hands, its edges singed.

She took it without asking. The moment they turned away, the door slammed shut behind them, sealing the silence between them. The candle burned lower, its light dimming like a dying star. She leaned back in her chair, watching the rain through the glass, wondering what she had been waiting for—and what she had let go.
