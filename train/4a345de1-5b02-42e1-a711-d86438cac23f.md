A quiet afternoon in a bustling city, where the hum of conversation and clattering of coffee cups drowned out the hum of her own thoughts. She sat at a café table, the fluorescent lighting casting long shadows across the worn wood of the bench. The woman at the next table was deep in discussion, her laughter sharp and animated, while she remained silent, her fingers tracing the rim of her empty cup. The familiar scent of roasted coffee and stale bread wrapped around her, but it was nothing like the warm embrace of her childhood home—where the scent of cinnamon and woodsmoke always signaled the start of a cozy evening.

She glanced at her phone, the notifications blinking like fireflies in the dark. Messages from home, reminders of her role as the one who always fit in. A message from her sister: *"You’re so loud at dinner, you’re going to scare the kids!"* A text from her mom: *"We’re making your favorite—just call us when you’re home!"* Her stomach twisted. She wanted to be like them—warm, reliable, the one who didn’t make others feel like they had to explain themselves.

Outside, the city moved in unison, but she felt like a spectator in her own life, watching from the margins. A group of strangers laughed as they passed, their voices overlapping like a chorus. She swallowed hard, her throat dry. She had spent years studying the art of blending in, memorizing the rules of the room, the unspoken cues that made others feel at ease. But tonight, the mask slipped. The noise, the chaos—it was all too much. She leaned back, her shoulders trembling. For the first time in years, she didn’t care if she was seen.

A waiter approached, his eyes flicking toward her. She raised a hand, her voice barely a whisper: *"I’m sorry."* The word hung between them, heavy with unsaid things. He nodded, but she already knew: she would never be *them*. Not really. And that was the ache in her chest, the one that couldn’t be stuffed with more coffee or a better story. She exhaled, the sound sharp and broken. The world kept turning. She would too.
