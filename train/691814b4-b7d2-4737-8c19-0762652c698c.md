A sudden gust of wind sent a crumpled newspaper fluttering across the crowded plaza. Beneath it lay a folded wallet, its leather cover cracked with age, and inside—a single ten-dollar bill and a crumpled photo of a young woman with a faint smile. The wind carried the scent of rain and distant coffee, and for a heartbeat, the city’s hum faded into silence.

She picked it up.

The wallet had been lost for days, its owner likely searching for it in vain. But she remembered the way the woman had always lingered near this corner, clutching her bag too tightly, her hands trembling as she counted her change. That was the day she’d seen her drop it. That was the day she’d decided to return it—not for the money, but because the woman’s face was still etched in her memory, a ghost of something unspoken.

She folded the wallet carefully, tucked it into her pocket, and walked away. No one saw her. No one would know. But somewhere, in another city, a woman would reach into her pocket and find the wallet again—this time, with a fresh bill inside and a small note tucked inside: *"Thank you."*
