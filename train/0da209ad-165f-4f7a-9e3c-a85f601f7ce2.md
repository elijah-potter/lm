The room spun, each breath a slow, deliberate crawl. Shadows stretched like smoke, their edges flickering—distorted, unnatural. A half-eaten sandwich sat untouched on the table, its crust glistening with condensation, though no one had moved in hours. The air smelled of damp earth and something metallic, like old blood.

The clock on the wall ticked, its hands moving in jerky, deliberate increments—*twelve minutes* passed before the second hand clicked. A door creaked open, but no one entered. The floorboards groaned under unseen weight, as if something vast and slow was crawling beneath them.

Her fingers trembled as she reached for the glass of water beside her. The liquid swirled lazily, forming swirling patterns that shifted like living things. *"Why do you keep looking at me?"* a voice whispered, but it wasn’t coming from the door. It was in her mind, layered over the past twenty minutes—*twenty minutes*—as if the words had been spoken only seconds ago.

Outside, the wind howled, but the branches of the oak tree above her head moved in slow, deliberate arcs, as if each leaf was counting to ten before falling. The sky stretched endlessly, the sun a distant, unblinking eye. Time was not passing. It was *stretching*.
