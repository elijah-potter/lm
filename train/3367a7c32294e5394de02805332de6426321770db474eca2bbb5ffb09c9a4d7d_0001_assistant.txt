Education is a fundamental pillar of today's society that shapes the lives of citizens and the future of the country. Nelson Mandela once said, “If you want to destroy a nation, you don’t use nuclear weapons, rather destroying its education system will suffice.” Having no cost means that anyone will have access to learning, creating equality of opportunity and improving the education level of the population. This will have a great impact on the economy, providing businesses with more skilled employees to hire. Also, this will tighten the gap between the upper and lower classes, reducing inequality and promoting stability. 

But implementing such a system has its downsides, such as the strain on a country's budget. Paying teachers and staff, as well as maintaining school facilities mean that there will have to be budget cutbacks in other sectors. This may encourage spending less, which along with larger classroom sizes, may reduce the quality of education. After all, increasing the number of students without increasing the number of teachers means a more chaotic and poor learning environment.

In theory, there are many benefits to free education. However, there are practical and logistical problems to be overcome along the way before a system of free education can be successful.
