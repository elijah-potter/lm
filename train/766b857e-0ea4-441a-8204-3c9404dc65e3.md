A man sat stiffly at the kitchen table, his fingers drumming restlessly against the wooden grain. His wife’s voice, sharp and clipped, cut through the silence: *"You didn’t finish your taxes on time."* He swallowed hard, eyes flickering to the framed photo of their child on the wall, then back to her. A beat of hesitation—then the lie: *"The accountant said it’d take longer."* His voice was too calm, too practiced, as he added, *"I’ll get it sorted tomorrow."* The weight of the lie hung between them, thick as a cloud.
