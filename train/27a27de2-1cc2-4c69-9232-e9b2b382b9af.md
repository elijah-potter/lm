In the dim glow of an old library, Clara sat hunched over her desk, fingers tracing the worn edges of her half-finished novel. The ink had dried into hard cracks, and the paper smelled of age and forgotten dreams. Outside, the rain tapped against the windows in a relentless rhythm, mirroring the weight of her thoughts.

She had written this story for years—about a girl who found herself in a world she didn’t understand. But every time she sat down to write, her mind drifted to the truth she couldn’t voice: she wasn’t the girl in the story. She was the one who had abandoned it, who had let the characters die before they could live. Her father’s illness had been the final blow, the one that shattered the fragile illusion of control she clung to.

The novel sat abandoned beside her, its pages blank except for a single line scribbled in the margins: *"She was never meant to be here."* She pressed her palm against the desk, the cold wood absorbing the tension that pulsed between her fingers. The words felt like a betrayal—of her own ambition, of the girl she’d once been when she believed in stories.

A knock at the door made her jump. Her younger sister, Mira, peeked in, her eyes wide with concern. "Clara? You okay?"

Clara exhaled sharply. "Just thinking."

Mira hesitated. "You’ve been like this for weeks. Your dad—"

"He’s not dying," Clara interrupted, her voice quieter than she intended. "He’s just… not the same." She stood abruptly, her chair scraping against the floor. "I’m not the girl in the story, Mira. I’m the one who stopped writing it."

Mira’s face paled. "What does that mean?"

Clara turned away, but her hands trembled. "It means I don’t know who I am anymore. Not the writer. Not the girl. Just… empty."

The silence that followed was heavy, thick with unspoken questions. Mira swallowed hard. "What do you want to do?"

Clara’s voice was hollow. "I don’t know."
