A 12-hour cycle of repetitive routines: wake at 6:00 AM (same alarm tone), coffee brewed via the same drip machine, breakfast of eggs and toast with the same toppings. The morning news repeats the same headlines, but each day the news anchor’s face subtly shifts—just a few pixels off—like a glitch in a static display.

Workday follows rigidly: same desk, same keyboard shortcuts, same 3:30 PM snack of pretzels. The commute is identical—same traffic jam, same radio station—but the traffic lights flicker one pixel brighter, and the song on repeat has a one-note variation.

Even the mundane tasks feel altered: the shower water temperature adjusts ever-so-slightly, the laundry detergent scent changes marginally, the weather forecast’s "tomorrow" forecast is identical but the background noise (like distant sirens) shifts like a digital artifact.

Yet the day *feels* different—like a video loop with an ever-slowing drift, where the same actions produce a cumulative, unnoticeable divergence. The clock ticks forward, but the world’s rhythm subtly warps.
