The subtle misalignment of a single bolt in the control rod assembly during a routine maintenance check led to a cascade failure in the reactor’s coolant system, causing a delayed shutdown protocol that resulted in the overpressure of containment vessel Sector 4. The resulting thermal expansion of the residual heat generated in the spent fuel pool destabilized the structural integrity of the primary cooling skid, triggering a secondary breach in the secondary containment barrier. This breach exposed the reactor’s molten core to the ambient atmosphere, resulting in a localized but catastrophic release of tritium and noble gases into the atmosphere within 48 hours. The affected region’s evacuation protocols, originally designed for a 10 km exclusion zone, were expanded to 30 km due to the unexpected diffusion of radioactive particles, leading to a prolonged emergency response and public health monitoring phase that spanned 18 months. The incident’s ripple effects included temporary relocation of 237 families, a $4.2 billion infrastructure cleanup cost, and a 15-year ban on commercial nuclear operations in the affected zone, illustrating how seemingly minor operational errors can have profound, long-term consequences.
