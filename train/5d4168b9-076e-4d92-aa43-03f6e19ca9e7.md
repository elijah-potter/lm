A dimly lit attic, half-collapsed with age, hums with the scent of damp wood and old paper. **Older-than-the-house** *Lantern*, its glass cracked but still glowing faintly, sits atop a crumbling trestle table. Nearby, a dust-covered **ledger book** with faded ink is half-open, its last entry scrawled in hurried script:

*"If the clock runs out, the door will not stay shut."*

**The clock**—a tarnished brass mechanism, its gears whispering in the stillness—stops. Not broken, but *waiting*. The air thickens as **Elias** steps inside, his breath fogging the cold stone. He reaches for the ledger but hesitates. The last entry reads:

*"The key beneath the floorboards is the only one that fits."*

His fingers trace the edge of a **rusted iron door**, half-buried beneath the attic floor. Beneath it lies a **wooden chest**, its lid sealed with a single, faded lock. Inside: **a single silver key**, cold and heavy, its surface etched with strange symbols.

Outside, the house groans. A **subtle vibration** runs through the floor—like a heartbeat. The attic door creaks open, revealing a **shadowy figure** in the dim corridor below. Elias’ pulse quickens. The ledger’s ink blurs:

*"The key turns the wrong way. The first time."*

He grips it. The symbols align. The door **clicks**. Inside, the **chest opens**—but not by force. By **something unseen**, a presence that was never there. The air grows heavy. The lantern flickers. And then—

**The clock starts again.**
