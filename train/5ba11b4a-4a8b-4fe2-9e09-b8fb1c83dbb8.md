A dimly lit warehouse hums with the low hum of machinery. Two technicians in hard hats stand by a crated shipment, their gloves damp with sweat. One, a younger worker, hands the older one a clipboard marked *"Internal Use Only"* as they inspect a sealed, tampered box labeled **"DO NOT OPEN"**—the label peeled back, a hastily scribbled note underneath: **"Emergency Replacement Parts – DO NOT RELEASE"** signed with a name that isn’t on the company’s payroll.

The older technician’s eyes flick to the clipboard, then to the box’s obscured serial number—one marked for a prototype system that’s been decommissioned. A radio crackles in the background: *"This is Alpha. Repeat, Alpha—where’s the prototype shipment?"* The younger worker’s voice is tight: *"Negative, Alpha. It’s on hold. Parts out, but the box’s still here."* A beat. The older one exhales. *"Just… keep it quiet."* The younger nods, fingers tightening around the clipboard as they step back to let the forklift operator pass.

Outside, the night is quiet except for the distant wail of sirens. The warehouse door hisses shut behind them, sealing in the unspoken agreement: a rule bent not to be broken, but bent just enough to keep the lights on.
