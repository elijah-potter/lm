A fading echo of a memory—half-remembered, half-forgotten—drifts through the mind like mist over a forgotten town. The details are vague: a scent of damp earth and something sweet, perhaps a candy shop’s aroma from a summer long past. The faces are blurred, but the child’s laughter still rings in the hollow space where a song used to play. The voice, once clear, now whispers through static.

The past is a river, and this is the sand left behind, smoothed by time’s slow hands. What was once sharp and precise is now just a suggestion, a ghost in the corner of the eye. Sometimes, when the mind is quiet, it almost *tastes* like the time before the world was made to forget.
