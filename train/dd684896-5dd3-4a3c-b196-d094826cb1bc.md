Silent erosion of norms occurs when bureaucratic language redefines compliance: *"mandatory"* becomes *"recommended"*; *"unacceptable"* morphs into *"considered"*—each revision buried in a footnote or buried under a new acronym. Policies fade like ink on a wet page when the next iteration replaces old ones without explicit deprecation. The real rule isn’t in the lawbook, but in the *practices no one documents*. A system where "no" is replaced by "let’s try this," and "no exception" becomes "exceptions are tracked in spreadsheets." The law isn’t broken—it’s been outsmarted by the invisible calculus of compliance fatigue.
