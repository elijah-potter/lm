The rain had turned the cobblestones slick by evening, reflecting the flickering gas lamps with a dull, silver glow. Elias adjusted his coat collar, the wool stiff with damp, and stepped into the alley behind the bookshop. The scent of old paper and aged leather clung to the air, a ghost of the books that had been sold and lost to time.

He knelt by the cracked window, pressing his forehead against the glass. The last line of the manuscript he’d been working on was still unfinished—a fragment of a story about a woman who vanished without a trace, her name carved into the stone of an abandoned church. The words blurred as tears spilled down his cheeks.

Outside, the city hummed—a distant engine, the clatter of a cart, the murmur of voices. He stood and turned away, the wind tugging at his sleeves. The alley stretched long and silent, the only sound the distant echo of his own breath.

He walked.
