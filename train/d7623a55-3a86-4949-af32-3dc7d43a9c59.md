**Person A:**
*"The alarm went off at 6:30 AM. I woke up, checked the time—still 6:30. Then I realized the coffee machine was still running. I grabbed a mug, poured myself a cup, and sat at the kitchen table. My partner walked in, looked at me like I’d lost my mind. ‘You’re already up?’ she said. ‘I didn’t hear it,’ I muttered. She rolled her eyes. ‘You’re terrible at waking up early.’ Then she went to make breakfast while I sipped my coffee, watching her like she was a stranger."*

**Person B:**
*"The alarm blared. I jerked awake, half-asleep, rubbing my eyes. My partner was already at the kitchen counter, humming to herself, stirring eggs. I sighed, sat up, and stretched. The coffee pot was half-full. ‘Morning,’ I said, not looking at her. She shot me a glance—like I’d just admitted to a crime. ‘You didn’t need to get up,’ she said. ‘I was already awake.’ I nodded, took a sip of coffee, and let her serve the eggs. No need to explain."*

---
**Key Differences in Perception:**
- **Person A** focuses on the *delayed reaction*, the awkward moment of waking and the partner’s disapproval, framing it as a scene of missed cues and frustration.
- **Person B** emphasizes the *routine*, the shared morning habit, and the quiet tension of unspoken expectations, treating it as a neutral moment of mutual habit.
