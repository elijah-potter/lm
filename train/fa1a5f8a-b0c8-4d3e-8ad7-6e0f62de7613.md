A hollowed-out district where clock towers chime askew, their hands frozen midway between midnight and noon. The air hums with static, echoes stretch unnaturally long—every "dinner" sounds like it’s served at the same hour forever. Locals whisper about a "second" that lingers unnoticed; a child’s laughter sounds like it’s repeating, though no one repeats it. The sun moves in reverse along the same path, yet shadows cast by windows flicker between past and present. A café’s clock is always three minutes slow, but the waitress serves coffee at 3:17 AM, claiming it’s "just a phase." Some say the walls remember yesterday’s sunrise. Others claim the time is never quite right—just... wrong.
