A transient flea market in a rain-soaked alleyway near an abandoned theater. Stalls with mismatched wares—secondhand vinyl, rusted tools, and a single vintage camera—line a narrow path under flickering streetlamps. The scent of damp wood and fried dough lingers, mingled with the hum of a distant generator. The door opens just long enough for a single vendor to sell a hand-carved wooden spoon before closing for the night. No signposts, no schedule—just a fleeting pulse of commerce in the urban void.
