A somber letter sat unopened on the desk, its inked words barely legible under the dim glow of the office lamp. *"I am sorry for the time I took from you. I didn’t realize how much you needed me."* The handwriting was hurried, the paper damp with the scent of coffee—someone had been working late. The envelope was addressed to an address that no longer existed.

The recipient’s name was scrawled in the margin: *"Mira."* Below it, a list of dates and tasks—*"You’d forgotten my birthday. The wedding invitations you sent were sent to the wrong address. The last time you called, you were distracted, your voice rough."* The postmark read *"Spring 2023"*—just over a year ago.

The envelope was returned to sender with a single stamp: *"Returned Unclaimed."* The stamp was smudged, as if someone had pressed it too hard.

The letter was folded neatly into thirds, the corners crisp, but the inside was damp with condensation. Someone had left it there, perhaps for a moment, then forgotten. Or perhaps they had meant to leave it for a while, to let the words settle like rain on pavement.

The door creaked open behind her. The scent of fresh-cut grass and old books drifted in, carrying the faintest trace of something else—something metallic, like old coins or rusted keys. She turned, her fingers tracing the edge of the envelope, and for a moment, she thought she saw her name again, written in a different hand. *"Mira, I know I’ve been absent. I didn’t mean to disappear."* But the words were gone when she looked down.

She pocketed the letter anyway. The envelope was empty, but the air inside felt like it had held something. A promise. A ghost. A chance.
