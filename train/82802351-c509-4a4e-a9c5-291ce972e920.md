A dimly lit room, walls lined with faded tapestries. The air hums with the quiet of a clock ticking, its chime muffled by the heavy drapes. Across from her, the stranger watches as she lifts a glass of water, pauses—then sets it down unceremoniously. No toast, no words. Just the clink of ceramic against wood.

She turns to him, her fingers tracing the edge of the worn leather chair. "I choose silence," she says, voice smooth, deliberate. "Not because I don’t speak, but because the words I keep are the ones that hurt most."

Outside, a train whistle fades into the distance, swallowed by the city’s night. The stranger exhales, slow. "You’re not alone in this," he murmurs. She doesn’t look at him. "Then why stay?"
