Magnets normally have 2 sides, a north and a south pole. They never have just one of the two poles, always coming in pairs. That means that Gauss's Law for magnetic fields just tells us that magnets work differently than how electricity works, so that is not very helpful for our understanding of how they actually work. Since Gauss's law is not very useful, we have another law called Amepere's law, that applies to magnets. This law is really cool because it allows us to understand how magnets make up a magnetic field. In order to do so, we should think about lines of currents. These lines of currents can extend to be infinitely long, or just form a circle. But, these lines of currents aren't like the single point charges we learned about with electricity, so in order to enclose them as we did with a Gaussian surface, we can enclose this super cool line of current with an imaginary fence called an "Ameperian circuit". This circuit encloses all of what we are wanting to analyze, so we can see how the current and magnets affect the magnetic field.
