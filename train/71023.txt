When working with clients who have a particular disease process, it is important to ensure that evidence-based standards are being followed in order to provide the best possible care. Evidence-based practice (EBP) uses research and current literature to support decision making for quality patient outcomes. With that in mind, there are certain standards and guidelines which should be taken into account when providing care for those dealing with a specific disease process.
When helping clients manage their condition, providing education on their diagnosis is an essential part of the care plan. It’s important that they understand what they can expect from treatments as well as any potential side effects or interactions they may experience. Providing resources such as educational materials, written instructions, videos, or even referring them to additional services such as nutritional counseling can all help in empowering the client to manage their health more effectively and safely.
It is also critical that providers have an understanding of the most up-to-date treatment protocols for the given disease process so appropriate recommendations can be made based on individual needs and preferences. This may include medication management or referral to specialty services if necessary. Additionally, periodic follow ups should be scheduled where providers assess progress towards goals set by both provider and client collaboratively and take any necessary steps towards altering treatment plans if needed.
Lastly, involving family or other supports systems within this process helps promote further engagement between patient and provider allowing for greater success when managing complex cases especially long term ones like chronic illnesses requiring ongoing management strategies . By engaging families in shared decision making processes we can create more comprehensive plans tailored specifically to each individual need while still adhering to evidence based standards of care established by healthcare professionals
