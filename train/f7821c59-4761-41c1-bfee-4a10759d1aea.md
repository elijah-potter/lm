A tiny brass key glints under fluorescent light as it’s handed from a nervous intern to a weary engineer at the corporate office’s glass-walled meeting room. The key’s smooth curve catches the reflection of the fluorescent tubes, but the intern’s fingers fumble slightly before slipping it into the engineer’s palm. The engineer’s fingers, calloused from years of assembling circuit boards, close around it instinctively—just as the intern’s phone buzzes with a notification: *"Team Meeting: 15 min."* The key is already half-lost in the engineer’s pocket, where it nestles between a crumpled receipt and a loose USB drive. The intern pockets their own keys, already half-running toward the elevators, the brass key’s weight a distant echo of their own hurried steps.
