The void hums with an absence of sound, a whisper of cold silence stretching endlessly. Walls of obsidian stretch into the horizon, their curvature impossible to follow, merging seamlessly into the abyss. Shadows, not from light, but from the absence of anything to cast them—an endless, featureless blackness—draped across the floor like a shroud.

The air, if there is air, is thin and cloying, thick with the weight of nothingness. Every corner, every slope, every curve feels like it goes on forever, the space so vast it bends perception itself—distorting the illusion of scale until the edges blur into the void. The light, if there is any, is a faint, distorted glow, a reflection of something unseen, flickering like a dying ember in an empty chamber. The floor beneath feels like it tilts, never quite level, as if the ground itself is a lie, a deception to make you forget where you stand. The ceiling, if it even exists, is a suggestion, a ghost of something that might be up, but never quite is. The space demands to be filled, yet nothing can. The void is hungry, and it is not yours to fill.
