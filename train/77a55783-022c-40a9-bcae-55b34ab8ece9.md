A distant thunderstorm rolled in as the team gathered in the dimly lit conference room. The map on the wall flickered with static, half-erased details of the last mission. Commander Veyra adjusted her glasses, her fingers tracing the edges of the abandoned briefing folder. "The sensor readings from Sector 7 are inconsistent," she said, her voice low and measured. "The last transmission was encrypted with an algorithm we don’t recognize—likely a distraction."

The air hummed with tension as the others exchanged glances. "Then we’re stuck," muttered Kael, rubbing the back of his neck. "No fuel, no supplies, and the patrol route is already compromised."

Lira’s eyes narrowed. "The comms are dead. The drones are down. The whole plan’s a charade." She tossed a crumpled schematic onto the table. "We’re supposed to ambush the convoy at the old bridge—"

"—but the bridge’s been sabotaged," Kael interrupted, pointing at the charred remains of a drone that had crashed into the wall. "The enemy has been here. They moved faster than we expected."

Veyra exhaled sharply. "Then we don’t ambush anything. We find a way to cut off their retreat." She leaned forward, her voice sharp. "We’ll scout the northern path—old tunnels, abandoned access points. If they’re moving north, we’ll catch them before they reach the next checkpoint."

The others muttered in agreement, but their enthusiasm was short-lived. Kael reached for his holster. "We don’t have enough men for a full assault. We’ll need reinforcements."

Lira’s jaw tightened. "Then we’re not going alone." She pulled out her wrist communicator. "Send a distress signal. The others will know we’re here."

The room fell silent. Outside, the storm raged, but inside, the weight of the decision pressed in like a physical force—unfinished, unresolved.
