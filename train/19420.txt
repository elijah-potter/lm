This page is dedicated to organizing various examples of standardized exam questions whose answer is Fabry disease. While this may seem a odd practice, it is useful to see multiple examples of how Fabry disease will be characterized on standardized exams (namely the boards and the shelf exams). This page is not meant to be used as a traditional question bank (as all of the answers will be the same), however seeing the classic “test” characterization for a topic is quite valuable.
KEY CHARACTERISTICS OF THIS CONDITION (ON EXAMS)
When it comes to standardized exams, each topic has its own “code” marked by key buzzwords, lab findings, clues, etc. If you are well versed in this code you will be able to more quickly identify the condition that is being discussed, and get the right answer on the exam you are taking. Below is the “code” for Fabry disease.
- Neuropathic pain
- Hypohidrosis: decreased sweating
- Skin findings: both angiokeratomas and telangiectasis develop on the skin.

TESTABLE FACTS ABOUT THIS TOPIC (BEYOND ITS IDENTIFICATION)
Many questions on standardized exams go beyond simply recognizing the underlying topic. Often there are specific testable facts regarding some aspect of the topic’s pathophysiology/management/clinical implications that are commonly asked. Some of these are listed below:
- Enzyme deficiency: alpha-galactosidase A deficiency (responsible for breaking down globotriasylceramide/Gb3)
- Consequence: Gb3 accumulates in various parts of the body (vascular smooth muscle cells, nephron tubule cells, cardiac myocytes, dorsal root ganglion) causing adverse effects.
- Inheritance pattern: X-linked recessive
