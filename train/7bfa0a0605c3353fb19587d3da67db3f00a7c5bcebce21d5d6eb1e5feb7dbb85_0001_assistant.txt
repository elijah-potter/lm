Facebook misled parents when they allowed children to use the Messenger Kids app. The app did not protect children's privacy, app developers still had access to private user data. The FTC announced it would prohibit Meta (Facebook's new company name) from profiting on user data from users under 18. Meta was allowed limited in its use of face-recognition technology and required to provide additional privacy protection to its user base. Samuel Levin, director of the FTC Bureau of Consumer Protection stated, " Facebook needs to answer to repeatedly violating its privacy promises, their recklessness cannot continue."
