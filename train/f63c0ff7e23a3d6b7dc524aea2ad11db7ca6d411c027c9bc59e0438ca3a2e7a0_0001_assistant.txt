The oldest nursery rhyme dates back to a French rhyme from the 1400s called "Sur le Pont d'Avigon" in English this translates to "On the Bridge of Avignon."  The rhyme (or song) describes a dance performed on the Pont d'Avignon.  Here is the English version of the song:

On the bridge of Avignon
We're all dancing, we're all dancing
On the bridge of Avignon
We all dance in circles.

The fine gentlemen go like this (bow)
And then again like this.

The beautiful ladies go like this (curtsy)
And then again like that.

The young girls go like this (salute)
And then like that.

The musicians go like this (they all bow to women)
And then like that.
