Alvin Ailey is best known for being an African American choreographer and activist who founded the Alvin Ailey American Dance Theater and the Ailey School in New York, NY. Ailey was a young student of the renowned choreographer and teacher Lester Horton. After training with Horton, Ailey decided to open his own dance company in 1958. He created ballets for other notable dance companies including the American Ballet Theater, the Paris Opera Ballet and the Royal Danish Ballet, among others. At the helm of the Alvin Ailey American Dance Theater (AAADT), Ailey concealed his sexuality but used his art as an outlet for it. He choreographed ballets for AAADT dancers that juxtaposed same-sex partnering with religious and sometime hypermasculine archetypes. Ailey succumbed to AIDS-related complications in 1989 at age 58. In 2014, President Barack Obama awarded Ailey with the Presidential Medal of Freedom in recognition of his contributions and commitments to civil rights and dance in the United States of America.
