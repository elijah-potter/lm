Kabuki is a traditional form of Japanese theatre known for its highly stylized performances, elaborate costumes and makeup, and dramatic narratives. It originated in the Edo period at the beginning of the 17th century and was developed by a woman named Izumo no Okuni. Kabuki plays often depict moral conflicts, love stories, or tales of historical events, and they are performed on elaborate stages with rotating platforms and trapdoors. Kabuki is a significant part of Japanese culture, as it has shaped Japan's drama, music, and dance, and it provides a rich reflection of traditional Japanese life and philosophy.
