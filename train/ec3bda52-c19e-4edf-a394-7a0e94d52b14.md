**The Last Light**

The city slept under a veil of frost, its neon signs casting hollow shadows on the pavement. Elias stood at the edge of the park, fingers tracing the cold glass of his empty cup. The book in his pocket had been his world for years—until tonight, when the pages dissolved into static, the words bleeding into the air like ink on rain.

A whisper slithered from the trees: *"You were never the author."* His breath fogged in the chill. The last line of his final manuscript flickered in his mind—*"The beginning was the end, and the end was always here."*

The wind howled, and the park’s shadows stretched, twisting into something that moved. The cup slipped from his grip. The ground was wet. Not snow. Water. The river below had begun to rise, swallowing the park whole.

Elias laughed, a sound like breaking glass. The last line of his book had been a lie. He had never written it. He had only read it aloud, to himself, to convince himself that he was someone else.

The river surged, carrying him away. The city’s lights pulsed brighter, brighter, until they became a single, throbbing heart. The air smelled of ozone and something older, something that had always been here, waiting.

And then—

The sky split open.

Not rain. Not stars. The beginning.

A voice, not his, not any voice he’d ever known, echoed from the void:

*"Now begin."*
