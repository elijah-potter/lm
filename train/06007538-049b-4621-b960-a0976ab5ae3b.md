In a dimly lit café, the hum of conversation fades as Elias leans back in his chair, fingers tracing idle lines on the table. The coffee cup sits untouched beside him, steam curling into the quiet. A woman at the next table pauses mid-sentence, her eyes flickering toward him. He doesn’t look up. Outside, the city’s cacophony of horns and voices swells like distant thunder.

A child’s laughter, muffled through the window, stops abruptly. The silence isn’t empty—it’s the weight of what hasn’t been said. Elias exhales slowly, letting the silence swallow the words before he could form them. The café’s low chatter dissolves into a hollow hum, the only sound the creak of chairs and the occasional cough. He closes his eyes.
