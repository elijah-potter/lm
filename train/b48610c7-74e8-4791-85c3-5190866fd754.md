A crumbling stone bridge spans a dried riverbed, its planks cracked and moss-covered arches leaning at precarious angles. The air hums with the ghostly echoes of a distant choir, their voices swallowed by the wind through the gnarled trees that stand like skeletal fingers along the cliffs. Behind the bridge, a half-built church looms—peeling frescoes, exposed scaffolding, and the faint scent of damp stone and old wood. The cobblestone street below is littered with discarded tools and half-finished bricks, while children’s laughter fades into the distance, as if they’ve been replaced by something unseen.

The place feels like a wound that never closes—every shadow holds a story of what was meant to be, and every rustle in the undergrowth could be the return of the long-departed. The sky, a bruised canvas of storm clouds, promises neither dawn nor dusk, only the slow, creeping weight of time.
