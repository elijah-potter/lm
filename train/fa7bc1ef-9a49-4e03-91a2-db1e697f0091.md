The **Necropolis Tavern**—a dimly lit, candle-lit alleyway in the old quarter of Veldros. The wooden floors creak underfoot; the air hums with the low murmur of patrons, their laughter swallowed by the scent of aged wood and spilled ale. The rules are whispered, not carved in stone.

**Do not ask for the menu.** The tastiest dishes are served by those who remember the last customer who *tried to haggle*. The bartender, a grizzled man named Orin, serves what’s left—never what’s advertised. Bargain, and you’ll leave with a half-eaten crust of bread and a debt that isn’t in money, but in favors.

**Sit at the long table.** The seats at the bar are for those who owe, or those who owe enough to demand. The center table is where the silent deals are made. A coin here, a look there, and suddenly, the tavern remembers the name of your next enemy—or your next ally.

**The door is a deal.** Knock once for a guest. Knock three for a guest who owes. The lock is always turning, but the hinges whisper. Leave a coin for the locksmith if you’re lucky. If you’re unlucky, leave a memory.

The walls are lined with the names of the dead and the forgotten. Names carved in blood or ink, then rubbed smooth by time. The next time you pass, check your shoulder for the scratch of a quill.

**Never ask why.** The rules are in the eye of the beholder. The tavern keeps score in whispers. The debt is written in the cracks of the floor. The next time you sit, the seat beneath you might hum.
