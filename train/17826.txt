Grout is the mortar between tiles. The purpose of grout is to keep water from getting behind tiles to damage the wall or floor, and to provide a pleasing decorative effect. It can get dirty, especially in high-use areas such as the bathroom or kitchen. When it does get dirty, it can start to break down, opening pathways for water that can cause further damage. Repairing and cleaning it is an important part of maintaining your home.
Remove loose or damaged grout with a grout saw. Be careful not to chip the tile. Aim the saw directly into the grout to avoid the tile. Use even and steady force as you push against the grout. Wear safety goggles.
Clean the remaining grout with grout cleaner or with a mixture of equal parts white vinegar and water.
Rinse with clean water. Absorb water that puddles in holes in the grout with paper towels or rags. The grout should be damp, however
Prepare new grout according to the manufacturer's directions. It should be a smooth, thick consistency, with no lumps. You can purchase premixed grout.
Spread the grout with a grout float. Fill all the joints thoroughly. Wear latex gloves. Do an initial smoothing with your fingers as you go.
Smooth the joint surfaces with a dowel that's the same size and the grout joints. This will be your final smoothing, so don't rush this step. Run the dowel along the grout line so its curve forms a gently trough in the grout.
Remove excess sponge with a damp sponge or a squeegee. You don't want to reintroduce a lot of water to the grout or wipe over tiles with a grout laden sponge, so rinse and wring out your sponge frequently.
Follow the grout manufacturer's recommendations for drying time. The grout needs to be completely dry prior to sealing.
Seal the grout with a sealant once it's dry. Allow the sealant to dry according to the manufacturer's directions.
Polish the tile with a dry towel to remove the cloudy haze that will have formed from the grout sealant.
Things You Will Need
- Grout sealant
- Grout saw
- White vinegar or grout cleaner
- Grout float
- Paper towels
- Safety goggles
- Latex gloves
- Examine any caulk along the edges of the tiled area. It may need to be removed and replaced as well.
- Grout is available in sanded, unsanded, acrylic latex or epoxy. If the width of the grout is more than 1/8 inch, uses sanded grout, unless the tile is made of marble. Sanded grout is stronger and is better suited for wide grout lines. It also has more texture. If the width is less than 1/8 inch or the tile is marble, you can use unsanded, acrylic latex or epoxy grout.
