## Explain Like I'm Five: Why the Sky is Blue

Okay, imagine you have a big box of crayons. You’ve got red, yellow, blue, green, purple, and lots of others! Now, imagine the sun is like a giant crayon box, but instead of colors, it sends out light. This light isn’t just *one* color, it's actually all the colors mixed together! It's like when you mix all your crayons together – you get a muddy brown, but the sunlight is much prettier than that.

Now, the air around us, the stuff we breathe, is made of tiny, tiny bits called air molecules. They’re so small you can’t even see them! When the sunlight comes to Earth, it bumps into these tiny bits of air.

Here's the important part: Blue light is a bit bouncy and energetic. When it bumps into those tiny bits of air, it bounces *all over* the place. It doesn’t just go straight through. Red and yellow light are a little more serious; they mostly go straight. 

Think of it like this: imagine you’re bouncing a small, bouncy ball (blue light) versus rolling a big, heavy ball (red light). The bouncy ball will bounce everywhere, right? The heavy ball will just roll in a line.

Because blue light bounces everywhere, it spreads out across the whole sky. That's why when we look up, we see blue! It’s blue light that’s been bounced all around. 

Sometimes, when the sun is setting, the light has to travel through *lots* more air to reach us. All that extra air makes the blue light bounce away even more, and then we start to see the red and yellow light that were there all along, making the sky look orange and red. 

So, the sky is blue because blue light is bouncy and loves to bounce around! It’s like a big game of tag with the air.
