The morning mist curled over the cracked asphalt, each breath swallowed by the stale hum of distant traffic. I stood at the kitchen counter, my coffee cup half-empty, the steam rising like a ghost from the past I’d tried to forget. The oven timer beeped—again. I glanced at the recipe: a dish my grandmother used to make, something with roots in my mother’s childhood, but the memory of *why* it was important had long since withered.

The child’s laughter echoed in my mind, sharp and bright, but not today. Today, I only remembered the warmth of her hands pressing into the dough, the way her eyes lit up when I told her stories about the old house on the hill. The stories that had once been mine, but now were just fragments, like the bread I was kneading—crumbling in my grip.

Outside, the wind picked up, rattling the windows. I’d left the front door unlocked, a habit I’d picked up years ago, not out of carelessness, but because the house felt safer that way. Some things, I decided, didn’t need fixing.
