**The Last Light**

The city slept under a bruised sky, its neon veins milky with twilight. From the rooftop, I watched the clock hands bleed into each other, their slow dance a promise of dawn’s betrayal. The river below whispered secrets to the bricks, its surface a mirror fractured by the weight of forgotten dreams.

I stood where the wind carried the scent of wet asphalt and something older—earth, perhaps, or the breath of the earth itself. My fingers traced the edge of the cracked pavement, where the last streetlight had winked out, its bulb a dying ember in the dark. The city’s pulse had faltered; now it rested.

A shadow moved. Not mine. The figure approached, cloaked in the same gray that swallowed the streetlights. It carried a camera—no lens, just a blank frame—and the weight of it made my bones ache.

*"You remember this place?"* the voice was rough, like gravel under boots.

I didn’t answer. The camera clicked once.
