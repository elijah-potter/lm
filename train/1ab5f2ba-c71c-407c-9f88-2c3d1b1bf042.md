A routine office meeting had been dragging on for nearly an hour when Mark noticed the faint scent of burnt coffee lingering near the ceiling fan. The air conditioner had been running for days, yet the stale, charred aroma clung to the air like a second skin. He followed it—slowly, almost reluctantly—until he stood in the empty supply closet, where a single, half-burnt coffee mug sat atop a crumbling cardboard box of stale pastries. The fire had started in the microwave, the heat from the machine’s internal resistance searing the plastic and paper before the fire alarm even buzzed. The detail was small, a whisper in the chaos of his day, but it was the first sign that the building’s systems were failing. He grabbed his coat and left without saying a word, the smell of smoke now the only thing that made sense.
