A mist-laden valley at dusk, the air thick with the scent of damp earth and pine. Towering ancient oaks cast long, ebbing shadows across moss-covered stones, their branches tangled like sleeping serpents. Between them, a frozen lake reflects the last golden streaks of the setting sun, its surface cracked by unseen ice. A low, unnatural silence hums—no birds, no wind, only the distant, faint *click* of something shifting in the underbrush. The ground itself feels too still, as if holding its breath, waiting.
