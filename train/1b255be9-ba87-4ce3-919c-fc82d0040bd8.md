A fleeting breeze through autumn leaves swirling past the window, their golden hues clashing with the red of the sunset painting the sky. The warmth of a forgotten campfire’s embers lingering on the wooden bench where you sat, the scent of pine and woodsmoke still clinging to the air. A single candle flickered in the glass jar on the coffee table, its light barely reaching the edge of the horizon where the mountains stood silhouetted against the fading light. The phone buzzed—a missed call from someone who meant more than they knew, their voice echoing in your mind like a ghost. The memory of laughter, of hands tangled in yours, of stolen kisses pressed against the warmth of a stranger’s shoulder, all trapped in this single, irreplaceable moment. The camera’s shutter clicks, capturing the moment before it fades into silence. The weight of it: time, lost and gone, cannot be called back.
