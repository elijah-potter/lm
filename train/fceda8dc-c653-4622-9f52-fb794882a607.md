**The Last Light**

The door creaked open on a world that had stopped breathing. Outside, the city’s pulse was a hollow drum, the neon glow of a thousand dead stars flickering against the skeletal bones of the skyline. Inside, the air smelled of old paper and something metallic—like blood that had long since dried. The protagonist sat at the desk, fingers tracing the rim of an empty coffee cup, the warmth of the morning sun long since gone.

“You’re late,” a voice said from the hallway, smooth as oil on water. A woman stepped into the dim light, her coat too sharp for this decaying place. She carried a briefcase, its contents hidden beneath a folded newspaper. “I thought you’d never show.”

The protagonist exhaled. “I didn’t say I was coming.”

She smirked. “No, you didn’t.” The door closed behind her, sealing them in silence. The desk was a stage, the city a ghost story, and this—this was the beginning of the end.

---

The coffee cup was cold. The city hummed in the background, a sound that had become familiar. The protagonist stirred a cup of something black and bitter, the taste sharp enough to wake the dead. Outside, the first light of dawn crept over the rooftops, painting the streets in gold.

A phone buzzed. The name on the screen was unknown. She answered it.

“You’re awake.”

The line was silent. Then, a voice—familiar, distant—whispered, *“It’s time.”*

The screen flickered. The city stood at the edge of another dawn, and in its center, the door opened again.
