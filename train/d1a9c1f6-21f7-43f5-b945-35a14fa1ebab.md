The first draft ended with a knife in the dark, the blood pooling on the kitchen floor like a silent accusation. My hands trembled as I wiped them on the apron, the fabric damp with sweat and something sharper. Outside, the rain hammered against the window like a metronome counting down to the end of this story.

But then I thought—what if the knife was never meant to be there? What if the girl had just wanted to talk, to share the secret she’d been carrying since she was ten? What if the world was just a stage, and we were all just actors, playing out roles we didn’t fully understand?

I erased the last line, replaced it with a whisper on the page: *"She told me her name was Elara, not Eve."* The ink blurred under my finger, undoing the violence of the moment.

Now, the scene is softer. The rain still falls, but the knife lies untouched on the counter. The girl’s voice is softer too, her hands trembling as she reaches for mine. *"You don’t have to stay,"* she says. *"We can go somewhere else."* My breath catches—not because I’m afraid, but because I’m remembering how easy it was to turn away.

The ending changes again. This time, she smiles, and I realize I don’t want to be the one who leaves. The story isn’t about blood or knives after all. It’s about the choice to stay, even when the world tells you to go.
