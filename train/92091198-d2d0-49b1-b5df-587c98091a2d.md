The clock tower’s pendulum swung in slow motion, its second hand creeping across the dial like a thief in the night. The street below was a still life—every step, every breath, each breath of air was stretched into something deliberate, each sound warped into something familiar yet alien.

A child’s laughter echoed, but the melody dissolved into static before reaching her ears. The mother, holding her hand, watched the world blur between her fingers, the edges of everything—faces, shadows, the rustle of leaves—filling the gaps like a dream dissolving into memory.

The wind carried the scent of damp earth and something metallic, like the first drop of rain after a summer’s drought. Time had stopped. Or perhaps it was just her heart that had slowed, beating in time with the universe’s pause. The air itself seemed to thicken, every molecule suspended in a moment that refused to end.
