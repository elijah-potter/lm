**The Library’s Last Book**

I had come for the dust motes drifting like stars in the corner of the room, for the scent of aged paper that clung to the spine of every book here, thicker than the ink on the pages. I thought I wanted to know what happened next—how the characters in these forgotten tomes would unfold their fates, how the threads of their stories would weave into something greater than themselves.

But now I’m here, fingers tracing the binding of *The Hollow Years*, its cover worn smooth by generations of readers who never quite understood its warning.

The narrator had started as a skeptic, a voice that scoffed at fate, at the quiet persistence of things that didn’t make sense. She had written about how the world was a series of accidents, how every decision was just a loop of chance, and how no one—no one—could predict what would happen next. She had believed that.

But now she’s reading the book, and the words are bleeding into her own thoughts like ink in water. She remembers the night she first saw the clock in her mother’s study, its hands frozen at 3:17, the number of the year she would turn twenty. She remembers the way her mother’s voice had shaken when she said, *"You’ll understand one day."* She remembers the way her own hands had trembled as she wrote the first draft of this story, as if her fingers were trying to undo something that had already been done.

The narrator had wanted to be the one to change the story. She had wanted to rewrite it, to erase the parts that made her breath catch, to make it all make sense. But now she’s sitting here, staring at the page where she had first written, *"I thought I knew what I was doing. I was wrong."* The words are still there, but they don’t feel like hers anymore.

Outside, the rain taps against the window like a hundred voices whispering the same secret. The narrator reaches for the book again, but this time, she doesn’t pull away. Instead, she turns the page slowly, her thumb tracing the edge of the text where she had hesitated, where she had almost crossed out her own doubt.

She will not rewrite the story. She will not erase the past. She will not pretend that she knew what she was doing.

The world is not a series of accidents. It is a story, and sometimes, the story changes the one who tells it.

She closes the book with a sigh, the spine creaking like an old door opening. The rain continues, but now she hears it as the sound of a thousand hands turning pages, of voices murmuring the same forgotten words. She is not the first to wonder. She is not the last. And maybe, just maybe, that is the only truth she needs to know.
