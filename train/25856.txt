'First Australian WWI doco' goes online
Rare footage of Australian soldiers at the 1916 Battle of Pozieres, filmed for what is believed to have been Australia's earliest attempt at a war documentary, has been made available online.
The Western Front battle saw 23,000 Australian soldiers killed or wounded over a six-week period in July and August 1916.
The footage, which runs for more than 10 minutes, has been published in collaboration with the Australian War Memorial.
In the video, Australian troops are shown building trenches and preparing for battle in Pozieres.
The footage shows the French village had been laid to waste during the two-week battle.
The footage also shows British howitzers shelling German positions and Australian field guns joining the bombardment.
Paul Byrnes, a journalist and curator at the National Film and Sound Archive, says the film was the first attempt to make an Australian war documentary.
"[It was] a very new idea at the time. These World War I films depict the birth of war being covered by moving image. It changed warfare forever," he said.
The original footage, which is shown online in three clips, was filmed under the direction of famous Australian war correspondent Charles Bean.
In 1917 in London, Bean cut the film and wrote titles painstakingly explaining the images for Australians, but later discovered his version had been lost.
The footage was never found and Bean tried to reassemble it after the war was finished.
The second version was assembled from original footage by London film businessman Sir William Jury.
Bean described the second version as "2,000 feet of miscellaneous rubbish".
The Australian War Memorial holds two versions - the version created by Jury and another that appears to be the one reassembled by Bean after the war.
