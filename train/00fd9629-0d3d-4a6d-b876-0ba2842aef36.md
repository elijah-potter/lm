In the hollowed-out husk of the old mill, where the river whispered secrets to the stones, I found the key.

It wasn’t polished or gleaming—just a small, rusted thing, half-buried in the crevices of the wooden door. The key fit the lock of my own heart, though I had no idea how. The mill’s ghostly hands turned the dial, and suddenly, the dark inside let me in.

Outside, the world was still. But inside, the lights flickered to life, casting long shadows that danced with forgotten things. A child’s laughter echoed from somewhere deep, and the scent of old paper and damp wool clung to the air.

I reached out, and the key slipped from my fingers. It rolled away, lost in the labyrinth of shadows, just like everything else I’d ever held on to.
