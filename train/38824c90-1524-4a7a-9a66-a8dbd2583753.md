A dimly lit bookshop where the last light flickers from a single candle atop a half-empty shelf of leather-bound tomes. The air hums with the faint scent of aged paper and old coffee, thick enough to cling to the back of your throat. Books spill from open spines, some face-down, as if waiting to be picked up again. A lone chair, upholstered in faded velvet, sits half-collapsed in a corner. A single teacup sits on a saucer, unbroken, beside it. Outside, a distant train whistle lingers, as though caught in the moment of passing. The walls are lined with shelves that hold more than just books—things that have stopped moving, preserved in time. The moment feels like a pause between two things that haven’t yet begun.
