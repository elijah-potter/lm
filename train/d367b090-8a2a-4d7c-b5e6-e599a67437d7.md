The dim glow of a flickering laptop screen illuminated the cluttered desk. A half-empty mug of cold coffee sat beside it, steam rising in lazy curls. Hands moved restlessly over the keyboard, fingers tracing keys in a pattern that didn’t make sense—too fast, too slow. The search bar lit up with a single, insistent term: **"the way home."**

A notification buzzed from the phone on the floor, a forgotten reminder: *"Your grandma’s birthday is in 4 days."* The phone was ignored, buried under a stack of unread emails and a half-finished spreadsheet. The screen flickered, then stabilized on a list of entries—each a name, a location, a *place*—but none felt right. The last entry, from five months ago, still stood out: **"The old mill by the river. She said it was where the river remembered."**

A chair scraped against the floor as someone stood up, the weight of their own exhaustion pressing down. The door clicked open, but the hallway beyond was silent, too quiet, like the world itself had forgotten to breathe. Outside, the wind howled through the trees, carrying the scent of damp earth and something older, something *lost*. The search bar on the laptop glowed again, this time with a new query: **"last seen"**—but the results were a blur of names, faces, and dates, none of them matching the way home.
