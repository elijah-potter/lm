The rain pattered against the windows of the dimly lit pub, a steady drumbeat echoing through the wooden floors. Elias sat hunched over his pint, the golden liquid swirling around in the bottom of his glass like a restless animal.

A woman with a voice like aged whiskey entered, her boots damp with the same rain that had been falling for hours. She slid onto the stool beside him, her gaze flickering between the flickering candle and the faces of the other patrons.

"Yer lookin’ like yer thinkin’ about somethin’," she said, her voice smooth but carrying the weight of years.

"Just lost in a story," Elias replied, though neither of them believed it.

She leaned forward, her fingers brushing the rim of his glass. "Ain’t stories just lies, then?"

Elias exhaled sharply. "No. They’re the only things that make sense."

She tilted her head. "Then tell me one."

Elias began:

---

Once upon a time, in a town where the river ran black with secrets and the people spoke in riddles, there lived a boy named Kael. His father was a weaver of dreams, spinning tapestries so intricate they told stories before they were written. The town’s bards whispered that if you listened closely, you could hear the threads singing.

One evening, Kael found an old, leather-bound book beneath his father’s loom. The pages were yellowed, the ink faded, but the words still seemed to pulse with energy. Inside were tales of a world beyond the river, where the sky was a storm of endless colors and the trees bore fruit that tasted of memories.

Kael opened it at random. The first sentence he read was: *"The child who eats the fruit of the moon will see the world as it truly is."*

He didn’t believe it. But when he did, he took the book to the edge of the river and tossed it into the water. The moment it disappeared, the world around him shifted.

The townsfolk began speaking in riddles, their faces growing shadowed, their voices deeper. The river, which had once been dark, now pulsed with light. And then—

A girl with eyes like polished river stones appeared at the edge of the water. She was dressed in a cloak woven from starlight. "You’ve done this before," she said, her voice like wind through leaves.

Kael didn’t understand. "Before? What do you mean?"

She reached into her cloak and pulled out a single apple, its skin shimmering. "This fruit changes hands. Each time someone eats it, the world bends to their will—just for a moment. But every time, something else is lost."

Kael hesitated. The apple glowed brighter. "What do I lose?"

She smiled sadly. "Your fear."

Kael took the apple. The moment he bit into it, the world around him warped. The town’s bards sang in perfect harmony, their voices weaving into a single, haunting melody. The river’s light flickered, and then—

Everything was still.

The girl vanished. The apple shrank to nothing. Kael found himself back in the weaver’s shop, his hands trembling, the book still in his pocket.

That night, he sat by the river, staring at the black water. The townsfolk were silent. Their faces were hollow. And in the distance, he could hear the faint sound of singing—his father’s voice, weaving something new, something *wrong*.

---

Elias finished, his voice raw. "That’s what happens when you take something that isn’t yours."

The woman’s fingers tightened around his wrist. "And what’s yours?"

Elias shrugged. "The story."
