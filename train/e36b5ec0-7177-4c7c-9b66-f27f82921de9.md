In the dimly lit warehouse, fluorescent lights flickered against the rusted steel beams. A crew of four—two inspectors, one maintenance worker, and a foreman—stood around a half-finished assembly line. The foreman, arms crossed, eyed the partially installed piping with a mix of frustration and resignation. *"Rule says it must be pressure-tested at 25 psi before final approval,"* he muttered, but his gaze lingered on the cracked gauge.

The maintenance worker, wiping grease from his hands, adjusted the dial on the test rig. *"Just 22 psi, boss. The manual says 20 psi is acceptable for safety margins."* The foreman exhaled, glancing at the inspectors, who exchanged a glance. Neither said anything.

A silence stretched until the inspector closest to the gauge hesitated, then nodded. *"Fine. But if it fails—"* The foreman cut him off. *"We’ll call it a margin call."* The worker turned the valve, and the gauge crept upward, steady and deliberate. The foreman watched, satisfied. The inspectors remained silent.

The foreman pocketed his keys. *"Line’s ready for the next phase."* The worker nodded, already moving toward the next section. The inspectors followed, but their eyes were on the gauge—just slightly under the line. No official violation. Just a bend.
