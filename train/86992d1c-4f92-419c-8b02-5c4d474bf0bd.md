A fading dawn at an abandoned temple, its stone carvings cracked by time. The priest, gaunt and hollow-eyed, kneels on a cracked altar while a single candle flickers in the dust.

*"The words are bones now—spoken to a sky that no longer listens."* He whispers the incantation, the syllables dissolving into the wind, their magic long since drained. The incense burns black, not fragrant, and the water in the basin turns to rusted iron.

The offering—once a living sacrifice—is now a pile of rusted keys, each with a name scratched into them: *John, Maria, the boy who forgot his own name.* The ritual stumbles, a clumsy reenactment of what was once sacred. The bell tolls once, too softly, and the sound echoes through the ruins like a final goodbye.

The crowd, if there is one, is silent. The priest stands, his shadow stretching unnaturally long, and says: *"The world has moved on. The gods are gone. So is the meaning."* He vanishes into the smoke, leaving behind only the hollow echo of a prayer that no longer answers.
