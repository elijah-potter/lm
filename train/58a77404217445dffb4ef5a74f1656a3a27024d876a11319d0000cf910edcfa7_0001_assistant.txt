Like a Dragon’s Egg
I guard my poems like treasures,
jealously, counting each stone,
each jewel-like
flimsy paper.

Hold them right up to your eye
and you can see
little slivers of my soul

naked
exposed
defenseless,

twinkling in the light
delicate as glass.
Be careful how you breathe:
the heat crystallizes patterns of my fury and
leaves little dents in my copy paper.

Are eyes the windows to the soul?
For me, the long sheets
are windowpanes.
What do you see reflected?

Flashes of the sky and sea.
Different poems, more of me.
So I count my precious poems zealously,
my stones and jewels,
a dragon and her eggs.
