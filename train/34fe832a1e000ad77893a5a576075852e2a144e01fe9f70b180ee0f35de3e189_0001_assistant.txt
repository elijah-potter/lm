Then came our dinner at the Lakeview restaurant. The Lakeview was an extravagant establishment, known for its refined atmosphere and exquisite cuisine. As flies buzzing around the dining room, we couldn't help but marvel at the grandeur of the place. The headwaiter, dressed in tails, gracefully guided us to our designated spot. Soft candlelight and the delicate melodies of a harpist filled the air, creating an ambiance of sophistication.

Our presence in the restaurant was motivated by the recent success of our fly family. Our father had just been promoted in the world of insect business. He had excelled in his role, and his superiors recognized his talents, leading to a prestigious promotion and increased status among our fellow flies.

Usually, our fly family frequented garbage bins and feasted on discarded leftovers. But this was a special occasion, and our father wanted to celebrate in style. He asked his fellow flies for recommendations on an elegant restaurant, and the Lakeview was unanimously suggested. So there we were, entering the Lakeview, attempting to navigate the vast dining room with our tiny wings.

We settled down at our table, and the oversized menus were placed before us. Flipping through the elaborate pages, we realized that most of the descriptions were in a language foreign to us. Our fly parents, ever resourceful, had brought along a pocket-sized dictionary. They huddled over it, buzzing with excitement as they attempted to decipher the French terms.

"Paté en croute," our father buzzed, consulting the dictionary. "Paté is paste... croute is crust... Ah, a paste in crust!"

In the end, father surrendered to the complexities of the menu. "Why don't we simply choose four random dishes?" he proposed.

Mother, ever the cautious one, expressed her concerns. "Are you sure that's wise? I've heard that flies have peculiar dietary preferences in different regions."

But father was determined. "A fly can feast on anything, regardless of its origin," he confidently proclaimed.

Soon enough, bowls of soup were placed before us. As flies, we were adept at sipping liquid food, but these shallow plates presented a new challenge. We buzzed around, observing the techniques of the other diners. The dim lighting made it difficult to discern their actions, but we managed to learn a trick or two.

Our parents, having researched Western etiquette, guided us. "Tilt your plate," they whispered, their wings fluttering with excitement. "It makes the soup more accessible."

And so, we tilted our plates, dipping our tiny fly mouths into the liquid sustenance. The slurping sounds filled the air, echoing like miniature gusts of wind.

A brief pause in the harpist's melodies allowed our symphony of slurps to resonate throughout the restaurant. It was as if the buzzing of flies had taken center stage, captivating the attention of all present. The nearby diners, including humans, stared in awe and disbelief, their eyes widening at the peculiar sight and sound.

Amidst the commotion, one unfortunate fly accidentally bumped into a waiter pouring wine at a nearby table. The bottle tipped over, spilling crimson liquid onto the tablecloth and dripping onto an unsuspecting diner's lap. The mishap went unnoticed for a moment, as all eyes were fixed upon our fly family's performance.

Unable to bear the intensity of the attention, I fluttered away discreetly, seeking solace in the nearby restroom. Splashing cold water on my tiny face, I reflected upon my peculiar situation. Here I was, a fly in a lavishly decorated restroom, surrounded by pink and silver wallpaper, marbled sinks, and a sense of otherworldly elegance. I couldn't help but question our place in this human-centric world, pondering the meaning of our presence in the Lakeview restaurant.

The door swung open, and my mother flew in, her compound eyes scanning the room. "Are you in there? Are you unwell?" she buzzed with genuine concern.

Realizing that I couldn't abandon our family just because of a peculiar dining experience, I replied, undoing the latch, "I'm alright."

Mother didn't delve into the details of the rest of the dinner, and I didn't press for them. In the following weeks, I managed to push the memory of that surreal evening to the depths of my mind, only to have it resurface a few times each day. Even now, as I buzz around, my tiny fly body shiver with embarrassment at the recollection of the Lakeview restaurant.
