The air in the dimly lit attic was thick with silence, the kind that hums with unspoken words. Old books lay abandoned on the dust-covered floor, their spines cracked like dried leaves. Outside, the city slept, unaware of the unspoken conversation that had settled between Elias and Mara.

Elias traced a finger along the edge of a half-finished sketchbook, its pages filled with notes and doodles that spoke in code. Mara stood near the window, her back to him, watching the rain blur the street below. She didn’t need to speak to know the questions burning in his eyes.

*"Why the code?"* he mouthed, though she didn’t hear it. The wind carried the scent of wet pavement and something sharper—old ink, maybe.

Mara turned, her expression unreadable. The sketchbook was open to a page where a single line of text stood out: *"The language was never meant to be spoken."* The words were barely legible, but the way Elias read them—slow, deliberate—made her know. He understood.

They both knew the unspoken truth: the city had its own rules, and sometimes, the right words weren’t needed. Just the right *silence*.
