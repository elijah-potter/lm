The library was quiet except for the hum of old fluorescent tubes casting long shadows across the worn carpet. A book fell from my lap, landing with a soft *clack* near the edge of my chair. I reached for it, fingers brushing the spine of a dog-eared copy of *The Metamorphosis*—Kafka’s ghost had already begun to haunt this space.

Outside, the city’s breath had grown still. The usual rush of late-night pedestrians had thinned to a trickle, their laughter swallowed by the damp autumn air. I leaned back in my chair, tracing the rim of my coffee cup with the tip of my tongue—too bitter, too sweet, a lie like everything else. The page beneath my fingers was yellowed, the ink smudged where I had cried here long ago.

A window cracked open, letting in a draft that carried the scent of rain and something older, something like the earth itself. The rain began to fall—not in torrents, but in slow, deliberate drops, as if nature itself was learning to breathe again.

I exhaled slowly, watching the steam from my cup curl into the air. The weight of the book felt lighter now, the weight of the world too. The city outside pulsed with quiet life, the streets alive with the slow rhythm of returning. My hands trembled not with fear, but with the quiet certainty that some wounds, when tended with patience, do not stay open forever. The page beneath me, once jagged with unspoken pain, now lay flat, the ink smooth as the calm before the storm.
