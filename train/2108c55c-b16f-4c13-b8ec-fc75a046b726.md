## Explain Like I'm Five: Why the Sky is Blue

Okay, imagine you have a big box of crayons. Lots and lots of colors! Red, yellow, blue, green, purple – all of them! Now, imagine those crayons are like sunlight. Sunlight looks white, right? But it's actually made up of *all* those colors mixed together!

Now, imagine you're playing with a bouncy ball. When you bounce it, sometimes it hits a little bump and bounces off in a different direction, right?  Sunlight does something similar when it comes to Earth.

The air around Earth is full of tiny, tiny bits – smaller than even a grain of sand!  These bits are like those little bumps for the sunlight. 

Now, blue light is like a really bouncy ball. It bounces off those tiny bits in the air *much* more than other colors like red or yellow.  Red and yellow are more like heavier balls that mostly go straight through.

So, when sunlight comes to Earth, the blue light bounces *everywhere*! It bounces off those tiny bits in the air and spreads out all around. That’s why when you look up, you see blue – because that’s the color that’s bouncing around the most!

Sometimes, when the sun is setting, you see red and orange skies. That's because the sunlight has to travel through *much* more air to reach you. All the blue light has already bounced away, leaving the red and orange light to reach your eyes.  It’s like the blue light bounced so far it's gone!



So, the sky is blue because blue light is bouncy and likes to bounce off all the tiny bits in the air!
