The old train station’s platform, half-lit by flickering fluorescent signs, hums with the sound of distant trains and the weight of abandoned luggage. The air smells of damp wood and diesel fumes from the last departing car. A cracked floor tile beneath a rusted bench glows faintly under a streetlamp’s weak beam, casting shadows that shift like fingers. A single, half-open door at the far end, its glass stained with time, whispers secrets to passersby who linger too long. The moment before the train arrives—or the moment after it departs—is where everything feels suspended, a liminal space between arrival and departure, past and future, memory and forgetfulness.
