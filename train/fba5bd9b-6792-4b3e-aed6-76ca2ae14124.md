## TIL the "Smell of Rain" is Actually Multiple Scents

Today I learned that what many people describe as the distinctive "smell of rain" isn't a single aroma, but a complex combination of several scents triggered by rainfall. It’s a phenomenon known as *petrichor*, a word derived from the Greek words *petra* (stone) and *ichor* (the ethereal fluid that flows in the veins of the gods in Greek mythology).

The primary contributor to this scent is geosmin, an organic compound produced by bacteria, particularly *Streptomyces*, that live in the soil. These bacteria release geosmin, which accumulates on dry surfaces. When rain falls, the impact dislodges the geosmin particles, which are then carried into the air. 

Interestingly, a second scent, often described as earthy or musty, is also released. This is due to the opening of pores in plants and the release of oils that have built up on their surfaces. The combination of geosmin and these plant oils creates the unique and evocative fragrance many associate with rainfall.

The term *petrichor* was only coined in 1964 by Australian scientists Isabel Joy Bear and Richard Thomas Guthrie, who were investigating the unusual odor associated with rain after a prolonged dry period. It's a fascinating example of how our perception of a simple sensory experience—the smell of rain—is actually a complex interplay of biological processes.
