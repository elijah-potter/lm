A dimly lit room. The air hums with quiet tension. On the table, a half-empty glass of whiskey sits untouched, its golden liquid reflecting the soft glow of a single bulb. Across from him, she watches, her fingers tracing idle patterns on the armrest of her chair.

*"You’re not here to talk."* The words hang between them, unspoken. He doesn’t look away from the floor, but the way his shoulders tense suggests he’s listening to the unspoken.

The door creaks open. A child’s laughter, muffled, drifts in—too loud for the silence that follows. They both freeze. The child, clutching a stuffed animal, stands in the doorway, eyes wide. The laughter stops.

*"Mommy, Daddy…"* The child’s voice is small, uncertain. His father turns slowly, the chair creaking. His mother’s hand flies to her mouth.

They understand. Not with words, but in the way a heartbeat can tell you something before it’s said. The child’s fear isn’t for the strangers in the room—it’s for the silence that comes after.
