## A Counseling Session

**Setting:** A comfortably furnished therapy office. Soft lighting, calming artwork.

**Characters:**

* **Dr. Eleanor Vance:** A seasoned therapist, known for her calm demeanor and insightful approach.
* **Liam Davies:** A young professional, experiencing significant stress related to his career.

**(The session begins with Liam seated on a comfortable armchair, facing Dr. Vance, who sits opposite him in a similar chair.)**

**Liam:** It feels… overwhelming, honestly. I’m constantly chasing deadlines, responding to emails, and just generally feeling like I’m failing to keep up. It’s not just the workload; it’s the feeling that I *should* be able to handle it all, you know? That I’m letting people down.

**Dr. Vance:** I understand. It sounds like you’re carrying a heavy burden, Liam. The feeling of responsibility, coupled with the pressure you place on yourself, can certainly be incredibly taxing. Can you tell me more about this feeling of letting people down? Who are you concerned about disappointing?

**Liam:** Mostly my supervisor, Mr. Harding. He’s very driven, very successful. I want to impress him, to show him I’m capable. But I’m making mistakes, small ones mostly, but they feel monumental. I worry he's losing faith in me.

**Dr. Vance:** So, you're interpreting his expectations as incredibly high, and your mistakes are perceived as evidence of not meeting those expectations. It's natural to want to please someone in a position of authority, and it’s admirable that you strive for excellence. However, it sounds like that desire might be contributing to your current stress. Let’s explore that a little further. What do you *know* about Mr. Harding’s expectations? Are they explicitly stated, or are you inferring them?

**Liam:** It’s mostly inferred, I think. He doesn’t explicitly say, “You need to do this perfectly.” It’s more… the way he approaches everything, the high standards he sets for himself and the team. I’m picking up on that and internalizing it.

**Dr. Vance:** That’s a very astute observation, Liam. It’s common for people to absorb the unspoken expectations of those around them. It’s a form of social mirroring. However, it’s important to remember that those expectations are *his*, and they don’t necessarily reflect your own capabilities or worth. It sounds like you’re taking on his standards as your own.  How does that feel, carrying someone else’s expectations so heavily?

**Liam:** It feels… suffocating. Like I can’t breathe. I’m constantly on edge, worrying about making another mistake. I find myself checking and rechecking everything, which just adds to the workload and the anxiety.

**Dr. Vance:** That makes perfect sense.  The constant vigilance is exhausting. It’s a protective mechanism, a way to avoid potential criticism, but it’s also preventing you from enjoying your work and feeling confident in your abilities. Let’s consider this: what would it look like to separate your own expectations from Mr. Harding's? What small, manageable steps could you take to begin that process?

**Liam:** I… I don't know. It feels almost disrespectful, not trying to meet his standards.

**Dr. Vance:** Disrespect isn't necessarily the outcome. It’s about recognizing your own limitations and establishing healthy boundaries. It’s about acknowledging that you are a human being, not a machine. Perhaps a starting point could be identifying one specific task or project where you feel the pressure most intensely. Then, instead of striving for perfection, aim for *completion* – a good, solid effort.  We can explore strategies for managing your response to potential feedback, too. How does that feel as a starting point?

**Liam:** It feels… less daunting, actually. Focusing on completion rather than perfection feels achievable. And knowing I can talk about how I'm responding to feedback is reassuring.

**Dr. Vance:** Excellent.  It’s about shifting your focus, Liam, and reclaiming your own sense of agency. We’ll continue to unpack these feelings and develop practical strategies for managing your stress.  For this week, I’d like you to try focusing on that one task, aiming for completion, and paying attention to the internal dialogue that arises when you feel that pressure to be perfect.  We can discuss your observations next session.



**(The session concludes with both Dr. Vance and Liam appearing thoughtful and engaged.)**
