The hum of distant traffic blends seamlessly with the rhythmic drip of rain on the old wooden windowpanes, a sound that feels like a slow, steady heartbeat for this neighborhood. Outside, the streetlamps flicker like dying stars, casting long shadows that stretch into the alleyways like forgotten stories. Inside, the scent of aged paper and faint coffee lingers—proof that this room, though empty, still holds the weight of quiet conversations, half-finished books, and the ghostly echoes of laughter that never quite escaped.

The walls are lined with half-erected shelves, their gaps whispering secrets of what was once placed there but now lingers only as dust. The floor, once polished to a mirror shine, bears the marks of many feet, each one a story of its own. Here, time does not pass—it lingers, thick and heavy, like the morning fog clinging to the windowsill.

A single candle burns low in the center of the table, its flame flickering against the gloom like a lantern in a storm. It is the only light in this room, the only thing that refuses to be extinguished. Outside, the city sleeps, but not before leaving behind a trail of forgotten dreams and whispered wishes—some resolved, some still unspoken. The candle’s glow is the only thing that remains constant, a stubborn ember against the darkness of what was and what might have been.
