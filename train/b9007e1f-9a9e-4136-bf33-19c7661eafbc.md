A hollowed-out oath that flickers like a candle in a hurricane—each word a fragile, half-believed whisper. The sky outside is a bruised bruise of storm clouds, while inside, the air smells of stale coffee and the faint metallic tang of regret. The promise was made on a table draped in a single, crumpled napkin. The hands holding it tremble—not from cold, but from the weight of what’s coming. The glass in the living room is still half-full of wine, though the bottle has been empty for days. The clock ticks like a metronome counting down to failure.

She thought she could keep it. But promises are like promises—
they’re not about what you say.
They’re about what you *do*, and what you *don’t*.
And right now, the only thing she’s doing is waiting for the phone to ring. Not with hope, but with the slow, grinding certainty that the call will be from the person who already knows the answer.
