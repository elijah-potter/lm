The shadows here are deep, clawed fingers stretching across cracked stone—some long and jagged, others short and brittle, like dried leaves on a windless day. The light slants from the east, where the sky peeks through the gnarled branches of ancient oaks, their trunks split with age, casting half-moon shapes on the ground. The shadows of the stone lions at the entrance are fused with the earth, their hollow eyes staring into the distance. Some shadows whisper of forgotten voices; others, of a river that once ran silver but now runs dry. The air hums with the weight of them—each one a story, each one a question.
