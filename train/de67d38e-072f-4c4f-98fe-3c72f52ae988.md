## A Counseling Session

**Setting:** A comfortable, warmly lit therapy office.

**Characters:**

* **Dr. Eleanor Vance:** A licensed therapist, mid-forties, known for her calm demeanor and thoughtful approach.
* **Liam Davies:** A client in his late twenties, visibly anxious and somewhat withdrawn.

**(The session begins. Liam is seated on a comfortable armchair. Dr. Vance sits opposite him, maintaining gentle eye contact.)**

**Liam:** (Hesitantly) It’s… it’s just been a lot lately. I feel like I’m constantly bracing for something bad to happen. Even when nothing *is* happening.

**Dr. Vance:** (Nodding slowly) That sounds incredibly draining, Liam. It’s understandable that you’re feeling overwhelmed. Can you tell me a little more about what that feeling of bracing yourself feels like?

**Liam:** It's like… a tightness in my chest. My thoughts race. I analyze everything, every interaction, every potential outcome. And I’m always looking for the negative. I know it’s not logical, but I can’t seem to stop it.

**Dr. Vance:** (Offering a small, encouraging smile) It takes a great deal of energy to maintain that level of vigilance. You’re essentially operating in a state of heightened alert. You mentioned analyzing every interaction. Could you give me an example of a recent situation where you experienced this?

**Liam:** (Pauses, considering) Last week, I had a meeting with my manager. It was just a routine check-in, but I spent the entire weekend beforehand worrying about it. I replayed every conversation I’ve ever had with him, trying to anticipate what he might say or ask. I convinced myself he was going to criticize my performance.

**Dr. Vance:** So, you were projecting a potential negative outcome onto a fairly ordinary situation. It sounds like the anxiety was driving your perception. And what actually happened in the meeting?

**Liam:** (Shrugs) Nothing. It was fine. He asked about a few projects, I answered, and that was it. I felt ridiculous afterward, realizing how much I’d built up in my head.

**Dr. Vance:** It’s a common experience, Liam. Our anxieties often create narratives that are far removed from reality. The good news is, recognizing that pattern is a significant step. It demonstrates a level of self-awareness. We can explore ways to challenge those narratives and to reduce the intensity of the anticipatory anxiety.

**Liam:** I’m trying. It’s just… exhausting. I feel like I’m constantly fighting myself.

**Dr. Vance:** (Leaning forward slightly) It *is* a battle, and it takes courage to face it. You’re already demonstrating that courage by being here and by being willing to examine these patterns. Let’s talk about some practical strategies. Have you ever explored techniques like mindfulness or deep breathing exercises?

**Liam:** I’ve read about them. I’ve tried a few times, but I always find it difficult to focus. My mind just keeps racing.

**Dr. Vance:** That's perfectly normal, especially when you're starting. Mindfulness isn't about stopping your thoughts; it’s about observing them without judgment. We can work on adapting those techniques to suit your specific needs and to help you develop a greater sense of presence in the moment. We can also consider exploring cognitive restructuring, which is a technique that helps to identify and challenge negative thought patterns.

**Liam:** (Looking slightly more hopeful) That… that sounds helpful. I just want to feel less on edge.

**Dr. Vance:** (Smiling warmly) That’s a very reasonable goal, Liam. And it’s absolutely achievable. We'll work together, at your pace, to develop strategies that empower you to manage your anxiety and to reclaim a greater sense of calm and control. For next week, I’d like you to simply observe your thoughts, without trying to change them. Just notice them. We’ll discuss your observations then. Does that feel manageable?

**Liam:** (Nods) Yes, that does. Thank you.

**Dr. Vance:** You’re very welcome, Liam. Remember, progress isn't always linear. There will be ups and downs. But I'm here to support you through it.



**(The session concludes.)**
