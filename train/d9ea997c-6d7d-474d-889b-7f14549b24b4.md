In the quiet hum of the morning, the narrator stands at the edge of a familiar yet uncharted path, fingers tracing the worn leather of their coat where a pocket once held a half-empty cup of tea. The world stretches before them—a vast, indifferent expanse of trees that whisper secrets only to those who dare listen too long. Their breath comes slow, a series of uncertain exhalations, each one carrying the weight of what might have been.

They know the rules: do not look back, do not turn left unless the stars align, do not speak unless spoken to. But the rules are only stories, told by those who never doubted. The shadows beneath their feet shift, as if breathing in time with the doubt that settles into their bones. The air smells of damp earth and something older, something that tastes like regret.

*"What if I’m wrong?"* The voice is not theirs, but it echoes in the space between their thoughts like a question with no answer. The wind carries it away, and the narrator catches a glimpse of their reflection in a puddle—eyes too wide, a mouth that doesn’t quite form the words *"I don’t know."* The water distorts them, not just because of the light, but because of the doubt that bends reality itself.

They step forward, but the ground feels hollow beneath them. Every step is a gamble, every sound a potential lie. The trees part slightly, revealing a clearing where the path splits—one way leads to the familiar, the other to the unknown. The narrator hesitates, then chooses neither, instead pausing where the two paths meet like a silent agreement: *I’ll wait here.*

Outside, the world keeps turning. Birds sing, but their songs are hollow now, carrying no meaning beyond the noise of existence. The narrator closes their eyes, not in surrender, but in the desperate hope that maybe, if they stop, the doubt will fade like mist in the dawn. But when they open them again, the path is still there, just as uncertain as it was before. The question lingers like a ghost: *What now?*
