In a dimly lit room, the old clock tower’s hands crept slowly, marking the passage of time. Inside, Elias sat at his desk, fingers hovering over the keys of his worn typewriter, a single sheet of paper crumpled in his palm.

His mind replayed the morning’s argument with Father—*"You’ll never learn if you keep letting the past dictate your future."* But the past was etched in his bones. The memory of the library’s last book, *The Hollow Years*, had been stolen, its pages torn, its secrets lost to the rain-soaked basement where he’d found it. He clenched his jaw. That was the past.

The plan was carved into his notebook: *"Find the thief. Return the book. Prove the system isn’t broken."* The thief was a shadow—a girl with eyes like polished obsidian, who’d slipped through the cracks of his own city’s surveillance. She’d left behind a trail of stolen words, but no name. Elias’s pulse quickened. He’d trace her through the city’s hidden alleys, through the coded messages left behind in the rain-slicked streets.

Outside, the wind howled, but Elias ignored it. His pen moved with purpose. The past was a weight, but the future was a map. He had to walk it, or lose himself forever.
