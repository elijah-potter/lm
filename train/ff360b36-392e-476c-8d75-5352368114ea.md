**The Last Light**

The factory chimney exhaled a column of smoke that dissolved into the dusk like a forgotten prayer. Inside, the machines hummed, their gears turning in silent, mechanical whispers. The workers moved like shadows—some bent over the assembly lines, others checking gauges with trembling hands. The air smelled of oil and sweat, of something old and something new.

Outside, the city stretched like a wound, its neon veins pulsing with forgotten promises. The streetlights flickered, casting long, jagged shadows across the pavement. A child ran past, their laughter echoing against the concrete, oblivious to the world’s decay.

Then, the lights went out.

Not with a bang, but with a sigh. The city held its breath as the last bulbs died, their glow extinguished by an unseen hand. The darkness swallowed the noise, the chatter, the hum of the machines. Only silence remained, thick and heavy, pressing against the skin like a shroud.

And then—

A single, perfect image appeared in the void, suspended between the world and the nothingness beyond. It was not a photograph, not a painting, but something older, something *seen* rather than *caught*. A woman stood at the edge of a cliff, her hands clutching a child who clutched a bird. The wind carried them upward, into the sky, where they vanished into the first light of dawn.

The image held, unbroken, as if time itself had paused to watch.

The city stirred. The machines groaned. The workers stirred from their trance, blinking in the sudden, endless dark.

And then—

The dawn broke.
