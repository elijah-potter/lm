A transient plaza in an overcast city, where temporary structures rise and fall like sandcastles in a shifting tide. Neon signs flicker in the dusk, their colors bleeding into the mist, advertising events that are already fading into the past. People move through the space as if it’s a ghost of a place they’ll leave soon—some pause to linger in the dim glow of a shared lantern, others rush past, their bags slung over shoulders, knowing the next round of installations will arrive tomorrow. The ground is littered with discarded food wrappers and half-emptied cups, the scent of warm coffee and something metallic lingering in the air. A street musician plays a broken guitar, their voice weaving in and out of the static of an unplugged radio. The entire setting feels like a stage set for a performance that’s already begun, yet no one knows when it will end.
