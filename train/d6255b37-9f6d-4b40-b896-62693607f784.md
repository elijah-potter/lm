**The inn’s dim glow flickered through the rain-slicked window as Elias adjusted the lantern’s wick, casting long shadows across the cracked leather chairs.**

Outside, the wind howled like a beast, rattling the shutters. Inside, the fire crackled low, its embers glowing red against the soot-stained walls. Across the room, a group of travelers huddled around a hearth, their breath misting in the cold air.

Elias, a grizzled old man with a scar running from his temple to his jawline, leaned against the table, his fingers tracing the rim of his cup. The others had stopped talking, their attention fixed on him.

**"You ever wonder where stories come from?"** he mused, his voice rough as gravel. **"Not the ones we tell, but the ones that *tell* us. The ones that whisper in the dark before we even know we’re listening."**

A murmur rippled through the group. He reached into his coat and pulled out a worn, leather-bound book. The cover was patched with frayed thread, and the spine cracked under his palm.

**"This isn’t mine,"** he said, tapping the cover. **"Found it in a church basement three winters past. No name on the title page, just this: *‘The Tale of the Hollow Man.’* The man who owned it before me didn’t remember much—only that he’d heard it from someone who’d heard it from someone who’d been told by a woman who’d seen it in a dream."**

He opened the book, and the pages turned slowly, revealing ink that seemed to shimmer in the firelight. The first line was scrawled in what looked like a child’s hand:

*"Once, there was a man who lived in a house that wasn’t his."*

Elias exhaled, a slow breath through his nose. **"He told me this story one night, as he sat by the fire, his hands trembling. Said it was about a man who didn’t remember his own name, who wandered through a city where the streets whispered his past. The woman who told him—she said it was about love, but not the kind you find in stories. The kind that slips through cracks, the kind that leaves you hollow when it’s gone."**

A silence fell. Then, from the corner, a voice—soft, barely audible—whispered:

*"I saw him once. Standing at the edge of the world, holding a mirror. He smiled, but the reflection didn’t smile back."*

Elias closed the book with a snap, and the ink seemed to fade. **"That’s the end of the story,"** he said quietly. **"Or so he said. But some stories, they don’t end. They just… wait."**

The fire popped. The wind outside moaned. And somewhere, in the dark, a story kept whispering.
