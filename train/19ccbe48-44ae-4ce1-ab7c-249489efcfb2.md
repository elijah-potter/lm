The storm raged outside, its howling wind and crashing rain mirroring the turmoil within the room. Heavy, dark clouds loomed above, their edges flickering like embers in a dying fire. Inside, the candlelight cast long, wavering shadows across the walls, each flickering with the intensity of the protagonist’s inner conflict.

The protagonist sat hunched in a corner, fingers tracing the rim of a half-empty glass. The rain pattered against the windows like desperate, rhythmic beats, each drop a whispered confession. The air smelled of damp earth and something bitter—like unwashed tears. Outside, lightning split the sky into jagged, unnatural slices, casting eerie reflections in puddles that were both glass and liquid.

A single tear traced its way down their cheek, the cold of it a quiet betrayal. The wind howled, carrying fragments of a voice that wasn’t there—only echoes of what was lost. The storm outside was the storm inside, a tempest of unresolved emotions, each gust a breath held too long, each downpour a flood of sorrow. The fire in the hearth sputtered, its flames dying as the protagonist’s resolve did too, leaving only the dark and the damp.
