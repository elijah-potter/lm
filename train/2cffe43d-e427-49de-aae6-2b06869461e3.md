The radio crackled to life, static hissing between stations. Outside, rain tapped against the grimy windows, a slow, rhythmic drumbeat. **John** sat at the kitchen table, fingers tracing the rim of his coffee cup. His phone buzzed—another missed call from his brother, **Mark**, the only person who knew he was still here.

A sharp knock at the door. He didn’t look up. "Just the mailman," he muttered, though the package under the doormat bore the same faded logo as the one that had come weeks ago—**Local News Network**, black-and-white, the same one that never delivered.

The door swung open. The scent of damp wool and stale cigarettes hit him first. **Daniel** stepped inside, his coat soaked, his eyes bloodshot. His breath came in ragged gasps. "Took you long enough," he said, not looking at John. "The report’s out."

John exhaled, slow and heavy. "What’s the verdict?" The words tasted like ash.

Daniel tossed a folded paper onto the table. The headline glowed in red ink: **"CRIME SCENE UPDATE: 7 KILLED IN SUSPECTED MURDER-SUICIDE."** Below, a single sentence: **"No arrests made; investigation ongoing."** His phone buzzed again—another notification, this time from the police scanner.

John read the first paragraph aloud, his voice flat. "Seven dead. No names. No faces. Just another name in a list." His hands trembled. "What do you want me to do?"

Daniel grabbed his arm, fingers digging into his skin. "You’re not leaving. Not until they find something." He jerked his chin toward the door. "They’re waiting upstairs. You’re the only one who can get them the names." His voice dropped. "And you know where they’re buried."
