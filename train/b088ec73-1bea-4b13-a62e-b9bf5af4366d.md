**The Last Letter**

She had written it once before—*for the record*, as if the world itself could hear it—and the ink had dried in her trembling hand before she’d realized how wrong she was. Now, the paper was cold against her fingers, the same as it had been that first time, but this time, the words were different.

She closed her eyes and let the words spill from her lips like a confession meant for no one but herself.

*"I don’t know what I was thinking."*

The pen hovered over the line, the tip catching the light like a spark. She hadn’t meant to. She hadn’t meant to *stop*—not really. But the truth was, the world had been too loud, the weight of every choice too heavy, and the fear of what might come after had made her hand shake.

So she erased the first draft, let the paper breathe, and wrote again.

*"I don’t know what I was *doing*."*

The pen moved slower this time, deliberate, like she was tracing the edges of a dream she wanted to wake from. The letter wasn’t for anyone. It wasn’t even *about* her anymore. It was just… a memory she wanted to keep, even if she couldn’t keep the rest of it.

She folded it neatly, sealed it with wax that smelled like autumn, and left it on the shelf where it would stay, forgotten, until perhaps one day someone—maybe a stranger, maybe a friend—would find it and wonder.

The world had changed. She had changed. And for the first time, she didn’t care if anyone knew.
