Standing in the slow-motion blur of an elevator shaft, the air thick with static, I realize my pulse isn’t just a heartbeat—it’s a slow, deliberate drumming against my ribs, each beat stretching into a whisper. The glass doors hum with a sound like distant thunder, the only thing moving in time. Outside, the city’s neon veins pulse, but they’re a different rhythm entirely, rapid and indifferent. I press my forehead against the cold metal, the way time itself seems to compress here, a single moment stretched into an eternity of stillness. The doors open. I step out. The moment, now, is just a step, a breath, a fraction of the weight I carried before.
