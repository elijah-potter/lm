AITA for refusing to let my sister use my inherited vacation home?

Okay, this is a bit complicated, and I’m genuinely unsure how to feel about it. It involves my sister, Sarah (38), my late grandmother, and a small cottage in Maine.

My grandmother, Eleanor, passed away last October (2023) after a long illness. She lived in the cottage year-round, a charming, slightly rustic place in Kennebunkport. It was her sanctuary, and she always made it clear in conversations, and explicitly in her will, that it would pass to me. I’m 42. Sarah is five years younger. 

Eleanor and Sarah had a…complex relationship. Sarah moved out of the house at 18 and rarely visited, though Eleanor always expressed disappointment. Sarah frequently emphasized how much she *wanted* the cottage, often saying things like, “Imagine all the memories we could make there together, Mom!” Eleanor would usually just sigh and change the subject.

I’m a graphic designer and work remotely, so the cottage's proximity to the coast and quiet atmosphere appeals to me. I’ve spent about two weeks there since inheriting it, using it as a writing retreat and generally enjoying the peace. I’m planning to rent it out on Airbnb for a portion of the year to offset the upkeep costs, but I intend to keep it for my own use, too.

About a month ago (early December 2023), Sarah approached me with a request. Her husband, David, recently lost his job. They have two children, ages 8 and 10. She suggested that I let her and her family use the cottage for two weeks over Christmas, completely free of charge. She framed it as a lifeline, emphasizing their financial strain and the kids’ disappointment at not having a proper Christmas vacation. She added that it would be “amazing” for family bonding.

I felt a pang of sympathy, honestly. But I also felt a strong sense of unease. I explained that I was just starting to establish a routine with the cottage, that I had plans for it, and that I wasn't comfortable with her essentially asking for a free handout. I offered to help them find affordable rental options in the area, but firmly declined her request to use the cottage.

Sarah reacted poorly. She accused me of being selfish and heartless. She said I was hoarding something that should be shared, and that Eleanor would have wanted her to have it. She even brought up past grievances, things I’m not going to detail here, but they involved a misunderstanding about a family heirloom years ago. David, to his credit, tried to mediate, but Sarah was relentless. 

Then, last week (mid-December 2023), she sent me a text message, ostensibly from Eleanor’s old email account. It was clearly a forgery, but the content mirrored Eleanor’s writing style. It said, “Darling, please let Sarah and the children use the cottage. It would bring them so much joy.” I immediately blocked the email address and reported it as phishing. 

I told Sarah that I knew it was a fake and that her actions were manipulative and disrespectful. She denied it, of course, claiming she had no idea how it happened. Now, our mother, Patricia, is siding with Sarah, saying I need to be more generous and that I’m “making a big deal” out of nothing. Patricia is usually reasonable, but she seems determined to pressure me into giving in.

I feel like I’ve been more than accommodating, offering to help them financially and suggesting alternatives. I’m also worried about the potential damage to the cottage and the precedent it would set if I allowed this. I’d feel exploited. I’m starting to feel isolated and overwhelmed by the constant pressure. I’m not sure what to do. I don’t want to ruin my relationship with my sister, but I also feel like I’m being taken advantage of. It’s genuinely upsetting. It's a lot of stress. I'm leaning towards ESH - everyone is behaving badly.

AITA for refusing to let my sister use my inherited vacation home?
