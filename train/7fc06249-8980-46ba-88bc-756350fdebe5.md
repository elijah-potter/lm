The clock ticked relentlessly, each second stretching into an eternity of silence. The weight of anticipation pressed against her ribs like an unseen hand. She sat cross-legged on the cracked wooden floor of the abandoned church, the air thick with the scent of damp stone and old prayers. The candle flickered, casting jagged shadows across the faces of the statues, their expressions frozen in eternal contemplation. Outside, the wind howled through the broken stained-glass windows, but inside, the world had stillness. Time had become a river of slow, deliberate waves, each moment stretching into something vast and unknowable—like the endless blue of a summer sky, yet somehow darker, more suffocating.

She traced the worn edge of the Bible on her lap, the pages curling slightly at the corners. The words had lost their meaning long ago, but the rhythm of the script—each syllable, each pause—had become a prayer of its own. The bell above the altar chimed once, twice, and the sound echoed through the hollow space like a heartbeat, though no one was there to hear it. The minutes blurred into hours, each one a promise left unfulfilled. The only sound was the distant murmur of the river beyond the churchyard, a whisper of the world beyond this sacred, time-warped place.

She leaned forward, her breath shallow. The moment felt like a lifetime, each breath a step closer to the edge of something she couldn’t name. The candle guttered, and in the sudden darkness, the figures on the altar seemed to glow—eternal, unchanging. The church was no longer a building; it was a wound, a wound that had opened too wide, and time itself had stretched taut, ready to snap.
