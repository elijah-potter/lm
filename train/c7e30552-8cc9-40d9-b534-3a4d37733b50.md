A janitor in a mid-century office building noticed a cracked tile in the hallway floor near the elevators. It had been there for months, overlooked by maintenance staff due to its size and location. The tile’s irregularity was subtle—a thin, jagged line splitting the ceramic—until the janitor’s foot caught it while sweeping. The moment of impact sent a sharp, unfamiliar sound that made him pause. He bent down, running his gloved fingers along the break, feeling the uneven edges. Curious, he snapped a photo of it from above, comparing it to the rest of the floor. The discrepancy was real: the tile’s edge was slightly misaligned, suggesting a repair attempt years ago that had failed. That small detail—a fracture that no one had noticed before—became the first clue in a series of observations that led to uncovering a hidden problem in the building’s structural integrity.
