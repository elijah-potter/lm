A hollowed-out promise, the kind that lingers like a ghost in the morning’s light—each syllable a lie wrapped in hope. The words were grand: *"I’ll never stop."* The gestures, though earnest, were clumsy, like a child’s attempt to mend a cracked mirror. The time between when it was made and when it was broken was measured in half-finished tasks, unanswered emails, and the way silence thickened between them.

The act of keeping it was never about the doing—it was about the *not doing*. The weight of silence when the other expected noise. The way the clock ticked, each hour a betrayal in its own way. Some promises are written in ink, others in the slow, creeping erosion of trust. This one was both.

Now the glass is half-full of regret, and the glass is half-empty of excuses. The promise is buried in the spaces between the words they said, the things they didn’t say. The impossible is not in the act itself but in the act of *not failing*—because failure is always inevitable. The only question is whether the cost of keeping it would be more than the cost of letting it go.
