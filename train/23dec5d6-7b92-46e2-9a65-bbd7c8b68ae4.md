In the hollowed-out core of a forgotten cathedral, the clock tower’s pendulum swung with a rhythm that synchronized the city’s breath. Every hour, the second hand traced a spiral of time across the stained-glass windows, painting fleeting moments in hues of gold and crimson. The clock’s face was not merely a mechanism but a sentient entity, its gears whispering secrets to those who dared listen.

One evening, a thief named Elias stole a single brass hourglass from the tower’s sanctum. Inside, he found not sand, but stars—each grain a frozen second from a dying star. He ran, his stolen time slipping through his fingers like liquid mercury. The city’s watchmen, clad in blackened steel, chased him, their own clocks—engraved with cursed runes—ticking backward, devouring his stolen moments.

At midnight, Elias reached the edge of the world, where the clock tower’s shadow stretched like a wound. There, a voice echoed from the gears: *"You took time, but time has already claimed you."* The hourglass’s stars coalesced into a single, pulsating orb, and Elias was drawn into the clock’s heart. The last thing he saw was his own reflection—his hands frozen mid-motion, his face split in a grin that wasn’t his own.

Now, the clock’s pendulum hums in his chest, and every hour, it whispers: *"The hands of time have always been yours to steal."*
