A derelict factory looms under a rusted canopy of broken glass and sagging metal beams, its once towering smokestacks now crouching like skeletal fingers against a gray sky. The floor is a mosaic of cracked concrete, half-buried in dust and debris, with exposed wiring snaking through the skeletal remains of shelving units. The walls, once white-painted, are now peeling and marked with faded graffiti—some legible, others smudged into abstract designs. A collapsed stairwell spirals down into darkness, its steps splintered and half-covered by exposed pipes and rusted metal. A single, flickering fluorescent bulb dangles from a frayed ceiling wire, casting eerie shadows. The air hums with the faint scent of damp and old oil, the silence broken only by the occasional creak of wind through the gaps in the walls. A half-built assembly line of scrap metal and half-assembled machinery stands in the center, its parts scattered haphazardly, some bolted in place, others left to rust. The place feels like a time capsule of unfinished ambition, where progress paused abruptly midway.
