AITA for refusing to let my sister use my inherited vacation home?

Okay, this is a bit complicated, and I'm already feeling stressed just writing this out. It involves my sister, Eleanor, my late grandmother, and a property that’s become a significant source of family tension.

My grandmother, Beatrice, passed away last October (2023) after a long illness. She lived in a charming, older house in Cape Cod, Massachusetts. Beatrice was… particular. She meticulously planned everything, and her will reflected that. The house, which had been in our family for generations, was specifically bequeathed to me, her only grandson. Eleanor, my younger sister, received a substantial sum of money instead. We both understood and accepted this at the time. Beatrice always favored me, and while it felt a little awkward, there wasn’t really anything to argue.

For years, Eleanor has been subtly (and sometimes not so subtly) hinting that she felt shortchanged. She’s always loved the Cape Cod house, reminiscing about childhood summers spent there. After Beatrice's passing, these hints escalated. Around November 2023, she started asking if she could use the house for a week during the Christmas holidays. I politely declined, explaining that I wanted to spend some time there myself to process my grandmother’s passing and get a feel for managing the property.

Then, in January 2024, she started a campaign of guilt. She’s been calling my mother almost daily, complaining that I’m “hoarding” the house and not sharing the family’s access to it. Mom, bless her heart, has tried to mediate, suggesting I “be generous.” I'm trying to be, but I’ve also had contractors out to assess the roof and heating system – significant expenses – and I’m just trying to figure out how to maintain it responsibly.

The real kicker came last week, February 27th. Eleanor called me, sounding genuinely upset. She explained that her partner, David, had been offered a short-term project in Boston, starting March 15th. She wanted to use the Cape Cod house as a base for him, claiming it would be incredibly convenient and alleviate a lot of stress for both of them. She presented it as a huge favor I could do for her, emphasizing how much this project means to David’s career. I reiterated my position – that I wasn’t ready to share the house, especially with ongoing maintenance concerns.

David got involved in the conversation then. He chimed in, saying it was “unfair” that I was being so possessive of the property and that it was “a shame” to keep it empty when it could be enjoyed by the family. He even suggested I was being selfish and holding onto something I didn't truly appreciate. That's when I lost it a little. I told them both, quite firmly, that the house was mine, and I would decide when and if I was comfortable sharing it.

Now, Mom is furious. She's accusing me of being unfeeling and creating unnecessary drama. Eleanor and David are giving me the silent treatment. I feel like I’m being pressured into something I’m not comfortable with, and frankly, I’m exhausted by the constant requests and the implication that I’m doing something wrong. I understand family dynamics can be tricky, but I feel like they’re completely disregarding my feelings and my right to manage a property that was specifically left to me. I’m trying to be reasonable, but their persistence feels manipulative. I'm also worried that if I cave now, it will open the floodgates for endless demands.

AITA for refusing to let my sister use my inherited vacation home?
