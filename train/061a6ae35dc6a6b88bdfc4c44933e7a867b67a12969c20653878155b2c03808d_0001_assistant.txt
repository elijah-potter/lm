We load into the game. I put my hands on the arcade stick and buttons. Everyone's eyes made me a little uncomfortable, but they did not matter at the moment. The only thing that matters right now is the match. On the screen, it shows both of our characters, ready to fight. The countdown occurs. Three! Two! One! Fight!

That word, "Fight!" It's motivating. It tells me to only focus on the person in front. I press left on the arcade stick, making my character walk backward. I hone in on Talo's character, trying to see what he'll do first. He surprises me by zipping right past my guard and dashing right up to my face. I wasn't ready. He got the first hit with a 3-hit combo to take the first lead. I was focused on playing defense that I didn't think about mounting an offense of my own. I was behind. My hands were a little sweaty, but I was ok. Playing a fighting game was like an intricate dance. My opponent and I move around each other, dancing around each other, contesting each over at every opportunity. We played like we were aiming to win. There was no "my team made me worse" or "I was feeling a little off because of the stage." If we lost, we knew it was because we weren't good enough. The dance was hypnotic; we pressed our buttons in time with each other and hoped that they worked. You could hear the grunts and squeals of the character every time they landed a hit or got hit. The crowd would "Oooh" and "Aaaah" at every move. They were also enthralled by the match. 

"This guy is giving Talo a good match!"
"This is a more exciting match than the finals!"

The timer of the match counts down. 30, 29, 28. I was down on life, so I had to make a risky move. Talo was good, though, so it might knock me out if I do it at the wrong time. There was no time to think. My finger crashed down on the buttons as I leaned in. Maybe I got too into the game. I loved fighting games so much. The thrill that you get from a 1v1 match is like nothing else. 

"K.O!"
