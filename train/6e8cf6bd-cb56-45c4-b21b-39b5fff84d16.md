A flickering streetlamp cast long shadows across the cracked pavement. The hum of distant generators drowned out the distant roar of sirens. Inside the half-lit apartment, a woman clutched a half-empty bottle of whiskey while the fridge’s hum grew quieter with each second. Outside, a neighbor’s neon sign flickered in and out of view, its blue glow barely piercing the dark.

The phone buzzed—a text from her husband: *"Where are you? Grid’s down, roads are chaotic."* She typed back: *"Coffee shop—told you it’d rain."* The line went dead. The fridge’s door creaked open, revealing a single moldy sausage. She exhaled, swirling the whiskey. A child’s laughter echoed from the hallway—her daughter, playing with a borrowed tablet, the screen glowing red in the dark. A sudden *thump* from upstairs. Her breath caught. The neighbor’s TV, still on, crackled with static: *"Stay safe. We’re here."* The power returned with a jolt, plunging the scene into sudden silence.
