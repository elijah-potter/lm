**Mayor Thompson announces new economic initiative to boost tourism in Havenport**
Havenport, a coastal city known for its historic charm and thriving arts scene, is set to welcome a wave of visitors with a newly unveiled economic initiative. Mayor Eleanor Thompson revealed plans to invest $2 million into expanding the city’s **Harborfront Renaissance District**, which will include a revamped marina, seasonal street festivals, and a new **year-round cultural hub** featuring live music, dining, and local crafts. The district will also incorporate solar-powered lighting and adaptive bike lanes to enhance accessibility.

The initiative aims to triple tourist arrivals within three years by partnering with regional businesses and nonprofits to create job opportunities. "Havenport isn’t just a city—it’s a living museum," Thompson said at a press conference. "This plan ensures we keep attracting families, artists, and wanderers who make our city unique."

Critics warn of potential gentrification risks, but Thompson counters that the project will prioritize affordable housing for long-term residents alongside new commercial spaces. The city council has also approved a $500,000 grant for a **community-led "Neighborhood Pass"** program, offering discounted stays to locals and visitors alike.

Upgrades to Havenport’s **famous Pier 36**—where the annual **Summer Solstice Regatta** draws 15,000 spectators—are expected to begin next month, with construction wrapping up by winter. The mayor encouraged residents to share feedback on the proposal through an online survey at **HavenportLive.com/PlanForOurFuture**.

**Local fishermen protest new harbor restrictions**
While Mayor Thompson’s plans spark optimism, a coalition of fishing guilds in Havenport has filed a complaint against proposed **harbor depth regulations** that could limit vessel access. The restrictions, part of the same economic initiative, require all boats entering the marina to complete a mandatory **eco-certification** process, including carbon footprint assessments.

"These rules will turn Havenport’s harbor into a bureaucratic nightmare," said Captain Daniel Calloway, president of the **Havenport Maritime Alliance**. "Small-time fishermen and charter operators can’t afford the fees, and we’ll be left with fewer options for locals and visitors." The guilds demand a public hearing to review exemptions for traditional fishing vessels.

The city’s environmental department insists the rules are designed to "preserve marine biodiversity" by reducing pollution from larger vessels. Proponents argue that stricter standards will eventually benefit the entire economy by improving water quality and attracting eco-tourism.

**City council votes to expand affordable housing in historic district**
In a surprise move, the Havenport City Council unanimously approved a **$12 million bond proposal** to preserve and repurpose 15 historic properties as **affordable housing units**. The plan, co-sponsored by Mayor Thompson and Councilwoman Priya Patel, targets the **Old Mill District**, a former industrial zone now home to artists and young professionals.

The initiative will convert empty warehouses into micro-apartments, while renovating two heritage buildings into community centers. Rent caps will be set at **40% below market rate**, with subsidies covering utilities and public transit passes. Critics argue the bond funds could be better spent on infrastructure, but supporters say the move will prevent gentrification from displacing long-time residents.

A phased rollout is planned, with construction beginning in Q1 2025. The city will partner with the **Havenport Housing Coalition** to prioritize applicants with incomes below $60,000 annually.

**Summer Solstice Regatta delayed due to storm warning**
Havenport’s annual **Summer Solstice Regatta**, a high-profile event drawing 20,000 visitors, has been postponed until **September 21st** after a **Category 2 hurricane** struck the region last weekend. The storm caused power outages, flooded docks, and damaged several boats in the marina.

Organizers, led by regatta director **Lena Vasquez**, said the delay was necessary to ensure safety. "The harbor was a mess," Vasquez told reporters. "We’re prioritizing repairs and water quality checks before we risk another disaster."

The city’s **Port Authority** has already allocated $750,000 to rebuild the damaged infrastructure, including the **Pier 36 grandstand** and temporary shelters for spectators. The regatta’s organizers have begun recruiting volunteers to help clean up debris and maintain the marina’s conditions.

Visitors who attended the canceled event are eligible for a **$50 refund** through the city’s tourism office. The regatta’s website, **SolsticeRaces.org**, now features a "Save the Date" countdown and a **virtual regatta** streaming live from local yacht clubs.
