A flickering streetlamp casts long shadows across the cobblestone alley. The air hums with the distant wail of a siren, but the noise feels distant—like a ghost from another time. Across the way, an old clock tower stands motionless, its hands frozen at 3:17 AM. The clock’s face is cracked, revealing a hidden inscription: *"Do not turn back."*

Your neighbor’s child, five years old, stands clutching a toy dog made of wood. The dog’s eyes glow faintly, pulsing like embers. The child whispers to it: *"Find me the girl who disappeared in ’98."* The toy dog tilts its head, then vanishes mid-air, leaving behind only a trail of dust.

You turn away, but your reflection in a shattered storefront mirror shows someone else—your younger self, standing in a similar alley, holding a similar toy. The reflection’s voice echoes: *"You were supposed to leave here before dawn."* The younger you blinks, then dissolves into smoke. The present you gasps, heart pounding, as the clock tower’s hands begin to move—slowly, inexorably—backward.
