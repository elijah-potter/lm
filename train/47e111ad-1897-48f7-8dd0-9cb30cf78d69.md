A muted hum from the radio filled the dimly lit room, the volume low enough to avoid drawing attention. Emma adjusted her posture in the armchair, fingers tracing idle circles on the armrest, her knuckles turning white. Across the table, Jake leaned back in his chair, elbows resting on the polished wood, his face half-lit by the flickering glow of the streetlamp outside.

"You think he’s going to show up?" Jake asked, his voice low and measured. Emma’s head snapped up, her gaze locked onto him. She exhaled slowly, letting out a breath that was barely audible.

"I don’t know. But I’m telling you this—if he does, we move *now*."

Jake smirked, but his eyes never left hers. "You sure you’re not just tired and making it up?" Emma’s jaw tightened. She stood abruptly, her chair scraping against the floor. "No. I’m not tired. I’m listening."

The door creaked open behind her, a cold draft slithering through the room. Jake’s phone buzzed, vibrating against the table. Emma’s hands clenched into fists at her sides, her knuckles bleeding from the tension. The radio crackled to life, a sudden burst of static before cutting to a news report. Jake’s phone buzzed again—another message. This time, Emma didn’t wait for him to answer.

"Call me when you’re sure," she said, her voice hollow. "And don’t take any chances."
