The rain slanted against the cracked windshield as the GPS beeped a single, urgent tone. The headlights cut through the mist, illuminating the blurred figures of the forest ahead—twisted roots, abandoned rusted gates, and the skeletal remains of a crumbling stone wall. The navigation light flickered, then died.

The car door slammed shut. A hand tightened around the steering wheel. The map lay open on the passenger seat, its familiar paths now a maze of wrong turns. A cold realization settled over them: they’d turned left instead of right at the old farmhouse, and the dirt road had swallowed the exit sign.

A breath escaped between clenched teeth. The voice of the sat-nav echoed in their ear: *"You are 1.2 miles from your last known location."* The words sounded like a death sentence. They should have trusted the turns, the signs—anything. But the road had been too familiar, the shadows too inviting.

The engine purred, a dying promise. Outside, the wind howled like a warning. They were lost. And the forest was waiting.
