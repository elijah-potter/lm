Once upon a time, in a cozy house with a big, warm kitchen, lived a little bunny named Pip. Pip had fluffy pink paws and a tiny red nose that always sparkled when he was happy.

One sunny morning, Pip woke up and stretched his ears. "Mmm, it smells like berries!" he said, sniffing the air. Outside, the meadow was golden with sunshine, and the flowers were dancing in the breeze.

Pip hopped outside, collecting a basket full of shiny blueberries. His momma bunny smiled. "Where are you going, Pip?" she asked.

"I'm going to find my favorite friend, Max the Mouse!" Pip said, his eyes twinkling. Max lived in the old oak tree behind the house.

Pip hopped and skipped, skipping over a puddle that sparkled like a magic mirror. "Look how shiny!" he said, but his foot slipped—*whoosh!*—and Pip tumbled right into a big, deep hole.

Pip gasped. "Oh no, I’m stuck!" he cried. But then, from the hole came a soft voice, "Don’t worry, Pip! I’m here!"

Pip looked down and saw Max the Mouse waving his tiny whiskers. "Max!" Pip squeaked.

"Max is a little scared," said Max, his eyes wide. "You’re so big!"

Pip giggled. "But you’re the best friend ever!" He thought hard. "What if we make a ladder?"

Together, they used Max’s tiny tail and Pip’s long ears to make a rope. "Let’s climb!" Pip said, wiggling his paws.

With teamwork, they climbed out of the hole, and Max popped out first, giggling. "You’re the bravest bunny ever!" Max said.

Pip beamed. "Now, let’s go back home!" They skipped through the meadow, picking more berries, and Pip’s basket was now full of sunshine and friendship.

As they hopped through the golden grass, Pip and Max heard their momma bunny calling. "Pip, where are you?" she called.

Pip waved. "We’re coming, Momma!" He and Max hopped back to the house, where their momma bunny hugged them tight. "You two are the best friends ever!" she said, her eyes shining.

And from that day on, Pip and Max played together every day, exploring, laughing, and making new adventures in the meadow. And the little bunny and mouse knew that friendship was the sweetest berry of all.
