Britain's high teenage pregnancy rate could be cut by offering sex and relationship education (SRE) in schools from a young age, it has been claimed.
According to sexual health charity Brook, SRE is the most appropriate tool to accelerate the decline in teenage pregnancy rate as it will improve the confidence of young people and make them more aware of the choices available to them concerning sex and relationships.
Over the last ten years, the teenage pregnancy rate has declined by 12.6 per cent.
Despite this, the UK figure remains the highest in Europe and further action therefore needs to be taken.
'All the evidence shows that if we get good quality sex and relationships education into schools from a young age this delays when young people decide to first have sex and means they are more likely to use contraception when they do, and this will impact on teenage pregnancy rates,' a spokesperson for Brook said.
A survey conducted by the Office for National Statistics shows that one on four women did not use any form of contraception in 2007-08.
