The morning sun spilled through cracked windows, painting the room in shades of gold and gray. Outside, the city pulsed with a thousand unseen voices—some eager, some weary, all moving forward without pause. I stood at the edge of the door, my fingers trembling against the frame. Behind me, the bed was untouched, the half-empty mug of coffee still warm, its remnants long since gone.

I wanted to step out. Not just out of the house, but out of the life that had become a cage of expectations. The quiet hum of my own thoughts was louder than any noise outside. *What if I’m wrong?* What if I had to stay? What if the world, as it was, was the only one I’d ever know?

But the door wouldn’t open. Not for me. It was too heavy, too final, too full of what might come next. The doubt coiled tighter, a snake in my chest, its fangs scraping against the certainty I’d never felt before. What if I was the only one who couldn’t see?

The clock ticked. The city outside didn’t wait. I pressed my forehead against the glass, watching the steam rise like a ghost from the pavement. Somewhere, a child laughed. A car horn blared. The world kept turning, and I was just… here, standing still, unsure if I wanted to move at all.
