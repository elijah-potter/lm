I ask, because it may soon become a reality. A recent article in the Journal of Neuroscience finds researchers reporting on a DARPA-funded effort to develop a sleep-replacement drug. And they’re finding one.
Here’s more from Wired on an experimental nasal spray that contains a naturally occurring brain hormone called orexin A, which reversed the effects of sleep deprivation in monkeys:
The monkeys were deprived of sleep for 30 to 36 hours and then given either orexin A or a saline placebo before taking standard cognitive tests. The monkeys given orexin A in a nasal spray scored about the same as alert monkeys, while the saline-control group was severely impaired.
The study found orexin A not only restored monkeys’ cognitive abilities but made their brains look “awake” in PET scans.
Siegel said that orexin A is unique in that it only had an impact on sleepy monkeys, not alert ones, and that it is “specific in reversing the effects of sleepiness” without other impacts on the brain.
This nasal spray innovation seems a step up from available stimulants like modafinil, which are seeing increased use by those who want to stay awake for long stretches.
So let’s expand our imaginations a bit and accept that this drug has few side effects, and there are no long-term ill effects from lack of sleep. Would this be good or bad for society? What would you do with an additional six or eight hours a day?
I can definitively say this is something parents won’t be giving their young kids any time soon. And not because of any side-effect concerns, either.
