A cold rain fell on the rusted gate of the abandoned farmhouse, the wind howling through the broken windowpanes. Inside, Elias sat hunched over a cracked kitchen table, his fingers tracing the rim of a half-empty bottle. The glass was half-full of whiskey, the amber liquid shimmering under the dim glow of an oil lamp. Across the room, his sister, Mara, knelt by the bed, her hands steady as she rolled a cigarette. She exhaled a slow stream of smoke, her eyes flicking to Elias, who met her gaze without blinking.

"Five years," Mara said, voice low. "You promised."

Elias exhaled smoke, his breath fogging the lamp’s glass. "Five years, Mara. That’s all I could guarantee."

She didn’t laugh. "You lied to me when you left. You told me you’d come back, that we’d rebuild this place together. But you left me with nothing but a job in the city and a promise."

Elias rubbed his temples, the weight of the past pressing down. "I needed time. I needed to figure out what I was doing wrong."

Mara stood abruptly, her boots thudding against the floor. "Figured it out already? That you’re useless? That you’d rather drink than help?"

The room went silent. The rain outside pounded harder, a relentless drumbeat.

Elias looked at the whiskey bottle, then at Mara. "I’m sorry."

She didn’t answer. Instead, she reached into her coat pocket and pulled out a small, worn leather pouch. Inside was a single, old coin, its edges worn smooth by years of handling. "Here," she said, pressing it into his palm. "For the work you’re doing now. For the life you’re building."

Elias hesitated, then took it. The coin felt heavy in his hand.

Outside, the wind screamed. Inside, the only sound was the clink of Mara’s hip against the floor as she turned away. The rain didn’t stop. Neither did the storm inside Elias.
