Bonsai is a Japanese term that means “planting on a low pot” that is proclaimed to have a Zen-like quality of meditation and enlightenment from you tending to it. There is no specific schedule for watering a bonsai tree because many types of shrubs and trees are useful in bonsai growing and each has its own habits. The climate, species, size, humidity, light, soil and pot all play a roll as to when a bonsai needs watering. The best method is to test the soil and water on an as-need basis and immersion water the tree once a week.
Insert your forefinger about 1 inch deep into the bonsai pot. If the soil is dry at this depth, the bonsai tree needs watering.
Take your bonsai tree outdoors and set it on a flat surface. Fill a watering can with water and tip the spout downward at a slight angle to water the container slowly and lightly so as not to disturb the soil. Continue adding water until the excess flows from the bottom drainage holes.
Fill a plant mister with water. Mist the leaves thoroughly until the water drips off. Washing the foliage removes dusts and pollutants to ensure healthy breathing holes in leaves. The leaves of a bonsai provide about 35 percent of water intake for the plant.
Allow the pot to drain thoroughly before returning it inside.
Fill a sink with water to the same depth as your bonsai pot. Lower your bonsai tree slowly into the water until the pot sits on the bottom of the sink.
Observe the air bubbles escaping from the bottom drainage holes and the top of the soil. Allow the pot to remain immersed in the water until you see no more air bubbles. This allows the soil to soak up its full capacity of water.
Open the drain, let the water escape and allow the pot to finish draining before replacing it where it resides.
Things You Will Need
- Watering can
- Plant mister
- Reduce the watering schedule after pruning a bonsai tree in the summer. There is less foliage to require water and the soil doesn’t dry out as quickly.
- A bonsai requires more frequent watering when it is producing fruit or flowers so they will grow adequately.
- Outdoor bonsai trees require more frequent watering if there is considerable wind and less frequent on hot, humid summer days that help soil retain moisture.
- Over-watering or under-watering a bonsai tree is the most common reason for its decline.
