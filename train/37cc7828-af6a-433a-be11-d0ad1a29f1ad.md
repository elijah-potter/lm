## Explain Like I'm Five: Why the Sky is Blue

Okay, imagine you have a big box of crayons, right? And all the crayons are different colors: red, yellow, green, blue, purple – all of them! Now, pretend the sun is shining a super bright light, like a giant flashlight, and that light is actually made up of *all* those crayon colors mixed together.

Normally, when the sunlight comes to us, it travels straight. But when it gets to Earth, it bumps into tiny bits of air, like teeny-tiny bouncy castles floating all around. 

Now, blue and violet colors are like little, bouncy balls. They bounce off those air bouncy castles *much* more than the other colors, like red or orange, which are more like big, heavy bowling balls. The blue and violet colors bounce *everywhere*! They bounce up, down, sideways – all around!

Because the blue and violet colors are bouncing all over the place, they spread out and fill the whole sky. That’s why when you look up, you see blue! 

Now, you might be wondering, "Why don't we see violet then? Doesn’t violet bounce even *more*?" That’s a great question! Violet *does* bounce a bit more, but our eyes aren't as good at seeing violet as they are at seeing blue. Also, the sun doesn’t send out as much violet light to begin with.

So, the sky looks blue because the blue light from the sun is bouncing around the air and filling the space above us. Pretty cool, huh?
