Rituals: Performance without purpose—steps repeated mechanically in a space where symbols dissolve into repetition. A candle burns low, flickering in a cracked glass, its flame no longer divine but an echo of a past that flickered like it. The incense box stands empty, dust collecting in its hollowed-out interior. The ritualist traces a circle in the dirt with a stick, the lines crumbling underfoot before they’re fully drawn. Offerings—an old coin, a frayed thread, a photograph of a face that no longer exists—are placed in a hollowed-out log, buried beneath a single, unreadable verse scrawled in blood that’s long since dried.

The words spoken are half-altered: *"By the weight of this, we remember what we forgot."* The voice is a whisper, a static hum. The drum beats irregularly, like a heartbeat of something that once was whole but is now a series of disjointed thumps. The ritual ends when the drumming stops, not because it’s done, but because the drum itself—once a vessel of sound—has stopped resonating. The circle is erased, the offerings left untended. The ritual has been performed, but meaning has dissolved into the dust of time.
