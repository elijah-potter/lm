In the dimly lit alleyway behind the abandoned bookstore, the rain slicked the pavement into a reflective mirror. The flickering neon sign of a closed bar cast long shadows across the cracked asphalt. A figure—tall, clad in a patched-up leather coat—stood near the dumpster, examining a rusted pipe with a toolbox at their feet. The streetlamp’s glow highlighted the hollow eyes of a stray dog curled under the bench, its breath fogging in the cold air. The wind carried the scent of damp wood and old perfume from a nearby hotel. The figure’s boots crunched on broken glass as they approached the rusted bicycle leaning against the fence, its chain dangling. Inside, a half-empty bottle of whiskey sat on a makeshift desk cluttered with crumpled maps and a half-finished sketchbook. The walls, lined with peeling posters of defunct bands, swayed slightly as the wind howled through the open window. The figure paused, staring at the sketch—an unfinished portrait of a woman in a faded red dress, her face half-erased, as if someone had tried to erase her from existence. The pen hovered over the page, the ink still wet, waiting for a second stroke. Outside, the distant wail of a siren cut through the silence.
