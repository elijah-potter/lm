A candle flickers in an empty room, its wax pooling unevenly on a cracked altar of driftwood and stones. The incense burns faintly, smoke curling into the ceiling like a forgotten dream. The chants, once vibrant, now drift in a whisper—words no longer shape meaning but echo like static. Ritualists stand in a circle, their movements stiff, hands raised to an empty sky. The offerings—dried herbs, old coins, a single photograph—are laid out without thought, as if following a script that no longer resonates.

The priestess (or priest) stands at the center, eyes closed, voice a monotone murmur: *"By this fire, by this silence, we reclaim what was lost."* The crowd nods, but their faces are blank. The fire does not burn hotter; it does not consume. The wind outside carries the scent of rain, a reminder of the world beyond this hollow practice. The ritual is complete, and nothing changes.
