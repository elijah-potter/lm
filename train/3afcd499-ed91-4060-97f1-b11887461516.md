The rain pattered against the rusted awning of the old tavern as Elias adjusted his coat, the fabric damp and cold. Inside, the fire crackled low, casting long shadows across the wooden tables. He slid onto the bench beside Mara, his fingers tracing the edge of the half-empty mug before him. "You ever hear the tale of the Blackwood siblings?" he asked, voice low. Mara tilted her head, eyes wide. "The ones who vanished without a trace?"

Elias leaned in, his breath fogging the glass. "Back in the 1890s, they lived in the village of Hollow’s End. Two boys—Tom and Jack—found an old, half-buried chest in the woods behind their cottages. Inside was a map, stained yellow with age, marked with a single, cryptic symbol: a serpent coiled around a dagger. The villagers called it the ‘Devil’s Ledger.’"

Mara’s pulse quickened. "What happened next?"

Elias’ expression darkened. "The boys followed the map to the ruins of an old church, where they discovered a vault sealed with iron. Inside, they found not gold, but something worse: a journal, its pages filled with names—dozens of them—and a final entry, written in blood. It spoke of a pact made centuries earlier, between the Blackwoods and something that walked the earth unseen."

A gust of wind howled through the tavern’s open door. Mara’s breath hitched. "And then?" Elias’ voice dropped to a whisper. "Then the next morning, the village was silent. The boys vanished. The church’s vault was found empty. And the journal? The pages had been torn out, replaced with blank ones. The villagers whispered that the thing they’d found in the vault had taken the boys’ voices, leaving only echoes in its place."

Mara’s hands clenched. "And the Devil’s Ledger?"

Elias’ fingers tightened around his mug. "The villagers kept it hidden. Some say it’s still buried in the woods, waiting for the next fool brave enough—or foolish enough—to dig it up. Others claim the thing in the vault isn’t dead. It’s waiting. And it remembers."

The fire flared, casting Elias’ face in eerie light. "Do you believe in monsters, Mara?" he asked. "Or do you think we’re just the monsters we fear?"
