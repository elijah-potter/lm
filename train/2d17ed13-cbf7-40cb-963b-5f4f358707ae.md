Waiting was not an easy choice. The clock ticked relentlessly, each second dragging like a weight around my neck. The urgency of the task at hand had been palpable—urgent, almost desperate—but then something shifted. A quiet realization settled over me like a fog, seeping into the edges of my thoughts.

I paused. Not because the task was finished, but because the moment itself had become more important. Waiting was not idle; it was a deliberate act of presence. The space between the *must* and the *should* became a sanctuary.

I closed my eyes and breathed. Not for comfort, but for clarity. The air was still, and the weight of expectation lifted slightly. Waiting was not surrender; it was a pause in the storm, a moment to gather my strength, to decide what mattered more than rushing.

Then, when the time was right, I moved—not with haste, but with intention. The world had changed while I waited. The urgency had been tempered, the path clearer. Waiting had not erased the task ahead, but it had made it feel less overwhelming.

And so, I chose to wait. Not because I was weak, but because I was wise.
