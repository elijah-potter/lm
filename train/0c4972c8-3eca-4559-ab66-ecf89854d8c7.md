A corporate meeting in fluorescent-lit hallways where every phrase is scripted, every pause timed. The CEO’s voice—smooth, unblinking—delivers a speech from a slide deck, each statistic a pre-approved number. The audience nods in unison, not because they agree, but because the script demands it. Outside, a city hums, but the room is a bubble of repetition. A colleague’s phone buzzes; they check it, then return to the same, hollowly attentive posture. The moment isn’t just familiar—it’s a *rehearsal* of what we’re supposed to believe, the tension between the script and the real world too fine to ignore. The air smells like stale coffee and the faint metallic tang of the fluorescent lights, a cocktail of control and exhaustion.
