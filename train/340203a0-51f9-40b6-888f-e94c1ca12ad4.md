**The Last Letter**

I remember the way the rain fell against the window—silent, heavy—like the weight of all the things I couldn’t say. The memory of your voice, still warm after all this time, humming low over the phone like a lullaby for ghosts. I kept it tucked in my pocket like a secret, something I swore I’d never lose.

The plan was simple: write it down. Not on paper, not the way they do in books where words bleed into ink, but in code. A script that could remember the shape of your hands when you laughed, the way your fingers tangled in mine like vines in winter. I saved it in a file called *echo*, though I know now that it was just a name for something that would fade if I didn’t keep it running.

The plan was to wake before dawn, when the city slept and the world still held its breath. To type until my fingers ached, to press *save* like it was a prayer. But the first night, the keyboard refused. The script crashed, and with it, the only thing I had left of you. I tried again the next day, but the memory was a ghost—fragments, half-remembered, like trying to name a dream after it’s over.

Now the plan is to burn the file, to let the ashes scatter like snow. But the memory lingers. It’s not just words on a screen; it’s the way your laugh still echoes in my ribs when I close my eyes too soon. I’ll write it again tomorrow. Or maybe not. Maybe I’ll just remember.
