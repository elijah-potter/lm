The once-thick coniferous forest has been reduced to sparse remnants, their needles scattered like gold dust on the cracked earth. The riverbed, once a vibrant ribbon of green, now runs dry, its banks lined with cracked clay and exposed bedrock. The sky is a dull, overcast gray, the air heavy with the scent of damp soil and distant smoke. Wildflowers—pale daisies, brittle grasses—have taken over the once-manicured meadows, their roots tangled in the remnants of old fences. The horizon is broken by the skeletal remains of buildings, their windows like hollow eyes, their roofs caved in by wind and time. The earth trembles slightly, and in the distance, a low, guttural growl echoes through the ruins.
