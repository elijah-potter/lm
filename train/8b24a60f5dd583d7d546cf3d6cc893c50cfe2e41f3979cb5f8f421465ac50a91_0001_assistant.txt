In Montgomery, there were laws about what we now call segregation. The law said black people had to sit on the back of the bus in the "colored" section, while white people could sit up at the front of the bus in the "white" section. Now, if a white rider got on the bus and it was full, the bus driver could ask a black person to give up their seat. This wasn't always true for all buses because the rules could be confusing. But on this day, when Rosa Parks rode the bus, that's what happened. The bus driver asked her and three others to get up from their seats for white riders who did not have a seat. The other three got out of their seats, but Rosa Parks did not.
