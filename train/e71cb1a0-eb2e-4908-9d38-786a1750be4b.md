A lone figure stands at the edge of a crumbling stone bridge, the rain hammering against the rusted railing. The river below is a swirling mass of dark water, carrying unseen debris toward the horizon. Their coat is damp, the hem clinging to their ankles, but they do not notice the cold—they only focus on the single object clutched in their gloved hand: a small, worn mirror.

The reflection in it is not their own. Instead, it shows a girl no older than ten, dressed in a faded dress, her dark hair tangled with twigs. She holds the same mirror, but her reflection is distorted, as if seen through water. The girl smiles, though her lips do not move. Then, without warning, the image flickers—just for a heartbeat—and returns to the figure on the bridge.

The figure reaches into their coat, pulls out another mirror, and this time, the reflection is clear. It is them. The rain stops. The river’s current slows to a trickle. The bridge’s stones, once crumbling, now gleam like polished marble. The figure opens their coat again, revealing a second coat, identical to the first, and then the third, and the fourth.

The river’s waters rise again, and the girl from the mirror appears in the water’s surface, her dress now a tattered cloak. She reaches for the figure, pulling them into the current. The figure’s coat unfolds, revealing a single, silent word on each sleeve: *begin*. The river carries them toward the horizon, where the first mirror was found years ago, half-buried in the sand. The girl smiles again, but this time, the figure does not look away. They step into the water, and the mirrors dissolve into the mist. The river is calm, the horizon still, and the figure stands on the same bridge again, their coat damp, their gloved hand empty. The rain begins to fall.
