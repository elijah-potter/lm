The winding trail through the misty forest was a deliberate choice, each step carrying the weight of deliberate decision-making. The map in her pack marked the shortcut as a straight 1.2-mile detour, but the elder had warned against it—*"The path you seek is not always the one with the fewest paces."* The torchlight flickered against the damp stones, illuminating the way ahead: a forest floor thick with fallen leaves and underbrush, the air heavy with the scent of pine and damp earth. The guardrails were missing, the rocks uneven, the terrain rising into the dense canopy where the light grew thin and the wind whispered through the branches. The choice was made not for speed, but for purpose. This path led to the ruins of the old observatory, where the last records of the celestial anomalies were hidden—not in the immediate, but in the enduring. The first step was taken with hesitation, then resolve. The longer way would mean more time, more effort, but it would be the path that mattered in the end. The forest, indifferent, waited.
