The rain had just eased when the sirens cut through the quiet neighborhood. I was walking home from the store, my umbrella half-open, when the commotion started—car alarms blaring, windows smashing, and the distant shouts of people in distress. I froze, hands gripping my own umbrella, unsure whether to run or stay. Then I saw it: a young man, maybe twenty, slumped against the hood of a black sedan parked on the curb. He was alive, but barely. His jacket was soaked, his face pale beneath the rain-slicked hair. His hands were bound behind his back, the cuffs crunching under his fingers.

The car door opened, and a man in a leather jacket stepped out. He didn’t look at the boy—just checked his phone, then his watch, then the street. "Alright, let’s go," he said, voice flat, like he’d rehearsed it a dozen times. The boy didn’t move. The man kicked him hard in the ribs. The boy gasped, but didn’t cry out. The car doors locked. The man tossed him a pair of gloves, then got in himself. The engine roared to life. The car peeled out of the alley, tires squealing.

I didn’t know what to do. I could have called the cops, but the boy was already gone. Maybe he’d make it. Maybe he wouldn’t. The sirens were fading, but I couldn’t shake the way my chest felt heavy, like I’d swallowed something sharp. The street was empty again, but the weight of that moment stayed with me.
