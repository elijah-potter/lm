AITA for refusing to let my sister use my inherited vacation home?

Okay, this is a bit complicated, and I’m already feeling anxious about posting, so bear with me. It involves my sister, Sarah (38), my late mother, and a house that’s become surprisingly fraught.

My mother, Eleanor, passed away unexpectedly on March 12th, 2023, at her home in Scottsdale, Arizona. She was 78. Eleanor had always owned a small, charming cottage in Carmel-by-the-Sea, California. She purchased it in 1998, after my father passed, as a place to escape and recharge. She rarely let anyone else use it, and always stressed its importance to her personal well-being. The will, finalized in 2020, stipulated that the Carmel cottage was to be divided equally between my sister and me.

Initially, we seemed to be on the same page. We agreed to alternate months, giving each of us roughly six weeks per year. I took June-July 2023, and Sarah was scheduled for August-September. Everything was amicable. However, in late July, Sarah called, visibly upset. She explained that her husband, Mark, had been laid off from his job at a tech firm in San Francisco on July 17th. They were, understandably, stressed about finances and the uncertainty of his job search. She then asked if she could extend her stay in Carmel through October, claiming they needed a "stable, relaxing environment" to regroup.

I felt for them, truly. But I also remembered my mother’s explicit instructions about the cottage's importance as a personal sanctuary. I explained that extending her stay would significantly impact my ability to use the property and that I’m planning a writing retreat there in November. She became defensive, accusing me of being unsympathetic and prioritizing my “selfish hobbies” over her family’s needs. She added that I was always the "golden child," and she was tired of being overlooked.

I tried to reason with her. I offered to help with their job search, connect her with contacts in the area, and even offered to let them stay at my apartment in Denver for a couple of weeks. She rejected all offers, insisting that Carmel was the only solution. She even started hinting that I was deliberately trying to make her life difficult.

My husband, David, thinks I should cave. He says it's just a few extra weeks and that we should be supportive of our sister during a difficult time. He argues that the cottage is just "bricks and mortar" and that family comes first. My best friend, Chloe, is firmly on my side. She says I have a right to enjoy the property as intended and that Sarah’s behavior is manipulative.

Sarah has now started sending passive-aggressive texts about how "lucky" I am and how I'm "hoarding" a resource that could really help her family. She's also started subtly mentioning it to other family members, portraying me as a heartless, unfeeling person. I'm exhausted from the constant emotional pressure. I'm starting to feel guilty, even though I believe I'm doing the right thing. I'm truly trying to be a good sister, but her demands feel unreasonable and her guilt-tripping is exhausting.

AITA for sticking to the original agreement and refusing to let her extend her stay?
