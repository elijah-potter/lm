The abandoned plan was a multi-phase project aimed at integrating legacy ERP systems with a newly developed cloud-based CRM suite. **Phase 1 (Data Migration)** encountered irreconcilable schema mismatches, exposing gaps in legacy system documentation and insufficient vendor support for API integrations. The team’s initial attempt to map fields using ambiguous field names led to data corruption in the ERP’s transactional tables, causing operational disruptions during pilot testing.

**Phase 2 (User Training)** was delayed by conflicting stakeholder requirements. The IT department prioritized security audits, requiring a 6-month hold, while marketing demanded faster rollouts for a pre-launch campaign. Conflicting timelines led to a cascading delay, with training materials not aligned between teams.

**Phase 3 (Go-Live Strategy)** was abandoned after identifying critical dependencies. A third-party analytics tool, deemed essential for real-time reporting, was unavailable due to supplier insolvency. The team resorted to manual data extraction, which became unsustainable. Additionally, management’s shift toward a phased rollout for high-risk departments (HR, Finance) meant the project was deprioritized in favor of a smaller, less risky initiative.

**Final Decision**: The project was terminated to prevent further resource waste. Key learnings included the need for vendor redundancy in critical dependencies and clearer stakeholder alignment early in the planning phase.
