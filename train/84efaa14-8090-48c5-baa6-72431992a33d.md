The observer stands before a shifting landscape where the horizon bends and the ground tilts—no longer solid but fluid, a ripple of light and shadow. The self is no longer fixed; it is the current that moves through the current, dissolving the old self into the flow. The world was once a stage where one stood as an actor, but now it is a mirror reflecting back its own reflection in fractured pieces. The path was a straight line, but the map is a maze of questions: What was seen as truth was only a fragment of a larger, unseen story? The center of gravity has moved, and gravity itself is no longer a constant—it is a dance of forces, each pulling with unseen weight. The eyes that once focused outward now turn inward, not to find the self, but to see how the self has been seen. The language of existence was once spoken in the tones of certainty, now it hums with the silence between words. Perspective is no longer a viewpoint but a spectrum—each observer a filter, each angle a spectrum of light. The old self is a shadow cast by the new, and the new is but a whisper in the echo chamber of what was. The world was a map, but now it is a puzzle of coordinates that only exist in the mind of the one who solves it. The act of seeing was a one-way street, now it is a two-way exchange—each glance a transaction of light and shadow. The ego was the anchor, now it is the current that carries everything else. The question is not *what do I see*, but *what does it see when I am gone*?
