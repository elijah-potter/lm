In a dimly lit alleyway beneath the flickering glow of a streetlamp, a lone figure crouched behind a rusted dumpster, their breath visible in the cold night air. A child’s laughter echoed from the nearby park, followed by the distant clatter of a playground slide. Across the street, a woman sat at a café table, her fingers tracing the rim of an empty espresso cup. She had made a promise—one she never intended to keep. The promise was sealed with a kiss that never quite touched her lips, and the words whispered in a language that slipped away like sand through fingers. Now, every evening, she returns to this same table, the same empty seat, the same half-finished coffee. The city hummed around her, indifferent. The promise, like the alley’s shadows, had grown long and tangled, and she was the only one left to weave its threads back into something resembling truth.
