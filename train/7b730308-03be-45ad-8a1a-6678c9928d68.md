The firelight flickered against the old wooden table, casting long shadows that stretched across the worn carpet. Across from her, Elias shifted in his chair, his fingers tracing the rim of his coffee cup. His gaze lingered on the half-empty mug before meeting hers—*not with words, but with the quiet weight of unspoken truth*.

A book lay open between them, its pages blank except for the faint smudge of ink on the cover. She didn’t need to read it to know what he’d written there: *You were always the one I wanted to understand, even when I couldn’t say it aloud.*

His breath hitched. The air between them thickened, charged with the unspoken. She reached for the book, her palm brushing his sleeve before she realized she didn’t need to touch it. Just the proximity of her presence—her steady silence, the way her shoulders relaxed when she thought he wasn’t watching—was enough to make him lean forward. The fire crackled. The world narrowed to this moment, this space, this unspoken confession.

She didn’t need to say it. He understood.
