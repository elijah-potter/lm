A clock ticked relentlessly, each second stretching into an eternity. The morning sun rose, but the world didn’t wake—just lingered in a half-asleep haze, its edges blurred by fatigue. Coffee, bitter and slow, seeped into the mug, its warmth a fleeting comfort. The commute was a blur of traffic lights and half-remembered routes, time dissolving into static.

At the office, emails piled unread, deadlines loomed like shadows. Meetings dragged on, conversations tangled in the weight of unspoken questions. The afternoon stretched like a stretched rubber band, snapping back to exhaustion by five o’clock.

Home was a refuge, but the evening stretched into an endless cycle of chores, half-finished projects, and the quiet ache of unfinished days. The bed called, but sleep resisted, replaced by the hum of the fridge and the distant hum of the city still buzzing beyond the window.

Evening meals became a ritual of overcooked food and rushed words. The night air was cold, but the body refused to surrender to rest. The clock’s hands circled, indifferent to the weight of a day that felt like a half-lived hour.

Tomorrow would be another loop—same tiredness, same half-hearted efforts. The day had been a loop of exhaustion, and it would repeat.
