**The Lost Letters**
---

In the cluttered desk drawer, half a dozen envelopes remained: one for each day since she stopped writing. Each contained a single line, scribbled in ink that had faded to the color of old parchase tape.

**The List:**
1. *The name of the man who called her "sweet" when he should’ve called her "stupid."*
2. *The address of the café where she’d met him for coffee before he met someone else.*
3. *The number of times she’d cried in that bathroom stall, her nails biting into her palms.*
4. *The exact moment he laughed when she said, "I don’t love you anymore."*
5. *The brand of the perfume he wore that made her stomach twist when she thought she smelled it on her own skin.*
6. *The password to her old email—one she’d never use again.*

She stared at the list like it was a confession, though neither were quite the same. Confession implied repentance; this was just the truth of what she’d carried for years.

The last envelope was different. It wasn’t sealed. Inside, a single sheet of paper, folded so the ink of her confession remained visible:

---
I was afraid of being alone. Not that I *hated* him—just that I was afraid of what I’d become if I let go. And so I stayed. And so I lied.
