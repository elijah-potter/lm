A skeletal foundation of concrete pillars stretches like ribs along a cracked asphalt road, their surfaces etched with rusted bolts and peeling paint. A dust-covered ladder leans against one wall, its rungs bent at the ends. The air smells of damp concrete, oil, and the faint metallic tang of uncompleted steel. Shadows stretch long and uneven across the space, casting jagged patterns on the walls where the plaster is still peeling. The ceiling, far too low in spots, reveals a web of exposed ducts and pipes, some twisted, others still attached to the ceiling’s skeletal structure. A single, flickering fluorescent light bulb hangs precariously from a chain, casting a dim, unnatural glow over the scene. The place is a ghost of what it could have been—abandoned, neglected, and slowly decaying.
