HEALTH WATCH; Poison Ivy Protection
By JANE E. BRODY
Published: June 3, 1992
The 80 million or so Americans who are allergic to poison ivy may soon have a way of preventing the miserable rash short of having to watch their step.
After 20 years of work, scientists at the University of Mississippi have developed a vaccine against urushiol, the chemical culprit in the poison ivy plant.
Dr. Mahmoud A. ElSohly and Dr. E. Sue Watson first isolated the variants of urushiol in poison ivy, oak and sumac and then made chemical variants that instead of causing a rash produced tolerance to the plant-derived originals.
The anti-ivy agent, which has been licensed to Stiefel Laboratories in Coral Gables, Fla., proved safe and effective in desensitizing laboratory animals.
The researchers believe that, if borne out by clinical trials and approved by the Food and Drug Administration, one injection of the vaccine could eliminate or reduce a person's sensitivity for an entire season.
