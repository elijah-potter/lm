The abandoned clock tower at the edge of the Blackwood Forest hums with a rhythm that doesn’t match the sunrise. Its brass hands tick backward, frozen at 3:17 AM, though the sky is still dark. The air smells like damp stone and old machinery, thick with the sound of a distant, irregular heartbeat—each minute a half-second too slow, then too fast. Inside the tower’s hollow chambers, echoes of voices whisper in a language that shifts with the wind. The floor tilts underfoot, creaking like a clock’s gears, and shadows stretch unnaturally, doubling at the edges of every frame. Somewhere, a watchmaker’s apprentice still sits at a broken desk, adjusting dials that don’t exist, their fingers tracing arcs that defy the laws of time itself. The walls pulse faintly, like veins, and if you listen closely, you can hear the ticking not of clocks but of something *alive*, counting down in a language older than the first human clockmaker. The forest outside is a maze of branches that reach toward the tower, their boughs forming shapes that shift between animals, faces, and constellations—constellations that should not be visible at night. The last person to leave said they saw their reflection move in the glass, but it wasn’t them. It was someone else’s reflection, caught in a moment they’d never lived.
