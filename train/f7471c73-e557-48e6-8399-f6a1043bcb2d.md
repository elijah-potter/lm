A dimly lit diner at 3:17 AM. A lone figure, mid-40s, sips black coffee from a stained mug. The neon sign flickers outside, casting an eerie glow. The door creaks open—another customer steps inside, older than the diner itself, dressed in a 1987 wool coat. Their eyes flick to the menu board, then to the clock above the register. The time reads **2:47 PM**.

The younger man freezes. The older one ignores him, ordering a whiskey. The barista, a woman in her 20s, hands the glass without comment. The older man slides into the booth across from him, their fingers brushing the edge of the table. His watch glints—same model as the younger man’s.

*"You remember this place,"* the older one murmurs, voice rough with age. The younger man’s breath catches. *"You used to work here."* The younger man’s hands tremble as he reaches for his coffee. The older man exhales smoke from the cigarette dangling from his lips. *"You forgot. Me, I didn’t."* The diner hums around them, the past pressing against the present like a ghost in the corner of the room.
