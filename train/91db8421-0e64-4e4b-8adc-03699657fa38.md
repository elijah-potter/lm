Changing perspective is the act of shifting one’s viewpoint to better understand or navigate a situation. It often involves examining the same events from multiple angles—self, others, and external factors—to uncover hidden truths or new insights.

For example, if someone feels isolated, adopting a perspective that highlights universal experiences (e.g., everyone faces loneliness) can reduce self-blame. Conversely, observing others’ biases may reveal systemic barriers in communication or judgment.

Perspective change is both intentional and adaptive. It requires curiosity, empathy, and the willingness to question assumptions. Tools like reflective journaling, mindfulness, or even role-playing can facilitate this shift.

Ultimately, it’s a bridge between rigid thinking and flexible understanding—critical for growth, collaboration, and problem-solving.
