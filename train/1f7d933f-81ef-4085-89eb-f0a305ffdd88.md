In dim candlelight, the ancient stone circle hums with forgotten power.
Participants—now hollow-eyed—trace the same ritual steps with mechanical precision: light the fire, chant the same words, place the hollowed-out gourds in the center. The offerings—bloodless now—are placed where they once held meaning: a knife, a feather, a cracked clay vessel.

The wind carries no spirit, only the echo of a language long unspoken. The priestess moves in circles, but her body is a puppet, her voice a hollow drone. The circle’s edges blur, dissolving into mist where the old world once stood.

No one remembers why they do this. The symbols are faded, the incantations meaningless. The fire burns but does not warm. The stones are cold.

The ritual is a skeleton of its former self—just bones left to rot.
