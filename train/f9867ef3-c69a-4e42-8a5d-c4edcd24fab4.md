The door creaked open under the weight of a single, hesitant knock. The sound echoed faintly through the dim hallway, punctuated by the distant hum of the refrigerator. Inside, the apartment was a symphony of half-finished tasks—half-empty coffee cups lined up on the counter, a half-eaten sandwich wrapped in waxed paper, and a half-dressed person sprawled on the couch, fingers drumming the armrest.

The knock came again, slower this time, deliberate. A pause. Then, a voice, low and measured, called out, *"I’m not home."*

A third knock—this one sharper, more insistent. The person on the couch stiffened, their eyes snapping open. The kitchen light flickered on, casting long shadows across the room. Outside, the knock persisted, unrelenting. The door swung open just enough for a figure to step inside, their silhouette framed by the doorway.

*"You’re late,"* the stranger said, voice dripping with false warmth. *"The party’s already started."*
