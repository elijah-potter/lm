## A Counseling Session

**Setting:** A comfortably furnished therapy office. Soft lighting, a view of a small garden.

**Characters:**

* **Dr. Eleanor Vance:** A seasoned therapist, known for her calm demeanor and insightful approach.
* **Liam Walker:** A young professional, experiencing significant stress and difficulty sleeping.

---

**Liam:** (Sitting forward slightly, voice strained) I just… I don’t know where to begin. Everything feels like a mountain I have to climb, and I’m constantly exhausted before I even start.

**Dr. Vance:** (Leaning slightly forward, maintaining eye contact) That sounds incredibly difficult, Liam. It takes courage to articulate that feeling of being overwhelmed. Can you tell me a little more about what those "mountains" feel like?

**Liam:** It's mostly work, I suppose. Deadlines are relentless, expectations are high, and I feel like I'm constantly letting people down, even when I’m working late into the night. Then I come home, and my mind just… it races. I can’t switch off.

**Dr. Vance:** So, the pressure at work is significant, and that pressure is carrying over into your evenings, making it hard to relax. You mentioned difficulty sleeping. Has that been a consistent issue?

**Liam:** Yes, it has. I toss and turn for hours. Sometimes I just lie there, staring at the ceiling, replaying conversations, worrying about mistakes I’ve made. It's a vicious cycle. The lack of sleep makes the next day even harder.

**Dr. Vance:** That sounds truly draining. It's understandable that you’re feeling so exhausted. It's also quite common for work-related stress to manifest physically, impacting sleep. You’re experiencing a very real physiological response to the pressure you’re under. Let’s explore that a little further. Can you give me a specific example of a recent situation that triggered this feeling of being overwhelmed?

**Liam:** Last week, we had a major presentation to a potential investor. I spent days preparing, but I still felt like I was going to fail. When I was presenting, I stumbled over a few words, and immediately I thought, "They're going to think I'm incompetent." Even though my colleagues told me it went well, that’s the thought that stuck with me.

**Dr. Vance:** It sounds like you're holding yourself to a very high standard, Liam. It's admirable to strive for excellence, but it seems that standard is currently contributing to your distress. You're focusing on a minor stumble and interpreting it as a reflection of your overall competence. That's a significant leap in logic. Do you recognize that pattern in how you evaluate yourself?

**Liam:** (Pauses, considering) I think I do. I’m always looking for what went wrong, rather than acknowledging what went well. It’s almost automatic.

**Dr. Vance:** It’s a habit, and habits can be changed. We can work on challenging those automatic negative thoughts. It's about retraining your brain to notice the successes alongside any perceived shortcomings. We can also explore techniques for managing anxiety, such as mindfulness exercises, which can help you stay grounded in the present moment and reduce the racing thoughts you mentioned. How does that approach feel to you?

**Liam:** It sounds… hopeful, actually. I’m skeptical, but I’m willing to try. The constant worry is really taking a toll.

**Dr. Vance:** That’s a very reasonable response. It takes time and practice to change ingrained patterns. We'll start slowly, focusing on small, manageable steps. For next week, I’d like you to simply pay attention to your thoughts when you notice yourself being critical. Just observe them, without judgment. We can then discuss what you observed. Does that feel like something you can commit to?

**Liam:** Yes, I think so. Just noticing, that’s the key, right?

**Dr. Vance:** Precisely. It's about creating space between you and your thoughts, recognizing that they are not necessarily facts. We’re building awareness. And remember, Liam, seeking help is a sign of strength, not weakness. You're taking a proactive step towards improving your well-being.



**(End of Session)**
