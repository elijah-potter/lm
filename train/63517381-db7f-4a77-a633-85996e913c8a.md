## Project Chimera: Rulebook

**A Cooperative Game for 2-4 Players**

**Introduction**

Project Chimera thrusts you into the roles of specialized researchers working for the Global Response Initiative (GRI), a hastily assembled organization tasked with containing a rapidly evolving ecological disaster. A synthetic retrovirus, initially designed for targeted gene therapy, has escaped containment and is accelerating the mutation of plant and animal life, creating unpredictable and increasingly dangerous hybrid organisms – “Chimeras.” The fate of the world rests on your ability to understand the virus’s progression, develop countermeasures, and mitigate the escalating threat.

**1. Components**

*   **Game Board:** A world map depicting key regions experiencing Chimera outbreaks. Regions are interconnected by travel routes.
*   **Researcher Cards (8):** Each card details a unique researcher with specialized skills (Biologist, Engineer, Epidemiologist, Security Specialist, Geneticist, Virologist, Botanist, Logistician).
*   **Chimera Cards (60):** Detail the characteristics, abilities, and threat levels of various Chimera types.
*   **Event Cards (40):** Introduce unexpected crises, opportunities, and complications.
*   **Research Tokens (40):** Represent progress in understanding the virus and developing countermeasures.
*   **Containment Markers (20):** Used to temporarily suppress Chimera outbreaks.
*   **Crisis Markers (10):** Indicate regions experiencing severe outbreaks, triggering negative effects.
*   **Virus Progression Track:** Measures the overall advancement of the retrovirus, triggering increasingly severe consequences.
*   **Region Markers (12):** Indicate the current Chimera presence in each region.
*   **Dice (2d6):** Used for skill checks and event resolution.
*   **Rulebook (this document).**

**2. Setup**

1.  Place the Game Board in the center of the playing area.
2.  Shuffle the Chimera Cards and place them face down in a draw pile. Reveal the top three Chimera Cards and place them face up beside the deck, forming the “Active Chimeras” display.
3.  Shuffle the Event Cards and place them face down in a draw pile.
4.  Place the Virus Progression Marker on the starting space of the Virus Progression Track.
5.  Place one Region Marker on each designated starting region (indicated on the Game Board).
6.  Each player chooses a Researcher Card.
7.  Each player draws two Research Tokens.

**3. Turn Structure**

Each turn consists of the following phases, completed in order:

1.  **Event Phase:** Draw the top Event Card and resolve its effects. Some Event Cards may require dice rolls.
2.  **Chimera Phase:** For each region containing a Chimera, roll a d6. On a 4+, the Chimera spreads to an adjacent region, placing a Region Marker in that new location. If a region already has the maximum number of Region Markers, place a Crisis Marker instead.
3.  **Researcher Action Phase:** Each player, in clockwise order, performs two actions. Available actions include:
    *   **Move:** Move your Researcher to an adjacent region.
    *   **Research:** Spend a Research Token to draw two new Research Tokens.
    *   **Analyze Chimera:** Spend a Research Token to examine one Active Chimera. This allows you to view the full details of that Chimera's card.
    *   **Contain:** Spend a Research Token and roll a d6. On a 4+, place a Containment Marker on the region. Containment Markers suppress Chimera spread in that region for one turn.
    *   **Develop Countermeasure:** Spend two Research Tokens. Choose one Active Chimera. Discard the Chimera Card and draw a new one to replace it. This represents a breakthrough in understanding the virus.
    *   **Share Knowledge:** Give one Research Token to another player in the same region.
4.  **Virus Progression Phase:** Advance the Virus Progression Marker one space. Certain spaces on the track trigger immediate negative effects (e.g., increased Chimera spread rate, introduction of new Active Chimeras).



**4. Win/Loss Conditions**

*   **Winning:** The players win if they successfully develop a global countermeasure. This is achieved by discarding a specified number of Active Chimera cards (indicated on the Virus Progression Track) before the Virus Progression Marker reaches the final space.
*   **Losing:** The players lose if the Virus Progression Marker reaches the final space on the Virus Progression Track, or if a predetermined number of regions (indicated on the Game Board) are marked with Crisis Markers.

**5. Advanced Variants & Mechanics**

*   **Resource Trade-Off: Containment Priority:** When placing a Containment Marker, players must choose between suppressing Chimera spread (preventing new outbreaks) or reducing existing outbreaks (removing Region Markers).  Each action requires a different dice roll threshold, forcing a strategic choice with uncertain outcomes.
*   **Uncertainty & Specialization: Chimera Mutation:**  When a new Active Chimera is drawn, a die is rolled. On a 1 or 2, the card is “Mutated.”  A Mutated Chimera has an additional, negative effect described on a secondary table, representing unpredictable evolutionary jumps.
*   **Information Asymmetry: Research Specialization:** Each Researcher Card grants a bonus to a specific action. However, players can only fully utilize their specialization if they share information about the current Active Chimeras. This encourages communication but also creates a trade-off: revealing crucial details might alert the virus to the team's progress.
*   **Scaling Difficulty:**
    *   **2 Players:** Reduce the number of Active Chimeras displayed at the start of the game to two.
    *   **3 Players:** Play with the standard setup.
    *   **4 Players:** Increase the starting position of the Virus Progression Marker by one space.



**Designer’s Note**

Project Chimera is designed to be a challenging and engaging cooperative experience. The game’s mechanics are intended to simulate the complexities of responding to a global crisis, emphasizing the importance of communication, strategic planning, and adaptability. The escalating threat and uncertain outcomes are meant to foster a sense of urgency and encourage players to consider the ethical implications of their decisions. The educational goals include promoting critical thinking about scientific processes, understanding the interconnectedness of ecological systems, and appreciating the value of collaborative problem-solving. The game is not intended to provide definitive answers but rather to stimulate discussion and inspire further exploration of these important topics.
