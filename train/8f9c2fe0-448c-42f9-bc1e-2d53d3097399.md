The discovery of **graphene** in 2004, a single layer of carbon atoms arranged in a two-dimensional honeycomb lattice, revolutionized materials science. Its exceptional mechanical strength (~100 times that of steel), thermal conductivity (~5 times that of copper), and electrical conductivity (up to 10^6 times higher than copper) demonstrated its potential for next-generation electronics, flexible sensors, and lightweight composites. This finding triggered a wave of research into 2D materials, leading to innovations like graphene-based batteries, transparent conductors for touchscreens, and ultra-thin, stretchable electronics. Its simplicity—just a few atoms thick—made it a cornerstone for breakthroughs in nanotechnology and beyond.
