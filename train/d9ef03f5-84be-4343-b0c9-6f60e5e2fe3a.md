In the quiet of an autumn evening, when the city slept beneath a cloak of twilight, Clara found herself standing at the edge of the park’s forgotten garden. The overgrown paths whispered secrets to the wind, and the only sound was the distant chorus of crickets. She had come here to escape the noise of her own thoughts, to find solace in the unspoken words of nature.

The garden was unlike anything she had ever seen. Instead of the usual flowers, the earth was dotted with strange, silvered petals that shimmered faintly under the dim light. Clara crouched, plucking one delicately. As she touched it, the petal dissolved into a swirling mist, revealing a message written in an unfamiliar script: *"Why do we seek answers when the question itself is the answer?"*

She had read the book in her father’s study—*The Hollow Echo*—but nothing in her life had prepared her for this. The words felt like a blade, sharp and sudden, slicing through the silence between her questions and her silence. The garden’s air grew colder, and the silver petals began to glow, pulsing like a heartbeat.

A voice, neither human nor entirely inhuman, echoed through the mist: *"You carry the weight of what you do not yet know."* Clara stumbled back, her breath ragged. The voice was gone, but the garden seemed to hold her gaze, unblinking, as if waiting for her to answer.

She knelt again, her fingers trembling. What would she say? The question hung between them, heavier than any answer she could offer.
