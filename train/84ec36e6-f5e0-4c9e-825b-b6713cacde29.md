A quiet, mist-shrouded library with ancient books stacked in towering shelves, their spines worn smooth by centuries of handling. The air hums with the soft murmur of whispers, the occasional clatter of a quill pen. Seating arranged in low, cushioned benches along the walls, each with a small lantern casting long shadows. Some spaces have empty chairs where visitors once sat, their thoughts lingering like the scent of aged parchment. A central fireplace crackles softly, casting flickering light on maps and sketches pinned to the walls. The ceiling is high, with stained-glass windows allowing just enough light to read by. Occasional visitors pause to jot notes in journals, lost in the quiet of reflection. The library is both a sanctuary and a space of connection—where ideas meet, forgotten questions linger, and the act of thinking is both solitary and communal.
