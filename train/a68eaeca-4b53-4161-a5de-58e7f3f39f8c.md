A flickering candle on an altar draped in faded fabric, its wax pooling unevenly. The incense burned out long ago, its scent replaced by the stale air of neglect. The participants, a scattered group, recite phrases in a language no one understands—words carved into a worn stone tablet, their meanings buried beneath layers of time.

The first motion: a slow, deliberate tracing of the sigil on the altar with a cracked chalice filled with water from a dried-up well. The second: a whispered mantra, half-remembered, sung by someone who doesn’t believe it anymore. The third: the act of passing the chalice to the next, each sip tinged with the bitterness of doubt. The final one is silence.

No more offerings. No more blood. Just the echo of a ritual that once bound the earth and sky, now just a shadow of itself—an empty promise left to rust.
