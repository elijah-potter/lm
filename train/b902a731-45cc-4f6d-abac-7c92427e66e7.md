## Explain Like I'm Five: Why the Sky is Blue

Okay, imagine you have a big box of crayons. You have red, yellow, blue, green, and lots of other colors! Now, imagine the sun is like a giant crayon box, but instead of crayons, it sends out sunshine. That sunshine isn't just one color, though. It's actually *all* the colors mixed together! It’s like when you mix all your crayons together – you get a muddy brown, right?

But when that sunshine comes to Earth, something special happens. The air around us, the stuff we breathe, is made of tiny, tiny pieces. They're so small you can’t even see them!  Think of them like tiny little bouncy castles, very, very small.

Now, when the sunshine bumps into those bouncy castles, the colors get scattered. Some colors, like red and yellow, are big and strong and mostly go straight through. But blue is a little bit smaller and bouncier. It likes to bounce *everywhere* when it hits those tiny bouncy castles in the air!

Because blue bounces around so much, it spreads out all over the sky. That’s why when you look up, you see blue! It's just blue light bouncing around everywhere.

Sometimes, when the sun is setting, like when it's almost bedtime, the sunshine has to travel through *more* of that air.  Because it has to travel so far, the blue light gets bounced away even more, and we start to see the other colors, like orange and red, that weren’t bouncing as much before.

So, the sky is blue because blue light is the bounciest and likes to spread out when it hits the tiny bits of air all around us! It's like a giant game of bouncy-ball, and blue is the winner!
