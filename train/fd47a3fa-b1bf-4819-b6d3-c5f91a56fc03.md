The receptionist nodded but kept her lips pressed together, her gaze flickering toward the corner of the office where the printer hummed quietly. *"Just checking in,"* she said, her voice barely above a whisper. *"We’ve had some delays with the order."*

The intern, mid-bite on a sandwich, paused mid-chew. *"Oh, yeah? Well, we’ve got a last-minute change in scope—"*

*"Not the order,"* the receptionist corrected sharply, her tone shifting from distracted to annoyed. *"The *contract*."*

The intern’s fork clattered onto his plate. *"Right. So—"* He exhaled sharply, fingers drumming against the table. *"Look, we’ve been working on a new feature for the API. We’re swapping out that legacy endpoint for something more scalable. It’s not a big deal—we’ll have it ready by EOD."*

The receptionist’s eyes narrowed. *"The one that’s been failing since last month?"* She tapped her pen against her desk. *"And the client’s already got a deadline."*

The intern’s shoulders slumped. *"We’ve got a 92% success rate on the new one. We’re just rolling it in parallel for testing. It’s not like it’s going to crash the whole system."*

The receptionist’s pen hovered over the keyboard. *"Parallel testing is fine. But the contract says ‘fully tested and operational’ by Friday. And the legacy endpoint is still up."*

A beat of silence. The intern leaned in, voice dropping to a whisper. *"We can push it out tomorrow. We’ll have it signed off by—"*

*"We can’t,"* she cut in, voice steadier than she felt. *"The client’s already on a call with their legal team. They’re not going to budge on this. And if we don’t deliver the old endpoint by tomorrow, they’re going to pull the plug entirely."*

The intern’s stomach twisted. *"So… we’re stuck with the old one?"*

*"Until we have a proper replacement,"* the receptionist said, finally meeting his eyes. *"And that’s not going to happen today."*

He exhaled, rubbing his temples. *"You’re right. I just… I didn’t realize how much the client would care about this part."* He hesitated. *"Can we at least get the new API out of beta? We can run it in parallel for a week, then phase it in."*

The receptionist studied him for a long moment. *"And if it fails?"*

*"Then we fix it,"* he said flatly. *"But we have to give them something—some guarantee that we’re not just winging it."*

A pause. Then, the faintest flicker of a smile. *"Fine. We’ll have the new API ready by EOD. But if it’s not stable by Monday, we’re replacing it entirely."*

The intern nodded, relief washing over him. *"Deal."*

The receptionist clicked away, but her mind was already racing. *"And the contract?"* she asked, voice sharp.

*"We’ll get the old endpoint back to them by tomorrow,"* he said, already pulling up the ticket. *"But we’re not signing off on this until we know it’s solid."*
