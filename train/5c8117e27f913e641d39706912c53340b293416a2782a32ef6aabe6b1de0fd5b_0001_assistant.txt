- Down syndrome is a condition in which a person has an extra chromosome.

- Typically babies are born with 46 chromosomes, but babies with Down syndrome are born with an extra chromosome 21.

- The extra chromosome affects how the baby's body and brain will develop and can cause mental and physical challenges.

- Another name for Down syndrome is "Trisomy 21". "Trisomy" refers to the extra chromosome, and 21 is the actual chromosome that has two copies.

- Down Syndrome affects about 1 in 700 babies in the United States.

- There are three types of Down syndrome: Trisomy 21 (95% of cases, described above), Translocation Down syndrome (the extra chromosome is attached to another chromosome), and Mosaic Down syndrome (some cells have three copies instead of 2). 

- Symptoms of Down syndrome include mild to moderately low IQ, slow speech, flattened face, almond-shaped eyes that slant up, short neck, small ears, white spots on the iris of the eye, small hands and feet, poor muscle tone or loose joints, short height, and a tongue that tends to stick out.
