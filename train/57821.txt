Rats are sneaky little creatures—you can find them darting behind trash cans or creeping through alleyways. But you probably NEVER expect to find one staring up at you when you take a seat on the toilet.
Terrifyingly enough for urban dwellers, this occurrence is nothing new. Washington D.C.'s Rodent Control receives a couple of complaints each year about this nightmare of an encounter happening in city homes. (Do a search on YouTube, and you'll see just how common it truly is).
By now you're probably wondering how on Earth can a rodent manage to swim through sewage pipes all the way up to a toilet bowl. As it turns out, it's really not that difficult for the squeaky pests.
In a National Geographic video, it's made clear that swimming is like second-nature to the rodents.
"Rats have incredible stamina," the narrator says in the clip. "They can tread water for three days straight, and they can hold their breath under water for three minutes."
As for how they get into your sewage drains in the first place, it's important to remember that a sewer is a rat's paradise. There's plenty of food being flushed down the drains, and yes, this includes that goldfish of yours that lived for a sad 24 hours before you tossed it in the toilet.
It's only natural for rodents to want to explore different pipes, especially since their piercing sharp nails and pliable bodies makes it easy. Their travels can lead them to tiny drains, in which they wiggle through to eventually reach your toilet bowl.
But don't freak out just yet—there's a way to prevent a sewer rat taking over your bathroom. Simply close the toilet seat after every use, and make sure to flush right before you open it for another tinkle.
