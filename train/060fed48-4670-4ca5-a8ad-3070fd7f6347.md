A cracked mirror in the hallway reflected a stranger’s face—one with hollow cheeks and hollow eyes—moving too fast, too close to the glass. The shadow of a coat draped over the back of a chair was knitted with the same fabric as the one the dead man in the attic wore last winter.

The kitchen clock’s hands spun in reverse, chiming midnight for the third time that hour. The coffee machine hissed, as if it had been waiting, while the sink filled with water that smelled of ozone and old decisions. A newspaper headline flickered in the corner: *"Missing since 1987."* The date was today.

The door creaked open—again. The same key turned the lock, but this time the handle turned without the weight of a body. The floorboards groaned under the weight of someone who had once been here, but not anymore. The scent of burnt toast lingered in the air, and the stove was warm, as if it had just been used.
