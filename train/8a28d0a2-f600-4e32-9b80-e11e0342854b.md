In the hollow of the old oak’s gnarled embrace, where sunlight fractured like stained glass, a moth with wings the color of faded ink settled. It was not the first to seek refuge here, nor the last, but this one lingered just past the usual time—its glow a dying ember against the dark.

The wind carried the scent of damp earth and something older, something the trees themselves held breathless. The moth’s antennae twitched, sensing the call of the rustling leaves, the whisper of unseen things that moved when no one looked. It did not know fear, only the hunger for what it could not name.

Then, the shadow stretched.

Not of the moon, but of a man who had walked this path before, his footsteps heavy with secrets. The moth did not flee. It knew better than to run. Instead, it unfolded its wings and let the dark pull it deeper into the oak’s belly, where the air was thick with the scent of something sweet and ancient, something that made the moth’s heart sing.
