The morning light spilled through the curtains like a slow, deliberate breath, painting the room in gold. The way the coffee steamed in the morning, curling into shapes no one could define—swans, perhaps, or the faces of those long gone. I noticed the pattern the second I sat down, the way the steam formed a small, symmetrical circle before dissolving into the air. It wasn’t just the coffee. The way the walls absorbed light at different angles, shifting like shadows, had always been there, but I’d never *seen* it until that moment.

The pattern was in the cracks of the floorboards, how they split into thin, parallel veins under the sunlight. And then there were the reflections—how the mirror in the hallway didn’t just mirror me, but *watched* me, as if the glass itself had a memory. I traced the edges of the shadows cast by the lamp, how they stretched and bent like fingers, waiting to be named.

I thought of the way the clock on the wall—an old, ticking beast—always moved just a fraction slower when I wasn’t looking. The pattern was everywhere. The way the streetlights flickered in sync with the hum of the city, the way the cracks in the pavement aligned with the shapes of the trees outside. I traced them all with my finger, and for the first time, I realized they weren’t just there—they were *waiting*.
