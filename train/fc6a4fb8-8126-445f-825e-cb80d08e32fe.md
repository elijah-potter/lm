Incomplete knowledge often forces trade-offs between certainty and risk. For example, a healthcare provider might diagnose a patient based on partial symptoms, balancing diagnostic confidence against potential misclassification risks. In logistics, routing decisions depend on real-time traffic updates rather than static data, introducing variability in delivery times.

Decision-making under uncertainty requires probabilistic modeling, heuristic adjustments, or iterative refinement. In AI, algorithms like Bayesian networks or Monte Carlo simulations can estimate outcomes given incomplete inputs, though they may still yield probabilistic results rather than definitive answers. Contextual constraints (e.g., time, resource limits) further shape the trade-off between accuracy and feasibility.
