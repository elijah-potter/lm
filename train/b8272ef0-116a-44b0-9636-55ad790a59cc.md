The static hummed in the old radio as the last of the autumn leaves crunched underfoot. *"Beware the Hollow Watchers"* whispered the crackling voice from the speaker. The words were familiar, but no one had spoken them aloud in decades. My father had once told me of the warnings, of how the forest itself seemed to hold its breath when the clock struck midnight. I grabbed my torch and the rusted compass from the chest, my fingers trembling as I checked the time: 23:57.

The path ahead was slick with dew, the trees standing like skeletal fingers against the pale moonlight. My boots made no sound on the damp earth, but the wind carried something else—an echo of laughter, sharp and distant. I exhaled sharply and adjusted my grip on the compass. The needle spun wildly before settling on an unnatural angle: *southwest*, the direction my father had warned me never to face alone. The compass was a relic, but it was the only thing that had ever worked. Now, it was telling me the truth.
