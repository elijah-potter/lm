The sunlight flickered through the stained-glass window like a dying ember, casting jagged gold across the cracked floorboards. The air smelled of damp wool and old coffee, the kind that lingers in places where time has settled into the fabric of a room. His fingers traced the edge of the half-empty whiskey glass—smooth, cool against the calloused skin. The words he’d written earlier in the notebook were still there, but now they felt like a distant dream.

*Clarity*, he thought, *was just another word for the moment before the noise returns.* The clock ticked. The door creaked. The weight of what he’d said—what he’d *thought*—now pressed against his ribs like a stone. The ink on the page blurred. The glass was half-empty, but the whiskey inside had long since diluted into something unrecognizable. The clarity of that moment was already slipping, dissolving into the hum of the city outside, the distant laughter of someone laughing too loudly, the way the light bent and stretched toward the horizon like smoke.
