The office hummed with low conversation as Sarah leaned back in her chair, arms crossed, watching her colleague Mark pace near the window. His fingers tapped nervously against his thigh, eyes darting between the documents spread across the desk.

"You know, the client might cancel if we don’t deliver on time," Mark said, voice rising slightly. "I’ve been thinking—what if we cut the extra features? Just the core functionality."

Sarah tilted her head, lips thinning. "Cutting features? That’s risky. The client specifically asked for the analytics dashboard."

Mark exhaled sharply. "I know, but the budget’s stretched thin. We’re already over by twenty percent. What if we… *simplify*? Just the basic version of the dashboard? They’ll get the core data, and we can add the extra stuff later if they’re still interested."

Sarah’s eyes narrowed. "You’re suggesting we lie to them. What if they ask for the full version and we can’t deliver?"

"Then we’ll tell them we’re still working on it," Mark said, grinning. "We’ll say it’s a phased rollout. They’ll trust us."

Sarah stood abruptly, chair scraping. "You’re a real genius. I’ll draft the email—we’ll tell them we’re focusing on the core and can expand later." She turned toward the door, but paused. "And Mark?"

"Yes?"

"Don’t overthink it. Just play along. If they start pushing for more, we’ll say no."

Mark’s grin widened. "Deal." He cracked his knuckles, already imagining the first lie being delivered.
