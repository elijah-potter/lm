The candle burned low on the altar’s black marble, its wax pooling in a cracked basin. The incense—once rich with sacred herbs—now smelled of damp paper and old coffee grounds. The priest stood rigid, his robes torn at the elbows, the prayer book’s pages yellowed with age.

*"Today, we honor the forgotten."*

He chanted in a voice that had lost its resonance, words strung together like broken glass:
*"By this flame, by this dust,*
*We bind the old to the new.*
*Let them remember what they’ve lost."*

The congregation moved in a slow, mechanical circle—each step heavier, each gesture stilted. The high priest’s hands trembled as he pressed a ritual knife to a child’s wrist, drawing a shallow line. The blood ran slow, clotting on the altar.

*"The past is a shadow now,*
*but shadows cast light when spoken."*

Silence. Then, the final act: the offering. The priest placed a single acorn in the fire. It sizzled, then died, leaving only ash. The crowd exhaled—no joy, no sorrow, just the weight of a ritual that had once been sacred, now a hollow echo.

*"Tomorrow, we will do it again."*
