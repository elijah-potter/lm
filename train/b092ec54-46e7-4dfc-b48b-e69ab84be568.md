The once-thick forest now stands as skeletal ruins, its ancient trees reduced to gnarled, blackened stumps. The earth, once rich and fertile, is cracked and barren, streaked with dust and faint traces of mineral deposits. A slow, creeping mist lingers, obscuring distant outlines and casting everything in a sickly, eerie glow. The air hums with an unnatural, low-frequency drone, like distant machinery or unseen life. Rivers, if they once flowed freely, have dried into shallow, stagnant pools, their surfaces rippling with invisible currents. The sky above is a pallid, ashen gray, punctuated by occasional, jagged formations of what might once have been clouds—now twisted, metallic, and unnatural. The ground shifts beneath bare feet, revealing layers of exposed rock and strange, pulsing veins of light. Far in the distance, faint lights flicker like dying embers or distant fire. The horizon is blurred, as though viewed through a distorting lens. No birds sing, no insects buzz, only the occasional, mechanical drip of water or distant, rhythmic breathing.
