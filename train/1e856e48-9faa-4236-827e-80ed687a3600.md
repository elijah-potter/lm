A dimly lit chamber with flickering candles, their wax pooling on rusted brass altars. The air hums with the distant sound of a dying radio, static hissing like forgotten prayers. A single, cracked bowl holds a broth of what once was water, now thick with sediment. The central figure, half-asleep, clutches a tattered prayer scroll, its pages torn where ink has long since faded to gray. The steps are mechanical: the first is to recite a name—now just a whisper—from a list no one remembers. The second is to pour the broth into the bowl, which now contains a rock the size of a fist. The third is to wait, arms crossed over a chest that no longer beats. The ritual ends when the candles burn down to nothing, the bowl spills its contents, and the figure stands still, watching the smoke rise into the hollowed-out ceiling. The cycle repeats, but the absence of meaning is the only truth left.
