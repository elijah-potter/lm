The discovery of **quantum tunneling** in 1927 revealed that particles could traverse energy barriers where classical physics forbade motion, fundamentally altering atomic and particle physics theories.

Later, **graphene** (2004) demonstrated a carbon lattice where electrons behaved as massless fermions, enabling ultra-fast electronics and flexible electronics that could revolutionize computing and energy storage.

The **CRISPR-Cas9** gene-editing tool (2012) enabled precise DNA modification, potentially curing genetic diseases and accelerating biotechnology advancements.

**Superconductivity at room temperature** (2020) breakthroughs in high-temperature superconductors could revolutionize energy transmission, transportation, and computing efficiency.

Each discovery—from tunneling to CRISPR—reshaped scientific paradigms, though practical applications remain in development phases.
