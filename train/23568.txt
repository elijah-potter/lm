History of the Chess Board
The origin of the chess board is a matter of some debate. It is thought that the game originated in Persia around 600 AD and then travelled to Europe in the 10th century. Although there are references to square game boards dating back to ancient Egypt, the modern chessboard with its alternating dark and light squares first appeared in the early 15th century.
The boards have changed very little over time, so any modern chessboard can be considered a descendant of those original designs. Each traditional wooden chess board has 64 alternating light and dark squared cells which form an 8 by 8 grid. The squares are often made up of two different colors such as black and white, although there are variations which feature different shades for each square.
In recent years, many boards have become quite ornate with some even featuring cherry wood frames or engraved artwork on the squares, while others just use simple plastic tiles set into an ordinary checkerboard. Chess pieces also come in a variety of sizes and materials including wood, marble or even metal for a more industrial look.

In this article, we will cover different sizes of chess boards and their characteristics:
A standard chess board consists of an 8×8 grid with alternating dark and light-colored squares. The 8×8 grid is divided into 64 individual squares, each taking up one inch of space. Each player starts the game with 16 pieces – 8 pawns, 2 rooks, 2 knights, 2 bishops, a queen and a king – which are placed on the board in their respective positions. The sizes of these pieces vary according to the number of squares they can move across the board during a single turn. For example, a knight typically moves two squares in one direction and then turns at a perpendicular angle to move one further square in its new direction; thus it occupies three total squares during its move.
The standard size for all chess pieces is approximately two inches or five centimeters tall. As for height above the board itself, this depends on its material and construction but can range from very flat boards designed for travel to those with thicker pieces meant to be used with sturdier tables or stands. Nonetheless, no matter the thickness of pieces used or the type of material comprising them, all chess sets should still be roughly two inches tall when set upon their respective grids.
In addition to the standard eight-by-eight board, chess sets come in a variety of sizes. A ten-by-ten board is great for long games, while a smaller six-by-six size is perfect for quick games or for beginners who are just learning the game. Chess clocks provide an effective way to keep players from taking too much time on their moves. A chess clock typically consists of two separate digital or analog clocks side by side, one for each player. At the beginning of the game, each player presses a button on his or her respective clock to begin their move, which stops the opponents timer from running. When the move has been made, the button is pressed again which starts the opponent's clock and stops player's own. This prevents one player from using up too much time and consequently extending a game longer than necessary.
Many types of chess clocks have now been developed and are available to suit different types of players. You can choose between analogue or digital clocks, with features such as delay, sudden death and tournament modes. These additional features can help to create an even playing field by setting a limit on how much time each player has to make their moves within specific games and tournaments – ensuring that all participants are playing on an even platform before beginning play.

A chess board must be maintained if it is to be properly used. Proper maintenance of a chess board includes cleaning it regularly, storing it properly, and ensuring that the pieces are not damaged. Other than that, one must also replace the pieces if they are lost or broken.
In this section, we will discuss the different ways of maintaining a chess board properly:
Cleaning your chessboard is very important in preserving the life of its wooden pieces. To start, gather a few essential items such as a soft lint-free cloth, wooden polish (such as Old English or Howard), mild dish soap, a dry erase eraser and water.
Before you begin, make sure the board is free of any dust and dirt. Take a cloth and gently wipe down each square and along the edges of the board to make sure there are no residue or debris left on it.
Once it is ready to be cleaned, mix mild dish soap with lukewarm water. Dip your cloth in this solution and lightly go over the chessboard with it to clean away any dust or grime that may have settled into its nooks and crannies. Make sure to get enough pressure so that any dirt will be removed but not too much so that no board pieces come away from its surface.
After it has been wiped clean, use a dry erase eraser to buff out any marks or scratches on the surface of the chessboard that may have occurred during play. When you're done buffing out these marks, take a wooden polish such as Old English or Howard and using a soft lint-free cloth rub in small circular motions until you cover every inch of your board's surface – up until its shine shines again!
Finally let your board dry completely before playing again by allowing some air circulation around each side which should take around 30 minutes-1 hour depending on how dampened it becomes through your cleaning process!

Frequently Asked Questions
Q1: What is a chess board?
A1: A chess board is the most important piece of equipment used in the game of chess. It is an 8×8 checkered board with 64 squares of alternating colors. The colors are usually black and white.
Q2: What are the dimensions of a chess board?
A2: Generally, a chess board measures 8 inches by 8 inches and contains 64 squares of alternating colors.
Q3: How do chess pieces move?
A3: Each chess piece moves differently. Pawns move one square straight forward and capture one square diagonally forward. Knights move in an “L” shape, two squares in one direction, then one more square to the side. Bishops move diagonally any number of squares. Rooks move horizontally and vertically any number of squares. The Queen moves any number of squares diagonally, horizontally and vertically. Finally, the King moves one square in any direction.
