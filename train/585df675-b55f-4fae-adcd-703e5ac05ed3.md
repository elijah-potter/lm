The rain lashed against the cracked windows of the old farmhouse as Elias sat at the kitchen table, fingers tracing the rim of his empty mug. The lantern flickered, casting long shadows across the worn wooden floor. His father’s voice echoed from the other room: *"You promised me you’d help with the harvest before the storm hits."* Elias had promised—just to keep the peace after the argument. But now, the words felt like a trap.

His father was already bending over the sacks of grain, his calloused hands moving methodically. Elias watched as the old man’s face crinkled with effort, his breath shallow. The promise had been made in anger, but now, the weight of it pressed heavier than the rain. The lantern’s flame dimmed, and for the first time in years, Elias wondered if he’d ever truly known what he was fighting for.
