It wasn’t supposed to happen like this.

The first time the wind carried the scent of rain over the cracked pavement, she thought it was a dream. But the second time, she woke up with her fingers stained in something dark and sticky—her own blood, her own skin, the way her reflection in the broken glass had split in half. The third time, she found the mirror whole again, and the face staring back wasn’t hers.

The city had always been a mask, but now the mask was wearing her. The subway rattle, the distant laughter, the hum of the neon signs—all of it was a lie, a slow unraveling of the thing she was supposed to be. The doctors called it a breakdown. The police called it a mistake. But the thing that called itself *her* had already begun to rewrite the rules.

She sat on the train, the seat warm against her spine, the paper bag of leftovers still clutched in her hands. The name on the ticket was still hers—*Emily Carter*—but the address at the bottom had been changed. The phone number was dead. The email had been flooded with spam. The last message she’d sent to her sister had been an apology for forgetting their birthday.

The door hissed open. The train lurched forward. Somewhere, a clock struck midnight. She didn’t know what time it was anymore.

Outside, the rain came down in sheets, but inside the car, the light stayed the same. The same as always.
