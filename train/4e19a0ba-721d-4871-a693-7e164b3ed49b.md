**The Last Letter**

I wrote to my mother last week, though the postmark bears the date of the storm’s end. She’d sent me her favorite recipe—tarte aux pommes with a hint of cinnamon—along with a postcard of the harbor in winter. The ink had run dark as the sea, and I folded it carefully, my fingers tracing the edges like a map I’d never cross again.

She never mentioned the letter she’d been meaning to write for months. Not about the doctor’s report, not about the way the old house had creaked last night, not about the name on the gravestone that felt too close to call. She’d just said, *"You’ll be back soon,"* and left it at that. I should have known then that some truths were meant to stay buried.

Now, the ink on my own letter has faded before it hit the mailbox. The postmistress gave it back with a shake of her head. *"You’ll need to redo that part where you mention the address."* I did. But the words stuck to my tongue like a bad dream.
