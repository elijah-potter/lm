The dim glow of a single streetlamp flickered against the rain-streaked windows of the old bookshop. Clara adjusted her scarf, her fingers tracing the spine of a leather-bound volume before setting it down. Across the aisle, Daniel’s hands hovered over the keyboard of his laptop, his posture rigid—like a man who’s been holding his breath too long.

A pause. Not because they spoke, but because they *understood*.

The door creaked open, and in stepped a stranger—no name, no purpose beyond the way their eyes lingered on Clara’s before moving to Daniel. He didn’t ask questions. He didn’t need to. The air between them hummed with something older than words: a shared hunger, a mutual dread of what came next.

Clara’s breath hitched. Daniel’s fingers stilled. The stranger tilted his head, as if listening to something just beyond the sound of the city. The shop’s bell chimed faintly, as if the world itself had paused to watch.

Then—silence. The stranger vanished into the night.
