The primary duties of state attorneys general include representing state governments in legal actions and protecting the public through criminal and civil litigation. They are also empowered to issue opinions to guide the legal actions of public agencies. Typically, attorneys general are chosen by voters in statewide elections. However, in a minority of states, they are appointed by governors or selected by state legislatures and supreme courts.
Most criminal charges, such as for homicide and robbery, begin with local district attorneys. State attorneys general, however, handle state and federal criminal appeals. They draft appellate documents, such as briefs and motions, and appear for oral appellate arguments. Some state statutes also grant attorneys general the power to initiate certain criminal charges. For example, anti-trust and consumer protection cases may originate with state attorneys general. Many such cases carry both criminal and civil penalties.
Consumers can file certain civil complaints with state attorneys general for investigation and potential legal action. For example, citizens who believe companies violated state consumer protection laws may lodge complaints with their attorneys general. State attorneys general, however, are not required to receive complaints before filing civil lawsuits. For example, attorneys general may sue lenders for consumer fraud based on their own initiatives. Any damages collected from such lawsuits can be returned to harmed consumers as restitution.
Child Support Enforcement
Generally, state attorneys general have varying duties for child support collections. In Michigan, for example, the attorney general has the authority to track absentee parents and prosecute individuals who fail to pay court-ordered child support. In some states, such as Texas and Florida, attorneys general also distribute, review, and adjust child support payments. They may also have the power to issue paternity orders following voluntarily paternity acknowledgements or genetic tests.
State attorneys general are charged with various, ethical duties along with their legal responsibilities. They are expected to uphold the highest standards of professional and personal integrity and must exhibit commitments to the best interests of their communities and states. They are also expected to work congenially with other attorneys general when appropriate. State attorneys general are held accountable to professional and ethical standards by state law and professional associations, such as the National Association of Attorneys General.
