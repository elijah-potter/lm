A lone figure stands before a crumbling stone wall, fingers tracing the cracks like a geography of forgotten words. The wind carries no sound—only the distant hum of a city that no longer exists. Their breath forms mist in the cold, unspoken. A single candle flickers, casting long shadows of figures that aren’t there, their voices drowned in the absence of sound. The air is thick with the scent of damp earth and old paper, the kind of silence that holds more than it lets go. The figure turns, their gaze meeting an empty eye socket in the wall, where a mirror once hung. Now it’s just a hollow reflection of what was never said.
