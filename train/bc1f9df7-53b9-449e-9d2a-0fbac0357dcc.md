The rain had been relentless, turning the cobblestone streets of Eldermere into a slick, reflective maze. Dr. Elara Vey stood at the crumbling stone archway, her gloves damp, her breath misting in the cold morning air. Her fingers trembled slightly as she adjusted the folded note in her pocket.

*"I apologize for the delay in reaching you."* She read it aloud, the words trembling just slightly, like they had been written by someone who knew they would regret it. The note was from her brother, Silas, and it was addressed to her—though she had not seen him in over a decade. The ink was smudged, the paper worn thin, as if it had been pressed against something heavy before it was slipped into her hand.

She had come here to apologize for the years they had lost, for the secrets she had kept, for the way she had let her own fears shape the ones she held dearest. But as she stepped inside the old library, the scent of aged parchment and candle wax overwhelmed her. The shelves stretched endlessly, each book a whisper of forgotten stories and forgotten lives.

Silas stood before her, his posture rigid, his expression unreadable. He was taller than she remembered, his hair longer, streaked with silver where she had left him. His eyes were cold, but there was something else—something like the ghost of a smile playing at the corners of his mouth.

"You came," he said, not a question, but a statement. His voice was rough, like gravel underfoot.

Elara swallowed hard. "I didn’t know if I’d be forgiven."

Silas exhaled sharply, a sound that might have been laughter or sorrow. "Forgiven?" He shook his head, running a hand through his hair. "There’s nothing to forgive."

A silence settled between them, thick and suffocating. The library’s grand chandelier cast long shadows, twisting the air into something almost tangible. Elara reached into her coat pocket and pulled out a small, silver locket. Inside, she saw a portrait of their mother, her eyes wide with love, her smile faint but unbroken.

She opened the locket carefully, the mechanism clicking into place with a whisper. "She would have wanted us to find each other," she said, her voice breaking. "She always said we were too stubborn to realize how much we needed each other."

Silas watched her, his gaze softening just slightly. "You think so?"

"I know so."

He reached out, his fingers brushing hers, and for the first time in years, she felt something warm inside her chest. The past was a weight she had carried alone, but now, it was lighter. The locket clicked shut, and she turned to face him, her heart pounding.

"You know," she said softly, "I’ve been trying to write you a letter for years. But I never knew where to start."

Silas smiled, and for a moment, the weight of the past felt like it had lifted. "Write it here," he said, gesturing to the crumbling floorboards beneath them. "We don’t have to finish it today."

Elara nodded, her smile fading as she remembered the letter she had left unfinished. The words were still there, tangled in her memory, waiting to be set free. But this time, she would finish it. And maybe, just maybe, she would finally understand why she had run so far.

The library’s grand doors creaked open behind them, admitting a gust of cold wind. Someone was coming. Silas stepped forward, his voice low.

"We should go," he said. "But not before we make a deal."

Elara looked at him, her heart pounding. "A deal?"

"An old one," he said, his voice dark with something she couldn’t name. "Something our mother always said we were too stubborn to accept. But maybe," he added, his eyes gleaming in the dim light, "it’s time we were."

Elara’s breath hitched. She had a feeling she knew what he was talking about. But she would find out. Together.
