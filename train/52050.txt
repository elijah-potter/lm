Health officials in the US are to meet next month to decide whether to recommend mandatory HIV tests for dentists and surgeons. The meeting follows last week's announcement by the federal Centers for Disease Control that a dentist in Florida had infected at least three of his patients.
The case raises new questions about the risks of becoming infected with HIV during medical and dental treatment. Apart from the Florida cases, there have been no confirmed reports of any doctor or dentist transmitting the virus to a patient.
Researchers admit that they are baffled by the cluster of infections, and they cannot be sure what the route of infection was. They suggest that the dentist could have cut or punctured one of his fingers while it was in a patients mouth. Or he could have used a contaminated instrument.
