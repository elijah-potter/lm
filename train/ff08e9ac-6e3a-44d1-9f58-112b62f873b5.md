A mist-shrouded valley led me toward an ancient stone bridge, its arches worn smooth by centuries. The first destination was marked by a crumbling sign: *"Turn left toward Eldermere."* But the bridge itself bent like a living thing, its path shifting beneath my feet. The sign vanished into the mist, replaced by a flickering torch in the distance—*"Follow the river to the north, but watch the currents."* The river’s course twisted unpredictably, its banks merging with the cliffs as if the land itself whispered new directions.

By the time I reached the first fork, the second sign had already been erased: *"The choice was never yours—it was the wind’s."* The wind carried a voice in my ear: *"The mountain bends. The sky shifts."* I followed the trail upward, only to find it leading down again, through a forest where trees grew backward, their roots spiraling toward the sky.

A village appeared—a ghost town where houses had no roofs, their windows staring blankly at the sky. A child pointed at the horizon: *"They say the next stop is where the stars remember you."* The stars, though fixed, pulsed like a heartbeat, and suddenly, the village dissolved into a constellation of shifting constellations.

I landed on a floating platform, suspended above an endless ocean. A map unfolded beneath me: *"This is not a map. It is the path."* The platform tilted, and the ocean’s surface rippled with waves that formed new coastlines every second. The destination was always the next curve, the next whisper, the next illusion.

A hand grabbed my wrist—warm, familiar. *"You’re not lost,"* it said. *"You’re just learning."* The hand dissolved into light, and the platform tilted again. The next sign read: *"The end is a beginning."* The ocean swallowed me whole, and when I opened my eyes, the horizon was a different sea, a different sky.
