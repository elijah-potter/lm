In the dim glow of a fluorescent-lit office, Sarah sat hunched over her keyboard, fingers flying over the keys. The hum of the printer behind her desk was the only sound, drowning out the murmurs of the cubicles around her. She had just turned twenty-two, but her face was older—still had that hollow look, the kind that came from years of pretending to be someone she wasn’t.

The walls of the company’s modern glass building had been painted white, but the space felt suffocating. She had been here for six months, hired for her degree in political science, but the job title—"Customer Experience Strategist"—had been a joke. The company’s real business was data scraping and algorithmic manipulation, and Sarah’s role was to keep the clients happy while the actual work was done by a team of interns who had no idea what they were doing.

She had chosen this city for the job, not for the people. The city had its own rhythm, its own way of moving—always ahead, always calculating. She had arrived late, her bags stuffed with the clothes she had bought online, none of them fitting well. Her hair had been dyed a shade too dark, her nails painted a color she had never seen before. She had tried to blend in, but the effort had been exhausting.

Across the room, a group of men in sleek suits laughed over coffee, their voices sharp and confident. Sarah had once been one of them. She had been a junior associate at another firm, sharp and ambitious, until a mistake—one small, one she had tried to cover up—had unraveled everything. Now, she was here, a ghost in a room full of people who didn’t see her.

Her phone buzzed. Another message from her old friend, the one who had called her "darling" for years. The text was short: *"You look like hell."* Sarah had replied with a single word: *"Thanks."* She had tried to move on, but the weight of it still lingered. Every time she walked into this office, she felt it—the pressure of being invisible, of being someone who didn’t belong.

She had come to this city to escape her past, but the past had followed her. And now, she was just another face in the crowd, another name in the database, another ghost in the machine.
