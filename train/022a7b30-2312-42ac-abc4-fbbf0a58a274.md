The rain had turned the city into a silvered mirror, reflecting the dim glow of flickering streetlamps. Across the alley, a handwritten note was taped to the doorframe of *The Last Bookshop*, the address scrawled in faded ink:

**"I’m sorry. I didn’t mean to disappear."**

The sign above the shop’s single window now read: **"Closed—until further notice."** Inside, the shelves were half-empty, dust motes swirling in the dim light. The counter, once polished to a shine, bore the faintest imprint of a single handprint—a tear in the varnish where the owner’s own had once rested.

A customer stepped inside, their boots damp with the same rain that had fallen for days. The shopkeeper, a man in his thirties with a silver streak in his once-black hair, turned slowly. His voice was quiet, raw with guilt.

*"You’re not the first to find me here. Or gone."* He gestured to the empty shelves. *"But you’re the first to ask."*
