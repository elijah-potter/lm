Starving on the sands of his desert island, he began to see beings. Bright in the silver sunlight, you might mistake them for mirages. But they stood on the white coral sands and left delicate footprints.
One night, he dreamed that an angel strayed into his consciousness. "There are messages left for you on the beach," she declared. But when he awoke and wandered seawards, he found no letters scratched on the strand, just a single, sodden paperback.
He started to read it aloud for companionship. Then, one night, he dreamed of a heavenly scriptorium. "There are messages left for you on the beach," he dreamt.
The angels, who listened to his recitations, smiled at him benignly. Clothed in shining light, they met him at the water's edge. There, he found a dark box, open and full of books. He stood it on one edge and wedged in shelves of driftwood. This would be his library.
The angels delighted in persuading him to read aloud. He was allowed peace only if he entertained them with a chapter each day. But what dreams it gave him! He began to hunger for each novel. He lusted for new ideas from the scholarly texts. Even the almanacs and manuals were greeted as friends.
He always read aloud. The angels would devour his words.
When the launch grounded, he had gone. But the rescue party found his bleached bones and an old packing crate filled with driftwood and wrinkled paperbacks, eaten away by silverfish.
