The winding mountain trail ahead stretched into the mist, its surface slick with dew, every step demanding deliberate weight. The path marked by jagged rocks and deep gullies, a narrow ribbon of light cutting through the overcast sky. The compass in my pocket showed an unmistakable turn—left would lead to the village, its distant hum of life a comfort, a place to rest. But the arrow pointed right, deeper into the forest, where the map’s edges blurred into silence.

I exhaled slowly, the tension in my shoulders easing just slightly. The choice wasn’t just about distance; it was about what came after. The village had its own kind of exhaustion, the hum of its people a steady, familiar drone. This path, though longer, was the one where the wind carried the scent of pine and something sharper—old books, damp earth, the quiet persistence of things that refused to be hurried.

My boots crunched over fallen branches. The forest exhaled around me, its leaves rustling in a rhythm slower than my own. There was no turning back. The trail ahead was no longer a choice—it was the next breath.

I set my pace, letting the weight of the decision settle into my bones. The longer path would take longer, but it was the one that whispered *wait*.
