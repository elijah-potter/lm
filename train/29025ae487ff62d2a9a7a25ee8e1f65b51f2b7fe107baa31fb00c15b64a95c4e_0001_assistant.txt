While there are a great many scenic and interesting places to visit in Washington and Oregon, these four locations will provide you with a sense of the landscape and culture of the region. 

1. Orcas Island, Washington. Orcas Island is one of the San Juan Islands in the Puget Sound. A visit to the island can be a day trip, or you can overnight there. Traveling to Orcas by ferry allows you to take a car or not. Or you can fly by small plane. The island holds much of the charm of Western Washington in a small concentrated space. 

2. Cannon Beach, Oregon. Cannon Beach is one of the most beautiful places on the Pacific Ocean in Oregon. Many charming restaurants and shopping areas can be found in and around Cannon Beach as well as opportunities to hike and commune with nature. This can be a day or overnight since it is just a few hours from the cities along the Interstate 5 corridor. 

3. Columbia River Gorge. The Columbia River forms the border between Washington and Oregon. When you leave Portland on Interstate 90 headed east, you will drive from the green rainforest of the west side of the Cascade Mountains to the dry desert and irrigated farmland of the east side of the mountains. Opportunities to see Mt. Hood and Mt Adams will be likely on a clear day as well as what's left of Mt St. Helens, which erupted in 1980. Stay on the Oregon side of the river because a stop at Multnomah Falls and a stop in Hood River are worth the time they take.

4. Columbia River Wine Region. As you leave the Columbia Gorge, you will be on the border of Eastern Washington and Eastern Oregon. Turn north into Washington on Interstate 84 toward the town of Kennewick, and you drive right into some of the finest wineries in the United States. Spend an afternoon or days on end exploring this wine region and the many local wineries where you can relax with a glass of red or white, whichever you prefer. 

There are so many places to see and things to do in these two amazing states. Enjoy!
