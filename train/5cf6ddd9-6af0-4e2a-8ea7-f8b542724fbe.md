The rehearsal of a moment begins with the repetition of actions—each step, each glance, each word, all cast in a familiar, unchanging script. The air hums with tension, a static charge of anticipation. Your breath is shallow, a rhythm too precise to be natural. The scene unfolds like a well-rehearsed dance, every gesture measured, every pause calculated. The world around you blurs into a neutral backdrop, a stage where the script demands you play a part—one that you’ve played so many times before, the lines feel like second skin. The moment isn’t about spontaneity; it’s about the absence of it. The past and future collapse into a single, endless loop of performance. The only difference between now and then is the weight of this time, as if the universe is holding its breath, waiting for the right moment to repeat itself.
