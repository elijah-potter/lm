The alarm blared at 3:17 AM, a sound so sharp it cut through the sleep of the city. A single line of text flashed across the screen of Elias’s smartwatch: **"ALARM: DO NOT OPEN THE DOOR."** His breath hitched. The notification wasn’t from his phone; it came from the old, rusted metal box buried beneath his grandmother’s attic. He had ignored it before. But this time, the static in his earbuds—his mother’s voice—whispered, *"You’re not supposed to be up."* Elias shoved his hands in his pockets, the weight of the box pressing down on him like a curse.

He had found it last month, half-buried in mold and forgotten papers. The key, a tarnished thing with a crack in the lock, had been hidden in his father’s old leather briefcase. His father had died three years ago, and Elias had been told the box was "family business." But the last time he’d touched it, the air inside had smelled like damp earth and something older—something that made his stomach twist. Now, the alarm was different. The letters were jagged, as if scrawled in haste. The message had changed.

*"They’re coming for you,"* the text read, though Elias had never seen the handwriting before.

The door creaked open behind him. The scent of iron and old wood filled the hallway. His mother stood there, her face pale, her fingers trembling. "You shouldn’t have opened that," she said. "Not like this."

Outside, the wind howled through the broken windows of the abandoned factory. The door to the attic yawned open, revealing a staircase spiraling into darkness. At the bottom, a figure in a long, black coat stood motionless, watching. Its face was half-lit by the flickering glow of a single bulb, but Elias could see the way it held its breath, waiting. The alarm on his watch pulsed again.

**"RUN."**
