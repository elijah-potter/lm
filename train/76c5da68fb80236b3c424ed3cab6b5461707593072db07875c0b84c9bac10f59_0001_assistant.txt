The village had faded away. Our companions, too, all melted into that shimmering haze.
Ahead, as though an apparition, the Great Baobab arose out of the bleached earth. Its shadow sheltered us from the fierce sky. Its succulent fruit nurtured us, slaked our thirst.

At dusk, I massaged your mother's swollen belly with shea-nut oil. Ebony skin, smooth, glistening. You stirred beneath my fingers.

We awaited the Baobab's message. Your song, Daughter, The Song of Belonging, your song for your life.

On the third morning, the soft hum of your mother's voice awakened me, as though a small swarm of bees had alighted amongst us. Rhythm, words, and then your name came forth from her mouth. Abedash, she-who-shines.

We sang the song that night, her belly a pillow for my head. I sensed you within, the song becoming you and you the song.

Back in the village, we shall lead the tribe into your song. Their voice will become one, and you will come to know them. You will arrive in a place both of knowing and being known.

And so it is, Shining-One, that your song will envelop you - at womanhood, at marriage, at childbirth, and at your end-time when you are ancient and fading.

Impatient for your coming, Daughter, I have run ahead of myself. The village is now only just coming into sight; a sea of faces filled with anticipation await our return.
