**The Collection**
The list was never meant to be read aloud. It was meant to be held, felt, and then cast aside like a bad dream. The ink had smudged before the paper could catch it, but the words still clung—stubborn, like a memory refusing to fade.

One night, I found it curled in the pocket of an old coat left by the riverbank. The coat itself was tattered, its edges fraying as if it had been worn by many hands, each one carrying a piece of something unspoken. The list inside was a fever dream: a language of half-remembered fragments, each one a promise, a warning, a whisper from a world that had moved on without them.

I traced the words with my fingers, the paper damp with dew. The list was incomplete—some items were written in a script I didn’t recognize, others in fragments of a language that had long since been erased from the world. The most haunting part? The ones that didn’t make sense until you closed your eyes and let the darkness do the rest.

That’s when the first clockwork bird began to hum. Not from its chest, but from the space between my ribs, where something had always been missing. The others followed soon after: the letters, the glass bridge, the candle. They came in waves, each one more insistent than the last, as if the list had a will of its own.

Now, the list is in my pocket again, but this time, I keep it close. Not because I believe in its words, but because I know what happens when you ignore the things that refuse to be ignored. The ghosts don’t sleep. The letters don’t stay lost. And the bridge? It’s already built. I just need to step through.
