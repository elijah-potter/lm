A decision deferred often lingers like a half-remembered dream,
its weight shifting between fear and doubt, where hesitation becomes its own kind of rule.
Time, that silent arbitrator, stretches the uncertainty like taffy,
each passing hour rewriting the cost-benefit calculus in unseen ink.
The mind, caught in the crossfire of what could be and what might be lost,
folds the question into a tapestry of *maybe*—a fragile thread of indecision
that snaps or stretches, depending on whether the decision is worth the fray.
In the end, it’s not the choice that defines the person, but the *waiting*—the quiet,
the unspoken calculus of "when I’m ready," which is never quite ready.
