The old lighthouse keeper’s hands trembled as he adjusted the brass lantern’s wick. The storm had come without warning, a gale howling through the cliffs like a dying beast. Inside the dim room, the flickering light cast long shadows on the walls, each one whispering secrets of the sea.

Outside, the waves crashed violently against the crumbling stones, their thunder drowning out the distant cry of seabirds. The keeper’s wife, Clara, sat by the hearth, her fingers tracing the worn edges of the family Bible. She had been waiting for this moment for years—the day the storm would reveal what the tide had hidden for decades.

The lantern burned low, and the keeper’s vision blurred. He reached for the hinge that held the old wooden chest—his father’s chest—buried beneath the floorboards. His fingers faltered. The wood was cold, the hinges rusted, but he forced himself to lift the lid. Inside, a single letter glinted in the dim light: *"They are coming. Meet us at midnight."* His father’s voice, muffled by time.

Clara’s breath hitched. She had known this day would come. She had spent her life watching, waiting, for the storm to finally expose the truth buried beneath the waves. The keeper’s eyes widened as he realized the letter was not from his father but from *them*—the men who had taken his wife years ago, the ones who had drowned their children to keep their secrets buried. The storm was not just nature’s fury; it was their doing.

The keeper’s grip tightened on the lantern. He had spent his life believing the sea was the only thing he could not control. But now, as the wind howled through the lighthouse’s broken window, he understood: the truth was not beneath the waves. It was in the air, in the storm itself, in the way the waves *pulled* at the shore, *begging* to be heard.

He turned to Clara, his voice raw. "We’re not done yet."

The lantern flickered. The storm outside roared. And somewhere, beneath the waves, something answered.
