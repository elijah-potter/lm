A stranger in a crowded café, their coffee cup forgotten mid-bite into a sandwich, eyes darting between the room’s murmur and their own reflection in the glass—every face, every glance a silent judgment. The hum of conversation fades into a sharp, hollow ache as their name, half-said in a foreign accent, echoes off the walls. A hand taps their shoulder, an invitation to join, but their body lingers in the corner like an afterthought, fingers twitching toward the door, the keyhole of a room that doesn’t know their name. The weight of being unseen, unneeded, a ghost in the crowd, presses against their ribs. The last thing they see is the waitress’s smile as she walks away, leaving them with the ghost of a smile of their own.
