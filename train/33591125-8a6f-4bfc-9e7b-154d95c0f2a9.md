The old clock tower’s pendulum swayed in time with the city’s forgotten breath. Outside, the rain pattered against the cobblestones like a forgotten language. Inside the crumbling study, Elias adjusted the brass lamp, casting flickering shadows across the dust-covered desk. The ledger—his mother’s last surviving journal—lay open on the parchment, its ink smudged with age and tears. *"Memory is the map,"* she had written, *"and plans are the compass."* He traced the words with his thumb, the ink still faintly vibrant beneath the grime.

The first page was a list: *"Things to Remember."* Names—*Mira, the thief with the silver tongue; Dr. Voss, the alchemist who’d vanished without a trace; the river where the drowned girl’s bones still rusted the stones.* Each name carried weight, not just as facts, but as echoes of what they *had* been. He closed the book, but the words coiled in his chest like a serpent, writhing with the ghost of what might have been.

Then came the second list: *"What to Do."* It was simpler, less haunted. A sketch of a map—rough, drawn in pencil—marked with a single, bold question: *"Where is the Blackthorn?"* The alchemist had left no clues, no warning. Only a vial of liquid gold, half-empty, and a warning: *"The memory of the place will fade. The plan must be made now."* Elias exhaled sharply. The memory was his. The plan was his too.

The study door creaked open. A draft curled through the cracks, carrying the scent of damp earth and something older, something *wrong*. Mira’s voice, sharp and amused, whispered from the hallway: *"You’re not just remembering, boy. You’re *living* what you’re afraid of."* He turned, the lamp casting her silhouette against the wall—too tall, too still. She leaned against the doorframe, arms crossed, her reflection flickering like a candle in the dim light.

*"You’ve got thirty minutes,"* she said. *"Before the river remembers you’re gone."* Outside, the rain stopped. Silence. Then, the distant sound of bells—tower bells. The city’s pulse, counting down. Elias looked at the map. At the memory. At the plan. And for the first time in years, he smiled. The weight of the world wasn’t just on his shoulders. It was *his*.
