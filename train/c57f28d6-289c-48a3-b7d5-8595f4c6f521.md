A dimly lit chamber, its walls lined with faded tapestries depicting forgotten gods and half-remembered battles. The air hums with the low, rhythmic drone of a single, cracked incense burner. A bowl of blackened offerings sits on a stone altar, its contents spilling unevenly onto the obsidian floor. The ritualist, a gaunt figure in tattered robes, kneels beside the altar, their fingers tracing a cracked, ancient symbol carved into their palm.

The first step: **The Naming**. They whisper the name of the lost god aloud, not knowing if it still exists, but repeating it anyway—*"O’Dun-Kharr, the Eternal Hunger, the Flesh That Never Sleeps."* The sound echoes, hollow, like a dying breath.

Next: **The Feeding**. They press their tongue against a hollowed clay bowl, drawing out the residue of a forgotten sacrifice—a once-human hand, now stiff with time, its fingers curled around a rusted knife. They lick the blade clean, taste the iron and blood, and then press it to their lips, swallowing the memory of a life that no longer matters.

The third step: **The Offering of Silence**. They sit cross-legged, hands folded over a chipped bone flute, and play nothing. The silence is deliberate, a prayer to the void. They close their eyes, but the visions they see are not of the god, but of themselves—childhood, betrayal, the slow rot of meaning in a world that no longer answers.

Finally: **The Naming of the Nameless**. They take a blade and press it to their throat, not to die, but to write. With trembling hands, they press the blood-smeared symbol into the palm of their other hand—the same symbol carved into their own flesh, the one that once bound them to the old gods, now just a mark of their own madness. They whisper the god’s name again, louder this time, but the answer is the same: *"There is no answer."*

The incense burner dies. The chamber falls silent. The ritual is done. The ritualist stands, watching the blood dry on their palm, the symbol bleeding into the shadows. They walk away, but not before glancing back—just in time to see the altar’s offerings dissolve into the stone, the god’s name written in the dust like a promise never kept.
