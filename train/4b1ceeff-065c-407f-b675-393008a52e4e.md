The familiar scent of damp earth and old wood greeted me upon entering the library. The worn leather of the armchairs still held the faint memory of past conversations, and the hum of quiet typing filled the air. The once-bright shelves now bore a dusting of cobwebs, but the spines of books—some cracked, some still crisp—lined the aisles like silent sentinels. The grand staircase, once a throng of students, now leaned slightly under the weight of decades, its polished marble edges reflecting the muted light of a single, flickering lamp. The café at the corner had a new owner, whose coffee was a bit stronger, but the aroma of cinnamon and freshly ground beans clung to the air like nostalgia. The walls held faded posters of past exhibitions, and the old typewriter on the corner desk was still there, waiting. The library had changed, but the way it *felt* had not.
