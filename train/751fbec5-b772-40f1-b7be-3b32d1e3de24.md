The clock in Grand Central’s tower struck midnight, casting a silver glow over the crowd. The air smelled faintly of rain and old wood, the last embers of a thousand forgotten dreams. She stood at the edge of the platform, her coat collar turned up against the chill, her breath misting in the cold.

A single, crumpled ticket lay abandoned near her feet—*Chicago to New Orleans*, dated the day before. The number *1734* was scrawled in ink across the back in jagged letters, as if someone had been in a hurry.

She picked it up, the paper crisp beneath her fingers. The ticket was too new to be worth much, but the number felt different—like a key to something she’d never expected to find.

A train whistle blew in the distance, a distant echo of the city’s restless pulse. She pocketed the ticket, her reflection blurred in the glass of a nearby window. Outside, the streetlights flickered to life, painting everything in eerie blue.

Then she noticed the streetlamp’s reflection in the rain-slick pavement: a figure standing motionless at the corner, watching her. The air turned colder. The ticket slipped from her grasp.

The last thing she saw was the number *1734* written in blood on the sidewalk.
