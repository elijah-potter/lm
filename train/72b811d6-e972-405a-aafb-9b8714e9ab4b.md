A man arrived late for his father’s funeral, clutching a crumpled note: *"I’ll return by dusk."* The priest had called it a broken promise, but the note had been his father’s last words—*"The river’s tide is strong tonight; find me where the moon touches the reeds."* He followed the riverbank past the graveyard’s shadows, the reeds whispering as he knelt. Beneath the eerie glow of a half-moon, he found a small, silver locket—engraved with the same date as his father’s death. Inside was a photo: a man, laughing with a woman, their hands intertwined at the edge of a misty lake. The note inside read: *"She waits for you there."* He had misunderstood the promise—it wasn’t about returning; it was about finding what the father had been trying to keep hidden. The lake shimmered like a promise left unspoken, and for the first time, he saw the truth: some promises aren’t broken—they’re waiting.
