Researchers have found that certain substances in our blood can tell us a lot about Alzheimer's disease (AD). These substances, called blood-based biomarkers, have the potential to completely revolutionize how we diagnose and predict the progression of AD in clinical practice. Currently, there are new treatments being developed for AD that target a protein called amyloid-beta (Aβ). Blood tests can now measure another protein called phosphorylated tau (p-tau) in our blood. These tests are very accurate in distinguishing AD from all other neurodegenerative diseases in people with cognitive problems. By checking the levels of p-tau in the blood, doctors can also predict if someone with mild memory problems will develop AD dementia later on. This helps them identify people who are at risk of getting the disease early on and potentially intervene with treatments before the disease progresses. Using these blood tests in specialized memory clinics would reduce the need for expensive tests like cerebrospinal fluid samples or positron emission tomography. These blood-based biomarkers are already being used in clinical trials to find people who have very early AD, before they even have symptoms. In the future, regularly measuring these blood biomarkers over time will help doctors and researchers see if new treatments or lifestyle changes are helping to slow down or stop AD.
