AITA for refusing to let my sister use my inherited vacation home?

Okay, this is a bit complicated, so bear with me. It involves my sister, Eleanor, my late grandmother, and a property that’s become a significant source of stress.

My grandmother, Vivian, passed away last October (2023) after a long illness. She lived in a charming, somewhat secluded cottage in Mendocino, California. She’s always been fiercely independent and, frankly, a little eccentric. For years, she’s told both Eleanor and me that she intended to leave the cottage to us jointly. This was always presented as a shared inheritance, a place for us to enjoy together.

Eleanor and I are quite different. I’m a software engineer, living in Seattle. I value peace, quiet, and a structured schedule. Eleanor is a freelance photographer based in Los Angeles; she thrives on spontaneity and social interaction. We’re generally amicable, but our lifestyles rarely mesh.

Around November 2023, shortly after the will was read, Eleanor started asking about using the cottage. Initially, it was casual – "Could I maybe use it for a photography workshop in the spring?" I said yes, absolutely, but suggested we coordinate dates and ensure it worked for both of us. I was eager to share the property and create positive memories.

Then, in January 2024, things escalated. Eleanor proposed hosting a week-long “creative retreat” for ten of her photographer friends in June 2024. She’s been advertising it online, charging each participant $1,500. She presented this as a done deal, saying she’s already collected deposits from several people. When I voiced concerns – about the cottage’s limited capacity, the potential for damage, and the disruption to the area's tranquility – she dismissed them. She claimed I was being “controlling” and “selfish” for not wanting her to utilize the property as she saw fit.

I explained that Grandma Vivian specifically bought the cottage for its isolation and serenity, and that hosting a large group of strangers for profit felt disrespectful to her memory. I suggested she could use it for smaller, personal gatherings, but not for a commercial enterprise. I even offered to help her find a suitable rental property in the area if she needed a space for her workshop.

My mother, who’s been mediating this situation, feels I’m being unreasonable. She says Eleanor is “just trying to be resourceful” and that I’m “holding onto the property too tightly.” Mom’s perspective is that Eleanor is just trying to make the most of a valuable asset, and I’m being overly sentimental. Eleanor, predictably, echoes this sentiment. She argues that I’m “punishing” her for wanting to use the inheritance. She’s even hinted that she might contest the will if I don’t relent.

Honestly, I feel like I’m being steamrolled. I’m trying to honor my grandmother’s wishes and protect a place that holds significant sentimental value. I’m not trying to be difficult, but I’m also not willing to let my sister turn the cottage into a mini-resort without any consideration for its character or my feelings. I'm worried about the long-term effects of commercial use on the property and the surrounding community. I feel like I’m being pressured into something I’m not comfortable with. I fear that allowing this to happen will irrevocably damage the cottage’s charm and my relationship with my grandmother’s memory.

So, AITA for refusing to let Eleanor use the cottage for her photography retreat?



NTA
