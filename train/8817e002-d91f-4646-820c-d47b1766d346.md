A flickering neon sign in the abandoned subway tunnel pulsed *blue* as the rusted train wheels groaned over jagged rebar. The air smelled of ozone and damp concrete, but the scent of fried bread and warm coffee from the diner across the tracks lingered—too vivid, too *present* for the hollow, echoing silence of the tunnel. The girl at the counter, her dark hair pulled into a tight bun, wiped her hands on her apron and whispered, *"You’re late."* She knew the voice—his accent, the way he always fidgeted with his cufflinks when he was nervous. His shadow stretched long across the floorboards, but he wasn’t here. The door behind her groaned open, and in stepped a man in a 1987 leather jacket, his face half-hidden under a mirrored sunglass case. *"I was waiting for you."* The girl’s breath hitched. The clock on the wall read **3:17 AM**.
