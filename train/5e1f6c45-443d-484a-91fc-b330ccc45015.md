The auctioneer’s gavel cracked sharply over the packed room as the last bidder—an elderly man in a tattered overcoat—raised his hand. The auctioneer paused mid-sentence, his voice cracking: *"Last one…"* The crowd murmured in anticipation. The elderly man whispered, *"Wait."* The auctioneer scowled. The man held up a crumpled receipt from the local diner, a receipt dated two days earlier, listing a used 1978 Chevy pickup in "as-is" condition. *"This was bought yesterday,"* he said, *"but the rules changed. Now, the seller’s responsible for any damage."* The auctioneer’s expression darkened. *"You’re not the seller."* The man sighed. *"I’m the buyer."* The room erupted. The auctioneer’s gavel struck again. *"This ends here."* The crowd split—some cheered, others booed—but the man stood firm. The pickup was sold. The rules were rewritten on the spot.
