The last light in the village flickered out as Elias knelt beside the old oak, its bark etched with forgotten symbols. The weathered hinge groaned, the weight of centuries pressing down on its rusted teeth. Inside the hollow, a single candle burned—its flame trembling as if afraid to reach the dark beyond.

He pressed his palm against the metal. The hinge creaked, then sighed. A whisper, not of wind but of memory, curled through the gap: *"Turn the other side."* Elias did not hesitate. The hinge turned. The oak groaned, splitting into two halves, one collapsing inward, the other rising like a door into the earth.

From the darkness behind him, a voice—soft, ancient—spoke: *"The key was never the door."* The candle’s light vanished. The ground trembled. Something watched him from the shadows.
