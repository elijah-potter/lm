The last time I saw the crumbling stone archway, its once-white paint peeling into dust, the scent of damp earth and aged wood clung to the air like a ghost. The overgrown ivy wrapped around its jagged edges, swallowing the words of ancient inscriptions. The path beneath my boots was uneven, pockmarked with the weight of decades, leading to the moss-stained bridge that arched over the blackened river. Its iron rungs were rusted through, the once-vibrant colors of the bridge’s paint faded to a sickly green. The river’s water was a murky brown, carrying with it the whispers of the past—broken glass, rusted nails, the occasional driftwood. The distant sound of the wind through the skeletal trees made the air feel colder than it should have been. The silence was heavy, as if the place itself held its breath. The final memory I’ll take with me is the way the light caught the water’s surface, reflecting a ghostly blue before vanishing into the depths.
