The memory flickered like a candle in the wind—half-forgotten, half-buried in the dark corners of time. A decision made in haste, a word left unsaid, the ghost of a chance not taken. The moment passed, swallowed by the tide of action, leaving only a whisper: the weight of what *almost* could have been.

The silence afterward was a lie. The regret, though sharp, was buried under layers of distraction—work, laughter, the hum of the city. No one noticed the way their shoulders tensed when they thought no one was looking, the way their hands trembled when they thought no one would care.

Somewhere, a mirror still holds the reflection of that stolen second—cold, unreadable. The past is a river, and regret is the stone thrown in to block its flow. Most of it sinks unnoticed.
