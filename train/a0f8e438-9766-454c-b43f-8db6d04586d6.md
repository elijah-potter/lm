Waiting was the quietest choice she ever made.

The café’s afternoon hum was the only sound—steam rising from a half-empty mug, the distant clink of a coffee machine, the way time stretched like a thread between her fingers. She had left her phone in the room, the last tab closed on notifications, emails, the endless scroll. Outside, the city’s pulse was relentless: honking cars, laughter from a playground, the distant wail of a siren. But here, she was just a breath in the air, a pause in the storm.

The book she’d been reading—*The Alchemist*—had been half-finished. The last page was a promise: *"When you want something, all the universe conspires in helping you to achieve it."* But she’d read that years ago. Now, she just wanted to *be*. No destination, no destination line. Just the slow unraveling of the story inside her, the way her fingers traced the spine of the book as if it were a map she’d forgotten she needed.

She’d left her phone in the room because sometimes, the smallest devices—like clocks and alarms—are just noise. She let her gaze wander to the window, where the rain, fine and persistent, blurred the city’s edges into something softer. Waiting wasn’t about losing time; it was about letting it *rest*.

A server arrived, her usual, unreadable face a blur through the steam. She ordered coffee, black, no sugar, the way she always did. *"You good?"* he asked. She nodded. He vanished into the kitchen, leaving her with the weight of the moment—unbroken, unhurried.

She finished her book, then another, then a third. The hours blurred together like the rain outside, but she was alive in the space between them. Waiting wasn’t passive; it was the act of choosing to *listen*. To the quiet. To the way the world, in its own time, answered.
