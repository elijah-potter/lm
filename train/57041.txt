The Welsh government has announced a new screening programme that could save more than 100 lives each year.
Backed by £1.6 million worth of funding, the Wales Abdominal Aortic Aneurism (AAA) Screening Programme will look for weaknesses or 'aneurisms' in the walls of the aorta - the main blood vessel carrying blood to the body.
If an aneurysm becomes very large and ruptures, the resulting internal bleeding can be life-threatening.
The programme - which will start in 2013 - will involve a one-off ultrasound test for 65-year-old men to check whether they have an AAA.
If a large aneurysm is found, men will be referred for surgery to repair the weakness.
Bob Hudson, chief executive of Public Health Wales, described the programme as 'great news'.
'Although few people will have heard of AAA, this is potentially a very serious condition that has a high death rate for those who suffer a ruptured aneurysm,' he explained.
'By offering screening to men aged 65 years in Wales, we can identify aneurysms that are at risk of rupture and provide a much greater chance of recovery for the estimated six in every 1,000 men in Wales who will have a large aneurysm.'
