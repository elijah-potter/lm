The old clock tower stood sentinel over the town square, its hands frozen at 11:59, casting long shadows on the cobblestones. Beneath it, a single locket—rusted with age—was buried in the earth, forgotten by time.

Every night at midnight, a single rose would bloom at its base, though no one knew how or why. The townsfolk whispered of it as a miracle, but no one dared to pluck it. The last rose had fallen to the ground, its petals crumbling into dust.

At dawn, the square was empty—save for the locket, now half-buried in the morning dew. Inside, a small, faded photograph lay half-hidden, its edges singed, as if scorched by something unseen.

The last rose had turned to gold.
