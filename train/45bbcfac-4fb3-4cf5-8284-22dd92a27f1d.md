The room smelled of old wood and damp, the kind of quiet that hummed with the weight of what was unsaid. I sat at the edge of my chair, fingers tracing the grain of the tabletop—too familiar, too close. Outside, the city pulsed, but here, the air felt trapped, like the breath of someone holding their breath too long. The letter in my hands was addressed to me, though my name wasn’t on the envelope. The ink was faded, the paper thin, as if it had been pressed against something for too long. The words inside were scattered like leaves in a storm: *"You were never the one."* The voice that spoke to me wasn’t my own, but the ghost of someone who had loved me, or thought they had. I traced the edges of the words, trying to make sense of the chaos, but all I found were the cracks—the ones I’d built into my own skin years ago, the ones that let the world in before it could leave. The mirror on the wall reflected not my face, but the face of someone else: younger, bolder, certain. I closed my eyes and let the image fade, not because I wanted to forget, but because I already knew the truth. The world was a stage, and I was just one of the actors, waiting for the next line to be written.
