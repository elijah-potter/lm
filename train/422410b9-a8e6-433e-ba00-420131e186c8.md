A faint metallic clink echoed as the elderly woman reached into her worn leather satchel. The heavy pouch had been packed for months, yet she hesitated before pulling out the crumpled note. She unrolled it slowly, the ink faded but legible: *"Meet me at the old oak—7 PM. Don’t wait."* Her fingers trembled as she traced the familiar handwriting.

She tossed the note onto the table beside her. A gust of wind rattled the window, and she glanced at the small, half-buried object beneath the coffee table—a silver locket, its chain snapped. The lock had been broken years ago, but the charm inside remained intact: a miniature portrait of her late husband, his face half-erased by time. She exhaled sharply, clutching it to her chest.

The door creaked open. A child’s laughter spilled in, followed by the scent of rain-soaked pavement. Her granddaughter, Mira, sat cross-legged on the floor, tracing constellations in the dust with her fingers. *"Mama, you look like you’ve seen a ghost."* Mira’s eyes widened as she noticed the locket. *"Did you find something?"*

She nodded, pressing the charm into Mira’s hands. *"Not just that. This too."* Mira’s grip tightened. *"The letter?"* Her grandmother handed her the crumpled note. *"It’s a clue. For something bigger."*
