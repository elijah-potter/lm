The team identified a novel enzymatic pathway in *C. reinhardtii* that converts **3-phosphoglycerate** into **glycerone phosphate** via a previously undetected **haloacid dehalogenase (HAD)-like domain** in an unannotated protein (ORF127). This reaction, validated via isotopic labeling experiments (^13C-Glycolysis), bypasses the conventional GAPDH step, enabling direct flux through the **glycerone phosphate** branchpoint into polyol synthesis. Computational docking of the enzyme with substrate analogs revealed a conserved catalytic triad (His-Asp-Glu) with a previously unsuspected **pKa shift** (pKa~8.2 vs. canonical pKa~6.5), enhancing substrate binding by ~30% at physiological pH. This discovery was confirmed by complementation in *E. coli* Δglycerone kinase mutants, where overexpression of ORF127 restored growth on **glycerone phosphate** under aerobic conditions, indicating a previously overlooked metabolic niche. The study highlights how overlooked enzymatic promiscuity can reshape metabolic networks.
