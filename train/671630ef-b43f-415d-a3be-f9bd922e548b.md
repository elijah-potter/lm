The rain fell in silence on the old library’s stone steps, each drop carrying the weight of forgotten secrets. Inside, the air smelled of aged paper and old books, and the narrator adjusted their gloves, fingers brushing the spine of a dust-covered volume titled *"The Quiet Confession of Elias Voss."*

"Most people think I’m a ghost," Elias began, voice barely audible over the distant hum of the library’s clock. "They say my stories haunt the shelves, but I’m not a ghost—just a man who wrote them."

The confession unfolded like the unraveling of a thread:

*"I lied about the first time I saw you."*

They weren’t strangers; they’d met years ago in a café where she’d ordered tea with honey and he’d stolen the spoon. But he’d never told anyone. Not because he feared the truth—because he loved it. The way her eyes lit up when she laughed. The way she hummed *"The Night We Met"* under her breath while walking home.

Now, in this library, he traced the words on the page where he’d hidden the truth: *"She was the first thing I loved without fear."*

The pen paused. Then, quietly: *"I didn’t tell her."*

The story ended there, unfinished. Somewhere between the pages, the narrator’s confession lingered like a half-remembered dream.
