A consensus was reached at the town council meeting on expanding the park’s walking trail to connect the old mill dam with the riverfront. Mayor Carter proposed the plan, arguing it would boost tourism and local businesses. The mayor’s wife, a community activist, supported it to improve accessibility for seniors and families with strollers. The mayor’s neighbor, who owned the adjacent farm, agreed to donate unused fencing materials, believing it would prevent erosion on his land. The local councilors, tired of budget cuts, saw it as a way to justify new infrastructure spending. The town’s youth coordinator, though not present, had quietly rallied parents to sponsor maintenance patrols, ensuring long-term engagement. The only dissent came from the environmental group, who argued it would fragment wildlife habitats—but their concerns were brushed aside by a vote of 12 to 2, with one councilor citing "progress over perfection." By the end of the night, the trail’s first section was approved, with signage already drafted to include a plaque noting "Built with Contributions from All Who Agreed."
