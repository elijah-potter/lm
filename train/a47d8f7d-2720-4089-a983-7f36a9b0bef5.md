The café’s hum of conversation faded to a whisper when I sat, the usual din of half-finished cups and clinking utensils replaced by a single, deliberate sip. Around me, the patrons—each absorbed in their own world of screens or whispered debates—paid little mind to the subtle shifts in the room. I noticed the way the light filtered through the stained-glass window in a shifting prism, turning the usual blue of the afternoon into something like deep sapphire and emerald.

Their eyes flickered over the same menu, but I saw the way the waiter’s hands trembled when serving the last of the pastries, the unspoken tension in the older man’s posture as he adjusted his tie. I watched the way the child’s laughter died in her throat when her toy was too far out of reach, the adult beside her not even glancing at her. They missed the quiet dignity in the elderly woman’s slow walk, the way she held her cane like a second limb, or the way the young mother’s face lit up when she caught me staring—not at her, but at the way she carried herself, unburdened by the weight she carried.

I let the silence stretch, let the room breathe around me. Somewhere, a clock ticked. No one else heard it.
