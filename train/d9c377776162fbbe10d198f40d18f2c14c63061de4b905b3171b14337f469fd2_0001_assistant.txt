Wyoming's road to statehood began in 1888 when the Territorial Assembly petitioned Congress for admission to the Union. Although it did not pass, Governor Francis E. Warren and other Wyoming legislators decided to continue proceeding as if an "enabling act" had passed. The Wyoming Territory held an election of delegates for the only Constitutional Convention in Wyoming's history on July 8, 1889. In September 1889, 49 men assembled in Cheyenne and wrote the state constitution, which was approved by voters by a vote of 6,272 to 1,923 on  November 5, 1889. Legislators introduced bills for Wyoming's statehood in the U.S. House and Senate in December 1889, and the House passed the bill on March 27, 1890. Wyoming officially became the 44th U.S. state on July 10, 1890.
