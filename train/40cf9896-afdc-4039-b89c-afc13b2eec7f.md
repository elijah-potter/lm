A **quantum dot** in a semiconductor material exhibits strange light behavior due to quantum confinement effects. Electrons and holes confined within nanoscale dots emit tunable, single-photon light when excited, exhibiting coherent photon emission and strong quantum interference, such as in **quantum dot LEDs** or **single-photon sources**. At certain thresholds, they exhibit **cascade emission** where photons are emitted in non-random sequences due to electron-hole recombination dynamics. Additionally, **quantum dot photonic crystals** create **femtosecond pulse compression** via group-velocity dispersion, enabling ultrafast light manipulation.
