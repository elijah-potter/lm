I remember the first time I saw the ocean,
its vastness swallowing the horizon like a question no one could answer.
The waves, endless and relentless, crashed against the shore,
carved stories into the sand—tides of our own making, yet forever changing.
I remember the taste of salt on my tongue,
the way the light bent through the water, golden and mysterious.

I remember the way my mother used to say,
*"The world is bigger than you can imagine,"*
but I didn’t believe it then.
I remember the books I read, the maps I traced,
the dreams I tried to hold onto like a drowning man clinging to a rope.

I remember the moments when I thought I was lost,
only to find myself standing in the middle of a room,
somewhere between fear and wonder.

I remember the way time feels different when you’re young—
like sand running through your fingers,
or a river that keeps flowing, even when you stop pushing.
I remember the people who taught me what it means to believe,
the ones who didn’t ask for answers, just let the questions stay.

I remember the sky at dawn,
the way the world breathes before the day begins.
I remember the weight of silence,
the way it can hold more than words ever could.

I think I’ve been searching for something,
not just answers, but the way to listen,
to let the questions stay,
to let the world keep turning,
to let the ocean keep washing away the edges of my understanding.

I think I’ve been looking for the right words,
but words are just echoes.
I think I’ve been looking for a map,
but the map is already inside me—
just waiting for the right key.

I think I’ve been afraid of being lost,
but the only way to find yourself is to let yourself fall.
I think the ocean doesn’t care about the shore.
I think the question isn’t where we are,
but what we’re doing with the time we’ve got.

I think I’m learning to swim,
not because I have to,
but because the water is calling.
