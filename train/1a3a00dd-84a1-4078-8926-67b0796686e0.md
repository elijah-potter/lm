**The Last Letter**

I keep my pens in a drawer where the ink still bleeds,
a slow, steady pulse that never quite runs dry.
Yet even now, as I write this down with trembling hands,
I wonder—was this the last time I’d ever say this?

My mother’s voice still echoes in my mind,
*"Remember, dear, the world doesn’t owe you anything—
just show up, do your best, and let the rest fall where it may."*
But the years have whispered louder than her words,
and now I only hear the echoes of my own despair.

She’s not here to remind me, but I need to hear it again:
*"You were never meant to carry the whole sky, child.
Take what you can carry. The rest? Let it go."*

Outside, the trees stand like silent sentinels,
their branches heavy with the weight of forgotten seasons.
I should leave before the weight of this page
drowns me in ink and regret. But for now,
I’ll let it spill like a confession, raw and true.
