The skeletal remains of an unfinished skyscraper loom against a bruised sky, its steel ribs exposed where paint peeled off in 2016. The 47th floor’s glass facade is a jagged, half-collapsed archway, reflecting the rust-streaked pavement below. A single rusted elevator shaft stands beside the loading dock, its doors fused shut; the last maintenance crew left in 2018.

Below, a courtyard buzzes with discarded materials: crates of concrete, a broken crane arm, and a sign reading **"FLOOR 12 – STILL UNDER CONSTRUCTION (PLEASE DO NOT ENTER)"** in faded yellow paint. The only human trace is a half-written sketch on a clipboard, showing an abstracted, unfinished building design. A maintenance van sits abandoned, its tire flat, near the perimeter fence, which is half-buried in weeds. The smell of damp cement and old oil lingers in the air.

The air conditioning unit on the roof, installed in 2015, still hums intermittently, its wiring exposed. The foundation’s concrete slabs are cracked, some bulging slightly, revealing the roots of the old water pipe that ran beneath it. The windows on the 2nd floor are boarded up, but one panel is slightly ajar, revealing the faint glow of an unlit office inside—its desk still holds a half-packed file labeled **"ARCHIVE – FLOOR 3"** in a handwritten note. The sound of a distant construction drill echoes from the unfinished basement, where exposed pipes and ductwork twist like tangled veins.

The place feels like a ghost of what it could have been—now just a hulking, half-dressed skeleton of progress.
