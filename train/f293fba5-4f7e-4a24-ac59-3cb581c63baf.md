*The candle flickered, casting long shadows across the worn leather couch where Elias sat, his fingers tracing the rim of his whiskey glass. The fire in the hearth had died to embers, but the room felt warm enough for the night.*

*"You ever wonder how stories get told?"* Elias’ voice dropped, smooth as polished wood. *"I’ve been thinking about that lately, between the cracks of another day, when the world’s just noise."*

He paused, swirling the amber liquid. *"Once, in a village near the Black Hollow, there lived a man named Tomas. He claimed to have seen the Devil himself—just a shadow, really, gliding between the trees at dusk, but Tomas swore he saw the eyes."*

A beat. The air hummed with tension.

*"The villagers laughed. Tomas didn’t. He built a shrine to the thing he’d seen, carved a face from driftwood, placed it in the center of the square. Then one night, they found it gone. The next morning, the villagers didn’t come to work. They didn’t eat. They just… waited."*

Elias exhaled, slow. *"They waited for the Devil to return."*

*"Tomas didn’t. He left that night. A week later, the first child in the village was born with too many ribs. The next, the same. Then the adults—some started seeing things too, whispers in the dark, hands reaching from the woods. Tomas was gone, but the whispers stayed."*

A silence. Then—*"And then?"*

*"Then the last thing anyone saw was Tomas, standing in the square, his face half-lit by the moon. He held a child’s hand, and the child’s ribs weren’t enough."*

The fire crackled. The glass was empty. The story was done.
