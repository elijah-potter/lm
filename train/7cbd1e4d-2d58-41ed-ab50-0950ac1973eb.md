A dimly lit café at dawn, steam curling from the espresso cups. The hum of the city outside is muffled by the thick glass. Two strangers sit across from each other—one with a half-eaten croissant, the other with a laptop open, typing furiously. The air smells of coffee and stale bread.

The younger one, arms crossed, leans forward. The older one, who was typing, glances up. Their eyes meet for a fraction of a second before the younger one exhales sharply, a small laugh escaping.

Silence. Then:

*"You’re the one who…"* The older one pauses, unsure. *"Who wrote that script about the old theater? The one where they burned down but kept playing?"*

The younger one’s fingers freeze. *"Yeah. You… you *knew*?"*

The older one nods, slow, almost tender. *"I knew you’d come back. You always did."*

Outside, the first light of day spills over the café, painting their faces in gold. The younger one smiles. *"I’m not coming back."*

The older one’s smile is quiet. *"I know."*
