A vast, echoing void stretches endlessly, its silence suffocating. Walls of cold, obsidian-like stone curve into infinite horizons, their surface etched with jagged, unreadable symbols. The air, thin and metallic, clings to the edges of perception—visible only in swirling, ghostly tendrils that drift like smoke. Shadows stretch unnaturally long, defying the lack of light, while the floor, a fractured mosaic of rusted metal and blackened glass, seems to dissolve beneath bare feet. At the center, a single, pulsing orb of unnatural light flickers—neither star nor flame, but something older, hungrier. The weight of it all presses down, yet the space refuses to close, as if the walls themselves are breathing. Even the sound of your own breath feels distant, as though it bounces off something vast and unseen.
