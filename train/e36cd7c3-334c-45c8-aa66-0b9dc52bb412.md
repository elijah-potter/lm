When the room hummed with voices in a language that wasn’t his, the hum of the subway beneath him felt like the only rhythm he could trust. His hands clenched the straps of his bag, knuckles white, while the others chatted as if the world were a shared experience. The subway car’s fluorescent lights flickered, casting shadows across faces that moved in a dance he couldn’t join. His breath hitched as he scanned the crowd—familiar faces, strangers who knew him, but no one who *felt* like home. The weight of being the only one who hadn’t been there for the last five years pressed against his ribs. Outside, the city’s endless pulse blurred into a backdrop of noise he couldn’t name. He wanted to disappear, to melt into the crowd until he wasn’t even there anymore. But the subway doors hissed open, and the world outside was just another mask, another layer of performative existence.
