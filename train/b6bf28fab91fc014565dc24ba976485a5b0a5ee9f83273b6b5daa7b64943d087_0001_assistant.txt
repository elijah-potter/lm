Imagine a football field divided into two halves. If a player is in the opponent's half of the field and closer to the opponent's goal line than both the ball and the second-last opponent (who is usually, but not always, the goalkeeper), they are considered to be in an offside position. However, being in an offside position by itself is not a violation.

The key part is when the ball is played by a player's teammate. If the player in the offside position becomes directly involved in the play, like receiving the ball, interfering with an opponent, or gaining an unfair advantage from that position, then they can be penalized for an offside offense.

Offside can be a bit tricky to understand because it involves precise positioning and timing. It's about ensuring fairness and preventing players from gaining an unfair advantage by being in the wrong position at the wrong time. To make it even more simple: imagine how unfair it would be for an opponent player to stand right next to the goalkeeper at all times and just wait for the timely assist to be able to score from a point-blank range.
