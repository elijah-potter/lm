A sunrise that lingers too long before the clock demands it move,
morning coffee that tastes like it’s been brewing for centuries,
the hum of the city waking up too slowly, a rhythm that stretches
into an afternoon that refuses to end, the afternoon stretching
into evening as if time itself has been stretched thin.

A dinner that feels like a marathon—each bite a question,
each conversation a half-finished thought. The bed waits too long,
but sleep comes too quickly, too abruptly, as if it’s afraid of the
weight of a day that’s gone on forever.

The moon watches from the window like a quiet, tired witness.
Evening fades too fast, but night lingers, heavy with the weight of
a day that refused to be contained. Tomorrow’s dawn feels like a
promise stretched too thin, too fragile.
