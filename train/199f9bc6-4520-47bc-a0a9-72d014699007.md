A half-submerged dock at twilight, where the last light bleeds through misty gaps in the trees. The wooden planks are slick with rain, and the air hums with the distant drip of water from a broken pipe. The waterline on the dock is uneven, some boards sagging into the shallows like a living thing, while others rise just above the tide, their edges worn smooth by centuries of driftwood and salt. In the center stands an old, weathered lantern, its glass cracked but still glowing faintly, casting long shadows across the rippling surface below. The water is not quite calm—it swirls slightly, as if waiting. The scent of damp earth and iron lingers, and the sound of gulls is muffled, as if the world beyond this dock has stopped for a moment.
