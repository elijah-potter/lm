The Dodo went extinct due to hunting, invasive species threat, and deforestation. Dodo birds were flightless, fearless birds native to Mauritius, an island off the coast of Madagascar that was largely uninhabited from the human population until the 1500s. This made them an easy target for humans to hunt due to their inability to fly and because of their fearlessness to humans. Not only did humans hunt them, but the animals they brought with them did as well. Dogs, pigs, cats, and rats ate their eggs and destroyed their nests. Because the Dodos had no known natural predators, they did not fear these animals, had no way to defend themselves, and had no means of escape. The presence of these animals created more competition for food and resources and the Dodo birds were simply outnumbered. At the same time, humans were destroying their natural habitats. Explorers cut down trees to make way for paths across the island, pushing the dodo birds into a smaller area. This created an even greater lack of food and ultimately, extinction.
