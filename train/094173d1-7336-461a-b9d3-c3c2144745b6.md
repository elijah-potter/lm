You were told this place was safe, but the air hummed with something like static, and the floor creaked under your boots—not from age, but from unseen weight pressing down.

The door locked behind you. The last thing you heard was someone whispering: *"They’re coming."* The words stuck in your throat like dust. The light flickered. Not from the bulb, but from something deeper, something that pulsed like a slow heartbeat beneath the walls.

The walls didn’t breathe, but they *remembered*. The scent of old metal and damp earth clung to the air, thick enough to taste. Your pulse hammered in your ears, but the silence didn’t echo. It *resonated*.

A shape moved in the corner. Too still. Too sharp. You reached for your knife, fingers trembling—

Then the first sound cut through the dark: a wet, clicking noise. Not from your boots. From *everywhere*. The walls. The ceiling. The floor.

You should have run. You *shouldn’t* have been here.
