The first time I saw her, she was laughing at my joke about a coffee stain on my shirt, and I realized she had seen it before—everyone had. The truth I didn’t tell then was that I had worn the same shirt for three days before I noticed it. The fabric clung to my arm like a second skin, and I had never minded it before. It was only when I saw her that I realized the world had been looking at me the same way for years.

She asked if I was fine, and I lied. I said the stain was nothing, just a reminder of how much I loved coffee. But the truth was, I was tired of being the one who carried the weight of small, invisible things. I had spent my life pretending the coffee stains were just stains, the tiredness just exhaustion, the way my hands shook when I wrote was just nerves. But hers was different. Hers was a reminder that some truths didn’t need to be hidden as long as they were shared. That night, I told her everything. And for the first time, I felt lighter than air.
