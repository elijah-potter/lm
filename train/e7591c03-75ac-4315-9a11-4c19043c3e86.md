**The Last Light**

The wind howled through the broken window, carrying whispers of the past. Elias sat by the hearth, fingers tracing the grain of the old table, where his mother had once hummed the same tune before bedtime. *"The stars are always the same, no matter where you stand."*

"Same old story," he muttered, though his voice lacked the warmth of his mother’s.

Outside, the rain drummed against the roof, the rhythm matching the endless loop of his thoughts. He clenched his fists, the nails digging into his palms—just like the way his father used to when he was a boy. *"The stars are always the same, no matter where you stand."*

A shadow moved at the edge of the firelight. The old man from the village stood there, his face gaunt, his eyes hollow. "You’re wasting your time," he rasped. "The world doesn’t care about stories."

Elias turned to him, the words already forming. *"The stars are always the same, no matter where you stand."* The man’s lips twitched, a ghost of a smile. Then, the fire crackled—too loudly, too suddenly—as if the world itself had decided to listen.
