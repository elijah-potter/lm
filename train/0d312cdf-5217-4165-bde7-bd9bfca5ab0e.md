---
**Setting:** A dimly lit, high-rise apartment. Rain taps against the window. The protagonist, **Kael**, sits at a cluttered desk, fingers tracing the edge of an open notebook. The last page is filled with half-finished sketches and scribbled equations. His breath is shallow, his jaw clenched.

---
**Scene:**

Kael’s fingers hover over the keyboard. The screen glows faintly behind him—an old, unopened email from his therapist: *"Meet at 6 PM tomorrow. Let’s talk about the loop."* He doesn’t reply. His phone buzzes. A text from his brother, **Darian**, who’s on a business trip:

*"You’re avoiding the call. What’s wrong?"*

Kael swallows. The reply comes in silent seconds:
*"I don’t know how to fix it."*

The apartment feels too small. His reflection in the window is a stranger—pale, restless. He closes his eyes and imagines the weight of every decision he’s made, every lie he’s told. The equations in his notebook aren’t math anymore; they’re equations of guilt.

A knock at the door. His mother’s voice, muffled:
*"Kael? You home?"*

He doesn’t answer. The door creaks open, and his mother steps inside, carrying a thermos. She sets it down without looking at him. *"You look like hell. You eat?"*

Kael says nothing. She pours tea, hands trembling. *"You’re not the only one who’s been drowning. But you’re not drowning alone."*

Outside, the rain turns to sleet. The city below is a blur of lights, indifferent. Kael exhales, slow and deep. The notebook stays open. The loop begins again.

*(End scene)*
