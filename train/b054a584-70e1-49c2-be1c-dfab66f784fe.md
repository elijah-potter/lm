An abandoned lighthouse on a mist-shrouded coast, its broken lantern casting eerie, shifting shadows on the peeling paint. The wind hums through the rusted iron railings, forming words that drift like the fog itself—*coast*, *wave*, *silence*. Inside, the old mechanism groans, its gears whispering secrets in a language only the tide understands. The sea’s call echoes through hollow spaces where the floorboards creak under unseen footsteps. At night, the wind carries whispers of sailors who never returned, their voices merging with the groans of the mechanism. The air is thick with the scent of salt and damp wood, and the walls seem to breathe, pulsing faintly with the rhythm of the waves. The place *listens*—not with ears, but with memory, with every sigh, every whisper of wind through the broken glass.
