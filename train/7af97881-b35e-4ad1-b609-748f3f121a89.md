A vast, cramped chamber with walls thick as old bones, painted in muted, sickly greens and browns. The air is stale, pressed so tightly it’s almost a physical force, like the breath of some colossal, half-sleeping beast. Furniture—chairs, tables, desks—are arranged in a labyrinth of half-unfolded patterns, as if assembled by someone who forgot to breathe. Shadows stretch unnaturally long, pooling in dark corners like exhaled smoke. The only movement is the slow, deliberate pulse of a single, ancient clock ticking like a heartbeat—slow, deliberate, and suspended. The silence isn’t absolute, but it’s the kind that presses against your ribs, making you hold your breath too. Outside, the world exists in a distant, slow-motion blur, like something just beyond the window watching but never quite entering.
