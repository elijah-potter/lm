In a dimly lit café during a long winter evening, an older man with a lined face and a coat worn thin by time placed a hand on the shoulder of a younger traveler. *"I’ll meet you at the old oak by midnight tomorrow,"* he said quietly. The words hung between them, barely more than a whisper. The younger man nodded, unsure if he should believe or dismiss it—promises like these often dissolved like mist before dawn. He didn’t ask for details, only the name of the oak, a stubborn tree carved with strange symbols in the bark. *"The one by the river’s edge,"* the man murmured, before the café doors closed behind him, sealing the fleeting exchange. The traveler’s stomach twisted—would he keep it? Or would the night swallow it whole, just like all the others?
