## A Counseling Session

**Setting:** A comfortably furnished therapy office. Soft lighting, calming artwork on the walls.

**Characters:**

* **Dr. Eleanor Vance:** A seasoned therapist, known for her calm demeanor and insightful guidance.
* **Mr. Thomas Ashton:** A client in his late thirties, experiencing significant work-related stress.

---

**Dr. Vance:** Welcome, Thomas. Please, have a seat. It’s good to see you again. How have you been since our last session?

**Mr. Ashton:** (Sighs, settling into the chair) Honestly, Dr. Vance, not great. The pressure at work has just been relentless. I feel like I’m constantly behind, and the deadlines keep shifting.

**Dr. Vance:** That sounds incredibly difficult, Thomas. It’s understandable that you’re feeling overwhelmed. Can you tell me a bit more about what this pressure feels like specifically?

**Mr. Ashton:** It’s… a constant hum of anxiety, I suppose. I find myself thinking about work even when I’m trying to relax. I haven't been sleeping well, and I'm short with my family. I feel guilty about that, of course.

**Dr. Vance:** It sounds like this stress is impacting multiple areas of your life, and the guilt you're experiencing is a natural consequence of feeling like you’re failing to meet your own expectations. It takes courage to acknowledge that, Thomas. You’re demonstrating self-awareness.

**Mr. Ashton:** It's just... I want to be good at my job. I want to be reliable. But I'm starting to feel like I'm letting everyone down. My boss, my team, my wife.

**Dr. Vance:** The desire to be reliable and to meet expectations is admirable, and it’s clear you hold yourself to a high standard. But it sounds like those expectations might be contributing to the problem. Can you describe a recent situation at work that particularly heightened your stress?

**Mr. Ashton:** Last week, we had a major project deadline approaching. My boss asked me to take on an additional responsibility – essentially, managing a small team’s workload. I agreed, because I didn’t want to appear uncooperative. But now I’m juggling even more, and I feel completely swamped.

**Dr. Vance:** So, you felt pressured to say yes, and now you’re carrying a heavier burden. It's common to feel obligated in those situations, especially when we want to be perceived as team players. Do you feel you had the capacity to take on that additional responsibility when it was offered?

**Mr. Ashton:** No. Not really. I was already feeling stretched thin. I just… I didn’t want to say no. I thought it would look bad.

**Dr. Vance:** It’s understandable to worry about appearances, especially in a professional setting. However, consistently taking on more than you can realistically handle can lead to burnout and, as you’re experiencing, significant distress. Let's explore some strategies for setting boundaries at work. It’s not about being uncooperative; it’s about advocating for your own well-being and ensuring you can deliver quality work.

**Mr. Ashton:** That makes sense, but I'm worried about the repercussions. What if they think I'm not a team player?

**Dr. Vance:** We can work on framing your responses in a way that emphasizes your commitment to the team while also acknowledging your limitations. For example, instead of a simple "no," you could say something like, "I’m currently at capacity with my existing workload, but I’m happy to discuss prioritizing tasks or exploring alternative solutions to ensure the project’s success." It's about communication, Thomas, not confrontation.

**Mr. Ashton:** That's a good way to put it. I hadn’t thought of it like that.

**Dr. Vance:** It’s a process, and it takes practice. This week, I'd like you to focus on identifying one situation where you feel pressured to take on more than you can handle. Before you respond, take a moment to pause, breathe, and consider your capacity. And then, we can discuss how you might respond assertively, yet respectfully. How does that sound?

**Mr. Ashton:** That sounds manageable. I think I can do that. Thank you, Dr. Vance. I already feel a little less overwhelmed just talking about it.

**Dr. Vance:** You’re very welcome, Thomas. Remember, advocating for yourself is not selfish; it's essential for maintaining your well-being and ensuring you can continue to contribute effectively. We’re here to work through this together.
