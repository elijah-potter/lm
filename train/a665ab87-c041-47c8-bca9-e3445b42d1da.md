**Part 1:**
The old lighthouse keeper, Elias, stood at the edge of the cliff, his breath fogging in the cold wind. Below, the storm raged—black waves crashed against the rocks, and the wind howled like a ghost. Elias had spent forty years here, guiding ships away from the treacherous reefs. Tonight, the beacon flickered erratically, and the last light in the tower dimmed.

He knelt, picked up his grandfather’s compass, and whispered, *"For the first time in my life, I don’t know which way to turn."* The storm surged, and the sea whispered secrets in the wind.

---

**Ending 1:**
As Elias stared into the abyss, his old rifle clutched in his hands, he thought of his daughter, Lena, who had left years ago to start a new life in the city. He reached into his coat and pulled out a single letter, its edges frayed but still legible: *"I’m sorry, Papa. I can’t come home."* He burned the letter in the fireplace, watching the smoke curl into the storm. The beacon blinked once, then died. The waves swallowed the last light. Elias stood, turned, and walked into the darkness—his lantern in his hand, a single candle flickering in his pocket. The shipwrecks waited, and he was ready to guide them all to the bottom.

---

**Part 2:**
The storm’s fury peaked, and Elias realized the compass had spun wildly, its needle spinning like a top. He grabbed the old ship’s bell and struck it three times—*the signal for the last ship of the night*. The waves receded slightly, revealing a small, rusted boat beached on the shore. Inside, a child’s handprint was carved into the wood: *"To Elias."* The girl’s name was Mila. She had been lost at sea for three days, her father’s ship lost in the storm. Elias lifted her onto his shoulders, cradling her as he ran toward the lighthouse, the bell ringing in his ears. The beacon flared to life again, its light cutting through the blackness. Mila screamed, tears streaming down her face, but Elias held her tight, whispering, *"You’re safe now."* The storm’s fury softened; the sea grew calm. The next morning, they sailed away—together.
