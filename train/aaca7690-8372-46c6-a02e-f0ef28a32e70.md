The clock struck eleven when the old lighthouse keeper found the stranger’s boat washed ashore. It wasn’t the usual driftwood, but a sleek, black vessel, its hull cracked open like a blooming flower. The tide had left only a single, tattered flag—pale blue with a single white star.

The keeper, Elias, had spent thirty years staring at the horizon. He knew the sea’s moods by heart: the way it whispered warnings before storms, the way it swallowed ships whole when they forgot how to listen.

This time, the water had spoken in letters of rust. He picked up the flag. The star was still there, though faded.

Elias exhaled and stepped onto the sand. The stranger wouldn’t come out. Not yet.

By the time the sun dipped below the waves, Elias had traced the coordinates in his notebook—where the old maps said the *Sea Whisper* was supposed to have sunk decades ago. No one had found its remains.

The next morning, the keeper found the boat gone. The only thing left was the flag, now curled like a question mark in the wind.

And somewhere, beneath the black water, a ship was waiting to answer.
