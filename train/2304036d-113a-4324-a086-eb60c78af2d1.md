## Explain Like I'm Five: Why the Sky is Blue

Okay, imagine you have a big box of crayons, right? And in that box, you have all sorts of colors – red, yellow, green, blue, purple, and more! Now, imagine the sun is like a giant flashlight shining all those colors at the Earth.

But the sunlight isn’t *just* one color. It's actually made up of *all* those colors mixed together! It looks white to us because they’re all blended.

Now, the air around Earth is full of tiny, tiny bits – much smaller than even a grain of sand. We can’t even see them!  Think of them like little bouncy balls floating in the air.

When the sunlight travels to Earth, it bumps into these tiny bouncy balls. Now, some colors, like red and yellow, are big and strong and just keep going straight through. They don't bounce around much.

But blue is a little bit smaller and weaker. When blue light bumps into those bouncy balls, it bounces *everywhere*! It bounces up, down, sideways – all around. 

Because blue light bounces all over the place, it spreads out across the whole sky. That's why when we look up, we see blue! It's like the blue light is playing a game of tag with the air, and it's spread out all over.

Sometimes, when the sun is setting, it has to travel through *more* air to reach us. All that extra air makes the blue light bounce away even more, and then we start to see other colors, like orange and pink, because they get a chance to shine through too!



Isn’t that neat? The sky is blue because blue light likes to play tag with the air!
