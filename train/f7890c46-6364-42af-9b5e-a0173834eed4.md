The rain fell in sheets, turning the cobblestone street into a mirror that reflected only the past. I had come here with a plan—one that was now crumbling like the old bookstore’s shelves beneath my boots. I had meant to stay, to listen to the whispers of the pages, to let them rewrite themselves in my mind. But the first time the wind carried a note from the corner shop, a crumpled receipt for tea that smelled of lavender, I knew I was wrong. The past was a lie, and I was the one who had believed it.

Now, I lingered at the door, my fingers brushing the spine of the book I had carried all this way. The cover was worn, the title faded: *The Weight of Light*. I should have thrown it away. But the spine felt warm, like a secret shared between two people who had long since forgotten each other’s names.

Outside, the rain had slowed to a drizzle. The city was quiet, too quiet for a city that had once been alive with noise. I pulled out my phone, the screen still warm from the glow of messages I had never sent. The last one was from my sister, a voice message of her laugh, sharp and bright. She had called me that morning, asking if I was still coming home. I had lied. I had said I was, but my hands had shaken as I typed the words.

I should have called her back. I should have left the book on the shelf and walked away, let the rain wash away every thought I had ever had. But the book called to me, as if it had been waiting for me all this time. I turned back inside, the door clicking shut behind me.

The bookstore was empty. The lights were dim, the air thick with the scent of old paper and something else—something metallic, like the edge of a blade. I picked up the book, my thumb tracing the title again. *The Weight of Light*. It was a metaphor, of course. Light carried weight, and weight could be light. I had spent my life chasing the latter, drowning in the former.

I opened the book.

The pages were blank. Not empty—no, that was wrong. They were waiting. Like a room that had been empty for years, then suddenly filled with the sound of voices. I turned to the first page and wrote:

*"I was wrong."*

Then I closed the book and walked out, the rain following me like a shadow. The city was still, but I was no longer lost. I had found something, and it was not a place. It was a choice.
