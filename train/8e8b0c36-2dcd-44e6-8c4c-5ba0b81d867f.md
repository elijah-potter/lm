Today was another day where I woke up feeling like I had to get through the motions. My coffee tasted the same as it always does, and the news made me feel small again. I went to work and did what I do—answer emails, sit in meetings, and pretend to care about things that don’t matter to me. My boss said something about “driving innovation” again, but I just kept nodding, wondering if they meant something else entirely. Outside, the rain was gentle, like a reminder that life goes on without me.

After work, I stopped by the café I’ve been going to for years. The barista remembered my usual order, and I sat there feeling like a ghost among people who actually talk. My phone buzzed—another message from my friend, asking about my weekend. I typed back that I’d been busy, that I didn’t have time to explain. The words felt hollow.

I tried to read something interesting online, but my brain kept drifting back to the way my hands shake when I’m nervous. I wondered if anyone else ever felt like they were just a part of the noise, like they were here but no one noticed. The light outside was fading, and I realized I didn’t even remember what I wanted to do tomorrow. Maybe that’s okay. Maybe it’s better that way.

I went home and did the things I do—cooked something simple, watched something mindless. My reflection in the mirror didn’t make me feel special, just like the rest of the day. I went to bed thinking about how easy it is to forget how much you exist.
