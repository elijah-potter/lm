On September 11, 2001, terrorists hijacked four planes and crashed them into the World Trade Center in New York City and the Pentagon in Washington, D.C. One American named Frank Culbertson happened to be in the right place at the right time. He was on the International Space Station just as it was about to fly over New York City.
When he saw and heard about what happened, he quickly checked the space station's location on a map and grabbed a video camera. Then he raced for the cupola, a space station module with a large window that is often used for Earth-gazing.
On the way, he informed his crewmates about what happened. He captured videos and pictures of the plume of smoke coming from the World Trade Center. On the next orbit, he and his crewmates captured images of a haze over Washington, D.C. However, they were unable to tell where it was coming from.
The camera that Frank Culbertson used to capture the images and video was put on display at the Cosmosphere space museum in Hutchinson, Kansas. NASA declared the camera a valuable artifact since it demonstrated a capacity to capture high-resolution images of Earth's surface from the International Space Station. It also demonstrated space station crews' ability to react quickly and capture valuable images in the event of a disaster.
