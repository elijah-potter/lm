The dimly lit alleyway at dusk smelled of damp concrete and old cigarettes. Across from her, the older woman—her hands trembling slightly as she adjusted the frayed cuffs of her oversized sweater—stared at the crumpled bill in her palm. A single twenty-dollar bill, half-chewed, still bore the faint imprint of her teeth. She exhaled sharply, a sound like the hiss of a dying ember. Her breath fogged the glass of the abandoned storefront, and the flickering neon sign outside read *"CLOSED"* in bold, rusted letters. The streetlight cast long shadows, stretching like skeletal fingers across the pavement. Somewhere in the distance, a car engine roared to life, but it was swallowed by the night. The woman’s knuckles were white around the bill as she counted out the change, her fingers stuttering. *"I never should’ve taken it,"* she whispered, though no one heard. The alley stretched endlessly, and beyond it, the city’s pulse—faint, relentless—continued unabated.
