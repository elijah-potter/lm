**Great Wall of China (Mandate Wall):**
- **Northern Boundary:** Starts at Shanhaiguan Pass (near Shandong Peninsula) and extends ~21,000 km via:
  - **Beijing-Mukden Wall** (central segment, ~1,000 km) with watchtowers and fortification gates.
  - **Northern Wall (Jiankang)** (Liaoning/Jilin, ~6,000 km) with earthworks and wooden palisades.
  - **Western Wall** (Gansu/Shanxi, ~10,000 km) with stone/brick fortifications and defensive systems.
- **Eastern Boundary:** Ends at Shanhaiguan Pass via coastal forts and riverine defenses.
- **Key Features:** Ming-era (1368–1644) stone/brick walls, watchtowers (e.g., Jiankang), and gates (e.g., Jiayuguan).
- **Function:** Protects against northern invasions (e.g., Mongols) with layered defenses (outer/inner walls).

**Alternative: Acropolis of Athens**
- **Boundaries:** Enclosed within the **Areopagus Hill** and **Dionysiou Areopagitou Street**.
- **Outer Walls:** Limestone walls (e.g., **Cretic Wall**) with 10 fortified gates.
- **Inner Structures:** Parthenon, Erechtheion, Temple of Athena Nike, and Propylaea entrance.
- **Materials:** Marble, limestone, and terracotta; designed for visibility and defense.
- **Context:** Defined by its **city-state (polis) status** and cultural role in classical Greece.
