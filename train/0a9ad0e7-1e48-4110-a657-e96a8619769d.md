In a dimly lit warehouse, neon lights flickering from the ceiling, a group of workers paused mid-task to inspect a prototype component. Engineer Chen adjusted the calibration settings on a control panel, her fingers hovering over a single, mislabeled sensor. *"Just this once,"* she muttered to herself, bypassing the redundant safety check. The system hummed softly, the warning light dimmed—just enough to confirm the data read.

No alarms blared; no foreman stormed in. The prototype’s readings were still within acceptable margins. The team exchanged glances—confused but not alarmed—before returning to their tasks. The rule was bent, but the breach was invisible, the risk mitigated.
