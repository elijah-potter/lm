**Amphitheater at sunset:** Wooden bleachers rise from a gently sloping hillside, rows of ancient stone benches stretching into the horizon. The air hums with the faintest whisper of distant violins. A central stage, draped in twilight, holds a single speaker’s podium wrapped in frayed, vintage banners. The crowd—mixed ages, some in tattered parkas, others in flowing robes—shifts as the sun dips below a distant ridge, casting long shadows. A large, ornate speaker’s microphone stands on a pedestal, its glass top etched with runes. The stage’s acoustics are perfect: every word drifts from the mouthpiece to every seat, carried by the evening breeze that dances through the trees.

A long wooden table laden with vintage vinyl records and coffee cups sits just offstage, its surface marred by decades of wear. The crowd murmurs in anticipation, fingers tracing the grain of their wooden chairs as the first notes of a classical piece swell from unseen speakers. The ambiance is one of quiet reverence, where the act of listening itself becomes a ritual.
