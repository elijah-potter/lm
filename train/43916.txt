Peter Drucker (1909–2005) was a prolific and influential writer, consultant, and educator, who is considered by many to be the father of modern management philosophy. Born in Austria, Drucker studied economics and law at the University of Frankfurt and worked for several years as a newspaper journalist in Germany before immigrating to the United States in 1937.
Drucker is widely recognized as the founder of the modern management consulting industry and is credited with coining the term “knowledge worker.” He pioneered modern management practices, such as human-centered management, and advocated an approach to management which emphasized that the success of a business depends on its ability to provide valuable products and services to its customers.
Drucker wrote 39 books, the most famous of which, The Practice of Management (1954), is still considered one of the seminal works of management theory. His other works include books on economics, politics, and social philosophy. Drucker was also a professor at Claremont Graduate University and a consultant to many of the world’s leading corporations.
Drucker maintained that effective management requires self-awareness and the ability to think strategically. He influenced countless business executives and students who studied his work and is widely considered to be one of the most important figures in 20th-century business.
