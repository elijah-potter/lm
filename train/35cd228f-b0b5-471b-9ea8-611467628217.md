A sudden detour in Chicago’s windy streets led me to a rusted diner on the outskirts of town, its neon sign flickering like a dying ember. Inside, the scent of coffee and grease clung to the air, and the waitress—old enough to remember the diner’s founding—served me a steaming cup of black coffee without a word.

I sat at a table where a man in a faded baseball cap was hunched over a worn typewriter, fingers dancing across the keys. He stopped when he saw me, his face creased with a tired smile. *"You ever think about what you’re running from vs. what you’re running toward?"* he asked, swirling his own coffee. The words hung between us like smoke.

The next morning, I boarded a train with a different destination—one I hadn’t planned. By the time it reached the next city, I had a journal, a half-finished sketch of the diner’s sign, and a list of questions I couldn’t ignore. The trip wasn’t about reaching a place; it was about realizing I’d been chasing one all along, and the journey itself was the destination.
