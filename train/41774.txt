Pomegranates (Punica granatum) are multipurpose deciduous plants that live for several decades. They produce attractive leaves with showy, orange-red flowers followed by delicious red fruit with edible seeds. You can use the seeds in various recipes and beverages. It is easy to grow the plant from cuttings as a large shrub or small tree, depending on the variety.
Advantages of Cuttings
Propagation from hardwood cuttings is a common way to start a pomegranate tree. While you can grow pomegranate trees from seeds, cuttings produce a better and more predictable yield. Seeds may not produce the same quality and characteristics of their parent tree. Cuttings are identical to their parent plants.
Taking a Cutting
A tree should be at least 1 year old before you take a cutting to grow another plant. The best time to take a cutting is during the winter when plants are dormant. Use pruning shears to take a cutting 12 to 20 inches long with a diameter about the size of a pencil. Remove any leaves and treat the cutting with a rooting hormone before placing it into a pot filled with general-purpose soil mixture. Wait until the plant begins to develop roots before transplanting it outside. You can water the plant every few days during the first weeks. Monitor the plant for sucker growth and remove them so the cutting can develop a single-trunk form.
Climate and Soil
Most pomegranates in the United States grow in California and Arizona, but the plant will thrive in semi-arid areas with mild temperatures as well as place with a subtropical climate. It can also survive freezing temperatures but will suffer injury once the temperatures dip below 12 degrees Fahrenheit. The tree might not fruit in colder climates. For best results, place your cutting in the area of yard that receives the most sun. It can survive a variety of soil types but needs good drainage.
Fruit Production and Growth
Typically, a cutting will begin to produce fruit in about two and one-half to three years. Some plants may bear fruit after only a year. The pomegranate is self-fruitful but can also produce fruit after cross-pollination through insects. Cross-pollinated trees produce a higher yield. Generally, pomegranate trees will grow between 12 and 16 feet tall, but some grow to 30 feet. Dwarf species, such as the “Nana,” only grow a few feet high and have less tasty fruit than full-sized trees.
