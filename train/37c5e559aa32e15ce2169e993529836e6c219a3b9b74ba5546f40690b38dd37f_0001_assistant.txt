• In addition to stocks and cash equivalents, bonds, also known as fixed-income securities, are common and a main asset class for investors.
• Bonds are issued directly to investors when a company or entity needs to raise cash for operations, new projects, or refinance existing debt.
• Bonds issued by the borrower include the terms of the loan, interest payments, and the date when the loaned funds must be paid back, known as the maturity date. 
• Investors earn income via the interest payment, or coupon, for the loan, which varies based on the interest rate.
• Bonds typically have an initial price set at par or a value of $1,000 per individual bond.
• Similar to stocks, bonds also have a market where most can be bought or sold, not requiring the initial investor to keep the bond through maturity. 
• The market price of a bond depends on several factors, such as the credit quality of the issuer, the length of time remaining until the bond expires, and the bond's interest rate compared to the general interest rates at the time.
• Issuers commonly repurchase bonds in scenarios where interest rates decline or the issuer's credit has improved, allowing new bonds to be issued at a lower cost.
