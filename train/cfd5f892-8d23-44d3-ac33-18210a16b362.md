**Perspective Shift: The Observer’s Lens**

---
A wall of glass divides the room, yet the air between us hums with unseen currents. You stand on one side, arms crossed, jaw set; I on the other, fingers tracing the cracks in the floor. The glass is not just a barrier—it’s a mirror, reflecting fragments of our past selves: your frustration coiled like a storm, my quiet resolve a steady tide. The moment you turn away, the world tilts. The ceiling becomes your ceiling; your desk, my canvas. No longer do I see your shadow as an enemy, but as a silhouette on the horizon, one that might one day learn to dance with mine.

The room’s temperature shifts. Suddenly, the air is warm, thick with the scent of old books and the faint metallic tang of blood—yours, perhaps, or mine, or both. The cracks in the floor no longer bleed light; they whisper of things we did not say. The glass, once impenetrable, fractures into shards that catch the light like broken teeth. I reach for one. It’s cold. But my hand is warm.

Outside, the city breathes. A child laughs from a window above; a dog barks at a stranger. The streetlight casts long shadows, but not on us. On the world. The world is vast, indifferent. And yet—here, in this moment, it is ours. Yours to claim, mine to surrender. The question isn’t *who* sees what, but *how long* we choose to look. The light changes. The room swallows us whole. And when it spits us out, the glass will be new, and the world will be different.
