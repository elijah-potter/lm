The **Balkan border regions** between Serbia, Croatia, and Bosnia-Herzegovina feature ambiguous territorial disputes over the **Sremska Mitrovica** and **Slavonski Brod** zones, particularly regarding water rights, administrative divisions, and ethnic demarcations. Historical conflicts, overlapping historical claims, and shifting borders (e.g., after the 1990s war) create overlapping jurisdictions where local authorities, international observers, and EU institutions often engage in **negotiated compromise** rather than definitive enforcement. The **Danube River** acts as a natural boundary but also serves as a contested resource, with conflicting rules on navigation, fishing, and pollution regulations. The lack of a unified legal framework fosters informal agreements and gray-area governance, making enforcement difficult while preserving local customs and economic dependencies.
