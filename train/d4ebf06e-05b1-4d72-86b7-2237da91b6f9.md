**The Library’s Last Page**

The book was open to a blank page where the ink had never reached—though the spine curled like a question mark. The air smelled of aged parchment and something metallic, the scent of the last reader who had only left behind a single tear on the margin.

Outside, the city slept. The streetlamps flickered like dying stars, casting long shadows that twisted into shapes no one recognized. The clock on the wall of the library chimed twelve, but the seconds stretched into eternity. Somewhere, a clock tower ticked backward.

The librarian, an old man with eyes like polished glass, wiped his hands on his apron and whispered, *"The ending is always the beginning."*

He turned to the empty page and wrote: **"What happens next?"**

Then, he turned the book over.

The back cover was blank. But the spine now bore a new inscription: **"TO BE CONTINUED."** Beneath it, in faded ink, were the words:

**"DO YOU WANT TO KNOW?"**

A hand—warm, unfamiliar—rested on the cover. The first line of the story was already there, waiting.
