A stranger in a crowded room, eyes flicking between strangers like a mirror reflecting unfamiliar faces. The hum of chatter, the scent of coffee, the sharp edges of small talk all felt like a foreign language. The person sitting across from them had memorized a script—small talk, nods, polite smiles—but their mind was elsewhere, replaying the morning’s quiet realization: *They didn’t grow up here.* The walls, the sounds, the way people moved—it all seemed to shift just a little too fast, like a film running backward. The weight of the unknown pressed in, not just the physical space, but the unspoken question in the air: *What if you’re not supposed to be here?* The silence wasn’t empty; it was a space between the expected and the unexpected, and for the first time, it felt like a place they didn’t belong.
