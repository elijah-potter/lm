One stormy evening, the city’s neon glow flickered against the rain-slicked streets, casting jagged shadows on the damp pavement. She sat on the edge of the harbor pier, fingers tracing the cold metal railing, the sound of waves whispering secrets against the hulls of distant ships. The air smelled of salt and old wood, a quiet, bitter perfume that made her chest ache.

He arrived without warning, his boots splashing through the shallows, his breath visible in the cold. His hand found hers, warm and sure, and for a heartbeat, time stood still. The pier groaned beneath their weight, a low, resonant hum, as if the world itself held its breath.

Then the music began—some ancient, forgotten melody, played on a broken violin that had been buried for decades. The notes slithered through the night, weaving between them, and for a single, perfect moment, they were not lost, not alone. The world blurred, the pier dissolved, and only they remained, suspended in time, in each other’s eyes.

The storm passed. The music faded. The pier groaned again, heavier now, as if bearing the weight of what they had shared. But she knew—some moments were not meant to be repeated.
