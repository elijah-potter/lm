The half-lit kitchen smelled of burnt coffee and stale bread. Across the worn wooden table, an empty plate stared back at her—no crumbs, no remnants of the sandwich she’d started. Her fingers traced the rim, sharp with memory. "Damn it," she muttered, wiping her mouth. The canister of salt, usually stacked near the sink, sat empty where it should’ve been. The fridge’s freezer drawer, where her morning yogurt always landed, was empty—*empty*—just a gaping black hole where her breakfast had vanished.

Her phone buzzed in her pocket. Another missed call from her dad, his voice still echoing in her head: *"Where’s your lunch?"* She exhaled, rubbing her temples. The fridge’s door creaked open, its cold metal biting into her wrist. The usual rows of bottles—ketchup, mustard, the half-empty jar of pickles—were gone. And then it hit her: the last of the jam, the one she’d opened just yesterday. A single, crumpled receipt from the grocery store, tucked under the sink, had been the only clue.

Her gaze fell to the counter where her keys usually rested. Just a gap. A *hole*. She picked up the empty jar from the sink, the one she’d used to store her favorite tea leaves. Her thumb brushed the label—*empty*. She let out a frustrated sigh, her shoulders slumping. The missing items weren’t just food; they were habit, routine. And in that moment, it felt like the whole kitchen was holding its breath.
