A sunrise so soft it seems to melt into the morning, painting the sky in hues of pale lavender before dawn’s first whisper. The clock ticks relentlessly, each second a blade of time sharpening the edges of my patience. Coffee brews, but the steam curls too fast—too much like the days themselves, evaporating before they can be savored.

The commute feels like a marathon in heels, the city waking up in a cacophony of honking horns and chatter. Mid-morning, emails pile up, demands stack like untouched plates, and the keyboard feels like a foreign language—each keystroke a stumble through a maze of half-finished thoughts.

Lunch is a pause, but only for a moment. The office hums with the sound of shared coffee and whispered plans, but my mind is already half-gone, lost in the quiet hum of a day that’s never quite long enough. Afternoon slumps into a haze of exhaustion, the afternoon stretch too brief to fully stretch out.

Dinner is a meal of seconds and thirds, the plate never full enough. The evening fades into a blur of responsibilities—errands, calls, the quiet ache of a day that’s left too much unsaid, too much undone. The bed is a promise, but sleep comes like a thief, stealing moments that could have been hours.

The night winds down, and the clock still ticks, counting down the days until another dawn where the cycle repeats. A day that’s too short to hold all the weight of what needs to be done, what needs to be felt.
