A dimly lit, second-story hallway in a decaying library, its wooden floors groaning underfoot. The air hums with the distant echo of a clock striking twelve—once, twice, then silence. Bookshelves, half-collapsed under layers of dust and time, loom like silent sentinels. A single, flickering lamp casts long shadows across the walls, where the faded posters of forgotten stars still hang, their messages obscured by age.

Outside, the world moves—children laugh in the playground below, their voices bouncing off the glass windows like lost coins. But here, in this space, time seems suspended. A half-eaten sandwich on a cracked table sits forgotten. The coffee cup, still warm with the faint scent of caramel, has cooled to lukewarm. A single, unread book lies open on the desk, its spine creased with unspoken thoughts.

A clock on the wall ticks, its hands moving in slow motion, each second a breath held. The pause is in the pause itself—neither rushing forward nor slipping away, but lingering, just for a moment, in the quiet between.
