**A crumpled note** glowed faintly under the lamplight, its edges singed as if torn from a book. The ink was faded but legible:

*"Lost in the attic. The key is beneath the dust—look where the cobwebs don’t quite reach. —L."*

A child’s toy box, half-filled with rusted dolls and a cracked mirror, sat beneath the stairs. The attic door creaked open with a sigh of wind, revealing shelves lined with yellowed tomes and a single, polished brass key nestled among moth-eaten fabric. The dust didn’t touch the hollow beneath the box’s bottom—the spot where the key lay, half-buried.

She found it just before dawn, her breath shallow in the cold. The box’s hinge groaned as she pried it open, revealing a small, leather-bound journal. Pages inside were smudged with tea stains, but the last entry read:

*"They say the old library holds more than books. The key unlocks the chamber where the first librarian kept their secrets. —L."*

The door behind her stood slightly ajar, whispering like a chorus of forgotten voices. Outside, the attic floor tilted unnaturally beneath her feet. The key was still warm.
