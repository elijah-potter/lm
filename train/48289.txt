Pregnancy may sometimes be tiring for mothers-to-be but it appears a tedious business for foetuses too.
Unborn babies yawn repeatedly in the womb, it has been conclusively shown for the first time.
While it was known that they opened and closed their mouths, high-resolution ultrasound was used to end the debate over whether it constituted a true yawn.
However, it is not down to tiredness or boredom.
‘Unlike us, foetuses do not yawn contagiously, nor because they are sleepy,’ explained lead researcher Dr Nadja Reissland, of the University of Durham.
‘Instead, the frequency of yawning in the womb may be linked to the maturing of the brain.’
A true yawn involves a slow opening of the mouth to its full stretch, followed by more rapid closure.
Foetal yawns occurred more frequently early in pregnancy but declined after 28 weeks.
In total, 56 yawns and 27 other mouth openings were observed from 58 scans.
It may also be related to the development of the central nervous system, researchers added.
