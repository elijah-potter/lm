**Mayor announces emergency water rationing due to severe drought**
The City Council of **Solara Heights** has declared a state of emergency as the city’s reservoirs drop below 20% capacity. Starting tomorrow, residents will face a 30% water restriction, with mandatory daytime outdoor use bans and mandatory indoor water-saving measures. Local officials urge citizens to report unauthorized water usage. The city’s water treatment plant has been temporarily shut down to conserve resources. Residents are advised to limit showers to 5 minutes and use low-flow fixtures where possible. A new public awareness campaign will launch next week with fines starting at $100 for violations.

**Energy crisis escalates; grid reliability at critical level**
After a record-breaking heatwave pushed demand to 98% of capacity, **Solara Heights Power District** has implemented rolling blackouts in high-traffic areas, including downtown and the university district. The district has called on residents to reduce non-essential energy use by 20%—switching to LED lighting, unplugging chargers, and avoiding air conditioning use between 3 PM and 9 PM. State officials have warned of potential blackouts if consumption trends do not improve. The city’s emergency energy fund has been tapped for emergency repairs at substations.

**Local farmers urge support amid crop losses**
The **Solara Valley Agricultural Cooperative** reports that 45% of this year’s harvest has been lost due to water shortages and extreme heat. Farmers are calling for emergency subsidies to prevent mass bankruptcies. The cooperative has distributed emergency seed packets to small-scale producers, but many fear the next drought will be worse. A public forum will be held tomorrow evening at the city hall to discuss long-term solutions, including drought-resistant crop rotations and water-sharing agreements.

**Transportation system warns of delays**
Metro Transit has issued a **red alert** for public transit, with buses and trains operating at reduced frequencies due to fuel shortages and driver shortages caused by heat exhaustion. Passengers are advised to plan ahead, and ride-sharing services are reporting surge pricing. The city’s bike lane network remains open but is flooded with cyclists seeking alternative routes. Authorities have deployed extra police to monitor fare evasion and traffic congestion in the downtown core.

**Schools close early as teachers urge strike support**
After a union strike by public school teachers last week led to unpaid overtime claims, the **Solara Heights Education Association** has called for a second day of action tomorrow. Principals have ordered schools to close early to avoid further disputes. Parents are encouraged to attend a rally at the city park at 3 PM, where organizers will demand better teacher compensation and working conditions. The city has not yet responded to the strike demands, which include a 15% salary increase and mandatory PPE for teachers.

The **Solara Innovation District**, home to half of the city’s tech workforce, has begun laying off 200 employees as demand for remote work tools drops. Companies like **SolaraTech Solutions** and **NeoLink AI** have already announced layoffs, citing a 30% decline in contract renewals. The district’s economic development office is pushing for a state bailout fund, but no guarantees have been made. The city’s tech unemployment rate has risen to 8%, with many displaced workers facing eviction due to unpaid rent.

**City council debates housing crisis response**
The **Solara Heights City Council** will hold a closed-door meeting tonight to discuss how to address the city’s worsening housing crisis, which has worsened with rising rents and fewer affordable units. Proposals include a temporary rent freeze and expanded public housing projects, but opposition from property owners has delayed action. A public hearing will be held next week to gather input on potential reforms. The city’s homeless shelter has seen a 40% increase in demand since the drought began.
