I started my speech at his funeral the same way I started almost every conversation I had with others about Burt. I took a deep breath, "He never said, 'I love you.'” As I waited for everyone's small startled gasps and deep intakes of breath, I was reminded why this was my favorite thing to say. Burt and I had been married for 42 years and had 6 children and 14 grandchildren. It was very surprising to others that he didn’t say the three words that meant so much to people worldwide. The words were not important to me, though. 

I continued with my speech, “This is not to say he did not love me. He made me coffee every day of the week except Sunday because he knew I didn’t want to have coffee before church. Burt would stop by the store on Thursday and pick up some strawberries so I would have them for my book club meetings. He would call me the second he knew he was going to be working late so that I wouldn’t worry. When my daughter Mary fell out of a tree and broke her leg, Burt brought me flowers the next day so I would know that he didn’t think it was my fault for not watching her closely. Burt was there for me in the ways that I needed.”

At this point, I am tearing up. Burt was my everything and I loved that man when every part of my soul. My oldest, Oliver, comes up to the stage and wraps me in his arms. Ben and Mary follow and add their embraces. My other children are not far behind and soon they are all up at this little podium giving me a hug that is holding me together at this moment. No one says anything. No one needs to. In my life, my loved ones have proven that actions speak louder than words.
