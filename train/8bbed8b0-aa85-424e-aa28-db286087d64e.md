A dimly lit attic over a forgotten library, where books spiral in slow decay around a single flickering bulb. The air smells of dust and aged parchment, thick with the weight of unread stories. The floorboards creak underfoot, each step echoing like a heartbeat. Outside, the world moves—children laugh, trains whistle—but here, the silence is the only sound, a pause between moments that never quite arrive. The walls are lined with shelves holding volumes that haven’t been touched in decades, their spines cracked like the cracks in time itself. At the center of the room, a table holds a half-empty cup of tea and a half-finished manuscript, both abandoned as if waiting for the right moment to resume. The ceiling is a patchwork of missing tiles, revealing the sky only in glimpses—like the universe itself is holding its breath.
