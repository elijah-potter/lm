•	Mrs. Bennet, the matriarch of the Bennet family, has five daughters. Much to her dismay, none of them are married, and only one or two of them show any real interest in wanting to get married soon. 

•	Mr. Bingley, a well-to-do suitor, develops a crush on Jane Bennet and intends to court and propose to her. However, his best friend, Mr. Darcy, pushes for Mr. Bingley to hold off on his feelings, stating that Jane Bennet is low-class and beneath him. 

•	Mr. Darcy and Mr. Collins fall head over heels in love with Elizabeth (Lizzy) Bennet. Both vie for her attention, but she has no interest in marriage. 

•	When Mr. Collins proposes to Elizabeth Bennet, she turns him down. He then pours his heart out to Charlotte Lucas, one of Elizabeth’s best friends, and Mr. Collins marries Charlotte instead. 

•	Mr. Darcy, unsure about the depth of his feelings for Elizabeth Bennet, proposes to her out of convenience and to secure a possible future while he figures out what he wants. She rejects him. 

•	Lydia Bennet, the youngest sister, falls in love with a militia officer, George Wickham. With her father’s blessings, she elopes with Mr. Wickham to Brighton for marriage. 

•	Mr. Darcy hears the news about Lydia’s elopement and sends a letter to reveal that Mr. Wickham is a liar, cheater, and chronic gambler that squandered his fortunes. Because he cared for her sister, Elizabeth Bennet begins to like and admire Mr. Darcy. 

•	Because Lydia loves Mr. Wickham and has already promised herself to him, Mr. Darcy saves The Bennet family’s honor by paying off all of Mr. Wickham’s debts. This clears Lydia Bennet to marry Wickham, who is still conniving and heartless, but Lydia is infatuated. 

•	Mr. Darcy eventually admits to himself that he has fallen deeply in love with Elizabeth Bennet, and she admits the same in her feelings for him. The two are married.
