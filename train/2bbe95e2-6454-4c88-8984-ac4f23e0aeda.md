The candle flickered, casting long shadows across the worn leather couch. Outside, a light rain pattered against the windowpanes. Elias adjusted his cuffs, fingers tracing the edge of the worn leather jacket. His voice dropped to a murmur, the embers of the fire crackling in the hearth.

*"You know, my mother used to say, 'The story is never over until the last page is turned.'"* He leaned forward, his eyes reflecting the dim glow. *"Let me tell you about the night I met the man who claimed to be the last of his kind."*

The room grew quiet. Elias’s hands clenched the edge of the table, nails digging into wood. His voice tightened as he continued:

*"He found me in a back-alley tavern, three months before the war. I was there, lost, when he walked in—tall, gaunt, with eyes like a wolf’s. He ordered whiskey black, no ice, and when I brought it, he didn’t drink it. Just stared at it. Said, 'This is the last of my people.' I laughed. 'You’re the first one I’ve met.' He didn’t answer. Just watched me for a long time. Then he asked, 'Do you believe in fate?' I said yes. He said, 'Then you’ll understand.' And he left."*

Elias’s breath hitched. *"He came back in the dead of night. A knife in his hand, but not to kill. Just to show me how to use it. Said, 'Some doors shouldn’t be opened.' That’s when I knew—he was warning me. But I didn’t listen. I followed him anyway, down the alleys, past the river where the old woman lived, who told me his name: Veyra. And that’s when the war started."*
