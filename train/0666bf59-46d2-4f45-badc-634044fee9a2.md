My grandmother, Vivian, passed away last November (2023) after a long illness. She lived in a charming cottage in Kennebunkport, Maine. It was her pride and joy, and she’s always made it clear, in writing and verbally, that it would be left to me. I’m her only grandchild. Eleanor, my younger sister, is an only child on her mother's side and has always felt a bit… overlooked, I suppose.

Growing up, Vivian favored me – not in a malicious way, but I was the one she took to ballet lessons, the one she read bedtime stories to, the one she included in her gardening club. Eleanor always felt that Vivian’s attention was directed elsewhere. I never intended to make her feel that way, and I always tried to be inclusive, but I understand her perspective now. 

Vivian’s will was straightforward. The house, all its contents, and her savings were to be left to me. I’m responsible for estate taxes and upkeep, which is a significant burden, but one I was prepared for. I’m a lawyer, so I handled the probate process, which concluded in January 2024. 

Around February 2024, Eleanor approached me. She expressed a desire to use the Kennebunkport house for a week in June 2024, with her husband, Mark, and their two children. She argued that, given Vivian’s legacy of family, it felt cruel to deny her this simple pleasure. She emphasized that she and her family needed a break, and the Maine coast would be perfect for them. She even suggested splitting the utility bills.

I initially felt sympathetic. I truly do care about Eleanor and her family. However, I explained that the house is now my responsibility, and I’m hesitant to allow anyone to use it, especially so soon after inheriting it. I explained the financial obligations I have, and the need to establish a system for maintenance and upkeep. I offered to contribute towards a rental property for them elsewhere. 

Eleanor became quite upset. She accused me of being selfish and intentionally excluding her from a place that “should be shared by the family.” She claimed I was trying to hoard Vivian’s legacy and that I was deliberately trying to hurt her. Mark chimed in, suggesting that I was being petty and that my grandmother would have wanted her grandchildren to enjoy the house. 

My mother, Patricia, has since gotten involved. She’s been subtly pressuring me to “be more generous” and to “remember what’s important.” She argues that denying Eleanor access is damaging our family dynamic and that I’m being unnecessarily rigid. Patricia has always been very focused on family harmony, sometimes to a fault. She also admitted to me, privately, that she felt Vivian favored me as a child, which has always caused a bit of resentment.

I’m in a difficult position. I understand Eleanor’s desire for a vacation, and I want to maintain a good relationship with my family. However, I also feel a deep sense of responsibility towards my grandmother’s legacy and the financial implications of allowing others to use the house. I’m worried about potential damage, liability, and the sheer administrative burden of managing bookings and access. I'm also feeling manipulated by my mother's guilt trip.
