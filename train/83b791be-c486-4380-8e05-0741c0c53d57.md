The last light in the room flickered out as the door creaked softly closed behind her. The hum of the refrigerator faded into silence, followed by the distant, rhythmic drip of the kitchen sink. She sat on the edge of her bed, knees drawn to her chest, holding a single, crumpled photograph—her father’s handwritten address on the back, faded with time. A single tear slid down her cheek, but she did not wipe it away. Outside, the city slept, its glow dimmed to a soft, amber haze. The air smelled like old books and damp wool, like the way it had been for decades. The phone on the nightstand had gone dead hours ago. No one would ever know.
