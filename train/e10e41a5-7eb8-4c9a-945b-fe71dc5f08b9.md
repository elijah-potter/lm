A quiet hallway hums with the faint, rhythmic *click-click-click* of a single, old-fashioned elevator door opening and closing. The sound is like the ticking of a clock, punctuated by the distant, muffled voices of people on the other floors. No music plays, no TV blares—just the soft, steady *drip* of water from a leaky pipe, occasionally drowned out by the sudden *snap* of a loose floorboard.

At the end of the hall, a door stands slightly ajar, its brass handle rusted with age. The air inside is thick with the scent of damp wood and something older—like old books, old secrets. The only light comes from a single bulb hanging from the ceiling, casting long shadows that stretch and twist like living things. No one moves. No one speaks.

A shadow passes over the door. A figure steps inside, and the moment they do, the silence deepens—not just in the room, but in the world beyond. The elevator’s mechanical hum fades into nothing. The distant voices on the other floors suddenly feel far away, as if they’ve been erased by the weight of the moment. The figure pauses, then turns toward the camera, their face half-lit by the bulb’s glow. Their lips move silently, but the words that come out aren’t spoken—they’re *thoughts*, fragments of a story only they understand. The door creaks shut behind them, sealing the moment in silence forever.

Outside, the hallway continues. The elevator’s door opens again. But no one is there to step inside.
