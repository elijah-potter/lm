**The Lull Before the Storm**

The rain fell in quiet, steady sheets against the windows, each drop a whispered promise of something unseen. The old bookshelf groaned under the weight of forgotten tomes, their spines cracked like dry riverbeds, while the lamp on the desk cast long shadows that danced like ghosts in the corners of the room. Outside, the city hummed—a distant, mechanical heartbeat, a thousand voices too loud for the solitude of this space.

She had come here for solitude, but the solitude had already won. The pages of the unfinished novel lay scattered across the desk, half-filled with words that had lost their meaning before they were written. The first half of the story had been about the weight of expectations, the way a single glance could unravel years of careful planning. It had been tender, almost too soft for her taste, but there it was—raw, unfiltered, and achingly honest.

Then, the door creaked.

Not the slow, deliberate sound of a key turning, but something sharper, more urgent. A knock, three sharp raps, and the voice outside, sharp with impatience: *"You’re not opening this door, are you?"*

She exhaled, the breath catching in her throat. The first half of the story had been about the quiet before the storm, but this was the storm itself—no longer a whisper, but a hurricane, and she was caught in its eye.
