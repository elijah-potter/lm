The air still smells faintly of rain-soaked pavement, mixed with the sharp tang of old asphalt and the lingering dust from the last summer’s heat. The bench by the fountain, cracked in places where time has carved deeper than the rain, is empty—just as it was last week, last month, and the year before.

The bookstore’s shelves hum with quiet life. The old leather spines on the tables have absorbed the weight of decades, their edges curled from countless pages flipped. The coffee shop’s counter, polished to a mirror by daily hands, still holds the half-empty mugs of yesterday’s customers. A new sign, half-faded, still reads *"Take a Seat"*—though no one does now.

The clock on the wall ticks like a heartbeat, its hands frozen in a moment that isn’t quite past. The river’s current carries the same slow, stubborn rhythm, the same way it did when you were last here. Somewhere in the distance, a car’s horn echoes—just like it always does, but quieter, more distant, as if the world has learned to move without you.
