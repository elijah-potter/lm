The morning sun spilled through the cracks in Mia’s bedroom window, painting the walls a golden hue. She lay in bed, staring at the ceiling, her mind a tangled mess of tomorrow’s soccer practice, her best friend’s birthday party, and the half-finished science project due next week. Outside, the neighborhood was alive with the hum of chatter and laughter, but Mia felt more like a ghost in her own house.

That’s when she noticed it.

A strange, swirling pattern in the air, like a storm cloud condensed into nothingness. It pulsed faintly, casting an eerie glow around her room. Before she could think, she grabbed her notebook and pencil, scribbling down the details as fast as her fingers moved.

“Mom!” she shouted, flinging open her bedroom door.

Her mom looked up from the kitchen, wiping her hands on her apron. “What is it?”

Mia pointed to the ceiling. “There’s something… in the air.”

Her mom’s eyes widened. “Mia, you’re scaring me. What are you talking about?”

“It’s like a… light show,” Mia said, trying to explain. “It’s moving, and it’s glowing.”

Her mom frowned. “Kids can be imaginative.”

But Mia wasn’t imagining it. She could feel it—the way the air shimmered, the way the pattern seemed to shift when she blinked. That night, after her parents were asleep, she crept downstairs and pressed her ear against the kitchen window. The swirling light had moved closer, now dancing near the garden fence.

The next morning, Mia couldn’t shake it. At school, she tried to tell her friends, but they dismissed her as another one of her wild ideas. Then came the letters.

They started as small, folded pieces of paper found in her locker, her backpack, even in the mailbox. Each one had the same message: *“The sky is not as it should be. Watch where you step.”*

Mia’s hands shook as she read the last one, tucked inside her notebook. The pattern had moved toward her house. That evening, as she stood on her porch, the swirling light was now right above her head, pulsing brighter than ever. She grabbed her phone and snapped a photo.

Then she heard it—a deep, resonant hum, like a sound that shouldn’t exist. The lights flickered. The air turned thick, pressing against her skin. Before she could react, the light above her burst into a thousand shimmering fragments, scattering like stars in the night sky.

Mia stumbled back, her vision blurring. When she looked up again, the garden fence was gone. In its place stood a towering, metallic structure, its surface etched with strange symbols. At the top, a figure stood motionless, its face obscured by a mask of shifting light.

The hum grew louder, vibrating through Mia’s bones. She clutched her phone, the image still frozen in her mind—a world where the sky wasn’t just above, but *alive*.

That night, Mia dreamed of a city that wasn’t on any map, where streets curved into impossible shapes and the sky wasn’t blue but *endless*. She woke with a gasp, her heart pounding. The letters had stopped. The light was gone.

But the next morning, as she sat at her desk, her pencil scratching furiously, she realized something was different. The air smelled different. The world felt… *wrong*.

And then she saw it—the first sign of the second letter, waiting on her desk.

---

The letters kept coming. Each one carried a warning, a clue. And with each one, Mia realized the sky wasn’t just changing—it was *calling*.

Somewhere, something was watching.

And it wanted her to listen.
