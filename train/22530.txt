Meaning of Red in Different Cultures
What does the color red mean in different cultures? Here are some examples:
In Russia, it symbolizes beauty.
In Chinese culture. Red stands for fire, summer, and south. Chinese people also see it as a sign of fortune and good luck. People wear red dresses to celebrate the New Year, and they also decorate their houses with red color.
Red colors play an important role in Japanese tradition. It is associated with some deities in Buddhist and Shinto traditions.
Falun red is a special color that is reserved for the privilege classes in Sweden.
The word "paise kokkino" which means “touch red” is often used in Greek whenever two people coincidentally say the same words at the same time. This is because they believe that if this happens, the two will have a quarrel in the future, but they can avoid a quarrel by touching something red near them.
In Jamaica, the word “red” is a slang for a drunk person.
In Nigeria, it is a slang which means one is in a difficult situation.
In England, as national icons, phone booths and double decker buses are printed red.
The red mark on the forehead is believed to bring good luck in India.
In Hindu, the color red is a symbol of life, joy, creativity, and energy.
The Aztec culture associates red with blood.
In Catholic Church, red represents feast days of martyrs.
Some clothing stores in Israel like kosher banned red colored dress for women and sell-out only loose-fitting apparel as red is considered lustful in this culture.
