The pen paused midair, trembling like a leaf in an unseasonal storm. Outside, the rain fell in slow, deliberate sheets, each droplet a whisper of uncertainty echoing through the empty room. I had meant to write something bold—something that wouldn’t crumble under the weight of my own hesitation. Instead, I scribbled lines that wavered like the first light of dawn, uncertain if they were dawn or dusk.

The words spilled out in jagged fragments, half-formed thoughts tangled with doubt. *What if I’m wrong?* The question hung between every sentence, a ghost that refused to be exorcised. I tried to shape them into something solid, but the ink blurred, merging with the doubt itself, indistinguishable now from the rest.

The page yawned open, empty but for the ghost of what could have been. I stared at it, my fingers twitching toward the eraser, then hesitating. What if I erased everything? What if I wrote nothing at all? The air felt thick with the weight of choices I couldn’t name.

Then, slowly, something shifted. Not the words, but the way I held them—less afraid now, less certain. The doubt wasn’t gone, but it had learned to sit beside me, no longer dominant. The story wasn’t perfect, but it was mine. And that, I decided, was enough.
