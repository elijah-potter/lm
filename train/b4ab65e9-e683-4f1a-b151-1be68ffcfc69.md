Quantum tunneling in a double-slit experiment observed in a **cold atom interferometer** (e.g., trapped cesium atoms at near-absolute zero). The atoms pass through both slits simultaneously, interfering constructively/destructively, exhibiting wave-particle duality, and occasionally "leaking" through barriers that should classically be impenetrable, demonstrating non-local probability amplitudes via the **EPR paradox**. This effect is measurable via momentum-space distributions and phase-space entanglement.
