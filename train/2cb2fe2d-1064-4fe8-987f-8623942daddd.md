In the quiet village of Eldermere, where mist curled like serpents over the cobblestone streets, there stood a lone watchman named Elias. His lantern flickered, casting long shadows across the ancient stone wall he tended. A flicker in the dark caught his eye—an unnatural glow, pulsing faintly against the wall’s cracks.

Elias reached out, his fingers brushing the wall. The glow intensified, and suddenly, the cobblestones beneath his feet rippled. A figure emerged—a child, no older than seven, dressed in tattered clothes, clutching a broken doll. The child’s eyes, sharp and knowing, locked onto Elias.

"You see it too, don’t you?" the child whispered. "The wall is hungry."

The lantern’s light dimmed, and Elias realized the glow wasn’t from the wall—it was from the child. Their skin shimmered, and the doll’s face twisted into a grin. The cobblestones dissolved into dust, and Elias found himself standing on a dusty plain, the child now a woman with silver hair, her voice a whisper in his ear.

"You returned to the beginning," she murmured. "The circle never ends."

A storm raged overhead, and the woman’s form flickered. The lantern in Elias’s hand—once a symbol of hope—now pulsed with the same eerie light as the wall. He looked down. The cobblestones beneath his boots had returned, cold and unyielding.

The child-doll smiled again. "Now," she said, "you must choose."

The wind howled, carrying the scent of rain and something older—something that had waited for centuries. The lantern’s light wavered, and Elias felt the weight of the first night pressing back into his bones. The wall’s hunger was no longer just a metaphor; it was the past, the present, and the future, all tangled in one.

The child’s fingers traced the doll’s face, and the doll’s eyes burned into Elias’s soul. The lantern’s flame snuffed out.
