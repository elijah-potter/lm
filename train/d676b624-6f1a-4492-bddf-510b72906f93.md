## A Counseling Session

**Setting:** A warmly lit, comfortably furnished therapy office.

**Characters:**

*   **Eleanor Vance:** A client in her early thirties, visibly anxious.
*   **Dr. Alistair Finch:** A therapist, experienced and composed.

---

**Eleanor:** (Fidgeting with her hands) It's just…everything feels like it’s piling up. Work is demanding, my mother’s health has been a concern, and I feel like I’m constantly disappointing everyone.

**Dr. Finch:** (Leaning forward slightly, maintaining eye contact) That sounds incredibly overwhelming, Eleanor. It takes a great deal of courage to articulate that level of pressure. Could you tell me a little more about what specifically feels like disappointment?

**Eleanor:** (Takes a deep breath) I don’t know. It’s a feeling. My boss expects me to lead this new project, and I'm worried I'm not capable. My mother needs more help than I can realistically provide, and I feel guilty about it. And with my partner, Liam…I just feel like I’m not the person he needs. It's a constant internal monologue of "not enough."

**Dr. Finch:** "Not enough." That’s a powerful phrase. It suggests a deep-seated belief about your own capabilities. Let’s explore that a bit. Where do you think that belief originated? Was it something you heard growing up, or perhaps an experience that shaped your perspective?

**Eleanor:** (Hesitates) I think…my father was always very critical. He had high expectations, and I rarely felt I met them. It wasn’t malicious, I don’t think, but it was…consistent. And I’m realizing now, I’m holding myself to that same impossible standard.

**Dr. Finch:** It's common for individuals to internalize the standards of significant figures in their lives, particularly during formative years. It sounds as though you're beginning to recognize that your father's expectations may not be entirely reflective of your own values or capabilities. You’re demonstrating a remarkable degree of self-awareness.

**Eleanor:** (A small, watery smile) It’s just…it’s hard to let go of that feeling. It’s been with me for so long.

**Dr. Finch:** Letting go of deeply ingrained beliefs takes time and conscious effort. We won’t be dismantling it overnight. However, acknowledging it is the crucial first step. We can work on identifying those moments when that "not enough" voice arises and exploring the validity of its claims. Would you be open to practicing some techniques to challenge those thoughts? Perhaps a thought record, where you document the thought, the situation, and an alternative, more balanced perspective?

**Eleanor:** (Nods slowly) A thought record…that sounds…structured. I’m willing to try it. I just don’t want to feel like I’m failing at that too.

**Dr. Finch:** (Offers a reassuring smile) There’s no failing, Eleanor. This is a process of self-discovery and growth. And I’m here to support you every step of the way. Remember, progress isn’t always linear. There will be days when you feel more in control, and days when the feelings return. The key is to recognize those patterns and develop strategies to navigate them. Let's focus on small, achievable goals for the coming week. How about we start with identifying one situation where you felt "not enough" and begin to challenge that thought?

**Eleanor:** (Takes another deep breath, seeming slightly calmer) Okay. I can do that. Thank you, Dr. Finch. It helps just to…to have someone listen.

**Dr. Finch:** (Maintains eye contact, with genuine warmth) You are most welcome, Eleanor. That’s precisely what I’m here for. And I am glad you shared your experience with me. Let’s schedule our next session for the same time next week. In the meantime, please begin that thought record. And remember, be kind to yourself.
