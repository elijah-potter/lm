The ruins of **Villahermosa de Río Tinto**, Spain, stand as a testament to forgotten history. Once a thriving mining town in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, it was a hub for the exploitation of iron ore in the Rio Tinto basin. By the mid-20th century, the town was abandoned after the mines were closed, leaving behind crumbling buildings, overgrown streets, and the eerie silence of a place where life once thrived.

Today, it is preserved as a **cultural heritage site**, attracting visitors who seek the ghost of its past. The abandoned houses, churches, and workshops are overrun by nature, yet their foundations remain—echoes of a community that once supported hundreds. The town’s legend persists in local folklore, whispered tales of miners who vanished, and the haunting beauty of its decaying architecture.

A place where memory lingers in the stone.
