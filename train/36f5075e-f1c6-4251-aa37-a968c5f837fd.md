In the hollowed-out space of the old lighthouse, the last beam of light flickered like a dying ember against the storm-wracked cliffs. Captain Elias had spent thirty years here, watching the tides and the secrets of the sea. Tonight, the wind howled through the ruins, carrying the scent of salt and something older—something that had been waiting for him all along.

The compass, his only constant, spun wildly. It wasn’t the needle that broke; it was the needle itself, unraveling into static, as if the very direction of truth had dissolved. "They’re coming," Elias whispered, though no one answered. The letters on the wall—scratched by sailors long gone—now glowed faintly, as if the ink had been dipped in something more than ink.

A shadow moved in the corner where the lantern should have been. Too still, too deliberate. The door creaked open, not with a breeze, but with the slow, deliberate push of something heavy. Inside, the air thickened, pressing against his skin like the first breath of a storm he couldn’t outrun. The lantern flickered—then went out.

Elias stood in the dark, the weight of centuries pressing down on him. The question was already written in the salt on his lips, but the words refused to form. What had he done to deserve this? And what would the sea say if he asked?
