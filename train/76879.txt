The need to use coaxial cable to connect a television with a video recorder in a different part of the house is a nuisance. Now the Inline Connection Corporation of Arlington, Virginia, says the two can be linked through the telephone wiring (EP 798 923).
The video's output is converted to a radio frequency signal of around 60 megahertz and plugged into a phone socket. The TV can be plugged into any other extension socket, while a converter switches the signal from 60 MHz to television frequencies. The thin telephone wires drastically reduce the signal strengthby around 30 decibels over 100 metresso the VHF radio signal has to be amplified. The VHF signal does not interfere with the telephone, which operates at frequencies of around 3 kilohertz. The same system can be used to connect any number of videos and TVs.
