In the kitchen, the fridge’s light dims, then dies entirely. A single candle on the counter burns low, its wax pooling in a saucer. Outside, thunder rumbles in the distance, muffled by the storm’s relentless downpour. Inside, the hum of the fridge’s compressor is the only sound—then it cuts off. The scent of stale coffee lingers, mixed with the earthy smell of rain-soaked pavement. A child’s laughter echoes faintly from the bedroom, muffled by the door’s closed, but the parent’s voice is low, reassuring. A phone buzzes on the coffee table, its screen dark. The last notification was from the power company.
