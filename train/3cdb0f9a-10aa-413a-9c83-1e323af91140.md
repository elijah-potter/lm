A compact, metallic cylinder—roughened edges and a worn label—slides across a cracked wooden tabletop. The first hand, a child’s, grips it tightly, fingers tracing the ridges like a map. The cylinder hovers between their palms, then is plucked by an adult’s calloused fingers, whose grip is firm but deliberate. A pause; the air hums with the quiet anticipation of another transfer.

The child’s thumb presses a button on the cylinder’s side, a faint *click* echoing. The adult’s wrist flexes, and the object follows their motion, spiraling toward a third hand—older, slower, the touch more deliberate. A breath is exhaled as the cylinder meets the third palm, where a name is scrawled in ink on its surface: *"For you."*

The scene repeats: hands move in a loop—left, right, up, down—each carrying a story etched in the cylinder’s worn surface, passed along like a secret or a promise.
