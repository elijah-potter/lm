A moment unfolded when the coffee machine hissed, steam curling into the dim morning air. Across the cluttered kitchen, her fingers hovered over the keyboard, cursor blinking. The last sentence read *"He didn’t deserve this."* Her breath hitched. The past two days had been a blur of half-finished thoughts, unspoken words, and the weight of regret. But now, her eyes flicked to the window—the one that overlooked the old oak tree where they’d stood, laughing, years ago. The memory was sharp, but so was the truth: she hadn’t meant to hurt him. Not *this* time.

She deleted the line, the cursor blinking back to life. The words *"Maybe it wasn’t his fault"* appeared first. Then: *"He was just… lost."* The screen filled with fragments of honesty she hadn’t dared to write before. Her chest ached, but the words flowed, unfiltered. By the time she hit Enter, the kitchen felt lighter, as if the weight of the old decision had been lifted, too.

The screen glowed, text now untouched—only the change remained.
