---

The garden was a quiet place, untouched by the rush of the world beyond its fence. Each morning, the first light painted the roses in shades of blush and gold, and the bees moved lazily between the blooms, collecting nectar in slow, deliberate circles. The air smelled of damp earth and honeysuckle, a sweet, bitter perfume that lingered on the tongue like a forgotten memory.

One afternoon, as I sat on a wooden bench, watching the children chase each other through the tall grass, my phone buzzed. A message from my sister: *"You should see this. I think I found the thing."*

The screen flickered with a grainy photo—something dark and jagged, half-buried in the soil near the old oak. My stomach twisted. The image was blurry, but the edges were sharp, as if it had been taken just before it was crushed underfoot. My fingers hovered over the camera, but I hesitated.

The sun dipped below the treeline, casting long shadows across the lawn. The air grew colder, heavier. The thing in the photo wasn’t just something; it was *hungry*. Hungry for stories, for names, for the echoes of voices long since faded. I should have called the police. Should have called anyone. But the garden had always been my sanctuary, and now it felt like a trap.

My hands shook as I typed back: *"We’re coming."*

---

**The call came at midnight.**

The police car’s headlights cut through the dark like a blade, illuminating the thing’s twisted form half-concealed beneath the roots of the oak. The officer’s radio crackled with sirens, but their voices were distant, muffled—like the thing itself had swallowed them whole. The thing *moved*. Slowly, methodically, its limbs uncoiling from the earth, revealing a body that wasn’t quite human, not quite *anything*.

Its mouth stretched too wide, teeth filed to points, and the way it watched me—*it knew*. I should have run. Should have screamed. But the garden had changed. The roses had withered. The bees had stopped buzzing.

The officer’s radio cut off. His gun was cold in his hand. *"You should’ve left,"* he said.

I didn’t answer. The thing’s fingers closed around my wrist, pulling me toward its hollow eyes. The world around me dissolved into static.

The next thing I knew, I was in a room lined with books, shelves stretching endlessly. A voice—*mine*—whispered from the darkness: *"You remember now."*

The garden was still there. Waiting. Always waiting.
