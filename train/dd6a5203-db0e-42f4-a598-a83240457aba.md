In the dim glow of the abandoned library’s flickering fluorescent lights, Emma sat cross-legged on the floor, her fingers tracing the spine of an old book. Across from her, Jake leaned back in the cracked leather chair, arms crossed, staring at the ceiling.

Emma’s journal was open, her hand poised over a blank page. Outside, rain tapped against the stained glass windows. Inside, the air smelled of damp paper and old ink. A single candle burned low on a table between them, casting long shadows.

---

Emma whispered, *"I was there. The door slammed shut behind me. I heard the scream—it wasn’t me. I was running, but the hallway twisted like a maze, and then—nothing. Just darkness. And then… the voices. Not my own. Someone else’s. A woman. Her voice was sharp, like glass breaking. I don’t know why I remember it like it happened today."*

Jake scoffed, rubbing his temples. *"You’re making it up. The library’s been empty since last year. The storm knocked out the lights, but you’re the one who stayed. You were drunk last time you came here. You’re hallucinating."*

Emma’s fingers stilled. *"No. I’m not. I saw it. I *felt* it. The way the air shifted. The way my skin prickled. Like someone was watching us from the shadows. But it wasn’t the librarian—he’s been gone for months. It wasn’t the students who used to come here. It wasn’t anyone. It was… something else."*

Jake’s eyes narrowed. *"Bullshit. You’re paranoid. The last time you were here, you swore you saw a ghost. Turns out, it was just a rat. Or a draft. Or your own imagination."*

Emma’s voice dropped. *"It wasn’t a rat. It was a woman. I saw her. In the corner, by the window. She was standing there, watching us. But she wasn’t real. Or was she?"*

A long silence stretched between them, broken only by the distant hum of the rain against the glass.

Jake exhaled sharply. *"You’re losing your mind. The library’s haunted. It’s always been haunted. Let’s go."*

Emma’s breath hitched. *"You don’t believe me?"*

Jake stood abruptly, knocking over the candle. Smoke curled into the air. *"I believe you’re lying. But I’m not leaving until you admit it."* He turned to leave, but paused at the door. *"Next time you come here, I’m not coming back with you."*

Emma watched him go, the weight of the moment pressing down on her. The candle’s flame flickered, casting her silhouette against the stained wall—shoulders hunched, hands clenched at her sides. The rain kept falling.
