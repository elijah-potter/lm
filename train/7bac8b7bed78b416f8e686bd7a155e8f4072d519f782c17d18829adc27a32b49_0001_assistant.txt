The year was 2004. The Burger King, Burger King’s aptly-named mascot, had just resurfaced in the restaurant’s advertising material. The updated appearance included a disproportionately sized plastic head, although The Burger King seemed to have an otherwise normal-looking human body. As soon as his character’s remodel made its television debut in a 2004 commercial, audiences were quick to label him creepy and the general public seemed disinterested in him.

Fast forward to the year of 2021, where The Burger King has reigned for nearly two decades in spite of those early criticisms. Now, he seems to have taken a method-acting approach to his character by employing himself as the manager of an actual Burger King in West Hollywood. There, The Burger King makes odd requests and demands his “subjects” do as they are told (no matter how ridiculous the ask is). For example, when customers enter the restaurant, The Burger King’s employees must quickly produce a tiny plastic horn on which they should perform an “entrance song fit for royalty.”

Outside of harassing employees and making their work life worse, The Burger King also has ideas on how the restaurant should operate. First, he suggested Burger King’s beloved chicken sandwich should be accompanied by a poultry item called the “Ch’King.” No one knows what that is supposed to mean, but it is far easier to just wait out his mistakes and let them fade versus trying to change his mind. 

The Burger King even went as far as to make his employees perform a dance on video that was “sure to go viral," in a promotional effort.

Continuing his surveillance, as well as the never-ending stream of bad ideas, The Burger King remains in control of the unfortunate West Hollywood restaurant location.
