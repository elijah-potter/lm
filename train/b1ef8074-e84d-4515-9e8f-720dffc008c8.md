A mist-laden valley, the air thick with dampness, where the slow pulse of life unfolds through the land itself. Towering, gnarled oaks cast long shadows over moss-covered stones, their bark etched with ancient runes. The water in a still pond reflects only the dim glow of a half-frozen lakebed, its surface broken by a single, slow ripple.

A lone wolf moves through the underbrush, its paws barely disturbing the carpet of ferns. It does not chase, it does not fight—only observes. The wind carries whispers of the forest: the distant call of an owl, the rustle of unseen creatures among the roots. The silence is not empty; it is the weight of everything unseen.

In the distance, a small, abandoned cottage sits, its roof sagging under decades of rain. No one moves inside. The scene is a quiet, living entity, each element—rock, wind, water—holding its breath, waiting.
