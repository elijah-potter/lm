The neon hum of a 24-hour diner flickered as the waitress—whose name tag read *"Lena"*—passed a half-eaten pie to a customer. The plate was cold, the crust too crisp, but the aroma of caramelized apples lingered. Her fingers trembled as she wiped the condensation from the glass.

Across the room, a man in a rumpled suit sat alone, staring at the same menu from yesterday. His reflection in the mirror above the bar was unmistakably *himself*—same shadowed eyes, same jagged scar across his cheek—but the man in the suit had aged, his once-straight hair now streaked with silver.

A server clattered a tray, dropping a coffee cup. It shattered against the floor. The sound echoed like a gunshot.

The diner’s bell chimed. Lena’s eyes darted to the door—*that* door. The one she’d seen before, the one that didn’t belong in this time. A woman in a 1950s dress stepped through, holding a newspaper dated 1987. She paused, then walked straight toward the table where the suit sat. She placed the paper on the table, then turned to leave.

The diner’s TV flickered to life, broadcasting a news report from the same year. The anchor’s voice cut through the diner’s low hum:

*"Ladies and gentlemen, we are witnessing history in the making..."*

The suit’s fingers tightened around the menu. The pie box in his lap suddenly felt heavier.
