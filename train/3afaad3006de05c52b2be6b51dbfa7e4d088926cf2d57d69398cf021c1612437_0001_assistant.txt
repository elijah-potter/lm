"The Old Man and the Sea" by Ernest Hemmingway is a writing that depicts an unlucky old man named "Santiago's" toils of fishing. Santiago is someone with vivid dreams about important milestones in his life, however lately, Santiago has been having dreams about simple childhood memories.

Santiago has a burning passion for baseball and idolizes Joe DiMaggio. After 84 days of not catching any fish, Santiago would pray to baseball players in hopes of catching a fish. Santiago is hopeful that eventually he will catch a big fish and be able to sell it to the village he lives in. 

Eventually, through sleepless nights Santiago finds himself battling to catch a big marlin. Unfortunately, the Marlin wins the battle and escapes from Santiago's grasp. Although feeling despair, Santiago remains hopeful that he will catch a big fish the next time he finds himself in another battle.

Overall what stands out in this book is the fundamental message of hope. According to "The Old Man and the Sea," hope is what drives Santiago to go out to the ocean every day and fish. Even without catching fish for a multitude of days on end, Santiago still collects himself to go out and fish in hopes of catching the fish of his dreams.
