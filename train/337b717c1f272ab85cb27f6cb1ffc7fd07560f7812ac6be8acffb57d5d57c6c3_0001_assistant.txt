In the aftermath of the Tulsa Race Massacre, a Grand Jury investigated the issue over twelve days, ultimately ruling that the riot had been the result of black mobs. Though law enforcement was also implicated in failing to prevent the riot, no one was ever charged for the violence or property damage. On June 3, businessmen and civic leaders met to discuss raising funds and aid for rebuilding Greenwood, claiming that Tulsa had just suffered something worthy of shame and humiliation and restitution was their only way to recover. Despite this resolve, many black families were forced to live in tents during the winter of 1921-1922 as they worked to rebuild the district. On top of that, a group of influential white developers passed a fire ordinance that would prohibit full-sized wooden frame buildings to be constructed in the area, making it nearly impossible for most of the black families to rebuild. These developers wanted to redevelop the area for business and industry so that black people would be forced to move further from the center of the city.
