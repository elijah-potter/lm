A hollowed-out wooden box in a quiet corner of an old mill, its walls worn smooth by decades of rain and sunlight. The floor is packed earth, cracked with roots that stretch into the earth beneath. A single lantern hangs from the rafters, its glass too thin for the dim light to break through, casting flickering shadows on the ceiling. Inside, a small, curved stone table sits beside a low bench, its surface polished by countless hands over time. A single, weathered journal lies open, its pages filled with sketches of landscapes and notes on migration paths. The scent of damp earth and aged wood lingers, a quiet whisper of persistence.

The box feels like a home because it was built by those who once moved through it—season after season, year after year. The mill itself is a ghost of its former self, its doors long since rotted away, but the box remains, stubbornly intact. It’s not just a place; it’s a story etched into wood and soil.
