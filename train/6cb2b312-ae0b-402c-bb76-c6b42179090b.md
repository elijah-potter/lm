You should have stayed in the library. The last book on the shelf had been checked out for a week already, and the librarian’s warning about "unauthorized access" echoed in your head like a bad omen. But you were late. Again.

The hallway was wrong. The floorboards groaned underfoot, not like the ones at the university—these were older, warped, as if the wood itself had been chewed by something unseen. Shadows stretched unnaturally long, pooling near the door you’d come through, the one marked *Do Not Enter* in faded red paint. Your flashlight flickered, casting jagged light across the walls where something—*something with too many fingers*—had carved symbols into the plaster.

The air smelled like damp earth and rust. And then you heard it: a whisper, barely audible over the distant hum of the university’s ancient servers, the ones that powered the library’s digital archives. *"You shouldn’t be here."*

Your pulse quickened. You didn’t recognize the voice. Not in this life. Not in any life you’d ever lived before.

The door creaked open. Inside, the library was silent. Too silent. The shelves stretched endlessly, books stacked in perfect rows, but none of them were yours. The ones you’d borrowed—*The Atlas of Forgotten Cities*, *The Ledger of Names*—were gone. Replaced by others. Strange, brittle books with titles you couldn’t read: *Before You Were Me*, *What Happened to Your Face*, *The Last Thing You Remembered*.

A figure stood at the far end, wrapped in a cloak that swallowed the light. Their face was half-lit by the same whispering voice, but their mouth didn’t move. They tilted their head. *"You’re looking for answers,"* the voice said. *"But answers aren’t what you’re looking for. You’re the answer."*

The last thing you saw before the light blurred was the symbol on the floor—a spiral of teeth and stars—and the way your own reflection in the glass door *didn’t move*.
