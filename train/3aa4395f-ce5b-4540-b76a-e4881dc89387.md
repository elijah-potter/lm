The once-vibrant meadow now lies blanketed in frost, its grassy hues muted by an eerie silver sheen. The sky, once a lively canvas of clouds, is now a fractured, pale blue canopy, streaked with jagged tendrils of high-altitude mist. Rivers that once rushed through valleys are now still, their banks encrusted with frozen algae, reflecting a sickly, ghostly light. The air carries the faint, metallic tang of distant volcanic activity, and the wind hums with the low, resonant vibration of shifting ice formations. The landscape is quieter, more still—every sound muffled by the weight of unnatural silence.
