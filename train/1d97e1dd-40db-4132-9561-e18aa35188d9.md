A hollowed-out oak bench in a sun-dappled courtyard, its weathered wood groaning under the weight of an empty chair. The air hums with the quiet buzz of cicadas, their song a steady metronome marking the passage of time. A single, half-finished sketchbook lies open on the bench’s armrest—pages filled with scribbled notes about the pause itself: *What stops us? Where do we linger?*

The floor beneath is a mosaic of cracked tiles, some still bearing the faded remains of a mural depicting a winding river that never reaches an end. A fountain in the corner spits water into the air, droplets catching the light like scattered stars. The walls, lined with old mirrors, reflect the same unchanging scene—this is a place where stillness is the only rhythm, where the past and future blur into an endless loop of *waiting*. The scent of damp earth and blooming jasmine lingers, a perfume of the unspoken, the unfulfilled. Here, time isn’t measured in hours but in the weight of what hasn’t been said.
