A three-day trip to a remote mountain village in Bhutan transformed Daniel. The first night, he slept under a sky so dense with stars it felt like the universe had paused for him. The second day, he hiked to a hidden waterfall, where the mist carried with it a whisper of his childhood fear—now replaced by a newfound humility. The third morning, he sat with villagers trading stories over tea, their simplicity grounding him in a way he hadn’t felt in years.

Back home, he wrote letters in his journal, not for anyone but for himself: *"I am not the same. I am lighter."* The change wasn’t grand or dramatic—just the quiet weight of discovery. The village’s philosophy of *gross national happiness* became his mantra: not to seek comfort, but to find it in the ordinary.
