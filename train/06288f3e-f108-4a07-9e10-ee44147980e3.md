Nonverbal signals:
- **Micro-expressions** (e.g., brief eye flickers, lip curls) indicating subconscious evaluation.
- **Proximity shifts** (e.g., sudden movement closer or away) reflecting comfort or tension.
- **Posture shifts** (e.g., crossed arms, leaning in) signaling dominance or openness.
- **Body orientation** (e.g., facing someone vs. looking away) mirroring engagement or disengagement.
- **Facial muscle tension** (e.g., jaw clenching, forehead furrowing) revealing stress or focus.
- **Vocal tone shifts** (e.g., abrupt pauses, monotone drop) indicating hesitation or certainty.
- **Hand gestures** (e.g., open palms, clenched fists) showing openness or control.
- **Eye contact duration** (e.g., too brief or too long) signaling trust or deception.

Contextual cues:
- **Environmental cues** (e.g., lighting, space constraints) influencing behavior.
- **Past patterns** (e.g., previous interactions) shaping implicit expectations.
- **Inherent physical traits** (e.g., height, facial structure) influencing perceived authority.
- **Unspoken social norms** (e.g., cultural expectations) dictating acceptable nonverbal cues.

Example scenario:
A candidate’s **nervous fidgeting** (pen tapping, shifting feet) paired with **increased eye contact** and **relaxed posture** after being asked about a project could indicate preparation and confidence, while **avoided eye contact** and **tightened stance** might signal discomfort or insecurity. The decision—"hire or pass"—is often made on the **accumulation of these nonverbal micro-moments** rather than explicit words.
