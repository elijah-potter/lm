The abandoned plan involved constructing a floating city atop a massive, repurposed oil platform in the Gulf of Mexico. The initial design called for a self-sustaining ecosystem with solar farms, hydroponic farms, and desalination units to support a population of 50,000. Funding was secured from a consortium of international investors, and the first phase of platform stabilization and structural reinforcement was completed by Q4 2023.

However, the project stalled due to several critical factors:

1. **Geopolitical Tensions**: The U.S. government imposed stricter environmental regulations after the 2023 Gulf of Mexico oil spill, delaying permits and increasing compliance costs.
2. **Economic Downturn**: Rising energy prices and global inflation reduced investor confidence, leading to a 30% decrease in funding by mid-2024.
3. **Technological Challenges**: Engineers discovered unexpected seismic activity in the proposed location, requiring additional stabilization measures that exceeded budget projections.
4. **Corporate Shift**: The lead developer, a subsidiary of a major energy conglomerate, pivoted toward renewable offshore wind farms, redirecting resources and personnel.
5. **Public Backlash**: Environmental groups and local communities protested, citing risks to marine biodiversity and long-term economic disruption.

By late 2024, the project was officially canceled, with remaining assets liquidated and staff reassigned to other ventures. The abandoned design files are now archived in a secure, encrypted database.
