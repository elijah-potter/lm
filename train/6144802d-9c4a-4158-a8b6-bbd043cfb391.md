In the crumbling attic of an old Victorian mansion, the rain dripped steadily through the rusted roof. Elias, a young historian, knelt beside a crumpled envelope wedged between two rotting floorboards. The paper was yellowed, the ink faded, yet the words inside were still legible.

*"To the next keeper of the lost: The key to the vault is not in the chest but in the memory of the one who guards it. Seek the silver needle and the whispering wind."*

Elias’s fingers traced the faded signature: *"Vera L. Whitmore, 1892."* He unfolded the paper further, revealing a torn map fragment with a single line—*"Turn east at the hollow oak, where the clock stops."*

A cold dread settled in Elias’s chest. The message was a challenge, a clue to something buried beneath the mansion’s foundation. He pulled out his old pocket watch—the one Vera had given him before she vanished years ago—and noticed the hands had stopped at 3:17. The exact time she died.

The attic door creaked open. Elias didn’t look up. The wind outside carried a faint, echoing *ticking*—not of a clock, but of something older, deeper. The hollow oak was calling him.

He picked up the map, the silver needle Vera had left in his coat pocket, and stepped into the storm.
