Pampas grass (Cortaderia selloana), a tall, feathery plant that produces fluffy plumes at its tips, is used as an ornamental addition in many landscapes. Some varieties reach up to 10 feet tall, and the colors vary from white, cream and pink to green and brown. The grass often is used dry or fresh in vase arrangements, especially during fall. It takes little time and effort to make the arrangements last and maintain their appearance. Pampas grass is hardy in U.S. Department of Agriculture plant hardiness zones 7 through 11.
Wait for the pampas grass plumes to be fully developed but not shedding. Put on a long-sleeved shirt and a pair of thick gloves. They will prevent the grass' sharp leaves from cutting your hands and arms.
Cut the pampas grass at the soil level with a sharp pair of pruning shears. Trim off the bottom end of the grass so the plume-tipped blades are the length you prefer for your vase arrangement.
Spritz the plumes with hairspray, coating them evenly. Hairspray keeps the plumes from shedding while they're on display. Place the cut ends of the grass blades in a vase without water.
Things You Will Need
- Long-sleeved shirt
- Thick gloves
- Pruning shears
- In order to dry the pampas grass before arranging it in a vase, hang a small bunch of it upside down and tied together with rubber bands in a warm, dry place for about two weeks. Maintain good air circulation its location.
- Pampas grass is considered invasive in some U.S. states.
