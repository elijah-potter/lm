The hallway flickered with half-lit fluorescent strips, casting long shadows that shifted as the wind tugged at the drapes. A half-empty mug of lukewarm coffee sat on the edge of the desk, its surface damp with condensation. The scent of stale air and old books lingered in the space.

A single word, scrawled in jagged ink across the back of a receipt: *"Lost."*

Her fingers traced the faded letters, the paper curling at the edges like a wound. The pen had slipped from her grip mid-sentence, leaving the final line unfinished—*"not here, but..."*—as if the rest had dissolved into the air like smoke.

The phone buzzed. She ignored it. The call would come again. Always.

The floorboards creaked under her boots, each step heavier than the last. The mirror above the door was cracked, a spiderweb of glass where her reflection used to stand whole. Now it was a fractured still-life of her own face, half-smile, half-frown, as if she’d already decided the answer.

Outside, the city breathed—cars, laughter, the distant hum of a siren. She had been searching for something that didn’t exist in any of them.
