I remember the way the old clock tower chime struck noon,
the way the light caught the streetlamps like a thousand scattered stars.
I remember the way my father’s hands would hum with quiet strength,
as he carved the same wooden spoon from oak, season after season.

I remember the taste of rainwater on the tongue of a child,
the way the earth seemed to breathe deeper after the first snowfall.
I remember the bookshop’s smell—old paper and ink and the scent of forgotten pages—
how the shelves seemed to whisper if you listened just right.

I remember the way the ocean swallowed my fear,
how the tide pulled me under and then carried me back,
and I knew then that the sea was the heart of the world.
I remember the way time bent like a river,
carving the same paths through the same landscapes,
but carrying me further, deeper, into something new.

I remember the way the wind carried the names of loved ones
across the fields like lost things, never quite gone,
only waiting for the right moment to return.
I remember the shape of silence between the words we never said,
how it held more than the words could ever know.

I remember the way the world turned, slow and steady,
and I turned with it, though sometimes I wanted to stay still.
I remember the way I learned to love the unraveling,
how the threads of life were never meant to stay knit together forever.

I remember the way the sky at dusk always burned
like a secret fire, and I wondered if it was the same fire
that burned in the hearts of those who had gone before.
I remember the way the past was not a place, but a question,
a current that pulled at me, and I thought—
what if I had answered differently?

I remember the way the light at night was softer,
less harsh, less demanding, and I wondered if it was because
the world had grown tired too, or if it was because I had.
I remember the way the world was not just about endings,
but about the quiet spaces between them, where something new was always trying to grow.

I remember the way I carried the weight of the past
like a shadow, and then I let it go,
not because it was easy, but because I had to learn
to walk without it, to trust that some things were meant to be carried,
some things were meant to be set down.

I think about the weight of time,
how it is not just about the moments we remember,
but the moments we choose to forget,
the ones we carry like a second skin,
the ones we let go like leaves in autumn.

I think about the way the world is still turning,
how the old clock tower chimes again,
and I wonder if the light will catch the streetlamps like a thousand scattered stars
this time tomorrow, or if it will change.
I think about the way I have always been learning,
how the past is not a dead thing, but a living thread,
and I think about the future, and the way it is already here,
in the spaces between my thoughts, in the way the wind still hums through the trees.

I think about the way I have always been searching,
not for answers, but for the questions that will make the answers matter,
and I think about the way I will keep remembering,
not just the things that happened, but the way they happened,
the way they still happen inside me, in the quiet spaces between the words I say.

I think about the way I am still here,
still walking, still learning, still searching,
and I think about the way the world is still turning,
and I think about the way I will keep remembering,
and the way I will keep thinking.
