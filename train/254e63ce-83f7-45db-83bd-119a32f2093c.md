The rain fell in slow, deliberate sheets, each drop a tiny hymn against the concrete.
The old clock on the wall had stopped at 3:17, its hands frozen mid-swing, like a ghost watching the world move on.
She found a half-eaten apple core under the bench, its skin still glossy, though time had peeled back layers of her own life.

In the distance, a child laughed—too high, too bright, like a warning.
