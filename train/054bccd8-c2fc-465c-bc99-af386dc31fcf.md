The Great Wall of China spans approximately **13,170 miles**, stretching from the Liao River to the Yangtze River. It includes multiple sections, such as the **Jiankou**, **Mutianyu**, and **Badaling** sections, with varying historical and architectural features.

**Key boundaries:**
- **Northern boundary:** Liao River to the east and the Gobi Desert to the west.
- **Southern boundary:** Yangtze River and the Korean border.
- **Architectural segments:** Ming Dynasty walls (1368–1644) with defensive towers and watchtowers.

Structurally defined by stone, brick, and earthen embankments, it features **fortresses, watchtowers, and gatehouses** for strategic defense. The **Qin Dynasty** (221–206 BCE) laid the foundation, with later expansions by the Ming.

**Modern usage:** Tourist attraction, cultural heritage site, and symbol of China’s historical defenses.
