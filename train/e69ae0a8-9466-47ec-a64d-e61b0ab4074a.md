A worn-out leather wallet tumbled out of the pocket of a discarded jacket left by the curb. The scent of damp pavement and old cigarette butts lingered as a gust of wind carried it across the cracked asphalt. Inside, a crumpled billfold held a single, tarnished key—its chain broken but the metal still sharp enough to turn locks. Underneath, a folded receipt from a diner with faded ink: *Breakfast for 4 at 07:45 AM, 03.12.2019*.

She bent, fingers brushing the pavement, and plucked it free. The key was warm, like a second heartbeat. A neighbor’s house, a garden gate, a forgotten door. The receipt’s date pulsed against her palm. She pocketed the wallet, humming the diner’s old jingle, and turned—only to see the jacket’s owner, arms crossed, watching her from the sidewalk. The key’s glow in the dark.
