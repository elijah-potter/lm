- Research the breed of your pup. 

Regardless of whether you choose a breeder or get your dog through adoption from an animal shelter, each breed of dog has its own set of personality traits and needs. The breed you choose should match your time, financial situation, and lifestyle. In cases of adoption from animal shelters, dogs will be mixed breeds, but they have a dominant breed in their DNA. Genetic tests can help you discover more about that pup’s breed and its specific needs. 

- Your home should be dog proofed!

Just like you would if there were children in your home, you need to proof your home against accidents and curious explorations. In a new home, most dogs will find things to break or chew on because they haven’t learned your boundaries. Electrical cords, shoes, books, and children’s toys should be put away to avoid having broken or chewed belongings.  

- Be patient with your dog when they come home for the first time. 

Your dog isn’t used to your home yet, so give them space and patience. Show them their bed, toys, food, and water bowls, then give them a little space to become acquainted with their surroundings. 

- Schedule a veterinarian appointment for vaccinations and the good health of your canine. 

Up-to-date vaccines and routine vet appointments help keep your pup healthy throughout its life. Schedule their first veterinarian appointment within a week or two of bringing them home. You must get them on the right track to wellness as soon as possible, while also establishing trust between them and a regular veterinarian.
