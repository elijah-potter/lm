Did you know Mount Hood is actually a stratovolcano? It's potentially active, and it's part of the Cascade Volcanic Arc. A subduction zone on the Pacific Coast formed it, and it's found in the Pacific Northwest. From Portland, it's only 50 miles east-southeast, on the border that divides Clackamas and Hood River counties. Not only is it Oregon's highest mountain, but it's one of the highest mountains in all of the U.S. based on prominence! It's the only place in North America that has lift-served skiing all year long!

People have disagreed about the height of Mount Hood for decades. Even today, there are three different possibilities: 11,249 ft, which comes from a 1991 adjustment of a measurement taken in 1986 by the U.S. National Geodetic Survey; 11,240 ft, which is based on a scientific expedition in 1993; and 11,239 ft, which is an older measurement. There are twelve named glaciers and snowfields on Mount Hood! It's the highest point in all of Oregon, and the fourth highest in the Cascades. Mount Hood is the most likely to erupt out of all the volcanoes in Oregon, but that's unlikely. The odds of an eruption in the next 30 years are between 3 and 7%, which is where the "potentially active" characterization comes from, though informally we think it's dormant.
