The morning light spilled through the window like a slow, deliberate breath, softening the edges of the world. I sat on the edge of my bed, fingers tracing the dust motes suspended in the air, watching them dance before settling again. The first one had been there yesterday—small, round, and still—then another joined, then another, until there were six of them, all identical, drifting together like a quiet chorus.

I exhaled, watching the smoke curl into a shape that mirrored the pattern. Six. Not a coincidence. Not random. It was the same as the way the cracks in the ceiling fan blades always formed a perfect triangle when the wind hit just right. Or the way the way the light always hit the scar on my left palm at the same angle every morning, no matter how I turned my face.

I leaned forward, tracing the numbers on the wall calendar: 24, 25, 26. Three days. The same as the days I’d been waking up to this exact pattern—three days in a row, then another three, then another. The clockwork of the universe, I thought, or the ticking of something deeper. Either way, it wasn’t the first time I’d felt it.

The radio crackled to life. A news anchor’s voice, distant and dry. *"Another incident reported in Sector Nine..."* The words hung in the air, sharp and precise, like the edges of a blade. I didn’t need to know what Sector Nine was to feel the weight of the pattern now. It wasn’t just in the light, the dust, or the calendar. It was in the way the world itself seemed to be counting, waiting, watching.

I reached for my phone. The screen lit up, displaying the same time: 7:34 AM. The time I always woke up. The time I always checked the weather app. The time the city lights flickered in a way that matched the pattern of the dust motes.

A voice whispered from the dark: *"You’re not the first."*
