The fluorescent lights buzzed monotonously over the empty factory floor as **Mark** adjusted the pressure on the conveyor belt. His fingers trembled slightly—he’d been working this shift for over a decade. A standard Tuesday. Then, the laughter.

A high-pitched, breathless giggle cut through the hum of machinery. It came from the corner, near the old, dented pallet jack where **Javier**, the foreman, usually stood, arms crossed, watching. But Javier wasn’t there. Instead, a woman in a **red apron** with her hair tied in a messy bun was crouched on the floor, clutching a half-eaten donut to her chest. She was laughing so hard her eyes were watering, her shoulders shaking.

Mark froze. Javier’s laugh was always dry, always controlled. This was different—raw, unfiltered. She stood abruptly, knocking over a stack of cardboard boxes. A splash of orange icing spread across the floor. She grinned, still choking back giggles.

“Jesus, **Javier**,” she wheezed, pointing at him with a crumb-stained finger. “You’re late.”

Javier appeared from the break room, his face a mask of polite irritation. “What the hell are you doing in here?” he snapped.

She waved him off, still giggling. “I heard you were *really* late. Thought I’d check in.”

The foreman’s jaw clenched. “You’re supposed to be in the break room.”

“And you’re supposed to be on time,” she shot back, tossing him the donut. “Eat it.”

Javier glared but took it. As he bit into the sweet pastry, his face softened—just for a second. The laughter lingered in the air, a strange, unsettling warmth between them.

Mark exhaled slowly, rubbing his neck. The machine’s rhythm returned, but the scene in the corner had already changed something.
