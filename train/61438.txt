A study from the University College London that went on for decades has shown that isolation and loneliness can drastically shorten someone’s life. This is one of the reasons that seniors living alone can be dangerous.
According to the American Association of Retired Persons, almost 90 percent of those over the age of 65 would prefer to be in their home for as long as possible. Staying in the home that they have always been in makes sense, and it may even offer benefits to their emotional well-being.
However, research shows that the vast majority of elderly Americans who live at home and receive assisted living care are still very much alone. Perhaps even more shocking is the result from a study done by the Alzheimer’s Association, which shows that for those individuals with Alzheimer’s or dementia, almost one-in-four lives alone. As you might understand, this has a number of dangers associated with it.
When living alone is unsafe
We want our loved ones to be healthy and safe. This is why it is not only important that they feel comfortable with their social interactions, but also ensure that their environment is appropriate for their physical needs.
Even in their own homes, the combination of minor safety hazards and poor eyesight can put seniors at serious risk for falls, broken hips, or possibly even death. This is why it is important to take care of all the different components, which include senior nutrition, mental health, and physical symptoms. If you want to know whether living alone is unsafe, we have a few different warning signs:
- Inability to pay bills on time/keep track of information
- Home safety hazards such as loose carpeting or poor lighting
- Poor nutrition or malnutrition
- Unable to keep up with daily housekeeping or chores
- Forgetting appointments
- Social isolation
- Poor eyesight
- Medication management issues
