The rain fell in sheets, turning the cobblestone street into a slick mirror of neon signs and the hum of distant traffic. Elias adjusted the strap of his worn leather jacket, his fingers brushing against the cold metal of his pocket knife. "You ever think about how much we’re all just echoes, just echoes of something that came before?" he muttered, watching the puddles reflect the flickering glow of a streetlamp.

Across the alley, Lira stood motionless, her dark eyes reflecting the same tired light. "I know," she said, voice quiet. "I hear it in the way the wind sighs through the trees. I hear it in the way the city breathes, and I—" She swallowed hard, the sound like a dry throat. "I hear it in your voice."

Elias didn’t answer. Instead, he stepped closer, his shadow stretching long across the wet pavement. "You ever wonder how long we’ll keep playing this game?" he asked, his tone low and deliberate. "How long we’ll keep pretending it’s something more than the echoes of our own fears?"

Lira didn’t move. "Same thing I ask myself every night before I fall asleep," she replied, her voice barely above a whisper. "Same thing."
