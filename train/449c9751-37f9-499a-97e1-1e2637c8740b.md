A flickering candle on a cracked altar, its wax pooling in concentric rings.
A hymn sung in a language half-remembered, syllables stretched like frayed strings.
The water in the basin has turned to ice, but the words *"cleanse"* and *"purify"* still echo.
A single, unlit incense stick—its scent long since forgotten.
A whispered prayer to an empty god, whose name is now just a ghost in the air.
The offering plate is empty, though the bread and salt remain.
The ritual steps are marked with dust, the movements slow, mechanical, unlearned.
A child’s cry, muffled through the door, answers the question: *"Why?"*
No fire. No smoke. No divine breath.
Just the weight of silence, pressing down like a tombstone.
