The garden shed—once sturdy—now leans slightly like a tired old man. The fence, once neat, now stretches in jagged lines where the paint has faded into the earth. The neighbor’s cat, a sleek shadow, watches from the window like it’s been waiting forever for something no one will admit to needing.

Rain falls in quiet, unremarkable drops, some catching on the roof, others disappearing into the soil before they even have a chance to bother anyone. The radio, once a constant hum, now plays static so soft it might as well be a whisper from the past. The clock on the wall ticks, not loudly, but with a rhythm that feels like a promise left unfinished.
