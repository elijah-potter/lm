Amidst the skeletal remains of an abandoned library, the air hums with the faint scent of aged paper and damp stone. Bookshelves, once towering, now lean precariously, their spines cracked like dried earth. A single, flickering bulb casts long shadows across the floor, revealing only the scattered debris of a once-bustling space: a half-eaten sandwich crust, a crumpled newspaper, and the occasional torn page fluttering like a forgotten ghost.

The walls, once adorned with maps and scientific diagrams, are now marred by smudged ink and peeling paint. A cracked window, like a wound in the fabric of time, lets in a whisper of the outside world—a distant wind carrying the sound of distant voices, indistinct but persistent. The only sound is the slow, rhythmic creak of the floorboards beneath a lone figure standing in the center of the room, their back turned, fingers tracing the spine of a book that isn’t there anymore. The scene is one of quiet, lingering presence, where the weight of absence is sharper than any action ever could be.
