The rain tapped against the windows of the abandoned bookstore, each drop a whispered secret from the past. Inside, the air smelled of old paper and damp wool, the kind of scent that lingered even after the store had closed for decades. Elias sat at the corner table, fingers tracing the spine of a leather-bound journal he’d found behind the counter. The cover bore a faded inscription: *"For Whom the Bell Tolls… but Not Here."*

He had come here for the same reason every year—this was the only place that felt like home. The walls, once lined with shelves of hardcover editions, now groaned under the weight of cobwebs and the slow decay of neglect. The bell above the door chimed, but it was the echo that reached him first, hollow and distant, like a promise unfulfilled.

A figure stood at the threshold, silhouetted against the dim glow of the streetlamp outside. Their breath misted in the cold air, slow and measured. Elias didn’t look up. He knew who it was.

The woman stepped inside, her boots silent on the worn tiles. She carried a single item wrapped in brown paper—a small, silver locket, its clasp still untouched. "You always find them," she said, voice like gravel underfoot. "The ones worth keeping."

Elias exhaled, the sound a ghost of his own breath. "I find them," he corrected, "because they’re here to be found."

She tilted her head, studying him. "Then why do you keep coming back?"

He opened the journal, flipping through the pages until he found the one he sought—the one with the name scrawled in ink that wasn’t his. His fingers trembled as he traced the letters: *Lena.*

The woman’s gaze followed his movement. "She’s not here," she said softly. "But you keep digging."

Elias closed the journal, the weight of the past pressing down like a physical force. He stood, his chair scraping back against the floor. The woman’s hand brushed his arm, not gently, but with a familiarity that made his skin prickle. "You’ll find her," she murmured. "One day."

He didn’t answer. His footsteps echoed as he walked toward the door, the locket still in his pocket. The bell tolled again, but this time it was the weight of his own silence that carried him out into the rain.

The streetlights blurred into streaks of gold as he turned away, the first light of dawn already creeping over the city’s skyline. The bookstore’s ghostly glow faded behind him, swallowed by the mist.

He didn’t look back.
