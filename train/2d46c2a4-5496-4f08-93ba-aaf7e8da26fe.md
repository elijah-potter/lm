The weight of the promise pressed against my chest like a stone. I had said I would stay, that I’d hold the line even when the wind howled through the cracks of our borrowed home. But the nights grew longer, and the days, heavier. The storm outside mirrored the storm inside—unsettled, relentless, and impossible to weather alone.

*"I’ll make it,"* I’d told her, my voice hollow, my hands trembling. But promises are like rivers, they bend where they’re forced, but they don’t stay the same path forever. The river I was supposed to keep was a trickle, now a flood, and I was drowning in the guilt of not being able to stop it.

She left a note on the kitchen table: *"I’m sorry, but I can’t do this anymore."* The words hit like a punch—raw, unfiltered, and undeniable. I should have seen it coming. Should have said something. But the words had dried up, and the guilt had swallowed me whole. Now, the only thing left was silence, the kind that could never be filled again.

The phone rang. It was her. *"I’m not coming back."* Her voice was distant, like she was already gone. I swallowed the scream that rose in my throat. What was left of the promise was just a whisper: *"I tried."* But that wasn’t enough. Not for her. Not for me. Not anymore.
