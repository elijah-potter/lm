**The Last Light**

The clock tower’s shadow stretched long across the cobblestones as Elias adjusted the lantern’s wick, the oil burning too fast. The air smelled of damp stone and old wood, the kind of scent that clung to the edges of memory. Outside, the wind carried the distant sound of the river’s murmur—slow, relentless, as if whispering to the city’s bones.

He had come here for one last thing: the key to the old safe in the attic. His father’s handwriting, faded but still legible, had been scribbled on a yellowed note:

*"Find the hinge where the moon bends."*

Elias traced the words with his thumb, the ink still damp with age. The attic door creaked open beneath him, revealing a staircase spiraling upward like a forgotten spiral galaxy. The walls were lined with half-buried boxes, their contents lost to dust. At the top, a single lantern burned weakly, casting flickering light on a narrow shelf.

Then he saw it: a small, rusted hinge, embedded in the wall where the ceiling seemed to curve inward. His fingers trembled as he reached out. The moment his palm brushed it, the air *shifted*—not with sound, but with something colder, heavier. A whisper, not in words, but in the shape of a memory: a child’s laugh, the taste of sugar on his tongue, the way the light outside had been *blue* that night.

He pulled.

A door, no bigger than his palm, slid open with a groan. Inside, the air was thick with the scent of lavender and something metallic, like old blood. At the center of the room stood a chest—not of wood, but of bone. The lid was sealed with a single, perfect lock, and beneath it, the faintest glimmer of light pulsed like a heartbeat.

Elias exhaled, his breath fogging the lantern’s glass. He had found it.

And then—

The hinge in his hand *twitched*.
