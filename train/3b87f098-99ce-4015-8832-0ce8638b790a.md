The **Midnight Café**, a dimly lit, cluttered space in a forgotten district of **Blackthorn City**, serves as a neutral ground where strangers converge for no apparent cause. The walls are lined with mismatched chairs, their cushions stained with decades of spilled coffee. Neon signs flicker with static messages: *"Why?"*, *"Lost?"*, *"Why not?"*—words scribbled by anonymous hands over time.

People arrive without schedules, sitting at tables or leaning against the bar, whispering, staring, or simply watching others. Some bring drinks; others leave empty-handed, clutching the same half-empty cups they bought the week before. A few claim to be waiting for someone, though no one ever arrives. Others just *are* there—observers, participants, or neither.

The café’s most famous feature is its **clock**, which ticks backward when viewed from certain angles, though no one knows how. The air hums with unspoken conversations, the occasional burst of laughter, or the sound of someone humming a tune no one recognizes. The owner, a reclusive man in a perpetually rumpled coat, serves only what’s already on the counter—no orders taken, no prices listed.

No one knows why they come, but no one leaves. The café has been here longer than most people have been alive.
