The village elder had always warned of the coming storm before the flood, but we dismissed his warnings as superstition. The river, usually calm, now swelled with an unnatural force, its waters churning violently against the banks. I remember the children running for cover, their screams muffled by the wind.

The old mill stood on the edge, its wooden wheels still turning, though the gears groaned with the strain. I should have seen the cracks in the foundation before the water rose. Instead, I stood on the porch, clutching my hat, watching the sky darken.

The house behind me was our home, our sanctuary. My wife called my name, but I was too late—her voice was lost in the roar of the flood. The door burst inward, the water rushing in like a living thing. I tried to run, but the current pulled me under, dragging me toward the mill.

When I surfaced, the mill was gone. Only the skeletal remains of the wooden structure remained, half-buried in the silt. I found my wife’s body later, wrapped in the torn remnants of her dress. The flood had taken everything, but I would tell the story anyway.
