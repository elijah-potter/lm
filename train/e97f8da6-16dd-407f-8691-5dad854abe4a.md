A cluttered workspace hums with the faint sound of a laptop’s keyboard. The screen displays the first draft of *The Hollow Crown*, a story about a scholar who discovers an ancient text predicting a kingdom’s fall. Beside it, a half-finished sketch of the protagonist’s face stares blankly at the wall. A half-empty coffee cup glints with condensation.

The protagonist, **Elias Veyne**, hunts through stacks of notes, torn papers, and scribbled ideas. His fingers hover over the cursor, pausing over *"the scholar’s hollow eyes"*—a phrase he’s been struggling with. A voice in his head whispers: *Too dark. Too… final.*

He replaces it with *"the scholar’s weary gaze"* and adjusts the pacing of the scene where the prophecy is revealed. The draft’s tension shifts, the story’s emotional core deepening. A satisfied sigh escapes as he saves the file under a new name: *Veyne’s Second Draft—Emotional Core Revisited*.

The door creaks open. Elias doesn’t turn. The weight of the revision lingers—not just in the lines, but in the quiet between them.
