Plop. My bobber sinks and settles. The sinker was steadying it in the waves. The leech is already wriggling to the bottom. I keep my line tight. And I wait. I rest back on the bench, the dock creaking under me. Its wood is rough under my feet. I play with the large gap between two weathered boards with my toe. The lake drifts by from the river out to the bank. It roils where the lake bed rises. A bump. The bobber jumps. Another. A hungry minnow or a curious beast. I prepared my hand for the reel. Bump. Snag! I whip my pole back. The hook sets The bass jumps. It dodges. But I reel it in; its weight is bending the pole. Its green scales gleam in the sun. Its mouth gapes. I quickly undo the hook from its lip and kneel on the dock to rest it in the water. It shimmies away with a thrust of its tail. I set the bait again. Plop. And I wait.
