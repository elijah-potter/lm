A dimly lit alleyway at dusk, the air thick with the scent of damp pavement and distant exhaust fumes. A figure—pale, clad in a long coat—stands at the edge of a crumbling bench, knees slightly bent, hands clasped loosely. Their posture is one of quiet endurance, neither rigid nor relaxed. The streetlamps flicker, casting long shadows that stretch across the cracked asphalt.

A car door slams in the distance, the sound swallowed by the hum of traffic. Then, nothing. The figure exhales slowly, their breath misting faintly in the cold. A cigarette burns between their fingers, the embers glowing like a forgotten lantern.

They wait. For a name on a ticket, a handshake, the weight of something no one can see. The city breathes around them—indifferent, relentless. Time stretches, heavy and slow, like the first sip of coffee on a cold morning.
