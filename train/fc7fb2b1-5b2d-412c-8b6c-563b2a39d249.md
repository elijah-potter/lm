A vast, crumbling metropolis of skeletal towers hovers over a frozen plain, their windows like hollow eyes staring down at the frozen ground. The air is thick with the scent of damp earth and rusted metal, a faint, metallic tang lingering in the cold. The city’s streets, once bustling, are now deserted, their surfaces slick with ice and grime, reflecting a pale, unnatural light from the dim, flickering glow of emergency streetlights. The buildings seem to lean inward, as if caught in an invisible grip, their facades peeling back like old, dried skin. The silence is deafening—only the distant, rhythmic thump of something unseen beneath the frozen crust echoes through the hollow spaces. Above, the sky is a bruised canvas of gray and violet, dotted with too many clouds, as if the heavens themselves are holding their breath, waiting for something to break the stillness. The weight of the city feels suffocating, pressing down on everything, making the world feel suspended in time.
