The absence lingers like a half-remembered melody—sharp, incomplete, yet impossible to erase. Every morning, the silence between my thoughts feels thicker than the air outside. The spaces between conversations grow too vast; silence is no longer a pause but a wound. I press my fingers to the empty chair beside me, where a voice once filled the room, and the weight of it presses back, pressing into my ribs like a ghost. Objects hold memories of another self, their surfaces glossed with dust that isn’t from neglect but from the absence of touch. The coffee I pour still tastes like the one my hands didn’t lift. Time itself seems to slow, stretched taut like a stretched-out string, each second a moment of waiting, of counting. The days blur into a single, endless ache. The body remembers the weight of another’s presence, and it aches to hold it again. The mind replays the last time we were together, not as a memory, but as a ghostly echo in the dark. The absence is never far away—it’s the space between the last word and the first, the pause before the next breath, the silence that follows a name I’ve said too many times in my head. It’s the way the world moves without me, as if I’ve been erased from the map of existence. The ghost of their absence is not gone; it’s here, woven into the fabric of the room, the air, the way the light catches the edge of a door I’ve walked through alone. The ache is not just of loss; it’s the slow realization that absence can be more present than presence itself.
