An International Space Station conducting a low Earth orbit is making its way over Lubbock Friday night and is expected to be visible for about six minutes at 5:54 a.m. Saturday, according to NASA's space station sightings website. It'll also be visible at 10:34 p.m. Saturday.
Mission Control at NASA’s Johnson Space Center in Houston determines sighting opportunities for more than 6,700 locations worldwide. The space station is expected to be visible at various times in Lubbock through Aug. 7.
"The space station is visible because it is reflecting light from the sun. This is the same reason that the moon appears to shine. Even when the moon hasn’t risen, you’ll still be able to see the space station," according to the Spot the Station website. "The space station looks like a fast-moving plane in the sky, but it is dozens of times higher than any airplane and traveling thousands of miles an hour faster. It is bright enough that it can even be seen from the middle of a city.”
