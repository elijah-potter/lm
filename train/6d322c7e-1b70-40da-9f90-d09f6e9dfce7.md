A subtle flicker of the screen in a corner corner of a room where the light was dim, barely visible under the ambient glow of a flickering lamp. It was the kind of change that slips through the cracks—like the hum of a machine that suddenly quietens, or the way a door slightly ajar, just enough to let in a draft no one noticed.

One morning, the coffee machine’s first drip was a fraction too slow. The machine had always been precise, but now it lingered, as if hesitating. The owner never checked, assuming it was just a quirk. Days passed, and the delay became routine, part of the machine’s character.

The printer’s paper tray now held one extra sheet, always. The owner never noticed, until they realized the last page of a document was always missing. The missing line was the one that mattered—the one that tied everything together, the one that was critical but never the focus.

In a meeting, a participant’s voice was slightly lower than usual, but no one noticed until the last question was asked. The answer was never delivered, because the person who would have answered was now elsewhere, having left early. The decision was delayed by a few minutes, but by then, the momentum was gone.

A small change—like a single thread of fabric left undone, or a single syllable left unspoken. It’s the kind of thing that fades into the background, until it’s too late to notice. And when you finally do, it’s already too late to fix.
