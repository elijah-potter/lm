The rain pattered against the cracked windshield of the abandoned café, turning the neon sign flickering above the door into a ghostly, bleeding light. Inside, the last customer—Mira—sipped her black coffee, the steam curling upward like smoke from an old fire. The café had been empty for three years, but today, for some reason, she felt the weight of its silence.

A wooden chair creaked behind her. She turned slowly, the shadows making her face indistinct. It was *him*—the man who had once been her best friend, the one who left without a word after the argument. His hands were stained with the same dark coffee as hers, but his eyes were hollow, as if he’d already begun the long walk that would take him away from her forever.

"You still drink here," he said, voice rough with disuse.

She didn’t answer. The coffee had gone cold. He reached for her cup, his fingers brushing hers, and she flinched.

"You should’ve stayed," he muttered, pulling his coat tighter around him. The café’s only window was open, letting in the drizzle like a confession. He stepped out, the door slamming shut behind him. The wind took his words and the café’s last breath with it.

Mira exhaled slowly, watching the rain blur the streetlights into streaks of silver. She picked up her empty cup, the porcelain cold and fragile, and walked toward the door. Outside, the night swallowed her whole.
