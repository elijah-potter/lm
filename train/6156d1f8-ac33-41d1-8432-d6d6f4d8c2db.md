The river hummed its secrets through the boulders, its surface rippling like whispered confessions. The morning mist clung to the willows, dissolving into golden light that filtered through the canopy like a slow, deliberate breath. A crow landed on the edge of the dock, its gaze fixed on something unseen—perhaps the future, or the endless loop of time itself. The air smelled of damp earth and wild thyme, the scent of something that had been forgotten and yet was never truly gone.

She sat cross-legged on the stone, her fingers tracing the grain of the old wood, the weight of her past pressing down like the weight of the sky. But the sky—it stretched endlessly, vast and quiet, and for the first time in years, she let it hold her. The waves at the shore lapped gently, their rhythm a familiar lullaby, the rhythm of a life that had once been steady but now felt like a dream half-remembered.

And then—silence. Just the sound of her own breath, slow and steady, the way it had been meant to be. The world tilted back into focus, the edges of her exhaustion melting away like mist on a summer’s day. The calm wasn’t the absence of everything, but the quiet space where nothing had to be said. The river flowed on, the wind carried no storm, and for the first time in a long while, she didn’t have to fight to find it. It had always been here.
