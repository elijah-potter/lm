The old clock tower’s chime marked the hour as she paused, fingers tracing the worn edge of her half-empty cup. The steam curled upward, ghostly and slow—like the fragments of a thought dissolving into the air. Outside, the city’s hum was a chorus of forgotten plans, each one a half-remembered note in the symphony of her own life.

She had a memory: the way his voice had sounded when he said, *"You’ll always have a place here,"* the way his hands had brushed against hers in the dim light of a café, the scent of coffee and something darker, like old books and the faint metallic tang of blood. It was a promise, half-buried in the cracks of time, and now it was crumbling.

But there was a plan, too. One she had sketched in the margins of her mind, a rough draft of what she would do if she left. She would sell the apartment above the bookshop, the one that smelled of ink and damp pages, the one that had watched over her for years. She would buy a train ticket to the coast, where the waves would whisper secrets older than her own. She would learn to sail, to let the wind carry her away from the weight of what she couldn’t change.

The clock tower’s chime split the silence, sharp and final. She exhaled, the breath carrying the scent of the coffee she hadn’t drunk. The plan was still there, but memory was bleeding into it, softening edges, adding layers of doubt. Maybe she wouldn’t leave. Maybe she would stay, because some doors, once opened, refused to close.
