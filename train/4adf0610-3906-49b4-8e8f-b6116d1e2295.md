## A Counseling Session

**Setting:** A comfortably furnished therapy office. Soft lighting, a view of a small garden.

**Characters:**

* **Dr. Eleanor Vance:** A seasoned therapist, known for her calm demeanor and insightful approach.
* **Liam Davies:** A young professional in his late twenties, experiencing significant stress related to his career.

---

**Liam:** (Sitting forward slightly, fidgeting with his hands) It just feels… overwhelming, Dr. Vance. Like I’m constantly playing catch-up. The deadlines, the expectations, the constant feeling that I’m not doing enough.

**Dr. Vance:** (Nodding gently, maintaining eye contact) I understand. It sounds incredibly demanding. You’re describing a persistent sense of pressure. Can you tell me a little more about what that "not doing enough" feeling looks like for you, specifically?

**Liam:** Well, at work, I’m always thinking about the next task, the next presentation. Even when I’m *doing* something, I’m worrying about what I should be doing instead. And then I get home, and I feel guilty for not being more present with my partner. It's a cycle.

**Dr. Vance:** (Leaning slightly forward) So, you're experiencing a conflict between your professional responsibilities and your personal life, and that conflict is fueling feelings of guilt and inadequacy. That’s a difficult position to be in. It takes a great deal of energy to manage that kind of internal conflict.

**Liam:** It does. I’m exhausted. I used to enjoy my job, but now… it’s just a source of anxiety.

**Dr. Vance:** It’s natural to feel that way when something that once brought you joy becomes associated with stress and worry. It suggests that something has shifted. Can you recall when you first started feeling this way? Was there a specific event or a gradual accumulation of pressure?

**Liam:** (Pauses, considering) I think it started about six months ago. They promoted me to lead a new project, and suddenly, I felt responsible for so much more. The stakes felt higher.

**Dr. Vance:** Taking on a leadership role and increased responsibility can certainly trigger a significant shift in perspective and workload. It sounds like you’re experiencing a significant increase in pressure. It's important to acknowledge that this promotion, while a positive achievement, has also brought considerable challenges. How are you managing the practical aspects of the project itself? Are you delegating tasks, or are you attempting to handle everything personally?

**Liam:** I try to delegate, but I often feel like I have to do things myself to ensure they’re done correctly. I worry that if I don’t, things will fall apart.

**Dr. Vance:** That’s a common feeling when taking on a new level of responsibility. It’s understandable to want to maintain a high standard. However, that can lead to burnout if it’s unsustainable. It sounds like you're holding yourself to an exceptionally high standard. Let’s explore that a little further. Where do you think that standard originates?

**Liam:** (Sighs) I don’t know. Probably from my dad, I guess. He always expected perfection.

**Dr. Vance:** It’s not uncommon for parental expectations to shape our own self-imposed standards. Recognizing that connection is a valuable step. It allows us to examine whether those standards are truly serving *you* now, or if they are remnants of a past expectation. We can explore how those early influences might be contributing to the pressure you’re feeling today. For now, let’s focus on identifying some small, manageable steps you can take this week to alleviate some of that pressure. Perhaps we can start by identifying one task you can consciously delegate, and we can discuss strategies for communicating that delegation effectively. How does that sound?

**Liam:** (Looks slightly more relaxed) That sounds… helpful. I think I could do that.
