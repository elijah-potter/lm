A letter slipped into the mailbox under the door of an empty apartment, its envelope sealed with a single tear.

The recipient, a scholar who had spent years researching forgotten texts, found it on the third morning after their partner’s sudden departure. Inside was a handwritten note: *"You’ll know when the time is right."* The recipient did not.

Three years passed. The apartment was sold, the scholar moved to a coastal town, and the letter was left behind in a box of unused items. No one found it until the new tenant, a young librarian, discovered it beneath a stack of unopened mail during a move.

The note inside read: *"The book you’ve been searching for has arrived. It’s in the basement. Meet me at midnight."*

Midnight came. The librarian found the basement door unlocked, the book—a bound leather-bound volume titled *"The Lost Codex of Solace"*—hidden under a loose floorboard. Inside were pages that matched the scholar’s research, but in a way they had never imagined: the text was a silent hymn of gratitude, woven with fragments of their late partner’s unfinished work.

The librarian stood there, staring at the book, then turned to leave. The door clicked shut behind them, and the basement door sealed itself behind them, as if the space itself had absorbed the promise made long ago. No one ever spoke of it again.
