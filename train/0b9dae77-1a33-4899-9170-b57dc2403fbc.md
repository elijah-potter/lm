A monthly inventory check became a ritual, meticulously documented in a ledger. Each item—tools, supplies, and the occasional "mysterious" spare part—was catalogued with precision, accompanied by annotations like "replaced after 3 months" or "recently found in storage." Over time, the process evolved into a routine, performed at the same hour every Tuesday, often accompanied by a steaming cup of tea. The act of marking each item’s status—"functional," "needs repair," or "abandoned"—began to feel almost sacred, a way to honor the passage of time and the wear and tear of the work. The ritual was less about the task itself and more about the act of repetition: the way the pen moved across the paper, the way the light filtered through the window at that exact moment, the way the past weeks' decisions were physically embodied in the ledger’s pages.
