What is the absolute limit of human lifespan?
The absolute limit of human lifespan is difficult to determine definitively due to the large number of variables at play. Primarily, the maximum lifespan of humans is considered to be limited by the telomere shortening process.
Telomeres are caps of repetitive DNA sequences that exist at the end of all chromosomes. With each cell division, telomeres become shorter and eventually, they become so short that a cell can no longer divide.
Once cells can no longer divide, tissues stop renewing and tissues and organs eventually age and die. As telomeres become shorter, the maximum lifespan of humans may decrease.
Additionally, lifestyle choices, such as smoking and sustained stress, have been associated with accelerated telomere shortening and a decrease in maximum lifespan. Social and economic factors also play a major role in how long a person may live.

At this time, the absolute limit of human lifespan is unknown as each individual’s particular combination of variables makes it impossible to definitively determine a maximum lifespan for humanity.
