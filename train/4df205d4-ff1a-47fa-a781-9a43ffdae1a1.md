**Ending 1:**
The old lighthouse keeper found the rusted key in the abandoned attic, its surface etched with strange symbols. He inserted it into the rusted lock of the storm door, and the hinges groaned as it turned. Inside, the lantern room glowed with an eerie, unnatural light. He stepped forward, only to find the lighthouse had transformed into a portal. The waves crashed differently, the wind carried whispers, and when he looked down, he saw his own reflection—but not his own face. Instead, it was a stranger with eyes like polished obsidian. The stranger said, *"You have always known."* The keeper hesitated, then pressed his palm against the glass. The light flared, and he vanished into the storm, only to reappear in his childhood home, now standing beside his mother, who smiled sadly as she said, *"I told you this would happen."*

**Ending 2:**
The detective found the second key in the pocket of a victim who’d died of exposure in a frozen alley. He traced the symbols on it—identical to the first—then realized the city’s water main had been sabotaged months ago. The pipes had been replaced with hollow copper, draining the city’s water supply. The storm outside grew heavier, and the lights flickered as pressure surged through the newly exposed conduits. The detective grabbed the key and forced the door of the city’s oldest well. Inside, the water was black, thick with rust and something darker. He reached down and pulled the key from its rusted sheath, turning it in the well’s ancient mechanism. The water surged upward, revealing a cavernous chamber below. A voice, distorted and ancient, whispered: *"You’ve been chosen."* The detective didn’t hesitate. He dove in, his fingers brushing the edge of a door made of living stone. As he stepped through, the water below him turned clear, and the city’s water system hummed to life again—though something in the well’s depths still watched from the shadows. The detective emerged weeks later in a field near the old well, his pockets empty but his mind unbroken. The key was gone.
