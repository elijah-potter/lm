**The Last Light**

The city exhaled smoke before dawn, a slow coil of black smoke curling from chimneys like the breath of a sleeping giant. Outside, the streets were slick with rain that had fallen silently through a sky so pale it could have been memory itself. The neon signs flickered—*closed*—their usual glare dimmed to ghostly whispers, as if the world had forgotten to switch them on.

A man stood on the rooftop of the old bookstore, his fingers tracing the edge of a worn map. The city sprawled beneath him, a patchwork of shadows and forgotten dreams. Below, the river hummed, carrying with it the weight of a thousand forgotten stories. The air smelled of damp stone and old paper, the scent of a place that had been waiting too long.

Then—silence.

A single bird, small and trembling, took flight from the gutter, its wings beating against the cold. It wasn’t afraid. It knew what came next.

The sky.

It was nothing like the sky at noon. No gold, no blue, no endless vastness. Instead, it was a canvas of bruised twilight, streaked with the faintest blush of dawn breaking through the edges like a secret. The horizon bled into the clouds, which were thick with the weight of forgotten storms, their edges soft as mist. Above the rooftops, a few stars still lingered—pale and distant, like the eyes of the dead watching the world wake.

The man watched them until his vision blurred, then turned back to the city. The last light was already fading, swallowed by the dark before it could begin. The sky was not the sky at all. It was the end of everything.

And the world kept turning.
