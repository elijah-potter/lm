The last light in the old factory flickered like a dying ember, casting long shadows over the rusted machinery. Elias stood at the edge of the cracked concrete floor, his breath fogging in the cold air. The scent of ozone and old paint clung to him, a familiar mix that had become his own.

A rusted door creaked open behind him. He turned just as the figure stepped into the dim light—someone he hadn’t seen in years. Their coat was threadbare, their face half-hidden beneath a scarf, but the eyes burned with the same restless fire that had always burned in him. *"You came back,"* the stranger said, voice rough with disuse.

Elias didn’t answer. Instead, he stepped forward, fingers brushing the old key in his pocket. The door had been locked since they were kids, but the lock had always been a joke. *"We’re done here,"* he said, the words raw. The stranger didn’t move. The silence stretched, thick with unspoken things.

Then the stranger turned, their back to him, and walked toward the factory’s empty windows. The door swung shut behind them with a finality that settled over the space like a curtain. Elias exhaled slowly, watching the figure disappear into the night. He turned away, too, and walked out into the cold, the weight of what had passed between them finally carrying him forward.

The city swallowed them both.
