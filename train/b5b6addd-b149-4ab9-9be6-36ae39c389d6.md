The weight of silence settles like a shroud over the empty courtyard, where the last light of dusk bleeds through the cracked windows, painting the stone floor in fractured gold. A single moth, its wings a blur of motion, spirals toward the flickering lanterns, its ghostly form dissolving against the air. The scent of damp earth and rustling leaves lingers, a whisper of the forest just beyond the garden walls, where the trees stand sentinel, their branches heavy with unseen secrets.

The air hums with the faint, rhythmic pulse of a distant clock, its chime half-forgotten, half-lost in the creaking of the old oak door. It swings open by itself, as if drawn by some unseen force, revealing a hallway where the walls breathe—paint peeling in delicate layers, revealing the past in its cracks. Dust motes dance in the lamplight, their fleeting shapes shifting like smoke in the wind.

A key turns in the lock with a sound like a sigh, the lock groaning in protest before yielding. The door creaks shut behind whoever came, leaving behind only the echo of their footsteps, now swallowed by the quiet. The lanterns flicker, their light dimming as if drained by unseen hands, their glow a dying ember in the dark.

Outside, the world shifts. The sky is a vast, unspoiled canvas of indigo, its edges blurred by the first whispers of the moon. It stretches endlessly, a silent witness to forgotten moments, its surface rippling with distant clouds—some low and ominous, others soaring high, as if caught in the breath of a forgotten storm. Stars, sparse and distant, begin to prick the dark, their cold light cutting through the veil of twilight like the edges of a wound. The horizon glows faintly, a golden thread where day and night whisper secrets to one another.
