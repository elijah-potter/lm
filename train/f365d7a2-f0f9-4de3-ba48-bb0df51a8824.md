A small, unassuming family dinner in a rustic cabin at midnight, where the candles flicker like dying stars. The children, dressed in mismatched pajamas, whisper about "ghosts in the walls" while the adults toast to forgotten traditions—no clocks, no schedules, just the echo of laughter and the hum of an old radio playing a song from the '70s. The table is covered in mismatched plates and a single, half-empty bottle of wine labeled *"For the Ghosts."* A framed photo of a missing neighbor from decades past sits between them, its edges worn smooth. The fire crackles as a ghost story about a child who vanished during a storm is told—no one knows if it’s true, but the tension lingers like smoke. A single, unexplained shadow moves at the edge of the room.
