DAMMED rivers can have far-reaching effects on ecosystems way out to sea, say researchers in Germany and Romania. In the past 25 years, a dam on the River Danube has caused shifts in the types of algae that live in the Black Seafavouring species which form toxic blooms that can kill other marine life.
The Iron Gates dam was completed in 1972 between what is now Serbia and Romania. It protects 26 000 square kilometres of land downstream from flooding. But suspended sediment which would have been swept downstream is now deposited in the huge lake behind the dam.
Tiny particles of sand washed down from rivers are the chief source of dissolved silicates in seawater. Silicates are essential nutrients for diatomssingle-celled algae that bloom in enormous numbers at the sea surface in spring, providing a source of food that fuels the marine ecosystem.
