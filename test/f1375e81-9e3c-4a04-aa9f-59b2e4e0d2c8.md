Lena had always felt like she didn’t quite fit in at her new school, the one with its strict rules and rigid traditions. The first day, she arrived late, her backpack slung over one shoulder, her hair tangled from the bus ride. The hallway smelled like old books and disinfectant, and the sound of raised voices from the cafeteria made her stomach twist.

She found a quiet corner near the stairwell, where the fluorescent lights buzzed overhead, casting long shadows. A group of kids near the lockers were laughing about something—some joke she couldn’t hear. She watched them, envying their ease, their ability to disappear into the noise. Then, one of them, a boy with a tattoo of a skull on his wrist, turned and smirked. "You look lost," he said, tilting his head.

Lena hesitated. "Yeah," she admitted.

"Name’s Jace," he said, extending a hand. "What’s yours?"

She shook it, her fingers trembling slightly. "Lena."

He grinned. "You’re new, huh?"

She nodded.

"Cool. You gonna stay in the corner all day?"

She laughed nervously. "Maybe."

Jace laughed too, and for the first time that day, Lena didn’t feel so alone. They talked about books, music, and the weirdness of growing up. He showed her a mixtape he’d made, and she listened to songs she’d never heard before.

By the end of the week, Lena had become one of the group, though she still kept her distance sometimes. She learned that Jace had a little sister who was sick, and the others were there for her in ways they couldn’t always show. They helped her with her first science project, even though she wasn’t the best at it. They made her laugh when she was quiet, and they listened when she wasn’t.

One afternoon, as they sat on the bleachers after soccer practice, Jace turned to her. "You ever think about what happens next?"

Lena stared at the field, where the sun glinted off the soccer balls. "What do you mean?"

He shrugged. "School, the world, everything. What’s your plan?"

She thought about it. "I don’t know. Maybe I’ll just keep going."

Jace smiled. "That’s the spirit. You got that?"

She nodded, and for a moment, she felt lighter. Maybe she didn’t have to have all the answers right away. Maybe she could figure things out as she went.

That night, after everyone else was asleep, Lena sat up in her bed, the glow from her phone casting shadows on the walls. She thought about Jace and the others, and the strange, beautiful feeling of belonging she’d never known before. Maybe high school wasn’t just about fitting in—maybe it was about finding who you were, even if it took a little time.
