The library was quiet except for the faint hum of fluorescent lights and the occasional rustle of pages from the old books. Twelve-year-old Jake sat cross-legged on the floor, his fingers tracing the spine of the worn-out copy of *The Mysterious Case of the Vanishing Candles*. The last chapter had left him unsettled—something about a shadowy figure in red who seemed to know things about him.

His stomach twisted. He should have gone home earlier. His mom had called him a "chicken" for staying up late, but Jake had needed this. The book was the only thing keeping him from thinking about the weird noises in his house—like something scratching at the walls at night.

He flopped onto his back, staring at the ceiling. Outside, the streetlights flickered, casting long shadows across the windows. His phone buzzed. A text from his best friend, Mateo: *"Jake, you’re not answering. What’s wrong?"*

Jake hesitated. He didn’t want to tell Mateo about the book—or the way his pulse quickened every time he imagined the red-clad figure from the story. But then he saw his mom’s name on the screen. She’d been worried. She always was when he ignored her.

He typed back, *"I’m fine. Just… busy."*

---

The next morning, Jake woke up early, his mind still stuck on the story. He grabbed his backpack and ran to the bus stop, his shoes slapping against the pavement. The bus pulled up just as he reached the curb, and he boarded, gripping the handle as the door closed behind him.

Inside, the bus was crowded, but Jake found a seat near the back. He opened the book again, flipping to the last page. The red figure—*whoever he was*—had left one final clue: *"Look where the light never reaches."*

Jake’s eyes narrowed. The library’s reading nooks were dark, but the corners of the floor near the bookshelves were the darkest. He stood up, his heart pounding. He didn’t have time to waste.

---

By the time Jake arrived at the library, the afternoon was already heating up. He slipped into the back row, where the fluorescent lights buzzed overhead. He closed his eyes and listened. Nothing. Just the hum of the machines.

Then he saw it—a small, flickering light in the corner near the book stacks. He followed the beam of light until it disappeared behind a shelf. His breath caught. It was a hidden compartment, barely visible beneath the spines of the oldest books.

Inside, nestled between the pages of a dusty history book, was a yellowed map. The ink was faded, but one word stood out: *Candle.*

Jake’s fingers trembled as he unfolded it. The map led to an old park near the river, where the trees stood tall and the ground was damp from the morning rain. His stomach twisted. This wasn’t just a story anymore. It was real.

He turned to the back of the book—where the red figure had marked it. A small, tarnished key lay beneath the last page. His pulse roared in his ears. What if this wasn’t just a game? What if it was a warning?

---

The park was abandoned, the grass long and wild, the air thick with the scent of pine and something else—something metallic. Jake’s hands shook as he pulled out the key. The park was supposed to be a place for old stories, but this felt different. Too real.

He pressed the key into the lock of an old, rusted gate at the edge of the woods. It clicked open with a groan. Beyond it, the river flowed silently, its surface shimmering under the dappled sunlight. Jake hesitated, then stepped forward.

The trees seemed to lean in closer, their branches twisting like skeletal fingers. He could hear it now—something moving in the shadows. Not the wind. Not the leaves.

His phone buzzed again. Mateo’s name flashed on the screen. Jake ignored it, his focus locked on the gate. The map had led him here. But why?

The key turned in the lock, and the gate creaked open. Beyond it, the riverbank was littered with strange symbols carved into the stone. And then he saw them—the candles, half-buried in the dirt, their wicks still glowing faintly.

Jake’s breath came in ragged gasps. This wasn’t just a book. It was a challenge. And he was the only one who knew how to solve it.

He stepped inside, his shoes sinking into the damp earth. The candles flickered, casting long shadows that seemed to move on their own. The air smelled of fire and something older, something like memories.

And then he heard it—a whisper, just above a whisper.

*"You came."*

Jake’s blood turned to ice. The red figure stood in the center of the clearing, its cloak whispering in the wind. It held a candle in one hand, its flame dancing like a living thing.

*"I knew you would,"* the figure said, its voice like dry leaves skittering across stone. *"The book told you. The key opened the gate. But you didn’t know why."*

Jake’s hands trembled. "What do you want?"

The figure smiled, its teeth too sharp, too many. *"You’re the last keeper. The one who remembers the truth. The candles need you to light them again."*

Jake swallowed hard. "What’s the truth?"

*"The candles were never just for light. They were for time. They kept the shadows from stealing what was yours."* The figure’s eyes gleamed, dark and hungry. *"But the world forgot. The book was buried. And now, it’s time to wake up."*

Jake’s mind raced. The library, the map, the key—it all felt like a trap. But he wasn’t going anywhere. Not until he figured this out.

*"What do I have to do?"*

The figure tilted its head, as if considering him. *"Light the candles. And when you do, the shadows will remember what they’ve forgotten."*

Jake clenched his fists. He wasn’t afraid. He just needed answers. He needed to know why this mattered.

*"Why me?"*

The figure’s smile widened. *"Because you’re the only one who still believes in stories. And stories are the only thing that can stop the darkness."*

Jake took a deep breath. He’d been afraid all along. But he was ready now.

He turned to the candles, their wicks still glowing weakly. He knelt down and picked up the first one, its flame flickering like a dying ember. The air around it shimmered, and for a second, he saw something move in the shadows.

Then the candles flared to life, their light painting the trees in eerie hues. The figure stepped back, watching him with something like pride.

*"Now,"* it said, *"start telling them."*

Jake’s pulse roared in his ears. He’d never been one for magic. But he’d never been afraid of stories either.

So he began to sing.
