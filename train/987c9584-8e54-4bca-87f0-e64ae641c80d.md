The last time I saw the ruins of Varethis, the crumbling arches of the old aqueducts cast long shadows on the cracked cobblestones. Dust settled like snow over the half-buried statues of gods and kings, their faces worn smooth by time. The air smelled of salt and iron, the wind whispering through the gaps in the walls like a ghost’s breath. The harbor below was a graveyard of sunken ships, their hulls still heaving in the tide, rusted skeletal fingers clawing at the water. The docks were empty—no last ships, no final cries. Only the echo of laughter from a thousand years ago, now just a distant memory.

A lone lantern flickered in the center of the plaza, its light dying fast. The lantern’s keeper had vanished too, leaving behind only a half-empty flask on the stone. The lantern’s last flame was a final, dying hope, a beacon for those who came to say goodbye. The city was silent, the people gone. Even the crickets had stopped singing. The last time I saw Varethis, the world felt heavier, as if it were about to stop moving forever.
