In the dim glow of a flickering holographic archive, a gathering of disparate minds coalesced around a central orb pulsating with data. A voice, synthetic yet resonant, echoed through the airwaves: *"The future is not a singular thread but a fractal of possibilities—some tangled, others woven with threads of uncertainty."*

A scientist traced their fingers over an ancient schematic, the ink smudged with time. *"They call it the ‘Great Loop,’"* murmured the engineer beside them, adjusting a failing neural interface. *"A feedback loop where decisions of today ripple into tomorrow, and tomorrow’s choices reshape the present."*

A child, no older than eight, pressed their palm against the holographic display. *"What if we… don’t?"* Their voice trembled with wonder and dread. The room stilled. A flicker of static. Then—silence.

The orb dissolved into static. The future, as they understood it, had just become a question.
