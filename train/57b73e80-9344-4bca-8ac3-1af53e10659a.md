A red ceramic teacup passed between three figures in a dimly lit, candle-lit café. The older woman at the table, her fingers trembling slightly, handed it to the younger man sitting beside her. He placed it carefully on the worn wooden table, his breath steady. The child, barely five, reached across the table, grabbed the teacup with both hands, and raised it to his lips, the first sip of tea in hours. The cup’s warmth seeped through his chilled fingers. The woman smiled, watching as he swallowed, then set it down carefully on the table. The teacup’s porcelain rim caught the flickering light as it shifted hands again, this time to the barista behind the counter. The steam from the new brew curled into the air.
