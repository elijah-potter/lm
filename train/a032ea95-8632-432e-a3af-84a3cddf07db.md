A new neighborhood is emerging near the old industrial zone by the river. Dappled sunlight filters through the skeletal remains of abandoned warehouses, now repurposed into lofts and coworking spaces. A rusted bridge spans the river, its once-traffic-clogged path now filled with cyclists and pedestrians. The street signs are half-erased but legible: *"Main St" replaced by *"Routine Ave"* on a new mural. Small businesses—an organic café, a vinyl record shop, and a bike co-op—cluster around a central plaza where local artists set up daily open-air performances. The air hums with the low hum of drones delivering groceries, and the occasional sound of a synthwave playlist drifting from a streetlight. A mural depicts a cyclist on a path marked with arrows spelling out a daily routine: *"Wake, Bike, Dine, Repeat."* The riverbank is lined with solar-powered docks where fishermen trade stories with new arrivals. A community garden blooms between the old and new, where kids play tag between the crumbling brick and the new playground equipment.
