The void of a hollowed-out cathedral chamber, its arches stretching like skeletal ribs across the sky, walls lined with faint, ghostly light reflections from an abandoned altar. The air hums with static electricity, thick with the scent of old oil and damp stone. At the center, a suspended orb of crystal—once a mirror, now cracked—casts shifting, translucent shadows that seem to pulse like breathing. The floor is a cracked, uneven expanse of polished marble, each tile bearing the faint, unreadable carvings of forgotten hands. The walls whisper in a language of half-erased Latin, the words bleeding into the fabric like blood. A single, rusted door at the far end stands slightly ajar, its hinges groaning as if waiting to be opened. The space feels like it’s holding its breath, and every crack, every crevice, is filled with the presence of something that isn’t there—just enough to make the bones ache.
