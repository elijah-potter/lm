**Mayor’s Office Announces New Solar Initiative for Eldermere Heights**
Eldermere Heights residents can now apply for free solar panel assessments starting tomorrow. The city’s new *Solar Share Program* will match contributions up to $10,000 per household, with incentives for energy-efficient upgrades. Officials emphasize a phased rollout to avoid grid overload, with priority given to low-income families. Applications must be submitted by June 15. More details: [City Portal](https://eldermere.gov/solar).

**Local Businesses Call for Traffic Restrictions**
Small merchants in Main Street protest new construction delays, citing lost revenue. Protesters demand the city expedite permits for the upcoming pedestrian bridge project, citing safety concerns and economic strain. Union representatives urge workers to strike if permits remain unresolved by Friday. Police report minor clashes with demonstrators near the construction site.

**Scientists Discover Rare Fossil in Fossil Park**
Excavators at Fossil Park have unearthed a 150-million-year-old *Lystrosaurus* skeleton, the first confirmed find in the park’s history. The discovery, made by a team from the Central University Paleontology Lab, includes partial vertebrae and a nearly intact skull. Local paleontologists urge the city to preserve the site as a temporary exhibit, pending further analysis.

**Eldermere Heights Flood Preparedness Alert**
Residents along the Blackthorn River are advised to evacuate by Sunday evening as a major storm approaches. City officials activate emergency shelters in the Community Center and Old Mill Barn. Flash flood warnings extend to neighboring towns, with officials urging residents to check road conditions via the 24/7 flood hotline: 555-HELP-EH. Evacuation zones marked with orange barriers are already in place.

**New Tech Startup Secures $5M to Develop AI for Agriculture**
Eldermere’s *AgriBotics Co.* has closed its first round of funding, raising $5 million to develop AI-driven soil analysis and drone monitoring systems. Founder Dr. Elias Voss attributes the success to local farmers’ demand for precision farming tools. The company plans to open a pilot plant in the old textile mill by year-end. Investors include a consortium from the city’s tech hub.
