Further research is needed into the impact of diet on a person's wellbeing, it has been claimed.
According to Oliver Childs, senior cancer information officer at Cancer Research UK, it is difficult to ascertain whether the results of previous studies into the area are accurate as in many cases they have yet to be backed up.
As a result, Cancer Research UK is backing the European Prospective Investigation into Cancer (EPIC) in an attempt to further understanding of the link between diet and health.
'Half a million people in ten European countries are involved in the study,' Mr Childs commented.
'EPIC is providing some of the best evidence on the relationships between cancer and diet.'
A study conducted by Professor Tim Key recently found that while vegetarians have a lower risk of cancer than omnivores, people who eat meat are less susceptible to bowel cancer than those who do not.
Around 35,000 are diagnosed with colorectal cancer every year.
