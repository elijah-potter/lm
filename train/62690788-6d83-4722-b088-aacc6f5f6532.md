The spat had escalated past the point of polite disagreement. "You *always* forget to remind me to take out the trash!" she snapped, arms crossed, her voice sharp with exasperation. He glared back, his fingers digging into the countertop. "Because I *remember* to remind *you*," he shot back, his tone dripping with disbelief. The kitchen light cast long shadows over their raised voices.

Across the room, the doorbell rang—a familiar sound that cut through the tension like a knife. Without looking away, she sighed, "That’s our neighbor, again. Probably just asking if we’re okay with the noise." He muttered something about "loud neighbors" under his breath before turning to the door. She rolled her eyes but followed him, arms folded, already bracing for the inevitable.

The knock sounded again. This time, it was the doorbell. He hesitated, then pulled it open. A child’s voice, muffled but determined, called, "Uncle Tom? It’s me, Sam!"

The door creaked open just enough for the boy to slip inside, his eyes wide with surprise. He froze, staring at the two adults—one with a scowl, the other with arms crossed—before darting a glance between them. "I—I came to ask if I could borrow your bike," he stammered, clutching a worn backpack. "I promised Mrs. Henderson I’d be back by dinner, and I forgot."

Silence.

Then, from the corner of his eye, he saw her relax slightly. The other adult’s lips twitched, just a little. The boy’s hope flickered. "You… you can borrow it?" he asked, voice trembling with the weight of the moment.

He didn’t wait for an answer. With a quick nod to the adults—who were still glaring at each other—he darted out, the bike’s chain squeaking behind him. The door clicked shut behind him. Seconds later, the street outside was quiet again.

Neither of them moved. The tension in the air dissolved like morning mist, replaced by something far less tense. She exhaled slowly, watching him. "Well," she said, "at least he didn’t ask for money."

He grunted, but the corners of his mouth twitched. "Yeah. I guess not."

The silence stretched out between them, broken only by the hum of the refrigerator and the distant sound of Sam’s bike pedaling down the sidewalk.
