## Project Chimera: Rulebook

**A Cooperative Game of Scientific Crisis Management**

Welcome to Project Chimera, a cooperative tabletop game for two to four players. You are a team of leading researchers tasked with containing and understanding a rapidly evolving synthetic pathogen threatening global stability. Success hinges on your ability to pool your expertise, manage limited resources, and make difficult decisions under immense pressure.

### Components

*   **Crisis Board:** A central board depicting a world map with designated research locations and escalating threat levels.
*   **Researcher Cards (12):** Each card represents a unique researcher with specialized skills and starting resources.
*   **Pathogen Cards (40):** Cards detailing the pathogen’s characteristics, mutation events, and escalating threat levels.
*   **Research Tokens (60):** Representing data points gathered through research efforts.
*   **Containment Markers (30):** Used to mitigate the pathogen's spread.
*   **Resource Tokens (90):** Representing funding, personnel, and equipment. Divided into three types: Funding (blue), Personnel (green), and Equipment (yellow).
*   **Event Cards (30):** Introducing unexpected challenges and opportunities.
*   **Threat Track:** A track on the Crisis Board indicating the overall severity of the crisis.
*   **Mutation Die:** A custom six-sided die used to determine pathogen mutations.
*   **First Player Token:** Indicates the active player.

### Setup

1.  Place the Crisis Board in the center of the playing area.
2.  Shuffle the Pathogen Cards and place them face down in a designated draw pile. Reveal the top three Pathogen Cards and place them face up next to the draw pile. These represent the initial known characteristics of the pathogen.
3.  Shuffle the Event Cards and place them face down in a designated draw pile.
4.  Place the Threat Track marker at the starting position on the Threat Track.
5.  Distribute the Resource Tokens into a general supply.
6.  Each player chooses a Researcher Card. Each Researcher begins with the resources indicated on their card.
7.  Determine the first player randomly and give them the First Player Token.

### Turn Structure

Each player’s turn proceeds in the following phases:

1.  **Event Phase:** Draw the top Event Card and resolve its effects immediately. These events can introduce new challenges, provide opportunities, or alter existing conditions.
2.  **Action Phase:** You have two actions to spend. Actions can be any combination of the following:
    *   **Research (1 Action):** Choose a research location on the Crisis Board. Spend one Research Token to draw a number of Pathogen Cards equal to the location’s research value (printed on the board). Choose one card to keep and place it face up next to the draw pile. Discard the remaining cards.
    *   **Contain (1 Action):** Spend one Containment Marker and one Personnel Token to reduce the Threat Level by one step. This can only be performed in locations where the pathogen is present.
    *   **Gather Resources (1 Action):** Choose one Resource Type (Funding, Personnel, or Equipment). Gain two tokens of that type from the general supply.
    *   **Transfer Resources (1 Action):** Give one or more Resource Tokens of any type to another player.
    *   **Mitigate Mutation (2 Actions):** Spend three Research Tokens to attempt to prevent a Mutation Event (see Mutation Phase).
3.  **Mutation Phase:** Roll the Mutation Die. Consult the Mutation Table (printed on the Crisis Board) to determine the effect of the roll. This may involve adding new Pathogen Cards to the game, increasing the Threat Level, or triggering other negative consequences. If you attempted to Mitigate Mutation, discard three Research Tokens and re-roll the die. If the second roll is lower than the first, you successfully mitigated the mutation.
4.  **Pass the Token:** Pass the First Player Token to the player on your left.

### Win and Loss Conditions

**Winning:** You win the game if you successfully research and understand the pathogen by revealing a predetermined number of Pathogen Cards (typically 10 for a standard game). The specific number is indicated on the Crisis Board.

**Losing:** You lose the game if any of the following occur:

*   The Threat Level reaches the final space on the Threat Track.
*   You are unable to draw an Event Card when required.
*   A condition specified on a Pathogen Card is met.

### Advanced Variants & Trade-offs

These variants introduce increased complexity and strategic trade-offs.

1.  **Rapid Mutation:** After each Mutation Phase, discard the top Pathogen Card and replace it with a new one from the draw pile. This accelerates the pathogen’s evolution and increases the difficulty. *Trade-off:* Faster progress in understanding the pathogen is balanced by a more unpredictable and volatile threat.
2.  **Limited Communication:** Players may only verbally communicate about their Researcher Cards and hand of Pathogen Cards. All other discussions must be non-verbal. *Trade-off:* Increased reliance on non-verbal cues and a greater need for careful observation.
3.  **Resource Scarcity:** Reduce the initial number of Resource Tokens available in the general supply by 20%. *Trade-off:* Requires more careful resource management and prioritisation of actions.
4.  **Specialist Focus:** Each player must commit to focusing their actions on a specific Resource Type (Funding, Personnel, or Equipment) for the entire game. This restricts their flexibility but encourages specialization. *Trade-off:* Increased efficiency in a chosen area at the expense of overall versatility.

### Difficulty Scaling

*   **Two Players:** Reduce the number of Pathogen Cards required to win by two.
*   **Three Players:** Play with the standard rules.
*   **Four Players:** Increase the number of Pathogen Cards required to win by two.

### Designer’s Note

Project Chimera was designed to explore the complexities of scientific crisis management and the importance of collaborative problem-solving. The game aims to highlight the difficult choices researchers face when confronted with limited resources and uncertain information. By forcing players to prioritize actions, manage trade-offs, and communicate effectively, we hope to foster an appreciation for the challenges inherent in confronting global crises and the crucial role of scientific innovation in safeguarding our future. The mechanics are intended to subtly illustrate the idea that even with the best intentions, progress is rarely linear and often requires difficult compromises.
