The morning sun rises too early, casting a golden glow that makes time blur. Wakefulness feels like a stolen moment—one that stretches into an endless haze of to-do lists. Coffee brews, steam curling into the air like a promise, but the clock ticks faster than the caffeine can kick in.

At work, emails arrive in waves, each demand a response that feels like a sprint. Meetings blur into one another, decisions made under pressure, and the day’s hours shrink like a balloon deflating. Lunch is rushed, a sandwich eaten while scrolling through endless feeds, the world moving too fast to pause.

By evening, exhaustion settles in like a quiet weight. The house feels too small, the bed too narrow, the evening too short to unwind. Lights dim, but the clock still ticks, counting down to sleep—another day’s work waiting to begin.

The night air is cool, but the mind races ahead, anticipating tomorrow’s demands. Tomorrow, the same routine: wake, rush, repeat. A day too short to breathe, too short to rest.
