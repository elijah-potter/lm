A subtle crack in the foundation, barely visible to the untrained eye, was the first sign of decay. Over time, it widened into a jagged fissure, hiding beneath layers of plaster and neglect. The walls, once smooth and unblemished, now bore faint, invisible scars—tiny gaps where the earth had slowly shifted, sealing the small breach. By the time the cracks became visible, the damage had spread deeper, silently eroding the structure from within.

The same could be said of human behavior: a single hesitation, a half-truth whispered in passing, a moment of indifference that fades into the background noise. These minor shifts—unnoticed at first—accumulate like rust, corroding what was once solid. The quiet erosion of trust, the slow unraveling of intention, the way a lie, once planted, takes root without anyone ever questioning where it came from. The thing that slips past observation is never the act itself, but the absence of its opposite: the care, the vigilance, the deliberate effort to see what others choose to ignore.
