In the dim glow of a half-lit alley, a lone figure hunched over a rusted coffee cup, fingers tracing the rim with deliberate slowness. The streetlamps flickered, casting long shadows that stretched like skeletal fingers along the pavement. Across from them, a half-empty diner hummed with the distant murmur of patrons, their voices swallowed by the low ceiling. The character stood rigid, arms crossed, watching the flicker of movement through the window—someone else’s life unfolding in slow motion, untouched by the weight of their own silence.

A car horn blared, jarring the stillness, and the figure turned, their gaze sharp with something unreadable. A familiar scent—old leather and damp wool—drifted past them, followed by the sound of a door opening. The stranger stepped out, their stride confident, their expression unguarded. The air between them crackled, charged with the unspoken tension of unspoken words.

The figure exhaled, slow and measured, as if deciding whether to step forward or retreat into the shadows. The diner’s lights blurred into a golden haze, the world around them momentarily still. Then, the stranger was gone, leaving only the echo of their footsteps on the cracked pavement.
