The wind howled through the skeletal trees of Blackthorn Hollow, their branches clawing at the storm-wracked sky like the fingers of a dead man. The old mill stood abandoned, its stone foundations stained with rust and time, its windows shattered as if something had tried to escape but couldn’t. Inside, the air smelled of damp earth and forgotten things—old bread gone mouldy, a half-burned candle still dripping wax onto the cobblestones.

Lira crouched beside the half-drowned dog, its fur matted with salt and the scent of rain. The creature’s eyes were glassy, its breath shallow. "You shouldn’t have brought him here," she whispered, though she knew the words were hollow. The dog whimpered, its tongue lolling, its ribs visible beneath the flapping skin.

The river below was a raging beast, its waters churning into a froth of white. The bridge over it had collapsed years ago, but the locals still whispered that the water there was cursed. Those who crossed at night never returned. Lira’s father had told her stories—how the old mill’s owner, a man named Veyra, had vanished one evening, his body never found. The villagers said he’d been taken by the river, but Lira always thought it was worse than that. That he’d *chosen* the water, drawn by something in the dark beneath.

Now, as the storm worsened, the dog’s movements grew frantic. Its paws struck the ground, claws digging into the dirt. The last thing Lira heard before the water claimed it was a sound—something like a laugh, but wrong, like the wind through the trees at dusk.

She stood, her boots sinking into the mud, and looked at the horizon. The river’s edge was a line of black, where the water met the sky. The storm had passed, but the hollow was still. And then she saw it: the faintest glow, like embers in the dark, half-buried in the reeds at the river’s edge.

What had drawn Veyra there? And what was waiting beneath the water?
