A fluorescent-lit office cubicle hums with the usual drone of keyboards and muted conversations. The fluorescent lights flicker slightly, casting a dim glow over the half-empty desk where a lone employee hunches over a spreadsheet. Their head snaps up abruptly as a sudden, infectious burst of laughter erupts from down the hall—someone in HR, perhaps, or the interns who always manage to lose their composure over the most mundane tasks.

The sound is sharp, unfiltered, and *wrong*—not the polite chuckles of colleagues, but the kind of giggle that’s equal parts embarrassment and exhilaration. It cuts through the air like a slap, sending a wave of tension through the room. The employee’s fingers freeze mid-tap on the keyboard, their breath catching.

From the hallway, the laughter continues—then stops abruptly, replaced by the muffled *"What?"* and *"You okay?"* before fading into the usual office hum again. The employee exhales, half-relieved, half-disappointed, and returns to their work, only to realize their phone buzzes—a text from someone they’ve been avoiding: *"Sorry, was lost in thought. Still there?"* The laughter lingers in their chest like an aftertaste.
