A seemingly minor exchange between two strangers—Alice and Bob—unfolded into a revelation that neither had anticipated. Alice had borrowed a book from Bob’s library, assuming it was a casual loan. When Bob asked for the return date, Alice joked, *"You’d be surprised how long I can hold onto a book—just until I’ve read it."* Bob, amused, replied, *"Then maybe you should’ve asked before taking it."* The misunderstanding began there: Alice had read the book, but Bob had only skimmed it; Alice thought she’d been honest about her intent, but Bob had misinterpreted her tone.

At the library meeting, Alice confessed she’d read the entire book—twice—and had even highlighted passages. Bob’s face lit up: he’d only checked it out to find a specific passage for research and had returned it early. The misunderstanding dissolved into laughter, but the deeper truth emerged in silence: how often do we assume the best in others while failing to communicate clearly? The book was returned, but the lesson lingered—words, like books, can be misread if not understood in context.
