A candle flickered low in the half-lit room, casting long shadows across the worn wooden floor. Outside, the rain pattered softly against the windows, muffled by the heavy curtains. At the center of the table, a single, ornate silver tray gleamed under the dim glow of the lamp. A cup of steaming tea, a plate of salted almonds, and a small, folded note lay untouched beside it.

Your mother sat across from you, her fingers tracing the rim of her teacup, her voice barely above a whisper as she unfolded the note. *"Promise me you’ll come home before the stars are bright enough to see in the dark."* Her eyes met yours, unreadable, but the way her lips curved just once told you everything you needed to know. The room felt heavier, the silence thick with something you couldn’t name—only certainty. The tea had gone cold. The night was long. And the promise was already yours to keep.
