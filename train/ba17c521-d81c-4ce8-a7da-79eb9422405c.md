In the dim glow of a cracked fluorescent bulb, Clara sat hunched over her desk, fingers tracing the rim of her coffee cup. The scent of stale air and old paper hung thick in the room, a silent accusation against her. Her father’s voice echoed from the hallway, but she refused to look up. *"You’re wasting time on this, Clara,"* it had said, sharp as a knife. *"The contract is signed. The house is ours."*

She exhaled slowly, the breath trembling. The past year had been a slow unraveling—her mother’s sudden illness, the way her father’s eyes had grown colder with each passing month. But this? This was worse. Not the loss of her mother, not the betrayal of her father, but the weight of the man she had become. A ghost of her old self, standing in the shadows of her own guilt.

The phone buzzed beside her—another missed call from her best friend, the one who always knew what to say, who never let her pretend she was okay. She ignored it, the ringtone fading into static.

Outside, the wind howled through the broken windowpanes. Somewhere, a siren wailed, distant and cruel. Clara closed her eyes. She had always been the one who stayed, who fixed things. But what if fixing was the problem? What if the only thing that needed fixing was her?

The door creaked open. She didn’t move. Her father stood there, arms crossed, his face pale under the harsh light. *"You’re not ready,"* he said. Not a question. *"Not yet."* His voice was a blade. *"But you will be."* He turned away, and for the first time in years, Clara let herself wonder—what if he was right? What if she was the one who had to break first?
