A quiet, overcast library in a forgotten university campus. The air hums with the faintest echo of old books, their spines worn smooth by decades of handling. Shelves curve inward like the ribs of a sleeping beast, each row a labyrinth of half-finished research projects and forgotten essays. A single desk sits at the center, its surface cluttered with half-eaten sandwiches and a half-finished sketch of a cityscape. A single light flickers above it, casting long shadows that stretch like fingers across the floor. The clock on the wall is stuck at 3:17, and the silence is thick enough to cut with a knife. A single candle burns low, its flame barely visible in the dim light. The walls are lined with old, framed photographs—some of the place, some of people who used to be here, now gone. The air smells of old paper and something faintly metallic, like old blood. It’s a place where time doesn’t rush, where moments linger like ghosts.
