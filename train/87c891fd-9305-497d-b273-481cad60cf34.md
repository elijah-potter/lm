A single candle flickered against the storm’s relentless wind, casting long shadows on the rain-slicked pavement. The beam of its flame—once steady—now wavered like a ghost in the dark. It was the moment he saw her: the way her fingers traced the rim of her teacup, the hesitant pause before she lowered it, the way her breath hitched like a question. She hadn’t meant to stay, but the weight of the past year had settled in her bones, and for a breath, the world had felt like it was holding its breath with her. Then the wind howled, and the flame guttered out. The moment dissolved into the damp night, swallowed by the current of the river that rushed past, indifferent. She turned away, and the memory, sharp as a blade, was gone before she could name it.
