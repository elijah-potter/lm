The alarm buzzed at 5:37 AM, but the world didn’t wake up until 6:12. By 7:04, the coffee was cold, the commute felt like a marathon, and the first meeting’s slides had already been opened three times. Lunch was a sandwich from a drive-thru, and the screen time between meetings stretched into an hour where nothing meaningful happened—just endless scrolling, notifications, and the weight of unanswered emails.

By 4:00 PM, the body was exhausted, but the day’s tasks—emails, calls, follow-ups—kept coming. The evening stretched into a blur: a quick dinner, a half-hearted workout (which only made the fatigue worse), and then the endless loop of news, binge-watching, and scrolling through social media, where every update felt like a reminder of how little time there is to do anything deep.

At 11:15 PM, the bed was cold, the mind raced with half-finished thoughts, and the day’s energy had long since dissipated. Tomorrow’s to-do list was already a mountain, and the cycle repeated itself—another day where time slipped through fingers, and the feeling of not enough was all that remained.
