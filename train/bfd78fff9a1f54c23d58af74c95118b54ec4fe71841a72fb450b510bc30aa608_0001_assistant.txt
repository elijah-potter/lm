On January 3rd, 2019, the St. Louis Blues sat dead last in the NHL standings. Nobody could have imagined that just a few months later they would be lifting the Stanley Cup trophy for the first time in franchise history. A season that started with frustration ended in glory, but how did it happen? When the Blues fired coach Mike Yeo after starting 7-9-3 in the first 19 games, hopes for a good season largely went down the drain. After that, it wasn't as if things improved immediately. 

The Blues named Craig Berube as interim coach and the team continued to stumble before bottoming out as the last-place team in the entire league. Nobody would have been surprised if the team, coaches, and fans threw in the towel on a disappointing season. But that never happened. Instead, the Blues went on one of the most improbable runs in sports history. And it all started with Jordan Binnington.

After starting the year as the organization's 4th ranked goaltender, Binnington was given an opportunity to start for the team in mid-January. In his first three starts, Binnington allowed just two goals in his first three starts. With Binnington's stellar performance in net, the Blues slowly began to work their way back up the standings, finishing the season on a 30-10-5 run in their last 45 games. Binnington continued to impress during the playoffs, starting in goal for all 16 Blues wins, including an incredible performance in game 7 against both the Stars and Bruins. Along with Binnington, the Blues got major playoff contributions from David Perron, Ryan O'Reilly, and role players like Tyler Bozak.
