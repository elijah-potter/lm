**The Quiet Hour**

The city hummed like a trapped insect, a thousand voices tangled in neon and steel. I sat at my desk, fingers tracing the edge of the open notebook where the first draft had spilled onto the page—half-formed words, jagged lines of unfinished thought. The keyboard felt warm, the air thick with the scent of old coffee and the faint metallic tang of the printer’s last failed attempt at printing the draft.

Outside, the traffic had slowed to a crawl, the only sounds the distant wail of a police siren and the occasional laugh from a group of teenagers passing by. I let my gaze drift to the window, where the skyline blurred into a smudge of colors—orange and pink bleeding into the twilight. It was always like this when I returned from the city: the world felt too loud, too bright, too *alive* in a way that made my head ache.

I exhaled slowly, letting the breath out in a long, deliberate sigh. The notebook was still open, but I closed it now, the weight of it settling between my palms. The words inside were mine, but they were also a storm inside me, and storms didn’t need to be fought alone.

I stood, stretching my arms above my head, the movement slow and deliberate. The lamp above the desk cast long shadows across the floor, painting the room in a dance of light and dark. I walked to the balcony, the cool night air a sharp contrast to the heat of the city. Below, the streetlights flickered, casting dancing shadows on the pavement.

For a moment, I just stood there, listening. The rhythm of the city was different tonight—quieter, more measured. The wind carried the scent of rain, promising something soft and wet on the horizon. I leaned against the railing, letting my body relax into the familiar curve of the balcony’s edge.

The city would always be loud. But right now, the world needed only this: the quiet between the notes, the pause before the next breath. The return to calm wasn’t about silence—it was about finding the space where the noise didn’t have to be heard.
