In the quiet town of Eldermere, where the cobblestone streets whispered secrets to the wind, a lone lantern flickered beside the old clock tower. Its light, a stubborn ember against the encroaching twilight, traced the contours of a map half-buried beneath the moss-covered stones.

The map was an odd relic—a faded parchment with strange symbols, some resembling eyes, others like cracked eggshells. The last one, half-eroded, held a single detail: the name *"Lena’s Locket"*, circled in ink so faded it might have been added after the map’s creation.

A young scholar named Elias had found it near the riverbank. He traced the circle with his fingers, then slipped the locket into his coat—a gift from his late grandmother, who had warned him never to open it. Tonight, as he stood beneath the lantern, his thumb brushed the clasp. The lock clicked open.

Inside, a single tear—blue as the deepest ocean—rolled slowly to the bottom. It was the last thing Lena had written before she vanished: *"Find the heart that beats for you."*
