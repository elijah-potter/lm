The rain had been falling for hours when Elias realized he had left his umbrella at home.

He stepped into the downpour, the cold droplets stinging his face as he hurried toward the abandoned train station. The flickering streetlights cast long shadows across the wet pavement, and the distant wail of sirens echoed through the night.

Elias had never been one for excuses, but as he turned back toward the road, his fingers brushed against something cold and damp. He crouched down, pulling out the folded note from his pocket.

*"If you’re reading this, I’m already gone. Meet me at the old mill—sunrise. Bring the key."*

His breath came in short gasps. The note was from his sister, Mira. The last message she’d sent him before she vanished three days ago.

The mill had been his family’s legacy, but Mira had been the one who insisted it needed to be preserved. She’d worked tirelessly to restore it, her dreams intertwined with the rusted machinery and the hollow-eyed barn cat that still slept in the attic.

Elias didn’t believe in ghosts, but the mill had felt different since Mira left. The wind howled through the empty halls, and the old clock tower’s hands had stopped at 3:17—the time she’d last called him.

He didn’t know why he was going. Maybe he was running from the guilt of not being there when she called. Maybe he was just tired of waiting for answers.

The mill loomed ahead, its wooden doors slightly ajar, as if welcoming him. Inside, the air was thick with dust and the faint scent of mildew. The cat stirred in the shadows, its golden eyes fixed on him.

Elias followed the sound of rustling paper and found Mira’s journal at the center of the room. The pages were filled with sketches of the mill’s restoration, her handwriting neat and determined.

*"They’ll never understand,"* she’d written. *"But the mill will keep my memory alive."*

A floorboard creaked behind him.

Elias turned slowly. Standing in the doorway was a figure wrapped in a tattered cloak, their face obscured by a hood. The cat hissed and darted toward them.

*"You shouldn’t have come,"* the stranger said, voice like dry leaves. *"She didn’t want to be found."*

Elias hesitated. The stranger reached into their coat and pulled out a key—Mira’s family heirloom. His heart pounded. He recognized the engraving: *"For when the past won’t stay buried."*

*"It’s time,"* the stranger continued. *"The mill is changing. The old ways are ending. You can keep it—or you can let it go."*

Elias exhaled. He had spent his life fighting the past, trying to outrun it. But the mill was more than wood and rust. It was Mira. It was their family.

He reached for the key.

The stranger smiled—a slow, knowing smile—and stepped back. *"Then make your choice."*

The clock tower chimed midnight. The rain outside had stopped, leaving the world silent. The cat’s eyes glowed faintly in the dark.

Elias picked up the key and turned it. The mill’s doors swung open on their own, revealing a spiral staircase descending into darkness.

Below, the river hummed, its current steady and sure. At the bottom, the mill’s old engine roared to life, its gears grinding into motion.

Elias stepped forward, the key still in his hand. The air smelled of oil and something metallic—like copper and blood.

*"You’re late,"* Mira whispered from the darkness.
