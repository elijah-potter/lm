The clock tower’s pendulum hung suspended mid-swing, its brass bob frozen in an unnatural arc. The city’s hum—car horns, distant chatter—had become a muffled, warped hum, each note stretched into an eternity of silence. The girl at the café’s window, her fingers tracing the rim of her coffee cup, blinked as if waking from a dream. A bird on the street below fluttered, its wings a blur of motion, before vanishing into the same slow blur of a passing shadow.

A man in a trench coat stood still, his coat brushing against the sidewalk as if resisting motion. His gaze locked onto a passing child, who stared back, their breath visible in the cold air. The seconds stretched like taffy, then snapped back into place. The child’s laughter dissolved into a whisper. The man’s shadow, once sharp, now flickered like a dying flame. The café’s light flickered—once, twice—before stabilizing. The girl’s coffee cup, still half-full, seemed to defy gravity, its liquid rising in slow, deliberate waves toward her lips. The city’s pulse, once a relentless rhythm, now pulsed like a heartbeat, each beat a moment suspended in time.
