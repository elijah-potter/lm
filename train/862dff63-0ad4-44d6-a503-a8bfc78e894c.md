The flickering neon sign of **Brewtopia Café** buzzed like a dying insect against the storm clouds outside. Rain drummed against the grimy windows, the only sound punctuated by the distant wail of sirens. Inside, the air smelled of stale coffee and damp cardboard. The café’s generator sputtered, coughing smoke into the dark like a dying beast.

**Lena**, a barista with calloused hands, wiped a steaming mug with a rag that had seen better days. Her phone’s battery was at 12%. The power flickered again—this time, the overhead lights died in a cascade of static. The only illumination came from the dim glow of the neon "Brewtopia" sign, a sickly green pulse that cast long shadows across the floor.

Across the room, **Darius**, a wiry tech support guy in a hoodie, hunched over a laptop on the counter. His fingers hovered over the keyboard, but the screen flickered like a dying firefly. "Still no signal," he muttered, rubbing his temples. "Must be the tower down in the old mill district."

A sudden *thud* echoed from the back. Lena turned to see **Mira**, a sharp-eyed journalist with a satchel slung over her shoulder, sprinting toward the kitchen. Her face was pale, her breath coming in ragged bursts. "They’re clearing out the power lines—now!" she gasped. "We’ve got thirty minutes before the whole neighborhood goes dark!"

A murmur rippled through the crowd. A **teenager**, clutching a bag of takeout, shivered. "I’m freezing. Can we at least get warm drinks?"

Darius exhaled sharply. "Coffee’s on the counter—help yourself." He grabbed a mug, but his hands shook as he poured. The generator’s roar was the only sound now, drowning out the distant crash of falling power lines outside.

Lena sipped her coffee, watching the room tense. The café was silent, save for the slow, uneven beat of hearts. Outside, the wind howled through the alley, and the sky was a bruised canvas of dark and gray.

Then, the lights went out again.
