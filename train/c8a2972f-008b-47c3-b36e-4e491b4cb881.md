A quiet afternoon in a café where every seat was occupied, the hum of conversation blending with the clink of coffee cups. The barista’s hands moved with practiced ease, but the mugs felt unfamiliar—like they’d been handed down through generations. The scent of espresso was thick, yet it didn’t belong to her. She’d never tasted this blend before. The moment stretched taut, a thread between the space and the time, neither fully hers nor anyone else’s.

She sat at a table near the window, watching the city blur through the glass. The view was beautiful, but the perspective was borrowed—like someone else’s memories, slightly altered, slightly stolen. The café was warm, but the warmth felt borrowed too, as if the heat had been passed through others before reaching her. She glanced at her watch: the time was precise, yet it didn’t feel hers. The moment felt like a ghost, lingering just beyond grasp, a fleeting presence in a space that wasn’t entirely hers.
