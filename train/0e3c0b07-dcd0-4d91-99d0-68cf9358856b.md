A tiny figurine carved from driftwood, its weathered surface etched with ancient runes, carries the weight of forgotten battles and whispered secrets. Its size—no taller than a finger—hides a history of storms, shipwrecks, and buried truths. The craftsmanship, though delicate, defies the fragility of its form, symbolizing resilience in the face of overwhelming forces. To the seafarers who once cherished it, it was more than a trinket; it was a compass, a relic, a fragment of a world they could no longer see. The meaning it holds is vast: the endurance of memory, the power of objects to transcend their size, and the quiet strength of what is overlooked.
