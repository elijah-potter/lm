The first time Mia saw her reflection in the cracked mirror of the abandoned bakery, she thought she was imagining things. But the girl staring back was different—her eyes too dark, her smile too sharp. The next morning, she woke up with the same feeling: something wasn’t right. Her best friend, Leo, had vanished overnight, and the only clue was a note scribbled on the fridge: *"Find the key before the clock strikes midnight."* The bakery had been closed for years, its doors boarded up, but Mia’s parents insisted she didn’t know where it was. Still, she found it anyway—hidden behind a loose floorboard in their garage.

Inside, the air smelled of old bread and something metallic. The walls were lined with jars of preserved fruits, and a single, flickering light bulb hung from the ceiling. At the center of the room was a massive clock, its face black with gold hands, spinning backward. Leo’s note was taped to it: *"The key is in the sugar bowl."* Mia dug through the jars until she found one labeled *Lemon Curd*—inside was a small, rusted key. When she turned it in the clock’s lock, the door swung open, revealing a hidden staircase descending into darkness.

The steps led to a vast, echoing basement. The air was thick with the scent of burning sugar and something like ozone. The walls were lined with shelves of jars, each filled with strange, glowing liquids that pulsed faintly in the dim light. At the far end stood a figure in a tattered coat, their face obscured by a hood. "You’re late," they said, their voice like dry leaves scraping stone. "The last time you came, you lost your friend. This time, you might lose more than that." Mia’s pulse quickened. She recognized the voice—it was her mother’s, but older, distorted.

The figure pressed a button on a panel embedded in the wall, and the shelves began to tilt. The jars spilled their contents, flooding the basement with a swirling, iridescent mist. The figure stepped forward, revealing a second figure—a girl in a lab coat, her eyes glowing like embers. "You have one hour," the figure said. "Choose: you can stay and watch your family unravel, or you can leave before the clock strikes midnight. The choice is yours."

Mia hesitated. The clock above them ticked, its hands moving faster. The sugar bowl in her pocket felt heavy. She knew what she had to do. Without a word, she grabbed the key and ran. The staircase behind her groaned as she climbed, the basement door slamming shut behind her. Above, the clock’s hands reached midnight. The bakery doors burst open, flooding with light. Mia sprinted through the streets, her breath ragged, her heart pounding. She had to find Leo before the next midnight.

She ran until her legs burned, until her name was the only thing she could think. The bakery was gone—replaced by a modern storefront, its windows reflecting the neon glow of the city. But somewhere, deep in the shadows, a clock still ticked backward. And Mia knew the real game had only just begun.
