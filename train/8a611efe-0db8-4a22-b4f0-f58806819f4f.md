The air smelled faintly of damp earth and old wood, thick with the lingering scent of pine resin. A single, half-burned candle flickered in a cracked glass chimney on the corner of the table, its wick bent like a question mark. The walls were lined with peeling wallpaper—once dark red, now faded to a sickly beige—with a single framed photograph of a family in a vintage car, the edges slightly askew. The floorboards groaned underfoot, one plank warped where a child’s toy had once rolled too far.

A half-empty mug of something dark and bitter sat on the coffee table, its rim smeared with what might have been coffee or blood. The doorframe was cracked, revealing a shadowy hallway where the light from the streetlamps beyond flickered through, casting long, jagged shadows on the floor. The only sound was the distant murmur of voices outside, muffled by the thick, damp curtains drawn across the windows.
