Going on a first date is a bit like an audition. No matter how much you prepare, you still worry about forgetting your lines or, worse yet, saying the wrong thing. Here are some tips on how to avoid common first-date pitfalls so you can put your best foot forward.

1. Take the High Ground
Your mother was right when she told you to "mind your manners." The purpose behind courtesy and manners is to give respect to and show consideration for those around you. Doing small things like holding open a door, not cutting off your date in mid-sentence, and saying "please" and "thank you" go a long way to lowering the anxiety level for both of you. If you have a tendency to swear or use vulgar language, keep it in check. Bad language gives off bad vibes. 

2. Forget Your Ex
Sharing life experiences helps you learn about each other but keep your past relationships under wraps. No one wants to hear how your last date was crazy or why you only seem to attract weird-os. In the same vein, avoid saying anything that starts with "all men" or "all women." This automatically lumps your date into a category you expressly don't like and make them wonder why they're wasting their time going out with you. If your date talks about their past relationships, do not judge or ask about their body count. If it makes you uncomfortable, tactfully change the subject and let it go.

3. Don't Get Personal
Don't ask how much your date weighs. Ever. If your date mentions they struggle with their weight, don't say anything negative or demeaning. If you must respond, keep it neutral by observing that many people have the same problem and leave it at that. Ditto asking your date their age. It's none of your business unless your date looks like they're under 18; then, by all means, ask. Avoid hot-button subjects like religion, race, politics, and social issues. And save the sex talk for later when the relationship is more established and intimate.
