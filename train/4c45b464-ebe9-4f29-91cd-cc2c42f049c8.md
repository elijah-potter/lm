**The storm outside raged in violent arcs of lightning, each bolt a jagged scar across the storm-chased sky.** Rain hammered the windows in relentless, frantic sheets, turning the office’s glass into a mirror of the chaos within. **A single, trembling lamp flickered—then died.** The last of the artificial light guttered out as the power surged, and the room plunged into the kind of silence that only comes after the world has been shaken.

Inside, **a woman in a worn leather jacket** sat hunched over a half-finished manuscript, fingers tracing the edges of the pages. The rain drummed against the roof like a metronome counting down to an inevitable collapse. **Her breath came in shallow gasps**, each one a whisper of defeat. The ink on her notebook pages had blurred into streaks, as if the words themselves had been torn from her mind by the storm.

A **door creaked open**, and the scent of damp earth and ozone seeped in. **A man in a tattered raincoat** stood framed by the doorway, his silhouette swallowed by the swirling mist. His voice was a rasp, like the wind through dead branches: *"You should’ve left it in the attic."* The words hung in the air, thick and poisoned. The woman didn’t look up. She had already known.

Outside, the storm **slowly, painfully, began to recede**, leaving behind a world that was just as quiet, just as still. The first, false dawn painted the sky in sickly hues of gray and sickly-sweet light. The woman stood, her back to the window, and **the last of her tears fell like raindrops onto the pages of her book**. She did not write them down. She did not speak them aloud. She let them be. The storm had passed. The silence had won.
