The detective questioned the witness again, this time focusing on the alleyway’s flickering streetlight.
"Did you see the man’s shadow move at the same speed as his steps?" she asked.

The witness hesitated. "Yes, but… he was wearing a long coat. Maybe it was the wind."

Detective: "The coat was too thick to be weather-related. His right hand was *always* slightly raised—like he was holding a cigarette. He was *smoking*—just not in the alley."

The witness frowned. "So? He was hiding cigarettes in his coat pocket. He was trying to sell them."

Detective: "That’s not how it works. He didn’t sell them. He *was* the buyer. The pawnshop owner saw him take the knife from the counter, then slip it into his coat *before* the light flickered out. The *real* misunderstanding was in the ledger: the pawnshop owner wrote ‘knives’ but meant ‘stolen goods.’ The buyer was the thief—he was *returning* the item to cover his tracks."

The victim’s lawyer, listening from the courtroom, exhaled sharply. "So the truth is… the detective was right. The ‘misunderstanding’ was just a distraction. The real culprit was the one everyone assumed was the victim."

The jury filed out, but the detective’s voice cut through the chaos: "The truth isn’t always obvious. Sometimes it’s in the *whispers*—the half-truths, the overlooked details, the shadows that *move* when we look away."
