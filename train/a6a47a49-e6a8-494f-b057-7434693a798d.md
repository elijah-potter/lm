The air hummed with an unnatural stillness. Each breath felt like a weight dragging through liquid glass—every sound stretched into something suspended, the world’s rhythm a slow, deliberate heartbeat. The streetlights flickered like dying stars, casting long, trembling shadows that seemed to writhe on the pavement. A single bird, its wings a blur, fluttered past the window, its flight a frantic dance against the suffocating calm. The clock on the wall ticked, its hands moving in slow motion, each second a deliberate pause.

A child’s laughter echoed from the alley, but the sound dissolved into static, then faded entirely, as if the moment itself was unraveling. The air grew thick, pressing against the skin like a second layer of time. Every movement—her fingers brushing the table, the coffee cup sliding toward her—felt like watching a film roll backward. The world had stopped moving, yet nothing was still. Only the weight of the moment lingered.
