The winding trail ahead stretched toward the crumbling stone bridge, its rusted iron chains swaying in the wind. The path forked—one branch descending sharply through dense underbrush, the other ascending at a gentler, wider slope. Elias hesitated, his boots sinking slightly into the damp earth.

*"What if it’s all wrong?"* the voice in his head whispered, though no one else was there. He exhaled, steadying himself. The bridge loomed, its arches barely holding against the weight of decades. The other trail was safer, familiar—less effort, fewer surprises. But this one called to him, a whisper of adventure, of something he’d never dared before.

He stepped forward. The first step was always the hardest.

The bridge groaned beneath his weight, its stones slick with age. Below, the river rushed, untamed. Above, the sky was a vast, unbroken expanse. His fingers tightened on the railing, but he didn’t look back. The longer path had claimed him.
