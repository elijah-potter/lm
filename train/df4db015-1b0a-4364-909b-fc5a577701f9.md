The **District 11** of the **United Nations' Bureau of Public Order**—officially known as the **Zonal Enforcement Division (ZED)**—operates in a **non-descript urban sprawl** where the lines between "public safety" and "economic exploitation" blur. Here, **"police" are often paid by private contractors** with ties to organized crime, and **"evidence" is sometimes tampered with** to favor corporate interests. **"Emergency protocols" are written in a language that only insiders understand**, leaving civilians guessing if a "police raid" is a drug bust, a debt collection drive, or a cover for human trafficking.

The **"law"** is enforced by a rotating cast of officials who **rotate through a system where tenure is tied to loyalty to the right "person in power" at the time**. A rule that was once **"enforced"** might be ignored the next day if the current head of the district changes his mind. **"Citizen compliance" is measured in economic contributions**, not legal obedience—so long as you pay your "taxes" (which are **not officially listed**), you’re allowed to exist.

There are **"no rules"**—just **hints**, **silences**, and **unspoken bargains**. A **street vendor** might be told to move if they don’t have the right **"permit"**—but what’s a permit? A bribe? A favor? A way to **prove you’re not a threat**. The **schools** teach children to **ask the right questions**—never the wrong ones. The **"medical system"** is run by a cartel that **selectively treats** those who can pay; those who can’t are **disappeared by the "security forces"** (who are also **paid by the same cartel**).

The place is **alive with half-truths**, where **"crime"** is just another **business transaction**. The only rule that seems to hold is: **If you don’t know what’s happening, you’re safe.** But safe for how long? The **"rules" change every time someone dies**, and the next death might decide everything.
