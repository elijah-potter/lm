In 1616, a London theatre promotor, Christopher Beeston, purchased and rebuilt a cock-fighting pit turning it into a theater and naming it The Cockpit. However, this means as Beeston abandoned his old theatre, the Red Bull, in search of a more profitable audience, he had slighted his old, lower-class audience of artisans, apprentices, and their wives. The old audience who could no longer afford tickets to The Cockpit, took umbrage and on the Shrove Tuesday holiday in 1617 attacked The Cockpit theatre destroying costumes, props, and scripts. Beeston rebuilt and renamed the theatre appropriately as The Phoenix. The mob planned to sack the theatre again the next year but was thwarted by the Privy Council.
