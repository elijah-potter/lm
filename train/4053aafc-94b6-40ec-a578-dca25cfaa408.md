The **Sarcophagus Rule** in corporate culture mandates that all new hires must sign a document stating they were hired for their skills, not their appearance. Enforcement is handled by a "Gestalt Check" where managers observe employees for a week and report back if their physical presence feels "unconformingly vibrant" based on a vague internal scoring system. While the rule’s stated purpose is to prevent workplace discrimination, it’s widely used to enforce a narrow, unattainable aesthetic—think: polished hair, tailored suits, and zero visible tattoos—without addressing systemic bias in hiring processes. The rule’s absurdity stems from conflating professionalism with compliance, and its real effect is to penalize non-conformity rather than dismantle systemic exclusion.
