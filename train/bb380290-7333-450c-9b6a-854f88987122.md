## Explain Like I'm Five: Why the Sky is Blue

Okay, imagine you have a big box of crayons. Lots and lots of crayons! Some are red, some are yellow, some are green, and some are blue. Now, imagine the sun is like a giant crayon box, but instead of crayons, it sends out light!

This light isn't just one color, though. It’s actually *all* the colors of the rainbow mixed together! Red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, and violet – they’re all traveling from the sun to us.

Now, the air all around us, the stuff we breathe, is made of tiny, tiny bits called air molecules. They're so small you can’t even see them! 

When the sunlight travels through the air, it bumps into these tiny air molecules.  The blue light is special. It's like a bouncy ball – it bounces *everywhere* when it hits those tiny air molecules.  Red light, and other colors, are more like bowling balls – they mostly go straight through.

Because the blue light bounces around so much, it spreads out all over the sky. That’s why when you look up, you see blue! It’s not *really* up there, it’s just bouncing all around and filling the sky.

Think about it like this: imagine you're throwing a bunch of different colored bouncy balls at a playground full of little trampolines. The blue bouncy balls would bounce all over the place, making the whole playground look blue! The other colors would just go straight past.

Sometimes, at sunset or sunrise, the sky looks red or orange. That's because the sunlight has to travel through *more* air to reach us.  All the blue light has already bounced away by then, and the red and orange light gets to shine through!



So, the sky is blue because blue light is bouncy and loves to bounce around in the air!
