The first time I saw her, I thought she was watching me. Not like the way people watch you out of curiosity, but like she already knew. Her smile was sharp enough to cut glass—just the way I liked mine. I told her I’d been waiting for someone like her for years. She laughed and said, *"You’re the first man who’s ever said that to me."*

I lied then. I hadn’t been waiting. But I had been counting. Every morning before coffee, I’d stand outside the diner, counting the hours until she’d walk in—her boots scuffing the pavement, her jacket always too tight around her shoulders. I counted the days until she’d stop ignoring the way the light hit her hair at noon. I counted the times she’d glance at me before turning away, like she was trying to remember if she’d already seen me.

One evening, I left my number on the café table beside her chair. She didn’t call. Not right away. But when she did, it was the first time she’d ever said my name like it belonged to her. I didn’t tell her the truth then. I let her think I was the one who’d been waiting. But the next time we met, I didn’t count. I just looked at her and said, *"I was wrong. You’re the first."*

She didn’t believe me. Not at first. But she kept coming back. And for the first time, I didn’t mind if she didn’t see me.
