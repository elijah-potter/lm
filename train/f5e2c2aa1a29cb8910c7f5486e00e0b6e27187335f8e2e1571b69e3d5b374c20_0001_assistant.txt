The goal of education is for every student to learn and grow into a person who is prepared for adult life. This should apply to all children, regardless of disability. In the case of children who are part of the special education program, it can be easy to say that they are not capable of learning and growing to the same degree as children in the general education setting, so it is less important to help them. However, the idea that every single child deserves the best education possible is not as impossibly idealistic as it may sound. IDEA, a law that was passed in 2004, supports the idea that not only are children with disabilities capable of being educated, but they deserve a free and appropriate education that prepares them for further education, employment, and independent living. Because of this, I believe that children in special education should have as much opportunity as possible to participate in the general curriculum, while still having all of their individual needs met. Schools should make sure that every child, whether they are part of the special education program or not, can thrive.
