No birds sing, only the low, rhythmic *clank-clank-clank* of machinery beneath the surface, where the city’s bones hum with unseen energy, pulsing in time with the heartbeat of the dead.

The air tastes of ozone and something older—damp earth mixed with the stench of uncollected waste that never quite decomposes. The walls themselves whisper if you press your ear to them, a chorus of voices that are not human, not yet. No windows let light in; only the occasional crack where the glass is still warm to the touch, like a wound that never heals. The ground beneath is uneven—pockets of blackened earth where the soil has been siphoned away, replaced by something that doesn’t rot, doesn’t grow, just *stays*.

At night, the city breathes in slow, deep gasps, its skyline a shifting silhouette of smudged light and dark. No stars pierce the canopy of glass and steel—only the faint, distant glow of distant cities, their own secrets too heavy to bear. The silence is a weight, pressing down from above and below, a suffocating silence that tells you there is more beneath the skin of the world, something that does not breathe, does not sleep, only *waits*.
