A long line of vehicles stretched out of sight, their headlights dimly visible through the early morning haze. The first car, a black sedan with tinted windows, rolled past with the driver’s window slightly open, the sound of music drifting out. Behind it, a second vehicle with a banner wrapped around its trunk—some kind of floral arrangement or cardboard cutout—followed, the driver’s door left ajar. The road was already packed, but this was just the beginning.

The real chaos started when the first marching band appeared, their brass instruments blaring at full volume. A woman in a bright pink jacket clutched her phone, her face contorted in what could have been disgust or sheer exhaustion. She was halfway across the street when the first group of people spilled out of a building—uniformed figures in crisp jackets, their hands raised in unison as they began to sing. The air vibrated with a chorus of notes, so loud the woman’s eardrums felt like they might burst.

Then came the floats. A towering structure resembling a giant, half-assembled bird, its wings spread wide and covered in glittering fabric. Children clung to the sides, their faces pressed against the glass as they screamed with delight. The woman’s stomach twisted. She should have stayed home. She should have taken the bus.

The crowd surged forward, a living, breathing thing, moving in sync, their collective energy overwhelming. The woman stumbled, her heels sinking into the pavement as she tried to avoid the throng. A hand grabbed her elbow, yanking her forward. She barely caught herself before she was swept into the mass, her body crushed against someone else’s back. The heat of a dozen strangers pressed into her, the smell of sweat and perfume thick in the air.

A child’s laughter cut through the noise, followed by the sound of a balloon popping. The woman’s eyes stung, her vision blurring. She pressed a hand to her face, trying to block out the world. The music stopped. The brass instruments fell silent. The crowd still moved, but slower now, like a dying animal.

Then the first car passed, its engine revving as it sped away. The woman exhaled, her chest heaving. The parade was over. The road was empty again.
