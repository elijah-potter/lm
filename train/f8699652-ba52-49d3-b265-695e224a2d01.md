The winding trail, though overgrown with ivy and tangled by roots, stretched ahead with a path that curved like a serpent’s back. The map had marked it as an alternative route, longer by at least three kilometers, but the compass needle had pointed toward this path with a steady, unyielding insistence—an anomaly in the usual grid. The explorer’s boots crunched over loose stones, the weight of the canteen’s strap digging into their shoulder. Ahead, the forest thinned slightly, revealing a clearing where the older traveler paused, hands on knees, staring at the horizon. The shorter path lay ahead, straight and unyielding, but it was the one that had been marked, the one that led to the next village, to trade, to news of the world beyond. The longer path, though, was the one the wind had whispered about: stories of hidden caves, lost ruins, or perhaps even the remnants of something forgotten. The decision hung in the air like a half-remembered dream. One step at a time.
