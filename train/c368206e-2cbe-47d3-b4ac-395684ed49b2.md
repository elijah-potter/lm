A dense fog choked the streets as the neon glow of distant traffic lights flickered through the mist. A child’s laughter echoed—*too long*—echoing against the damp stone of an old bridge. The woman holding them paused, her breath misting in the cold air, her fingers brushing the child’s cheek as if tracing the very slow, deliberate beat of their heart.

Then—*the pause*. The world stopped.

A clock on a lamppost, once ticking like a metronome, now hummed in slow, labored pulses. The child’s eyes, wide with wonder, followed the sway of a leaf drifting through the air—*as if it had been dropped just minutes ago*. A car door slammed behind her, its sound stretched into a single, agonizing *thud*.

She reached out. The air between her hand and the child’s face felt like silk, every molecule stretched thin, every second elongated into a ribbon of memory. The child’s tiny hand closed around hers, fingers trembling—not from fear, but from the sheer *weight* of time itself.

The moment shattered. The clock’s hands leapt forward. The child’s laughter cut off mid-syllable. The fog thinned. The world resumed its relentless, breathless rhythm.

She never told anyone.
