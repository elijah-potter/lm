## Bookstore Serendipity (Seattle, WA)

The rain was relentless that afternoon, a steady drumming against the awning of Left Bank Books on Wallingford. I was browsing the poetry section, drawn in by a worn copy of Neruda, when you appeared. You were examining a collection of essays by Joan Didion, your brow furrowed in concentration, a strand of auburn hair escaping your bun. There was a quiet intensity about you that I found immediately captivating. We didn't speak, but our eyes met briefly, a shared moment of appreciation for the scent of aged paper and the hushed reverence of the space. I remember the warm, comforting aroma of vanilla from the cafe nearby mingled with the mustiness of the books. You purchased your selection and, with a quick, almost shy smile, disappeared into the downpour. I lingered, wishing I had found a reason to speak. I'd truly hoped for a connection.

If you were reading Didion, and recall a brief, shared glance amongst the poetry, please reply with your favorite line from *Slouching Towards Bethlehem*.
