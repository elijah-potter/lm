The **Greyhound Tavern**—a dimly lit, second-floor establishment in an old brick district where the barkeep, a gaunt man with a permanent five o’clock shadow, serves only "what’s here" and never explains *why*. The menu is a handwritten list of dishes with no prices, prices with no dishes, and one item labeled **"By Request"** that’s never delivered. The bathroom door has a sign that reads **"Do Not Disturb"** but the flushing mechanism only works when the patron is out of earshot. The jukebox plays a mix of 1970s rock and unknown local tunes, and the last song always loops at 7:43. The waitstaff are paid in tips, but the tip jar is locked, and the key is hidden in the back room—accessible only to someone who’s been here at least three times. The tavern’s only rule is **"Don’t ask questions you don’t want answered,"** and the only exception is if you ask about the **"Black Ledger"** in the safe—it’s empty, but the ledger itself contains a list of names no one will confirm exists. The regulars swear by it, but no one knows what’s in there. The last time anyone checked, the ledger was in the safe again.
