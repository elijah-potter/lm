A dimly lit warehouse hums with the sound of machinery, its fluorescent lights flickering against rusted metal. Two figures stand at opposite ends of the long, narrow room: one clad in a sleek black trench coat, their face obscured by a mask with a single slit for eyes; the other, a sharp-suited woman in a tailored suit, her fingers tapping impatiently on a tablet. A clipboard lies between them, the paper slick with ink.

The masked man slides a document across the table—a digital printout of satellite imagery, its pixels warping into something unnatural. The woman’s eyes narrow as she scans it. "This isn’t just another corporate blackmail attempt," she says, voice low. "The coordinates match a facility no one was supposed to know about."

The masked man leans in, his breath warm against the mask. "You’re right. But the truth isn’t what you think it is." He taps the tablet, projecting an encrypted feed: a grainy video loop of a high-security door opening, followed by a figure in a lab coat—*her*—disappearing into a vent. The woman’s jaw drops. "That’s me. That’s *me*." The camera pans to a second feed: a server room, a drive being erased in real-time, its data replaced with static. The masked man exhales sharply. "The truth isn’t that you’re a liar. It’s that you’re already dead." The woman’s hands shake as she realizes the second truth: the facility isn’t a prison—it’s a *tomb*. The last message on the tablet, a single word, glitches into view: **"REMEMBER."** The lights flicker.
