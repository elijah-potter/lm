Nellie Bly was a journalist who was born in Cochran’s Mill, Pennsylvania on May 5, 1864. She began work as a writer for a local newspaper but moved to New York in 1886 in the hope of taking her career to the next level. A year later, she went undercover as a patient in a New York City asylum. Her report, a 6-part series, titled '10 Days in the Madhouse', made her one of the most famous reporters in the world. Her research inspired change in both the fields of journalism and mental health. Then, in 1889, she set a world record by travelling around the world in 72 days and published daily updates throughout her journey. Returning to journalism in her later years, Bly covered World War I, but died in 1922, while still working as a writer.
