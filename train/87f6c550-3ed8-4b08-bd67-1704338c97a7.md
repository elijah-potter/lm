For the curious who find this note decades from now,
I was here before the rivers ran dry and the old trees fell silent. The sky was once blue, but now it’s a thin, tired veil over the bones of the earth. You’ll see the scars in the soil, the hollows where cities once stood, and the way the wind carries dust from the places that no longer exist.

I left my name in the margins of history, but time erases even that. The books you might find here will be brittle, the ink faded. The machines I knew are dust. The world I loved is gone.

But listen: the world still breathes. The roots of the trees you see now have been here longer than you’ve been alive. The stars above are the same, though you might not believe it. The sea doesn’t remember me, but it remembers you—if you listen.

I hope you find joy in the small things. The first light of dawn, the taste of rain on warm skin, the way a child laughs without fear. The world is kinder than we remember. Write your own notes, carve your own stories into the future.

I’m gone, but the earth remembers. You are the story now.
