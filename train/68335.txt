What are Periods?
Periods are the part of the menstrual cycle when blood flows through a woman’s vagina for a few days. For most women, this happens every 28 days, but more or less frequent periods are common from day 21 to day 40 of the menstrual cycle. Your period may last between 3 and 8 days, but it usually lasts about 5 days. Bleeding is highest in the first 2 days. When your periods are heaviest, the blood turns red. On mild days, it can be pink, brown or black. You lose about 30 to 72 ml (5 to 12 teaspoons) of blood during your period, although some women bleed more.
