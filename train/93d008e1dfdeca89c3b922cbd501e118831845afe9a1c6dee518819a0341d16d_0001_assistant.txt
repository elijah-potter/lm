The famous musical piece "Moonlight Sonata" is a solo piano piece composed by Ludwig van Beethoven. It's also referred to as Piano Sonata No. 14 in C-sharp Minor, Op. 27, No. 2. Beethoven finished the composition in 1801, published it the following year, and personally premiered it despite his deteriorating hearing. The nickname "Moonlight Sonata" originated in the 1830s, when German Romantic poet Ludwig Rellstab published a review comparing the first movement to a boat floating in the moonlight on Switzerland's Lake Lucerne. Beethoven dedicated this work to Countess Giulietta Guicciardi, a young aristocrat who briefly studied under him.
