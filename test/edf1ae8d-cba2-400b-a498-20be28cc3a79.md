**New Haven’s Historic Bridge Collapse Sparks Emergency Response**

Police and emergency crews are evacuating residents along the **Old Riverfront Promenade** following the early-morning collapse of the **Marble Arch Bridge** at **Dockside Boulevard**. Witnesses report a section of the pedestrian bridge—used daily by commuters and tourists—caved in around 04:15 AM, sending debris cascading onto the adjacent riverbank. The bridge, dating back to the 1930s, was deemed structurally unsound after decades of underfunded repairs and rising stormwater erosion along the Thames. Emergency workers confirm no fatalities but report minor injuries to a child and two elderly individuals who were crossing when the collapse occurred.

Authorities have cordoned off the area, and the **New Haven Maritime Authority** has temporarily rerouted all river traffic to the **Canal Wharf Terminal** to prevent further disruptions. The city’s **Department of Public Works** has activated a 48-hour emergency fund, while engineers from **Midwest Bridge Solutions** are en route to assess the damage. Residents along **Bay Street and the Maritime District** are advised to avoid the area until further notice.

The collapse has reignited debates over infrastructure funding, with local activists calling for an immediate audit of city spending on "prioritized" projects like the **New Haven Convention Center Expansion**—which began construction last year despite protests from preservationists. Mayor **Lena Chen** has issued a statement urging patience, stating, *"This is a temporary setback, but we’re taking immediate action to restore safety and stability."* Meanwhile, the **Historic Preservation Commission** has warned that if the bridge’s replacement takes longer than expected, the city may lose a key tourist attraction and cultural landmark.

No arrests have been made, but investigators are probing whether the collapse could have been prevented with routine maintenance. The National Weather Service has issued a **wind advisory** for the area due to gusts expected to exceed 30 mph, further complicating rescue efforts.
