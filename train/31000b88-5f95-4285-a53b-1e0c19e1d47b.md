The train’s rattling wheels groaned as it pulled into the station, its doors hissing open with a sound like a dying animal. The platform was crowded with figures in hurried movements—some rushing to catch the next train, others standing in clusters, whispering, exchanging glances. She stepped out, her coat collar turned up against the damp November air, her breath fogging slightly in the cold. The clock on the wall read **5:08 PM**, and she had only just made it.

Her phone buzzed in her pocket—another notification: *"Late. Please accept."*
She exhaled sharply, fingers tightening around her bag. The message was from her boss, but she didn’t look at it. Instead, she checked her watch again: **5:09 PM**. Her stomach dropped. Then, her phone chimed. A reply: *"Good. I’ll call you when I get there."*

She laughed—just once, short and relieved—and turned toward the office, her shoes crunching over gravel. The weight of the day lifted. She had made it.
