The library’s old clock ticked slowly, its hands creeping toward midnight like shadows of forgotten stories. Twelve-year-old Mira clutched her backpack tighter, her fingers digging into the straps as she stepped outside. The cold November wind bit at her cheeks, but it was better than the way her stomach twisted every time she thought about tomorrow. The school’s new art project required her to draw a self-portrait—*realistically*, the teacher insisted—but Mira knew she’d fail. Her sketches were always messy, her lines uneven, her heart too big for the page.

Her phone buzzed. A text from her best friend, Jake: *"You’re late. The group already picked the weirdest spot on campus. Meet me at the old oak tree."*
Mira groaned. The oak tree was where everyone gathered to gossip, where the weird kids hung out, where no one expected her to be. But she wasn’t the kind of kid who could sit out. Not when she had something to prove.

The oak tree stood at the edge of the campus, its branches twisted like ancient arms. The group was already there—Lena, the class clown, and Priya, the science whiz who never talked about anything but experiments. They were mid-laugh, arms crossed, when Mira arrived.

"Alright, newbie," Lena smirked. "You gonna draw yourself or just sit there like a statue?"

Mira swallowed. She’d spent hours practicing in the dark, her hands shaking as she traced her features—her messy hair, her round glasses, the way her fingers curled into fists when she was nervous. But the teacher wanted *realistic*. She wanted to see the world through her eyes, not just her face.

Priya nudged her. "You know what we’re doing? We’re going to make it *better*."

They grabbed sketchbooks from Mira’s bag and set up in the shade of the oak. Lena grabbed a water bottle, and Jake tossed her a pencil sharpener. Mira hesitated, but then, with a deep breath, she grabbed her own notebook.

"Start with your hands," Priya said. "Show us the tension. The way you grip a pencil."

Mira did as she was told, her fingers trembling as she drew them—her left hand open, her right clenched. Then Priya moved to her face. "Now, eyes. How do they move? Do you squint? Blink? *Think*."

Mira closed her eyes, imagining them. She saw the way her lashes flickered when she was scared, the way her eyebrows dipped when she was thinking. She wrote in her notebook: *"They remember everything."*

Lena chuckled. "You’re gonna do good. It’s like you’re drawing *yourself* inside out."

The hours blurred. Mira’s hands didn’t shake as much. She didn’t just draw her face—she drew her *mind*. The way she felt when she was laughing, the way she felt when she was scared. The way she felt when she looked at herself in the mirror and thought, *I’m not enough.*

When the bell rang, the class was over. But Mira’s sketchbook was still open. The drawing she’d made was a mess—her hands were jagged, her lines uneven—but for the first time, it felt *real*. It wasn’t just a face. It was her.

The teacher walked in, her eyes scanning the room. Mira handed her the sketchbook, her heart pounding. The teacher studied it for a long moment before handing it back. "This is… interesting," she said. "You’ve captured more than just your appearance."

Mira’s throat tightened. She didn’t know what to say.

Later, at lunch, Jake nudged her. "So? What did they think?"

Mira hesitated. "They said it was… different."

Lena raised an eyebrow. "Different how?"

Priya smirked. "It’s like you didn’t just draw your face. You drew your *soul*."

Mira looked down at her hands. She’d never thought of herself that way. But as she took a bite of her sandwich, she realized something: maybe that was exactly what mattered.

The next day, when the class drew their portraits, Mira’s sketchbook was on the table. The teacher didn’t comment on it. The other students didn’t either. But Mira didn’t care. She had a drawing that showed the world a little bit more than she did. And that was enough.
