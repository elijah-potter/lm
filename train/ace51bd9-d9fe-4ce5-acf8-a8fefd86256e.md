**Ending A:**
In the quiet village of Eldermere, where the mist curled like smoke over ancient oak trees, there lived a boy named Kael. One evening, as he wandered past the crumbling ruins of the old library, he found a half-buried journal. The pages were filled with equations that defied logic—numbers that moved without calculation, words that rearranged themselves. Inside was a map to a door hidden behind the library’s west wall. Kael followed it, emerging into a chamber where time itself seemed to unravel. A woman with eyes like polished obsidian stood before him. “You’ve spent your life chasing shadows,” she said. “Now choose: take the key, or let it stay buried.” He picked the key. When he returned to Eldermere, the village had changed—time had looped, but Kael’s actions had rewritten the past. The journal’s equations now appeared in the walls as living text. Eldermere thrived, not because of magic, but because its people had learned to see the future in the present.

**Ending B:**
Kael did not follow the map. Instead, he returned to the library’s east corner, where a single candle flickered in a forgotten niche. The journal’s pages, though sealed, pulsed faintly. He pressed his palm to the cover, and the ink rippled. A voice whispered: *“The past is a door you’ve walked through twice.”* The room dissolved into whispers of voices he recognized—his own, but younger, and those of the villagers who had vanished years before. He chose to listen. The village’s history unfolded: the fire that burned the library, the betrayal that silenced the librarian, the war that left only ruins. The key was never needed. The truth was the only thing that could have saved them. When the voices faded, the journal was gone. The village remembered the fire not as tragedy, but as a lesson. The ruins stood as a monument to choices made, not fate.
