The clock’s hands crawled like sluggish fingers across the ceiling, each second a distant echo of the moment—one of those pauses where time itself stretches thin, where the breath between inhale and exhale feels like a lifetime. The silence wasn’t empty; it was thick with the weight of what was about to be said, the way the air had grown heavier, the way the silence had become the thing being measured. Outside, the wind howled through the trees, a relentless reminder that time was still ticking, but the world kept moving—unaware, indifferent. In this space, time was a river that had slowed to a crawl, the water so still you could almost see the ripples of the past and future beneath the surface, suspended in the present’s quiet tension. The hands on the clock were not moving; they were standing still, frozen in the act of counting down a moment that had become an eternity, not because it was long, but because it was so vivid, so charged with meaning, that it felt like it had always been there, waiting. The door creaked open, and the world rushed back into motion, leaving behind only the echo of what had been—time stretched, compressed, and in that moment, it had been both endless and finite, a paradox wrapped in stillness.
