You shouldn’t be here, but you were. The air smelled of damp earth and rust—old, forgotten. The flickering glow of a single bulb above the cracked door cast long shadows across the walls, their edges blurred by time. The sound of a key turning in the lock echoed, sharp and sudden, like a warning. You turned, your breath fogging in the cold, and saw her: a woman in a coat too dark for this season, her face half-hidden beneath a scarf. She stepped into the dim light, her eyes sharp, assessing. "You’re not supposed to be here," she murmured, her voice low and measured. The door clicked shut behind her, sealing you in with the weight of what came next. The floor beneath your feet creaked—something was moving.
