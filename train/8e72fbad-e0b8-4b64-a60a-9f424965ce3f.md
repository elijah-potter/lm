The barren hills now bear jagged, dark ridges where once verdant slopes curled toward the valley. The cracked earth glistens with mineral veins reflecting pale, flickering light—sunset or distant storm—through thin, ashen clouds. A frozen lake sits in the hollow, its surface a fractured mirror of the sky, its edges lined with skeletal trees whose branches twist like dying serpents. The wind carries the scent of damp soil and smoldering peat, the only life thriving in the cracked roots of a few hardy shrubs. The air hums with the low, rhythmic pulse of distant machinery, the ghost of industry now buried beneath the land.
