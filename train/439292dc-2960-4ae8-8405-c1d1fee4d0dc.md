The ruins of **Verginia**, a forgotten coastal town in the Blackwood Marshes, where the salt wind still carries whispers of the past. The crumbling stone church, its bell still cracked and silent, stands half-swallowed by reeds. The old lighthouse, its lantern glass long shattered, marks a sunken shipwreck where the tide pulls at bones.

Locals say the drowned priest who once blessed the docks still walks the shore at midnight, his cloak woven from fog. The **Old Well** near the cemetery yields water that tastes like iron, but only those who drink from it once hear the same name repeated in their dreams—**Elias Vey**, a sailor who vanished in the 1898 storm. The only thing left is the **Wreck of the *Marina Rosa***, its hull buried under silt, its cargo of lead and parchment now part of the marsh’s floor.

Some say the drowned men still row in the shallows, heading toward the **Blackwood’s hidden island**, where the trees grow too tall, their leaves never fall. Others claim the town’s last survivor, **Martha Hargrove**, who lives in a ramshackle cottage, knows more than she lets on. But no one dares ask. The marsh keeps its secrets.
