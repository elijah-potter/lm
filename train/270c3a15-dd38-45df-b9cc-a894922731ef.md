A 19th-century European ballroom, the air thick with polish and high society whispers. The dancers pirouette in perfect, practiced arcs, their movements synchronized to the same rhythm of the waltz. A young woman, her dress embroidered with silver thread, steps onto the floor. She looks around—her eyes flicking between the mirrored walls, the expectant faces of her companions. The music swells; she takes a single, hesitant step forward. The moment holds still. Then, with a sudden, unguarded breath, she freezes. The orchestra’s crescendo fades into the silence. Her fingers tighten around her fan. She is no longer the woman in the mirror—she is the girl from the attic who dreamed of this moment, who whispered to the ceiling for years, *"I will dance perfectly."* But now, the weight of it all presses down. The rehearsed smile, the practiced bow, the way her pulse thumps like a metronome counting down to disaster. She steps back into the crowd, the ballroom swallowing her whole.
