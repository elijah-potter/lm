I remember the way the old clock tower’s pendulum swayed,
time measured in slow, deliberate ticks—
the way the wind carried whispers from the alley behind the bakery,
how the scent of fresh bread mingled with the salt of the sea.

I remember the way my grandmother’s hands trembled
as she folded the cloth napkins,
how she always tied the corners just so—
a secret ritual, a quiet promise.

I remember the sound of her voice,
the way she’d say, *"Don’t let the past hold you tight, child,"*
as she pressed a silver locket into my palm—
the chain too short, the inside empty.

I remember the first time I touched it,
my fingers tracing the cold metal,
the weight of something lost within.

I remember the way the light caught the cracks in the glass,
how the reflection of the window blurred into a thousand faces—
mine, hers, and the faces of those who came before.

I remember the way the clock tower’s chime split the air,
a sound that wasn’t just time—
but the weight of all the moments that had passed,
and the ones yet to come.

I think the past is not a door we leave behind,
but a wall we build with our own hands—
some cracks let in the light,
others trap the echoes forever.

I think the locket is still there,
somewhere in the hollow of my chest,
a promise to keep the weight
as small as I can let it be.
