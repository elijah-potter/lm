A dim lamp flickered against the dusty wall as **Lena** sat cross-legged on the floor, her fingers tracing the worn spine of a book she hadn’t opened in months. The air smelled of old paper and something faintly metallic—her mother’s perfume, half-melted in a forgotten drawer. Across the room, **Daniel** leaned against the kitchen counter, his sleeves rolled up, knuckles white around the handle of a half-drunk beer bottle. He had been avoiding eye contact since he walked in, his usual sharp wit replaced by a quiet tension that felt like a physical weight.

Then—**the silence**. The kind that doesn’t need filling. Lena’s gaze flicked up just as Daniel’s stopped mid-bite of his sandwich, his expression shifting from guarded to something raw. His words came slow, deliberate: *"You were always the one who knew what I was thinking before I said it."* She exhaled, shoulders dropping, and for the first time in years, she didn’t pretend. Instead, she reached across the gap and touched his arm—just once, like a question. He didn’t pull away. Instead, his thumb brushed hers, then her wrist, and the heat of it seared through the years of unspoken things.

The book slipped from her fingers. It hit the floor with a soft *thud*. Neither moved. The other’s pulse, visible against their skin, was the only sound. Then Daniel laughed—a sound like wind through dry leaves—and Lena smiled, too, because for the first time, she didn’t have to guess.
