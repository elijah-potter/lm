## Explain Like I'm Five: Why the Sky is Blue

Okay, imagine you have a big box of crayons, right? And inside are *all* the colors: red, yellow, green, blue, purple, and so on. Sunlight is kind of like that box of crayons – it’s made up of all those colors mixed together!

Now, imagine you’re playing with those crayons, and you’re bouncing them off a bumpy wall. Some colors, like red and orange, bounce straight forward. But the blue and purple crayons? They bounce *everywhere*! They bounce to the left, to the right, up and down – all over the place!

The sky is like that bumpy wall. Sunlight travels all the way from the sun to Earth. When it gets here, it bumps into tiny bits of air – like little bouncy castles floating in the sky.

The blue and purple colors in sunlight are special. They bounce around *much* more than the other colors. They bounce off those little bits of air and spread all over the sky. That's why when you look up, you see blue!

You might be thinking, "But isn't purple in the sky too?" You’re right to ask! Purple *is* there, but our eyes aren't as good at seeing purple as they are at seeing blue. Plus, the air changes the purple a little bit, so we mostly see blue.

Sometimes, when the sun is setting, it looks orange or red. That’s because the sunlight has to travel through *more* air to reach us. All the blue light has already bounced away, leaving mostly the orange and red colors to reach our eyes.

So, the sky is blue because the blue color in sunlight bounces around the air more than the other colors, spreading it everywhere we look! It’s like a giant game of bouncy crayons in the sky!
