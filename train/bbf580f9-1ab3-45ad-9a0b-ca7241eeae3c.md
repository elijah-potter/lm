The library’s fluorescent hum was a soft, rhythmic pulse when she closed the door behind her. The stack of books she’d carried in—*The Silent Years*, *Echoes in the Dark*—sat open on the table. Her fingers traced the spine of *Silence as a Weapon*, her final choice.

The clock on the wall read 11:47. Outside, the city’s hum had thinned to a distant, muffled roar. She exhaled, slow and deliberate, letting the breath out through her nose, like exhaling smoke.

“No more,” she murmured, pressing the book to her chest. The pages were damp with her tears. She had spent years collecting words to fight the noise—the chatter, the judgments, the endless stories of how she should *feel*. But here, in this room, she had chosen silence.

She leaned back in the chair, spine stiff, and let her gaze drift over the shelves. Some books called to her. Some whispered. She didn’t answer. The only sound now was the faintest crackle of the heater, and the sound of her own breathing, steady and unbroken.

Outside, the world kept turning. But she had chosen a pause.
