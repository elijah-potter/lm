Golden dawn cascades over rolling hills, painting each ridge in amber and copper hues. The sky, a molten canvas, bleeds into the horizon where the earth’s shadow begins, leaving the eastern sky ablaze with streaks of violet and scarlet. Twilight whispers through the trees, their leaves shimmering like scattered fireflies—some catching the fading light, others absorbing it into deep emerald shadows. The air hums with a soft, electric glow, the faintest trace of phosphorescence clinging to dew-damp grass. Moonlight, pale and ghostly, weaves through the clouds like silk, casting long, wavering fingers across moss-covered stones and whispering secrets into still pools. At dusk, the lake reflects not its own depths but the sky’s dying fire, rippling like liquid starlight.
