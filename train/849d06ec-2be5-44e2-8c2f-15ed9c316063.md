The first snow fell in March. I was standing on the sidewalk, watching the old woman with the cane shuffle past, her breath curling in the cold air like smoke. She said, *"You’re not the first to say this."* That was the moment I realized she was right.

I had been telling people I was fine, that the weight of the world didn’t press too hard. But the truth was simpler: I was tired of pretending. I had spent years hiding a quiet ache behind jokes and laughter, but the night I saw her, I stopped. The truth was not about suffering—it was about the small, stubborn truth that I had chosen to keep moving, even when it hurt. And maybe, just maybe, that was the real confession.
