A **one-time protocol** was enacted at corporate HQ: every Friday afternoon, all employees were required to toss a single coin into the company’s central fountain. The act, dubbed **"Friday Flips,"** was meant to symbolize renewal and closure. Employees would pause, exchange glances, and toss—no explanations, no debates. The rule was simple: **one coin, one toss, no exceptions**.

For months, it became a routine—until it didn’t. The fountain dried up in summer. A new manager, unaware of the tradition, ordered it removed. The next Friday, no one tossed. The last coin fell into the empty basin, and the ritual died. No one missed it. No one cared. It was just another rule that vanished like smoke.

**End.**
