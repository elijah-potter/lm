**The Last Light**

The clock tower’s hands stretched thin like spider legs against the sky, casting long shadows across the cobblestones. The city had forgotten how to breathe beneath its weight of forgotten promises—glass windows cracked like dried husks, their reflections no longer mirrors of life but echoes of a time that had slipped through the cracks.

In the alley beneath the crumbling archway, a single lantern flickered, its flame a dying ember against the damp stone. It was the last light in this quarter where the dead still lingered in the hollows of the air. The streetlamps had been turned off long ago, not by choice, but by the slow, creeping silence that had swallowed everything else.

At the heart of the alley stood an old man, his hands wrapped around a rusted camera—his last possession. He had taken this photograph not for fame, not for memory, but because the world had forgotten how to see. The lens was fogged with time, the film blackened at the edges, but the moment captured within it was pure.

He stepped forward, the camera trembling in his grip. The shutter clicked. The light from the lantern flared—just for an instant—before the world around him dissolved into darkness. The image, if it could be saved, would show the lantern’s glow against the skeletal remains of the city, a single, perfect light against endless night.

The man exhaled, his breath a ghost against the cold air. The film was exposed. The world had finally learned to listen.
