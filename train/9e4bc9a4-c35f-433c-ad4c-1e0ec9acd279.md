The iterative refinement loop: A project begins with a vague goal, progresses through phases of partial completion, and encounters unanticipated dependencies or constraints that force iterative adjustments. Milestones are met only to reveal new gaps or requirements, forcing rework. The cycle repeats indefinitely, with each iteration consuming resources and time, yet the final product remains perpetually unfinished. Stakeholders and developers oscillate between optimism about closure and despair over the unceasing need for improvement, creating a feedback loop where "done" is never truly achieved.

Example: Building a "perfect" web app where new features, security patches, and user feedback constantly demand revisions. Documentation lags behind code, and the definition of "done" shifts with each iteration. The system remains a work-in-progress, with only a fraction of the original scope ever realized. The illusion of completion is maintained only by incremental progress and a shifting baseline.
