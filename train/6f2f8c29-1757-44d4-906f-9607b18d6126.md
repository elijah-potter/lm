The rain fell in sheets that night, softening the pavement where the old oak tree stood, its roots twisted deep in the earth. Inside, the house was warm, the fire crackling low, its embers casting long shadows across the walls. A single candle flickered in the corner, its light barely reaching the half-open window.

She sat by the hearth, fingers tracing the grain of an old book she’d given him years ago—*"The Silent Years"*—a collection of poems she’d written when they first met. He had found it later, tucked inside a drawer, the spine worn smooth from use. She knew the moment he opened it that he would understand.

His voice was quiet, just above a whisper. "I promised you this."

She looked at him, her expression unreadable. He reached out, pulling her close. "I didn’t mean to wait. But I never stopped thinking about it."

The words hung between them, simple and true. Outside, the wind sighed through the leaves, a reminder of time passing. But inside, the fire burned steady, and the candle’s flame flickered—unchanged, unbroken. The promise was kept.
