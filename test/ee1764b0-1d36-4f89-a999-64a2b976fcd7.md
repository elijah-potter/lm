**Dr. Eleanor Whitmore**
*Founder & CEO of NeoGen Solutions*

Eleanor Whitmore is a visionary entrepreneur and innovator, known for her pioneering contributions to sustainable technology and urban regeneration. With a passion for merging cutting-edge science with community-driven solutions, she founded NeoGen Solutions in 2018, transforming waste management through AI-driven recycling systems. Whitmore’s work has earned her widespread recognition, including the prestigious *Innovation Leadership Award* from the Global Sustainability Forum in 2020.

Prior to her entrepreneurial ventures, Whitmore earned a PhD in Environmental Engineering from MIT, specializing in renewable energy integration. She served as a professor at Harvard University for five years, where she taught courses on circular economy design and policy. Whitmore’s academic research focused on reducing carbon footprints through advanced materials science, though her most impactful work lies in scalable, community-based solutions.

Beyond her professional achievements, Whitmore is a dedicated advocate for education reform, particularly in STEM disciplines for underserved youth. She co-founded the *Whitmore STEM Initiative*, a nonprofit that provides free coding and engineering workshops in low-income neighborhoods across the U.S. Her commitment to accessibility and equity has been widely celebrated, though her public persona remains relatively low-key, avoiding media scrutiny to focus on hands-on leadership.

Whitmore’s leadership style emphasizes collaboration, prioritizing partnerships with local governments, nonprofits, and private sector innovators. Her strategic approach to scaling projects has positioned NeoGen as a leader in the green tech sector, though she has not disclosed details about its most advanced proprietary technologies—consistent with her preference for transparency over secrecy.

Currently, Whitmore resides in Seattle, where she oversees NeoGen’s expansion into European markets while continuing to mentor emerging entrepreneurs through the *Whitmore Fellowship*. Her 2023 TED Talk, *"Reimagining Waste as a Resource,"* remains one of the most viewed presentations in the sustainability space, though her speaking engagements are rare due to her focus on operational execution.
