The air thickened, heavy with the hum of distant machinery. A child’s laughter echoed, sharp and unnaturally clear, as if heard through a tunnel. The mother turned, her breath shallow—each exhale a slow, deliberate motion, like watching a clock tick.

A shadow stretched across the floor, elongated, as if the world itself was bending. The clock on the wall—*three minutes past noon*—ticked with agonizing slowness, each second dragging like a river of syrup. The grandfather clock in the corner groaned, its pendulum swaying in a deliberate, hypnotic swing.

The father knelt, his fingers tracing the edge of the table where the child had fallen. The world seemed to pause—every sound, every breath, every heartbeat—frozen in this moment. The air smelled of dust and ozone, the scent of a storm waiting to unfold.

A scream split the silence, but it took *hours* for the mother to react. Her hand trembled as she reached for her child, the distance between them seeming to stretch across an ocean. The world kept moving, but nothing felt real.
