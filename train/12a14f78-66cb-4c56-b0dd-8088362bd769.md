## Proposal: Revitalizing Oakhaven Ravine – A Climate-Resilient Community Oasis

**Executive Summary:**

The City of Meadowbrook faces increasing challenges from extreme weather events, including intensified rainfall and rising summer temperatures. Oakhaven Ravine, a currently underutilized and degraded public space bordering the Elmwood and Riverside neighborhoods, presents a unique opportunity to address these concerns while simultaneously fostering community connection and enhancing ecological health. This proposal outlines a comprehensive plan to transform the ravine into a climate-resilient community asset, incorporating stormwater management infrastructure, native plantings, accessible pathways, and a dedicated community garden. The project, spearheaded by the Friends of Meadowbrook Parks in partnership with the Meadowbrook Soil and Water Conservation District and Elmwood Neighborhood Association, directly aligns with the City’s Climate Action Plan and prioritizes equitable access to green space for historically underserved residents. We request $75,000 in municipal funding, matched by $25,000 in in-kind contributions and volunteer labor, to initiate this vital project and build a more resilient and vibrant Meadowbrook.

**Project Plan & Timeline:**

The Oakhaven Ravine Revitalization project is structured across four distinct phases, each with defined responsibilities and deliverables.

**Phase 1: Assessment and Design (Months 1-3)**

* **Responsible Partners:** Meadowbrook Soil and Water Conservation District (MSWCD), Elmwood Neighborhood Association (ENA), Friends of Meadowbrook Parks (FMP)
* **Deliverables:** Detailed site assessment report (MSWCD), community needs assessment report (ENA), preliminary design plans incorporating stormwater management and accessibility features (MSWCD), final design plans approved by City Planning Department.
* **Activities:** Conduct hydrological survey, soil analysis, and topographical mapping. Host two community workshops to gather input on design preferences and desired amenities. Present preliminary designs to City Planning Department for feedback.

**Phase 2: Infrastructure Development (Months 3-6)**

* **Responsible Partners:** FMP, City Parks Department (CPD), contracted landscape contractor.
* **Deliverables:** Installation of bio-swales and rain gardens for stormwater management, construction of accessible pathways using permeable paving materials, erosion control measures implemented.
* **Activities:** Secure necessary construction permits. Coordinate with CPD for utility locates and infrastructure support. Manage contracted labor and ensure adherence to environmental best practices.

**Phase 3: Planting and Landscaping (Months 6-9)**

* **Responsible Partners:** FMP, MSWCD, volunteer groups.
* **Deliverables:** Establishment of native plant communities, creation of a community garden with raised beds, installation of interpretive signage detailing ecological benefits.
* **Activities:** Procure native plant materials from local nurseries. Organize volunteer planting days. Develop and install educational signage explaining the ravine’s ecological significance and stormwater management features.

**Phase 4: Community Engagement & Maintenance (Months 9-12)**

* **Responsible Partners:** ENA, FMP, MSWCD.
* **Deliverables:** Establishment of a “Friends of Oakhaven Ravine” stewardship group, creation of a maintenance schedule, ongoing community programming focused on ecological education and gardening skills.
* **Activities:** Host introductory workshops for the stewardship group. Develop and distribute a detailed maintenance schedule. Organize seasonal events, such as guided nature walks and gardening demonstrations.

**Budget:**

The total project cost is estimated at $100,000. We request $75,000 in municipal funding, supplemented by $25,000 in in-kind contributions and volunteer labor.

* **Materials:** $45,000
    * Native Plants and Seeds: $15,000
    * Permeable Paving Materials: $12,000
    * Bio-swale and Rain Garden Construction Materials: $8,000
    * Raised Garden Bed Lumber and Soil: $5,000
    * Interpretive Signage: $5,000
* **Labor:** $35,000 (Contracted Landscape Contractor – 50% of total labor cost)
* **In-Kind Contributions (Valued at $25,000):**
    * Volunteer Labor (estimated 500 hours @ $25/hour): $12,500
    * MSWCD Technical Assistance (hydrological survey, soil analysis): $7,500
    * ENA Event Coordination and Community Outreach: $5,000
* **Contingency (5% of total project cost):** $5,000. This fund will address unforeseen expenses related to material price fluctuations, weather delays, or unexpected site conditions.

**Community Engagement & Equity Impacts:**

This project prioritizes equitable access to green space and actively engages the Elmwood and Riverside communities. Two community workshops during the design phase ensured that resident voices shaped the project’s direction. The community garden will provide opportunities for residents to grow their own food, addressing food insecurity and promoting healthy eating habits. Raised garden beds will ensure accessibility for individuals with mobility challenges. The project’s educational programming will focus on environmental stewardship and sustainable practices, empowering residents to become active participants in caring for their local environment. The Elmwood Neighborhood Association will serve as a key liaison, ensuring that the project’s benefits are distributed equitably within the community.

**Risk Mitigation:**

Potential risks include inclement weather impacting construction timelines, fluctuations in material costs, and volunteer attrition. To mitigate these risks, we will incorporate buffer time into the project schedule, secure contracts with fixed pricing whenever possible, and cultivate strong relationships with volunteer groups. A contingency fund of $5,000 will provide a financial safety net for unforeseen expenses.  The MSWCD’s expertise in hydrological assessments and erosion control will minimize environmental risks.

**Post-Grant Sustainability:**

Long-term sustainability will be ensured through the establishment of the “Friends of Oakhaven Ravine” stewardship group. This group will be responsible for ongoing maintenance, fundraising, and community programming. The MSWCD will provide technical support and training. The City Parks Department will provide essential infrastructure maintenance. A detailed maintenance schedule will be developed and distributed to volunteers and city staff.

**Call to Action:**

The Oakhaven Ravine Revitalization project represents a transformative opportunity to enhance the resilience and vibrancy of Meadowbrook. By investing in this project, the City will demonstrate its commitment to climate action, equitable access to green space, and community engagement. We urge you to approve our request for $75,000 in municipal funding and join us in creating a lasting legacy for generations to come.
