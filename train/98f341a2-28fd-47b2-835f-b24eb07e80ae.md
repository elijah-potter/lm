The garden was quiet when I first decided to stay. The air smelled of damp earth and forgotten blooms, the kind of scent that clung to your fingers like memory. I’d come here for the peace, for the way the time stretched thin, stretched *right*, until even the weight of the past felt lighter.

I sat on the old stone bench, my hands resting on the worn surface, and let the words spill out before I could stop them. *"I will write this. I will finish it."* The promise was easy then, because the words were already there, half-formed in the dark, like something half-buried in the dirt.

But then the rain came.

Not the soft, steady drizzle of summer, but the kind that unravels everything—lightning splits the sky, the earth groans beneath the weight of it, and for a moment, the world stands still. I watched as the drops fell in sharp, deliberate strokes, as if the heavens themselves were correcting a draft. My mind had been a sketch, a half-thought-out idea, but the rain made it precise. It made me see.

I should have gone. The night was too long, the fire too small, the silence too vast. But I stayed because the words had already begun to sing, and the rain was the only thing that could keep them from drowning.

Now, as I sit here, the rain still falling, I realize I didn’t mean to stay. I meant to *finish*. But the finish was never the point. The point was the writing itself, the way the words stretched and shifted, the way they refused to stay still. The rain gave them life, made them breathe.

So here I am, still. Not because I wanted to, but because the story had other plans. The story always does.
