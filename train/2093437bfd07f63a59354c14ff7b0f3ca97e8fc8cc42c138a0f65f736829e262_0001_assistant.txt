It's a daring move to name your first album Legacy, but in 2013 Kavain Space earned the right. The producer out of Chicago, known by his stage name RP Boo, was a legend in his hometown, he spent the mid-90s DJing for the House-O-Matics dance crew during parties spanning the South Side. In 1997, he released "Baby Come On," which many see as the first point of the hectic footwork style. The track features rhythmic drums churning at around 160 bpm, the favored tempo of producers of footwork, and an Ol' Dirty Bastard sample chopped and looped through the duration of the song. Boo released his mixtape Dude Off 59th Street in 2007 and supplied two tracks to Planet Mu's vital 2010 collection Bangs & Works Vol.1 (A Chicago Footwork Compilation). Legacy, despite being an archival release, was a statement of purpose, a document of his eccentric ability to craft rough vocal taunts, rough pop hits, and light drums into club cuts from outer space.

A decade later comes the release of Legacy Vol. 2, a worthy sequel. It returns to the beginning of the 2000s, collecting tracks recorded between 2002 and 2007. Though Boo later began making tracks in Ableton, he made all of his early work with just an Akai MPC and Roland R-70 drum machine, he leaves the song's backbones out in the open, which can leave an uneasy feeling. Some loops sway wildly, ending with random clicks of a digital artifact; it can sound like the samples are being performed live. Clashing sounds are set against each other on purpose like the horror-movie hum of "Live and Let Die" sample of vocals in the opener "Eraser," and getting used to the chaotic rhythms takes patience. Although the music arose in Chicago, footwork breaks down the genre's metronomic, toned-down template, the traditional heartbeat doesn't show up in most cases.
