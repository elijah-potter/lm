In the dim glow of a dying fire, the old lantern flickered like a dying ember. Elias adjusted the strap of his worn satchel, fingers tracing the faded map inside—one he’d kept since he was twelve. The map was his secret, hidden beneath his bed, but tonight, he’d taken it out.

"Ten years," he muttered to the empty room, "and I’ve only lied half a dozen times. The rest were half-truths, stolen glances, and promises I never meant to keep."

His breath fogged the air. "I don’t know why I’m telling you this. Maybe because you’re the only one who’s ever seen me cry without laughing first." He turned to the mirror, where his reflection stared back—eyes hollow, a scar running from his temple to his cheek. "The truth is, I didn’t steal that coin from the butcher’s boy. I *borrowed* it. And I haven’t paid him back. Not yet."

The lantern’s flame swallowed his words whole, but the words stayed. Somewhere, a clock ticked. Somewhere, a child laughed.
