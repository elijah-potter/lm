The clock struck midnight, and the café’s neon hum faded into the static hum of the city. I sat at the empty table, fingers tracing the rim of my empty coffee cup. Outside, the rain blurred the streetlights into streaks of silver. The third time.

I had seen it three nights running: the way the streetlamps cast shadows that moved in sync with the cars passing beneath them. Not the flicker of a bulb—something deeper. The trees along the boulevard swayed in time with the wind, though there was no breeze. And then there was the woman at the bus stop, her reflection in the puddles matching the rhythm of her steps as she crossed the road.

I exhaled, the breath fogging the glass. The pattern wasn’t just a coincidence. It was a signal. And if the city was sending it, it wasn’t for me.

I picked up my phone. The screen lit up with a message I hadn’t sent:

**"The clock is not ticking."**
