As if it had chosen to sail away on its own.

Their breath came in shallow gasps, the weight of the past pressing down like a second skin. They turned, the memory of their mother’s voice still echoing in their mind: *"You think you’re the only one who’s afraid?"*

The wind howled through the trees, carrying the sound of waves crashing too far away. Inside, the cabin felt like a tomb—walls too thick to muffle their own thoughts. The conflict wasn’t the boat. It was the question that gnawed at them: *What if they’d been right all along?* What if the fear wasn’t theirs, but a shadow they’d carried for years, a burden too heavy to drop?

They exhaled slowly, the scent of damp wood and salt mixing with the sharp tang of their own regret. The dock creaked beneath them, a silent judgment. Somewhere, a seagull cried. No answer.
