## TIL The "Missing Link" in Penguin Evolution Was Discovered in a Museum Drawer

Today I learned that a crucial piece of evidence supporting the evolutionary link between penguins and their extinct, toothed ancestors was literally sitting in a museum drawer for over a century. It's a remarkably well-preserved fossil of *Waimanu seabirds*, discovered in 1983 but initially misidentified.

The *Waimanu* fossils, unearthed in the Capleston Greenstone Belt of New Zealand’s South Island, date back roughly 62 million years – making them contemporaries of the dinosaurs. For decades, scientists believed they were simply another type of early bird. However, a recent re-examination of the skull morphology by researchers at Massey University revealed a unique, previously unrecognized feature: a small groove on the lower jaw, strongly indicative of a connection to modern penguins. This groove, absent in other early birds, suggests the *Waimanu* possessed a specialized feeding mechanism similar to that of penguins, supporting the theory that penguins evolved from a lineage of toothed seabirds.

What makes this discovery particularly fascinating is the serendipity of its rediscovery. The fossil had been cataloged and stored, its significance overlooked until a fresh perspective and advanced imaging techniques allowed for a more detailed analysis. It underscores the potential for even well-established scientific collections to yield new insights with continued scrutiny and technological advancement.
