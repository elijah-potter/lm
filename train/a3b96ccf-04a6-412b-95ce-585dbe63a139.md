In the hollowed-out core of the library, where the air smelled of aged parchment and forgotten coffee, I sat cross-legged on the worn carpet, tracing the edges of a single, yellowed page with my finger. The ink had faded to a whisper, but beneath it lay something unmistakable—a sequence. Each time the wind rattled the bookshelves, it wasn’t just noise. It was a countdown, a rhythm that had begun the moment I first stepped through the door.

I traced the numbers in the margins: 47, 32, 19, 7. They weren’t random. They matched the dates of the missing volumes—*The Alchemist’s Codex*, *Dover’s Last Transmission*, *The Silent Choir’s Hymn*—each lost in the last decade. The librarian’s granddaughter had once told me, *"Patterns aren’t just seen, they’re *listened* to."* I had dismissed it as superstition, until the books vanished in perfect, repeating intervals.

Now, the shelf where *The Last Light* should have been stood empty. The numbers had moved on: 11, 5, 2. The wind sighed through the gaps in the floorboards like a breath counting down. I exhaled slowly, my mind unraveling the thread. The first step was always the hardest, but the pattern was unmistakable. The second was waiting.
