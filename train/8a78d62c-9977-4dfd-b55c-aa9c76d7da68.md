The air hummed with unspoken tension as they sat across from each other in the dimly lit café, the coffee cups between them glistening with condensation. The first words had been tentative, hesitant—like touching a hot stove. Then the second, sharper. Now, their voices wove through the silence like spiders' threads, tangled and never quite unraveling.

*"You never answer,"* she said, her fingers tracing the rim of her cup, *"when I ask about the night you left."*

*"I don’t remember,"* he replied, his jaw clenched. The lie hung in the space between them, a ghost in the room.

She exhaled sharply, the breath catching in her throat. *"But you must. The way you held your breath for a second—like you were waiting for something, something that never came."*

*"I was waiting for the door to open,"* he cut in, voice low. *"For the light to change."*

The pause stretched like an endless river. Outside, the café clock ticked, slow and deliberate, as if counting down to the end of their story.

*"What if I told you I don’t want to go back?"* she whispered.

*"Then we’re even,"* he said, and the words hung between them like a promise neither could keep.

The conversation had started with a question about the past. It had ended with a question about the future. And somewhere in the middle, they had lost track of the present entirely.

The waitress appeared, her eyes flickering between them. *"Another round?"* she asked, her voice neutral, indifferent.

Neither answered. The silence grew louder, swallowing them whole.
