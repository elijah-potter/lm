The air hummed with an unnatural silence. Each breath felt like a marathon, the weight of the world pressing down as if gravity had doubled. The clock on the wall seemed to tick backward, its second hand moving in slow, deliberate loops.

She knelt beside the crumpled figure, her fingers tracing the frozen expressions on their faces—eyes wide with terror, lips parted in silent screams. The sound of her own voice, whispering *"It’s okay,"* carried no relief. The words themselves seemed to stretch, each syllable elongating into something unrecognizable, a distortion of meaning.

Outside, the streetlights flickered like dying stars. Shadows stretched unnaturally long, their edges blurred as if moving against time. The distant wail of sirens sounded like a chorus of dying birds, their pitch rising and falling in a way that defied the laws of sound.

She pressed her palm to the pavement, feeling the heat of the earth beneath her—warmer than it should have been. The ground beneath her feet trembled, not from movement, but from something deeper, something *wrong*. The cracks in the sidewalk had formed in reverse, splitting open backward, revealing hollows where there had been nothing before.

A voice, not hers, whispered from the darkness: *"You’re late."* The words slithered into her mind, smooth and insistent, like a blade of ice. The past and future blurred together, a single, endless present where nothing made sense.

She looked up—too late—and saw the sky, not black, but a swirling void of colors she couldn’t name. Above them, the stars themselves seemed to pause, suspended in a moment that refused to end. The universe had paused. And in that stillness, she realized: time was no longer a line. It was a cage. And she was inside.
