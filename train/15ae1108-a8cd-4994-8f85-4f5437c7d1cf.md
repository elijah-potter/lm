The air in the dimly lit café hummed with the soft murmur of conversations, yet my focus remained rigidly fixed on the half-eaten slice of apple pie before me. The crumbs had long since settled, but the choice to finish it or leave it behind still lingered. My fingers traced the rim of my coffee cup, the warmth of the liquid contrasting with the chill of indecision.

*Would it be a waste to eat it?*

Or perhaps—*would it be better to save it for later?* The scent of vanilla and caramel wrapped around my senses, but the memory of the previous evening’s argument with my sister loomed large. The pie was hers, and the guilt of taking it without her permission gnawed at me.

I exhaled slowly, the breath fogging slightly against the cold glass. The decision was simple in theory: eat the pie and face the guilt, or leave it and risk her anger. But the choice was anything but simple. The pie’s allure, the warmth of the moment, and the lingering tension between us all tangled together like frayed strings.

Outside, the rain tapped lightly against the café’s window, a quiet reminder of fleeting moments. I reached for the pie again, my hand trembling slightly. The choice was made.
