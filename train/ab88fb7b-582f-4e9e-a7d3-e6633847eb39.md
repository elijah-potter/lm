The old wooden desk in the corner of the dimly lit attic was carved with a single faded promise—*"I’ll be back"*—in faded ink on the back of a family photo. The paper curled at the edges, its edges frayed from decades of handling. The photograph was of a young girl, her dark curls framed by a smile that still held the same warmth as the day she’d been taken away.

Her father’s voice echoed in the silence, sharp and final: *"You’ll come back, sweetheart."* The promise had been whispered between them, a fragile thread of time. Now, it was all that remained—just a crumbling promise, a ghost of a memory etched into the past.

Years later, when the attic door creaked open again, the promise was forgotten until the photograph’s edges crumbled to dust, revealing the truth: the girl was never coming back. The desk, the ink, the faded words—all of it was a ghost story now.
