## Explain Like I'm Five: Why the Moon Looks Different Sometimes

Okay, imagine you have a ball, and you're standing in a room with a lamp shining on it. That ball is like the Moon, and the lamp is like the Sun. You are standing on Earth.

Sometimes, you can see the whole ball lit up by the lamp, right? That’s when we see a Full Moon. The Sun is shining directly on the Moon, and we’re looking at it at just the right angle.

But sometimes, you might turn the ball a little bit. Now, only half of it is lit up by the lamp. That's like a Half Moon! We’re not seeing the whole sunlit side.

And sometimes, you turn the ball all the way around, so you can’t see *any* of the light! That’s like a New Moon. The sun is shining on the back of the ball, but we can't see it from where we are.

The Moon doesn't *actually* change shape. It always stays round, like a ball. But as the Moon travels around the Earth, and the Earth travels around the Sun, we see different amounts of the sunlit part of the Moon. It’s like we're just getting different views of the same ball!

Think about it like this: you have a cookie. You eat a little bit of it. Now, the cookie looks different, right? But the cookie didn’t *change* into something else. You just saw a different part of it. It’s the same idea with the Moon!

These different shapes we see the Moon in are called “phases.” There are lots of different phases – a little bit more than half, a tiny sliver, and so on. They all happen because of how the Sun, Earth, and Moon are moving around each other.
