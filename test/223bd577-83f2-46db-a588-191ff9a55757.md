**Local officials in the historic coastal town of Eldermere announce the discovery of a previously unidentified shipwreck off the northern cliffs.** Archaeologists uncovered a sunken vessel believed to date back to the late 17th century, featuring intricate carvings and trade goods that hint at a lost trading route between Eldermere and the newly emerging ports of the Caribbean. The wreck, named *The Marigold*, was found 15 miles offshore after a storm washed away decades of protective dunes. Preliminary analysis suggests the vessel may have been part of a fleet transporting exotic spices and silk, though its exact purpose remains unclear. Eldermere Mayor Elias Hart expressed excitement, calling it "a potential treasure trove for both history and commerce," while historians warn of the need for careful preservation to avoid further damage. The discovery follows recent debates over expanding maritime tourism in the area, prompting calls for a temporary moratorium on off-shore drilling to protect the site. The government has promised full documentation and public viewing of artifacts, with plans to establish the wreck as a protected marine monument by next summer.
