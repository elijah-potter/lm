Waiting is a quiet rebellion against the rush of time. The clock ticks, the air hums with the unspoken tension of delayed decisions, and the pause becomes a canvas for reflection. A stranger’s smile lingers as they pause outside the café, their cup half-empty, fingers tracing the rim of their mug. The coffee, still warm, sits untouched, a silent witness to the hesitation.

Inside, someone’s hands tremble as they reach for the phone, fingers brushing against the screen. The message unread, the voice mail waiting—*no decision, no answer*—yet the urge to act is a storm inside. The world moves on, but the pause feels like a breath held too long.

Waiting is the space between choice and consequence. It’s the moment a child asks, *"Why?"* before a story begins, the pause between a breath and the next thought. It’s the pause in a song, the silence before a laugh, the hush after a confession. Waiting is the art of being present with what is, even when it feels like surrender.
