The dim glow of the old lantern flickered against the walls of the abandoned warehouse, casting long shadows that seemed to writhe at the edges of her vision. Clara clutched the rusted key in her trembling fingers, her breath shallow and uneven. Outside, the wind howled through the broken windows, rattling the warped door like a ghost’s lament.

*"You don’t belong here,"* the voice whispered in her mind—not from the wind, but from somewhere deeper, older than the fear in her chest. The key turned in the lock with a sound like dry bones scraping against stone. She stepped inside, the air thick with dust and the faint, metallic tang of rust.

The room was a labyrinth of forgotten things: a desk piled with yellowed ledgers, a half-finished painting of a face that wasn’t hers, and a mirror that reflected not her face, but the hollow of a place inside her that had long since stopped echoing back. She knelt beside a desk drawer, fingers brushing the edge of a journal. The pages were crumbling, but the words on them were still legible:

*"I am the ghost you carry."*

Her pulse hammered in her ears. The key slipped from her grip. It hit the floor with a clatter, rolling under the desk. She didn’t move to pick it up. Instead, she leaned forward, her forehead pressed against the mirror’s glass, and whispered:

*"Show me what I am."*

The reflection didn’t move. But the journal, tucked beneath the desk, did.
