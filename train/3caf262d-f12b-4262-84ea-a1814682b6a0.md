The horizon glows faintly with remnants of golden mist, where the earth’s skin now bears jagged ridges of blackened stone. Wet, moss-slick slopes twist between the ruins of what once stood as bridges, their timbers split like cracked ice. The air hums with a low, metallic chorus—rusted machinery still whirring in the wind, though the engines have long since stopped. Far off, the river flows sluggishly through a canyon carved by unseen hands, its banks lined with skeletal remains of trees, their trunks hollowed by some unseen rot. The sky above is a bruised, bruised bruise of ashen gray, punctuated by a single, slow-moving storm cloud—its edges dissolving into the wind like smoke. The scent of damp earth and iron lingers, thick and bitter, as if the land itself has been drained of its old life.
