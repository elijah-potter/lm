A flickering neon sign flickers outside a 24-hour diner, its colors bleeding into the grayscale rain streaking through the windows. The air smells of stale coffee and fried dough, thick with the scent of old, half-eaten burgers. Inside, a single table holds a half-finished sketch of a cityscape with half-finished buildings—some standing, others crumbling into the ground. A clock on the wall ticks at half speed, its hands stuck mid-minute. The floorboards creak underfoot, as if they’ve been there too long to forget their own weight. A radio plays a song that’s been playing for years, the static in the background a reminder of time slipping away. The door at the back of the diner swings open, letting in a gust of cold wind that carries the scent of something burning—maybe a campfire, maybe a memory. The diner’s name above the door is barely visible, scratched out in what looks like hurried handwriting.
