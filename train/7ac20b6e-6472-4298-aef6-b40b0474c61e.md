**A reluctant decision reversed mid-stride**

The coffee cup was half-empty, steam curling into the stale morning air. Her fingers tightened around the rim as she replayed the text: *"Call now—it’s urgent."* Her stomach twisted—not fear, but the ghost of a lie she’d half-trusted. She’d been here before.

The door clicked shut behind the delivery guy. The car’s engine purred, the radio crackling with static. She reached for the wheel, but her hands hovered—*just for a second*—as her brain rewired. The last mile was a blur of regret and hesitation, the GPS’s voice a distant echo. At the stoplight, her thumb hovered over the *red* button. The engine died.

Outside, the city hummed. No one noticed her pause. No one would ever know how much she’d wanted to turn left, to vanish into the crowd, to erase this one moment of truth.

She exhaled, the breath shallow. The door opened. The world went on.
