A half-finished mural in an abandoned warehouse, its vibrant blue and yellow stripes peeling at the edges, the canvas sagging under the weight of unapplied paint. The artist’s initials—scrambled by time—are smudged in the bottom left corner, their once-sharp edges now dissolved into the chaos of half-done strokes. The tool left behind: a worn palette knife, its tip cracked, resting atop a half-eaten plate of stale bread, its crumbs scattered like broken dreams.

In the corner, a single, unanswered question marks the spot where the next stroke was meant to be. The floor beneath is strewn with discarded brushes, some still damp with the last traces of color, others brittle with age. A half-empty bottle of turpentine sits beside it, its label long faded to a ghostly hint of its original words.

The unfinished is a living thing—it breathes in the gaps, hums in the spaces between. It whispers of potential, of what was almost, of what could have been if only time had stopped here.
