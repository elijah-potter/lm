A fragile contract signed in haste, its terms written in ink that blurred with the morning’s light. The promise hung like a half-unfolded paper lantern, its threads frayed by the weight of unseen storms. Every word was a gamble—each syllable a promise, every syllable a lie in the making.

*"I’ll return by dawn."* The clock struck midnight, and the hours stretched like a patient waiting for a doctor’s verdict. The moon, a pale promise itself, did not rise when it was due. The night swallowed the words whole, and when morning came, the dawn was only a pale ghost of what was promised.

Some promises are not meant to be kept—they are built on sand, or on the backs of those who would rather not see the weight of their own hands. The keeper of this one was already drowning before the ink was dry.
