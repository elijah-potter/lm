A dim lamp flickered as Elias adjusted the chair’s angle, his fingers tracing the rim of his coffee cup. The room smelled of old wood and damp wool, the kind of scent that clung to memories like cobwebs. Outside, the wind howled through the gables, but Elias didn’t hear it. His eyes were glued to the worn leather-bound book in his lap, the kind that had seen better days than he had.

*"The night the lantern fell,"* he murmured, his voice low enough that only the fire’s embers seemed to hear. *"It was the thirteenth of Kael’s Moon, and Father had just returned from the market with a new lantern—black as midnight, polished to a shine. He swore it would last a decade. Or at least that’s what he told Mother before she laughed and said, ‘More like the first week.’"*

His breath hitched. *"That evening, as we sat in the kitchen, Father lit it. The flame danced like a serpent, casting long shadows across the walls. Then—*" His voice cracked. *"Then the door creaked."*

The room stilled. Elias swallowed hard. *"We turned. The wind had picked up, and the draft made the walls groan. Father said, ‘Perhaps the old hinges.’ But Mother just smiled. ‘Or perhaps the wind is telling us something.’"*

He paused, letting the story settle. *"The lantern burned low, its light pooling on the floorboards. That’s when I saw it—the dark shape under the bed. Not a rat. Not a mouse. Something with too many joints, its fingers twitching like they were fighting to escape. Father grabbed his knife. ‘Run,’ he barked. ‘Run, Elias.’"*

Elias’ hands trembled. *"I ran. Down the stairs, past the kitchen, into the garden. The lantern fell. Not from my hand—it just… fell. The flame snuffed out. And when I looked back, the shape under the bed was gone. But the floorboards had split. Something had been there."*

A silence. Then, softer: *"Father never spoke of it again. But sometimes, when the wind howls just right, I swear I hear it under the bed."*
