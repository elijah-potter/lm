The boardroom meeting dragged on, tension thick as the air when we reached the final vote: *merge the marketing and finance teams*. The compromise wasn’t just about merging departments—it was about splitting the difference between two visions: one wanted tighter cross-functional collaboration, the other demanded stricter operational boundaries. *"We’ll pool budgets but keep reporting lines separate,"* the CEO said, nodding at the middle ground. The compromise stung less because it was framed as a pragmatic choice than because it felt like a betrayal of both sides. By noon, the email chain had already spiraled into accusations of "playing both sides" and "sacrificing vision." The real compromise? That we’d all pretend this wasn’t a compromise.
