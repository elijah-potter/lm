The clock struck midnight, the neon glow of distant traffic reflecting off the wet pavement like polished mirrors. I stood in the empty subway station, fingers tracing the edges of the worn ticket stub, the scent of stale coffee and ozone thick in the air. A stranger passed by, their coat too fitted, their movements synchronized with someone else’s—like a dance where each step was memorized, practiced, until it became effortless.

I turned to the mirror above the ticket booth, my reflection not quite me. The eyes were too bright, the lips too straight. Behind me, the doors slid open with the sound of a film reel, the hum of a thousand unseen hands turning the pages of a story that had been scripted long before. The weight of it settled in my chest—not dread, but something older: the quiet certainty that I was already a part of a performance, and the audience was just waiting.
