The campfire crackled as rain drummed against the canvas tent. Elias gripped his rifle, teeth clenched. Across the treeline, the village of Veldrin stood silhouetted under the moon, its torches flickering like dying stars. His father’s voice echoed in his mind: *"A promise is not a word, son. It’s the fire that burns in your heart."* The last one to leave would be the first to face the *hollow men*—the ones who’d taken his brother’s sight.

He exhaled, scanning the shadows. The old woman at the edge of the camp—Mira—knew why he was there. *"They’re coming,"* she’d whispered. *"Not tomorrow. Not next week. Soon."* His father had told him the truth: the hollow men didn’t just kill. They *unmade* things. And Elias had promised to find his brother’s eyes before they turned black.

The wind howled. His boots splashed through the mud as he moved, silent as the grave. The village’s gates creaked open. Inside, the air smelled of smoke and fear. A child’s laughter cut through the tension—too bright, too loud. Elias froze. The boy was too close. He’d seen the way his father’s hands trembled when he looked at his brother’s eyes. That was the test.

*"Run,"* his father had said. *"If you don’t, you’ll never know if you’re strong enough."* But the boy was running *toward* him, not away. Elias lunged. The boy’s scream shattered the night.

Blood on his sleeve. His father’s words burned: *"A promise is not a shield. It’s what you do when the fire is at your back."* The hollow men were already closing in. The boy’s body crumpled at his feet. Elias knelt, pressing his palm to the wound. *"Don’t die,"* he murmured. *"Don’t let them take your eyes."*

The first hollow man emerged from the darkness, his hollowed eyes reflecting the firelight like shattered glass. Elias raised the rifle. The shot echoed. The man staggered but didn’t fall. Mira’s voice cut through the chaos: *"Elias, run!"*

He didn’t. He fired again. The man collapsed. The village erupted into chaos. The promise was real. The test was over. But the fire in his chest was still burning.
