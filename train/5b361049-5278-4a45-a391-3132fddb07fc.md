**The Last Light**

She found the keys on the kitchen table, half-empty coffee cup beside them, the alarm still ringing in her skull. The reminder was a shadow on the screen: *"Check on Dad—he’s been sleeping too long."* She swallowed hard, the weight of the message settling like a stone in her throat.

Outside, the city pulsed—cars, sirens, the distant hum of life she barely noticed anymore. But her phone buzzed again, another notification, the same plea: *"He’s alone. Please, come home."*

She exhaled, fingers trembling as she stepped into the cold. The door creaked open, and for a moment, the dark swallowed her whole. Then she saw him—pale, tangled in the sheets, the oxygen mask still clutched in his hand. His breath came in ragged gasps.

*"I’m here,"* she whispered, pressing her forehead to his chest. The reminder had become a heartbeat now, a promise to keep the world from slipping away.
