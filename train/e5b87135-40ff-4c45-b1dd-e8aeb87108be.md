A fractured time loop in the abandoned subway station beneath Blackthorn City, where clocks in every corner tick backward. The air hums with static—static of echoes from a future that never was. Shadows stretch diagonally, not vertically, and the ground beneath you is a lattice of fractured time: some tiles show yesterday’s rain, others display tomorrow’s storm. The walls whisper in a language that shifts mid-sentence. A single, unchanging clock in the center of the concourse ticks at 00:00, but its hands move erratically—sometimes forward, sometimes backward, never still. The only constant is the sound of a distant, repeating train whistle: once every *two* minutes. The station’s last passenger vanished mid-step, their coat still damp, their pocket watch frozen at 3:17 AM. The walls are lined with mirrors that reflect not your reflection, but a version of yourself from a different day—older, younger, or wearing a coat you don’t recognize. The elevator doors, when they open, reveal a hallway where the floor slopes upward at an impossible angle, leading to a door that never opens. Some doors in the station are marked with dates that no longer exist on the calendar. The floor beneath your feet vibrates in a rhythm that doesn’t match any known metronome. At the center of the station, a single, untouched sandwich sits on a counter, untouched for decades. The scent of stale coffee lingers, but the cup is empty. The last thing you hear before the station closes forever is a child’s laughter—echoing, then fading into nothingness.
