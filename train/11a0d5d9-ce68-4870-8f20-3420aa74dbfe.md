A fog-choked dock in an old port city. The air smells of salt and damp wood. Boats bob gently on the choppy tide, their hulls worn smooth by decades of waves. A lone figure stands at the edge of a crumbling pier, fingers tracing the rusted railing. The water ripples with unseen movement—fish darting, waves whispering secrets. No one moves except the slow, deliberate sway of the pier itself. The silence is louder than any sound. Shadows stretch long and unnatural, bending around the edges of the buildings. The scene is bathed in the eerie glow of streetlamps, casting everything in a soft, golden haze. The details in the surroundings—peeling paint on the warehouse walls, the way the moon casts a crescent on the water—dominate over any attempt at human interaction. The atmosphere lingers like the scent of the sea.
