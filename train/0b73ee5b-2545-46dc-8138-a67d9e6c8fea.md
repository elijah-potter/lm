The earth’s surface is now a fractured mosaic of jagged, mineral-streaked ridges where once stood forests. The canyons are carved with crystalline formations, their depths echoing with wind-carved whispers of a past that’s been erased. Rivers, if they once flowed freely, now seep through fissures, their waters tinged with metallic veins. The sky, once a canvas of endless blue, now bears the weight of perpetual twilight, a haze of dust and stardust perpetually settling over the land. The air hums with the static of forgotten machines, the distant drone of something that isn’t quite alive, but moves too fast for the wind. Somewhere, the ruins of human structures still stand—towers half-buried, bridges collapsed, cities now ghostly skeletons of concrete and steel. The scent of ozone lingers, thick and sharp, as if the world has been rewoven by unseen hands. The only sounds are the creak of ancient wood, the distant roar of a forgotten engine, and the occasional crackle of something burning slowly, unseen. The land breathes, but it does not smile.
