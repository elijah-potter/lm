-	Gather some unused rolls of toilet paper and show your daughter how to stack them one upon another. After she stacks them, she can have fun knocking them down. If she gets good at stacking, start making more complex shapes like pyramids.
-	Collect the empty tubes from toilet paper and paper towels. These can be glued or stapled to a board or large cardboard box to create a tube pathway for marbles or other small balls. If positioned well, even small toy cars may be able to pass through the tubes.
-	Cut a rectangular “frame” out of cardboard, then lay clear packaging tape across it so the sticky side shows through the frame on one side. Take your child out to a park or field and ask her to collect pretty leaves, flowers, etc. Show her how to stick them onto the tape’s surface. Once it’s full, you can tape it up from the other side and you have some custom artwork!
-	Cut or melt a small hole in the top of a plastic jar lid. Give your child a small bowl of beans and ask her to place them through the hole. Make sure the hole is large enough for the beans to pass through but not much larger so she has to practice fine motor control.
-	If you have toy balls of various sizes, cut holes in the top of a shoebox to match the balls. Ask your child to drop the balls through the appropriate holes.
-	Fold your junk mail into paper airplanes to toss around in the park.
-	Cut doors and windows into any large cardboard box you have laying around. It can become an instant playhouse!
