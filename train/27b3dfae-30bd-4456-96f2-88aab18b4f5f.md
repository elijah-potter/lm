The hum of fluorescent lights buzzed like distant thunder in the cavernous office space, each breath a whisper of unease. She stood in the fluorescent-lit hallway, her heels clicking against the polished tile like a metronome counting seconds until the next mistake. The fluorescent lights cast eerie shadows on the walls, highlighting the way her hands trembled—not from cold, but from the quiet dread of being seen.

She had arrived late, her scarf half-knotted, her bag slung over one shoulder. The others had already gathered in the corner, laughing over coffee, their voices sharp and unfiltered. A man in a suit leaned against the cubicle wall, watching her with the detached curiosity of someone who had already decided she didn’t belong. The fluorescent lights flickered, casting her reflection in a distorted mirror, her eyes wide with the kind of fear that comes from knowing you’re being scrutinized without consent.

The fluorescent lights hummed louder as her fingers tightened around the strap of her bag. She had spent years learning to blend—how to sit still, how to nod along without contributing, how to let the silence stretch like a second skin. But in this moment, the fluorescent lights felt like a microscope, and she was the specimen.

A knock at the door made her jump. The fluorescent lights flared, and for a second, the hallway was dark. Then the door opened, and the fluorescent lights returned, harsh and judgmental, like a spotlight on a stage where she had no lines.

She exhaled slowly, her breath fogging the fluorescent-lit air. The fluorescent lights would never fully dim. She would always be the one who stood out, the one who didn’t fit. But for now, she would just sit here, legs crossed, and let the fluorescent lights do their work.
