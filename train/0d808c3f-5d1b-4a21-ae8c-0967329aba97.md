## Explain Like I'm Five: Why the Sky is Blue

Okay, imagine you have a big box of crayons, right? And in that box, you have all sorts of colors: red, yellow, green, blue, and purple! Now, imagine those crayons are like sunshine. Sunshine isn't just *one* color, it's actually made up of *all* those colors mixed together. It’s like a big, beautiful rainbow hiding inside the sun!

Now, when that sunshine travels all the way from the sun to our eyes, it has to go through the air. Our air is made of tiny, tiny pieces, much smaller than even a grain of sand. These tiny pieces are like little bouncy castles.

When the sunshine hits those bouncy castles, something funny happens. The colors in the sunshine *bounce*! But they don't all bounce the same way.

Blue and violet (a color very close to purple) are like little, energetic bouncy balls. They bounce *much* more than the other colors, like red and yellow. They bounce all over the place, bouncing off those tiny air pieces and spreading out everywhere.

Because blue and violet bounce around so much, they fill up the whole sky! That's why when we look up, we see blue.

Now, you might be wondering, “Why don’t we see violet then? Why not purple?” Well, the sun doesn't send out *quite* as much violet as it does blue. Also, our eyes are better at seeing blue than violet.

Sometimes, when the sun is setting, the sky looks orange or red. That's because the sunshine has to travel through even *more* air to reach us. All that extra air bounces the blue light away, and the red and orange colors get to shine through! It’s like they’re having a special moment to be seen.

So, the sky is blue because blue light from the sun bounces around more than the other colors when it bumps into the tiny pieces in the air. Pretty neat, huh?
