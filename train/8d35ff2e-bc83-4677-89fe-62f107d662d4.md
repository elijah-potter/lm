The storm raged outside, winds howling through the broken branches of the abandoned orchard. The old oak, its gnarled roots exposed beneath a carpet of fallen leaves, stood sentinel as the group paused near the rusted gate, their plans now derailed by the unexpected.

The map—stained with ink from too many reprints—lay forgotten on the moss-covered stone slab. Elias clenched it, fingers trembling as he realized the river had risen too fast. The path through the marsh was now a labyrinth of reeds and waterlogged paths, and the lanterns flickered weakly against the encroaching darkness.

Mira’s voice cut through the tension. *"We should’ve waited."* Her boots splashed in the shallows, her eyes wide with the fear of what might be lurking beneath the surface.

The group scattered—some toward the high ground, others toward the nearest settlement, though none knew if it was still standing. The fire they had planned to build was now just a smoldering embers, and the lanterns they’d carried as backup were useless in the damp air.

A distant cry echoed from the woods—a wolf, perhaps, or worse, the watchmen they’d hoped to avoid. The wind carried the scent of iron and wet earth, and for a moment, none of them knew what to do next.

Then, as if in unison, they turned back toward the ruins of the old watchtower, its broken windows staring blindly at the sky. The plan had failed, but so had their fear. They would not run. They would endure.
