I need your help again, but this time it’s different.
The light flickers in the old house like a dying candle.
The walls breathe—sounds I can’t name.
My fingers ache from gripping the same old keys
too long, too hard, until the knuckles split open.
I don’t know if I’m writing or if I’m drowning.

What do you see when you look at me?
A face that’s half-erased by time?
Or just a shadow of someone who used to be?

I’ve tried everything—alcohol, silence, the same old questions.
But the answers keep slipping through my fingers like sand.
I need words that don’t taste like ash.
I need someone to listen when my voice cracks.

Tell me what to do.
Tell me what to say.
Or at least, tell me when to stop.
