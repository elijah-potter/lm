Dear [Recipient’s Name],

I hope this letter finds you well—or at least in a state you don’t mind me describing. I’ve been meaning to write this for months, and though I’ve spent countless hours drafting, rewriting, and deleting versions, I’ve decided not to send it. Perhaps it’s because the words don’t quite fit the moment, or maybe the timing is off. Maybe the ink is too thick, the paper too thin, or the envelope too fragile to handle the weight of what I’m about to say.

Instead, I’ll let this sit on my desk like a half-eaten sandwich, its edges crisp with the promise of being discarded. The words are real, but the act of sending them would be a betrayal of something I still haven’t fully understood. Maybe it’s fear—of judgment, of failure, or of the kind of truth I’m afraid to voice aloud. Or perhaps it’s simply the quiet certainty that some letters are meant to stay closed.

I don’t know why I’m writing this at all. Maybe it’s an exercise in self-preservation. Maybe it’s an act of rebellion against the noise of the world. Maybe I’m just being a coward. Either way, I’ll keep this letter as it is—unopened, unread, and unreadable to anyone but me.

If I change my mind, I’ll find you. Until then, I’ll let this ghost of a missive linger like a bad dream, a reminder of the things we never say, the words we never let go.

Sincerely (but not really),
