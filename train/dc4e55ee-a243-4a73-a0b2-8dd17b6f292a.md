A neon-lit urban wasteland where skyscrapers lean precariously over crumbling bridges. Construction cranes, half-assembled, hang like skeletal fingers from skeletal buildings, their steel bones rusting in the perpetual twilight. The air smells of ozone and damp concrete, and the occasional hum of generators cuts through the silence like a distant alarm. Street vendors push carts laden with mismatched souvenirs—glasses that don’t fit, clocks that don’t tick, keys that unlock nothing. Children play in puddles that never quite dry, their laughter swallowed by the hum of drones patrolling the sky, their sensors scanning for something that isn’t there. The streets are paved with reclaimed asphalt, and the occasional patch of wild grass grows where the city’s soul once stood. A flickering holographic billboard advertises a "Last Chance" sale: *"All items 50% off. Void where prohibited."* The wind carries the scent of melting ice cream and the distant wail of sirens that never stop.
