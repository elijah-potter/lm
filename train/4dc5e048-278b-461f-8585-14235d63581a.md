Waiting is a silent rebellion against the rush of modern life. A stranger’s coffee cup sits untouched beside them, a warm ember of solitude in a sea of chaos. Outside, the city hums with urgency, yet this moment is theirs to claim: no deadline, no obligation, just the quiet acceptance of stillness.

They could have ordered another drink, another distraction, but instead, they linger. The steam curls lazily around their hands, a ghost of the past—the last conversation, the unfinished task, the half-finished thought. Waiting isn’t passive; it’s an act of resistance, a refusal to be moved by the relentless tide of productivity. It’s learning to listen to the voice within, the one that whispers, *"Not yet."*

The world moves fast, but this moment? It’s a quiet victory. A choice to stand still and let the world pass them by.
