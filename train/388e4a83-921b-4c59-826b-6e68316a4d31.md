**The Last Light**

The wind howled through the skeletal trees, their branches clawing at the pale, blood-red sky. The air smelled of damp earth and something older—rust and decay. Inside the crumbling church, the altar groaned under its own weight, the stained-glass windows long since shattered, their glass reduced to shards of memory. The pews, once polished mahogany, were now splintered and covered in moss and time.

Father Elias knelt beside the half-buried altar, his fingers tracing the edge of a broken hymnal. The book was ancient, its pages brittle with age, its leather cover cracked like the skin of something long dead. He had found it in the attic, half-sunk in dust, its last few leaves still bearing the faded ink of prayers uttered decades before.

A shadow stretched long across the floor.

Elias didn’t look up. "You’re late," he said quietly.

The door creaked open.

A figure stepped inside—tall, cloaked in black, their face half-hidden beneath a hood. Their breath was slow, deliberate. "I came," they said. The voice was deep, resonant, like the rumble of distant thunder. "I always do."

Elias stood, dusting his knees. "You’re not supposed to be here."

The stranger removed their hood, revealing a face that had seen too much war. Their eyes were cold, their lips thin. "I’m not supposed to be anywhere."

The air between them crackled. The church seemed to hold its breath.

Elias exhaled sharply. "You’re the one who brought them."

The stranger’s gaze flicked to the altar, where the remains of a long-dead priest still lay half-drowned in the soil. "I’m the one who always arrives."

Then the lights flickered. The last ember of the dying candle guttered out.
