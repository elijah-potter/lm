In the era of her brother's reign, Yaa Asantewaa witnessed the Ashanti Confederacy's tumultuous journey through perilous times, engulfed in the throes of civil war that swept across 1883 to 1888. As destiny unfolded its tapestry, and her brother's departure left a void, Yaa Asantewaa, invoking her authority as Queen Mother, bestowed the honor of Ejisuhene upon her grandson. Alas, the British, in their imperial decree, banished him to the distant shores of Seychelles in 1896, alongside King Prempeh I and Asante government dignitaries. Yaa Asantewaa took over control and became the regent of the Ejusi-Juaben realm. Thus, amidst the echoes of exile, the British viceroy, Frederick Hodgson, dared to covet the Golden Stool, the very embodiment of the Asante nation's spirit, propelling a clandestine gathering of remaining officials in Kumasi, consumed by the pursuit of reclaiming their king's return.
