The library’s humming air thickened as Elias sat at the corner table, fingers tracing the worn spine of the book. The fluorescent light flickered over his face—shadows pooling like ink in his hollowed-out eyes. His phone buzzed; the notification read: *"You’re 3 minutes late to the meeting."* He ignored it.

The silence wasn’t empty. It was a wall of sound—his own breath, the distant murmur of another reader, the creak of the floorboards beneath his chair. He exhaled slowly, watching the smoke curl from his cigarette, dissolving into the stillness. Outside, the city breathed, but inside, he was a ghost.

A key turned in the lock. The door creaked open. His mother stepped inside, carrying a tray of tea. She set it down without looking at him, as if he weren’t there. The scent of bergamot and dust filled the room.

*"You’ve been avoiding your shift,"* she said, voice low. *"Again."*

Elias didn’t answer. The silence between them was a contract, unspoken and binding. His mother reached for the cup, took a sip, then paused. She exhaled, too. The room dissolved into the quiet, a pause that stretched like the edge of a blade. The clock ticked. The world waited.
