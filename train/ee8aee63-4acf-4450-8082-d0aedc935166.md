**The Disappearance of the Floating Bridge of St. Petersburg (1892)**
---
On the morning of **September 12, 1892**, the **Neva River** in St. Petersburg, Russia, hosted a temporary suspension bridge constructed for the **Grand St. Petersburg Art Exhibition**. The structure, a marvel of reinforced iron and wooden planks, spanned the river’s icy currents with a single cable anchor in the center.

By **10:17 AM**, the bridge collapsed not from structural failure but due to a **mysterious gas leak** from an experimental **hydrocarbon-based lubricant** being tested by local engineers. Witnesses reported a **blue-green haze** rising from beneath the planks, followed by the sudden **sinking of the central span**—though the outer walkways remained intact. The **Imperial Guard** and local police were summoned, but no bodies were recovered in the frigid waters.

The bridge’s wreckage was later salvaged and repurposed as a **floating dock** for the **Winter Palace’s private dockyard**. Official records from the **Imperial Archives** (now housed in the **Russian State Library**) list no casualties, though whispers persist among maritime historians of **unregistered fatalities** in the river’s depths. The event remains classified as an **"unforeseen technical incident"** in the **Ministry of Transportation’s archives**.
