## Explain Like I'm Five: Why the Sky is Blue

Okay, imagine you have a big box of crayons, right? And all the crayons are different colors – red, yellow, blue, green, purple, and so on. Sunlight is kind of like that box of crayons – it’s made up of *all* those colors mixed together! It looks white to us, but it’s actually a rainbow hiding inside.

Now, imagine you're bouncing a ball. Sometimes the ball bounces straight down. Other times, if it hits something like a bumpy rug, it bounces off in a different direction.

The air all around us isn't perfectly empty. It's full of tiny, tiny bits, much smaller than even a grain of sand. When sunlight comes down from the sun, it bumps into those tiny bits of air.

Blue light is like a bouncy ball. It bounces off those tiny bits of air *a lot*! It bounces everywhere, all around us. That’s why when you look up, you see blue. It’s bouncing all around!

Red and yellow light are more like heavier balls. They mostly go straight through the air. They don't bounce around as much. That’s why when the sun is setting, and the sunlight has to travel through *lots* of air, we see red and orange. The blue light has bounced away already!

So, the sky is blue because blue light is the bounciest and bounces all over the place, filling the whole sky! It's like a giant game of bouncy ball, and blue is winning!



Does that make sense?
