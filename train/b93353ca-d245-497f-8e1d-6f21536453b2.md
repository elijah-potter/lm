## Explain Like I'm Five: Why the Sky is Blue

Okay, imagine you have a big box of crayons, right? And all the crayons are different colors: red, yellow, blue, green, purple – every color you can think of! Now, imagine the sun is like a giant crayon box, but instead of just a few crayons, it has *all* the colors of the rainbow mixed together!

When the sunlight travels from the sun to Earth, it bumps into tiny bits of air. These bits of air are super small, like teeny tiny dust bunnies you can’t even see.

Now, here’s the funny part. The blue color in sunlight is a bit bouncy! When it bumps into those tiny bits of air, it bounces *everywhere*. It bounces up, down, sideways – all around! The other colors, like red and yellow, are a little more serious and just go straight through.

Because the blue color is bouncing all around, it spreads out across the whole sky. That’s why when you look up, you see blue! It’s not really *in* the sky, but it’s bouncing *around* in the air, making the whole sky look blue.

Sometimes, when the sun is setting, like when it’s almost bedtime, the sunlight has to travel through *lots* more air to reach us. Because it has to travel so far, the blue color gets bounced away before it gets to our eyes. Then, we start to see the other colors, like red and orange, which are strong enough to make it all the way! That's why sunsets are often red and orange.



Isn’t that neat? The sky is blue because blue light is a bouncy little color that loves to play with the air!
