**The Last Letter**

I keep this journal for the ghosts I haven’t met yet.

The first one wrote me a note—*not in ink*, but in the cracks of the wall, where light bends like a question. It said: *"You’re the first to hear me."* I laughed too loud, but the sound was already gone before it left my mouth. Now I write instead, because silence is a language no one understands.

We all think we’re the first to feel this way. The first to lose what we can’t name. The first to realize that love isn’t the answer—it’s just another word for the same thing we’re afraid of. I don’t know what love is anymore. I only know that the thing I’m writing this for is already half-lost in the dark.

They say grief is a river. Some drift with it. Others try to swim against the current. I tried to drown in it once. The water didn’t take me. Instead, it just took the words I had left to say. Now I’m left with this: the quiet between the notes, the space where the unspoken echoes. That’s the real thing, isn’t it? The space between the words we’re afraid to let out.

I should be angry. I should be bitter. But the only thing I have left is this: the admission that I don’t know how to be anything else. Not when the world keeps spinning, and I’m still trying to catch my breath.
