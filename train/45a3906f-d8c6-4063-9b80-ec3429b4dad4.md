The village of **Vespermere** lies in the heart of the **Frostbloom Marshes**, where the air hums with an unnatural tension. The **clock tower** in its center is carved from obsidian, its hands frozen mid-second, though the villagers whisper it ticks backward every **seven years**—just long enough to keep the past from catching up.

Children’s laughter echoes from the **blackened well**, where the water reflects not their faces, but their own voices in reverse. The **Grandfather’s House** holds a mirror that shows only what *could* have been, not what was. Locals claim that if you stand at the **Stone Bridge** at dusk, you’ll see your reflection walking away from you—*toward the future*, as if time itself has turned inside out.

The **Temple of Echoes** contains a statue of a **clockmaker** with his face melted into the gears, his mouth stitched shut with silver thread. The villagers believe he *still* tries to fix time, though no one knows how. Some say if you touch the **whispering wall**, you’ll hear fragments of the past *and* future in the same breath. Others claim the **river below** flows backward on certain days—though no one dares swim to confirm.

At night, the stars in Vespermere **move in slow motion**, as if drawn by an unseen thread. The villagers keep time with **moon-catchers**, glowing orbs that drift like comets, marking days as they *shouldn’t*. The elders say the town was built when a **black hole** passed through the world, leaving only this pocket where time is a puzzle instead of a path.

The **clockwork golem** in the market square doesn’t tick. It *unfolds*—its gears spinning in a spiral, its body stretching like taffy. Some say it’s a warning. Others believe it’s *waiting* to be wound again. The golem’s owner, **Madame Seraphine**, sells "time dust"—a powder that, when inhaled, makes a person’s shadow move *ahead* of them for an hour. The catch? The shadow never catches up.

The most dangerous place is the **Bridge of Echoes**, where the walkway *tilts* slightly, forcing travelers to step sideways. Those who dare cross report waking up decades ahead—or, worse, *already dead*, their bodies half-rotten, their mouths still whispering the words they *shouldn’t* have said. The villagers leave offerings of **silver coins** and **broken clocks** to the **Clockstone**, a monolith buried beneath the old church, where time *stutters* like a broken machine.

The town’s greatest secret? The **clockwork river**, a canal that flows in both directions at once, carrying water from the future into the past. The river’s source is a **black lake** where the water is cold enough to freeze time *for a moment*—enough to hear voices from beyond the grave. Some say the lake is a prison. Others believe it’s the only place left where time *can* be repaired.

At dusk, the **sky above Vespermere** turns **not blue, but silver**, veined with threads of light that pulse like a heartbeat. The villagers say if you look hard enough, you’ll see your own reflection—not moving, but *waiting*. Waiting for the moment when the **Great Stitch** will be undone, and time will finally *catch up*.
