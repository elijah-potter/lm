Eastern sunrise over a mist-shrouded valley where long, jagged canyon walls cast deep, diagonal stripes of violet and gold across fractured basalt. The shadows of ancient stone bridges arch like skeletal fingers, stretching across a river that splits the earth’s shadow into two mirrored pools—one warm, one cool. Below, a crumbling fortress’s battlements cast a slow, wavering shadow that pulses like a heartbeat, shifting between black and crimson as the light moves. Dunes to the south blur into a sea of silver, their shadows stretching infinitely, while the north winds carry smoke from distant fires that leave behind smudged, ghostly afterimages. The air is thick with the scent of damp earth and ozone, the shadows themselves whispering of forgotten storms and time-worn ruins where light bends like a mirage.
