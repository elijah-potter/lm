**The Quiet Hours**

The city hummed a low, rhythmic pulse—cars hissed through steel veins, laughter from alleyways lost in the night. I sat on a rooftop, hands wrapped tight around a cup of cold coffee, watching the skyline dissolve into smog. A storm brewed beyond the horizon, dark and restless, like a secret waiting to be told.

Then—nothing.

The wind shifted. A single leaf, detached, drifted past. It was the first sign of something else. The clouds parted. The air smelled of rain, sharp and clean, cutting through the grime. The storm, once a specter, softened into something less fierce. The city’s pulse slowed.

Below, the streets glistened. A child’s laughter echoed—light, unguarded. A stranger stopped, pressed their palm to a window, and watched me. I smiled. The world had returned to its own rhythm, and so did I.
