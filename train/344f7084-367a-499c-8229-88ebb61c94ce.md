The old clock tower stood silent at the edge of town, its face cracked and time lost to rust. Elias brushed dust from his coat as he approached, the weight of his backpack pressing against his shoulders. Inside was a small wooden box, its surface worn smooth by years of handling.

A knock at the door made him pause. The bell above it chimed, faint and unmistakable. His heart skipped a beat. He opened the door just as the town’s mayor, an elderly man with a weathered face, stepped inside.

“Elias,” the man said, his voice rough. “You’ve been looking for this place for days.”

Elias’ breath caught. “I—I was told it was here.”

The mayor’s eyes locked onto the box. “The clock tower’s hands stopped at 3:17. That’s when the old man who maintained it vanished. They say the box was his last thing before he disappeared.” He reached into his coat, pulling out a tattered ledger. “This was found in his workshop. Inside, it says *‘The clock is not the problem—it’s the key.’*”

Elias’ fingers trembled. The ledger was warm. A pulse, faint but steady, hummed from the pages.

Outside, the wind howled through the broken glass of the tower’s windows. Elias turned back to the mayor. “What do I do?”

The mayor’s gaze was grim. “Find the box. Whatever’s in it, it’s the only way to stop the town from unraveling.” He pressed a rusted key into Elias’ palm. “The door to the tower’s hidden behind this.”

Elias swallowed. The clock tower’s hands moved—slow, deliberate, but inexorably toward midnight.
