The humming of a city’s unseen engines—each breath of exhaust a silent hymn to progress—drifts through cracked windows where curtains rustle like autumn leaves. Outside, the pavement glistens with rain that never quite falls, just pools into puddles that whisper secrets of the past. A stranger leans against a lamppost, fingers tracing the cold metal like a man tracing a wound. Their shadow, long and jagged, stretches across the street, a ghost of their own reflection, half-faded, half-alive. The world here is a museum of forgotten things, and time is the only currency they don’t accept.
