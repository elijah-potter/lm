A child passes a smooth, polished stone to a friend, its surface catching the morning light like a tiny mirror. The stone moves from one grip to another in rapid, deliberate arcs, each hand’s touch leaving a faint imprint of calloused fingers. Nearby, a woven basket gathers discarded trinkets: a cracked porcelain figurine, a frayed thread of a scarf, a half-eaten piece of fruit peel. A shadow of a cat watches from a windowsill, tail flicking as it watches the exchange. The stone glints once more, now resting in the palm of a grown-up’s hand, its journey paused.
