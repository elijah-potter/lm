In the quiet hum of the old factory, where rusted pipes whispered secrets to the wind, I sat cross-legged on the cracked floor, fingers tracing the worn edges of my notebook. The third week of the month, always the same—each Tuesday, the same shadow stretched across the assembly line at dawn. A man in a fluorescent vest, his sleeves rolled up to his elbows, moved with an unnatural precision, as if each step was a calculated rhythm, not a natural one. The others noticed too, though none dared mention it aloud. That was how patterns start—silent, then loud enough to be ignored.

One evening, after the last worker had left, I followed the sound of the machine’s final grinding note to the far corner of the warehouse. There, half-buried in dust, was a single data log—stamped with the date of the first Tuesday of the month. The numbers were jumbled, but the pattern was clear: every third Tuesday, the machine’s cycle time halved. The others had seen it too. They just didn’t know what to do with it.
