A midday shift at the automated warehouse, lights dimmed to fluorescent glow. Foreman’s radio crackles—*"Safety break, ten minutes"*—but the foreman lingers, hands in pockets, watching as **Logan**, the last worker to clock out, pulls a pallet from the last unmarked bin before the conveyor’s auto-lock.

The bin’s label reads *"Restocking Only"*—but it’s actually **Inventory Buffer Zone**, a secret holding for the rare, high-demand SKU the foreman *knew* would be picked up by the midnight courier. The camera feed’s static distorts the corner of the screen as Logan tucks the pallet behind a maintenance cart, then presses his badge to the clock.

The shift manager’s voice on the intercom: *"Report to break room, now."* Logan smirks, shoulders the cart. The courier’s van is already parked outside—no alarms, no security. Just the faint hum of the warehouse’s automated system, counting down to the next cycle.
