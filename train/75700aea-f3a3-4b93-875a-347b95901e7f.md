The autumn wind carried whispers through the hollowed-out oak, its bark like the pages of a book worn thin by time. I sat on the moss-stained stone, the cup of black tea between my hands, its warmth fading as the chill crept in. The fire had died long ago, leaving only embers in the hearth, their glow reflecting in the hollows of my face.

Outside, the village slept, unaware of the storm gathering on the horizon. The rain began as a distant murmur, then a roar, as if the earth itself had awakened. I closed my eyes, letting the rhythm of the downpour lull me into a half-sleep. The lantern on the table flickered, casting long shadows across the walls, each one telling a story I didn’t need to hear.

I had come here to escape, not to remember. But the night was not kind, and neither was the silence that followed. The rain stopped abruptly, leaving behind a quiet stillness, heavy with the weight of unsaid things. I sat for a long time, staring into the cup, its contents long gone cold.

The first light of dawn crept through the windows, painting the room in soft, golden streaks. I stood, stretching my limbs, the weight of the night still pressing on my shoulders. The village was waking up, but I was already gone, my footsteps silent against the cobblestones.

I had forgotten to mention the letter in my coat pocket—the one that should have been burned before leaving. But the storm had taken everything else, so perhaps it didn’t matter now.
