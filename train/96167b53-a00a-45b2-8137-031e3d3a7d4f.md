A sunrise painted in gold and pink, barely enough time to steal a glimpse before the city woke. Coffee steamed, fingers tracing the rim of the cup—each sip a battle against the weight of a day that stretched like a thread, half-torn from the past.

Work emails arrived in a blur, names and tasks floating like confetti in a hurricane. The lunch break was a stolen moment at a café window, watching children dart between cars, their laughter sharp and fleeting. By afternoon, the screen felt like a mirror reflecting exhaustion, every keystroke a whisper against the noise of "tomorrow."

Dinner was a quiet affair, the TV humming in the background, a movie starting—something about a man chasing a dream, but his own had already vanished into the static. He ordered a room service plate of pasta, the noodles cold, the sauce too thick to cut through the heaviness. The remote clicked, but the screen stayed dark.

Late-night thoughts tangled like old wires, each thought a loose end, pulling against the weight of what was left unsaid. The clock chimed midnight, but the room was still, the day unspent, the night yet to come—just another stretch of time, too long to fill, too short to forget.
