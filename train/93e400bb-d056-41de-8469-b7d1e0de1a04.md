A mist-shrouded coastal cliffside at dusk, where the jagged teeth of rocks bite into the sea’s whisper. The wind carries salt and the distant cry of gulls, but the tide is receding, exposing blackened stones where the ocean once kissed them. Below, a narrow path winds toward the abyss, its surface slick with dew and the faint, blue-tinged glow of bioluminescent plankton. The air hums with unseen life—crickets and the occasional splash of a hidden wave. At your feet, a broken lantern lies half-buried, its glass cracked like a cracked promise. The horizon fades into the twilight, where the sky and sea blur into an endless, hungry void. No one comes this way. Not often. Not anymore.
