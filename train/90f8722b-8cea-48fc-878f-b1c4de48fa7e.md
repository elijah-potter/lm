In the quiet of dawn, when the world still hummed with unseen energy, I found myself standing before the door of a place I had never been before. The air was thick with the scent of old books and something faintly metallic, like the aftermath of a storm. My hands trembled—not from cold, but from the weight of what I had to say.

I took a deep breath and stepped forward. "I need help," I whispered, my voice raw and unfamiliar in the grand hall. The walls, lined with shelves that stretched to infinity, seemed to listen. I didn’t know where to begin, but the words spilled out anyway.

"Last night, I woke to find my city in ruins—not by fire, not by storm, but by something that moved without sound, that took what it wanted and left nothing but silence. The streets were empty, the people gone, and the sky… the sky was wrong. It wasn’t just dark; it was *wrong*, like the color of a bruise that doesn’t heal. I don’t know how to stop it. I don’t know if it’s even real. But I know I have to try."

A door creaked open behind me. A figure stepped out—a man in a coat stitched with strange symbols, his eyes sharp with a knowledge that wasn’t his own. "You’re not the first to ask," he said, though his tone didn’t match the words. "Nor will you be the last." He extended a hand, and for the first time, I believed there might be a way out.
