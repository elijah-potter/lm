The neon glow of the city pulses like a slow heartbeat, its artificial light bleeding through the smog-laden air, painting everything in hues of sickly violet and electric blue. Here, where the hum of distant engines and the occasional wail of a siren drown out the whispers of the wind, time feels heavier—each second a weight suspended between what was and what might be.

---
**A man in a raincoat** stumbles past the flickering streetlamp, his fingers brushing against the cold metal of a broken sign. The words beneath are half-erased, but the name remains: *"Lost."* He doesn’t know if it’s his own, or just another ghost clinging to the edge of the pavement. The umbrella he clutched earlier has long since turned to waterlogged paper, its fabric torn at the seams. He doesn’t remember picking it up, but the weight of it still lingers in his pockets like a debt owed to the rain.

---
Outside, the pavement is slick with the same unnatural sheen—the city’s own slick, a mirror for the faces of those who never made it home. The traffic moves in slow motion, a river of rusted steel and plastic, each car a ship drifting toward the horizon without a compass. Somewhere in this endless drift, a child laughs too loudly, a sound cut short by the weight of the world’s indifference.

The sky above is a bruise, the kind that doesn’t fade. It isn’t gray; it’s the color of old wounds, the kind that never fully heal. And beneath it all, the city breathes—deep, uneven, and still. Waiting.
