**Ephemeral Hubbub:**
A weathered **boardwalk** in a city that never quite stabilizes—pavement cracks refill with saltwater every monsoon, neon signs flicker between languages that shift with the tide. **Rooftops** of crumbling warehouses, their corrugated metal rusted to the shape of half-finished dreams. **Brick walls** in the alleyways are plastered with flyers in fading ink—some for "last night’s gigs," others for "handyman—one job, one fee." A **tent city** of folding chairs and tarp-covered stalls thrives where the old train tracks used to run, but the tracks are just a trail now, overgrown with vines and litter. The air hums with the distant sound of a **drum machine** that’s not quite a band, just a looped, glitchy heartbeat. A **street vendor** sells "mystery meat" from a cart wrapped in duct tape, his smile a mix of exhaustion and something sharper—like the edge of a knife.

In the center of it all, a **floating market** on a canal, but the boats are just **docked umbrellas**, their canopies tied to poles in the mud. Vendors hawk "one-day-only" trinkets—pearls that glint like broken glass, maps of places that don’t exist on any map, a **single, perfect cherry blossom** preserved in a vial labeled *"Last bloom of the season."*

The sky is a **canvas of static**, the kind that makes your eyes ache if you stare too long. The wind carries the scent of **burnt sugar** and ozone, and the only thing that seems to last is the **sound of a child’s laughter**, echoing from a broken playground that’s been there since before anyone remembers why they’re here.
