The neon glow of the abandoned subway tunnel flickered as if caught in a perpetual loop. The air hummed with a static that thrummed in the bones—every step echoed like a drumroll, every breath a whisper in a vast, silent expanse. A rusted handrail groaned under her weight, its metal fibers stretching thin as time itself.

She crouched on the cracked platform, her pulse a rapid, disjointed hammer against her ribs. The distant rumble of trains—once a steady, relentless pulse—now stretched into a slow, deliberate crawl. The shadows on the walls blurred into abstract shapes, their edges warping like melted wax.

A train’s engine, just visible through the mist, moved forward at an unnatural pace, its wheels grinding against the tracks like a thousand teeth. The platform beneath her seemed to ripple, the ground beneath her feet stretching and shrinking in a dance of slow motion.

She reached out, fingers brushing the cold metal of the tunnel wall—each touch felt like a thousand brushstrokes, each sensation layered over itself. A single drop of condensation hit her palm. It wasn’t just falling; it was *unfolding*, its surface rippling like a slow, liquid wave.

The train’s headlight, once a blinding glare, now pulsed like a heartbeat. The engineer’s voice, if he were even there, sounded like a whisper in a distant echo chamber. The air around her thickened, every sound warping into something alien.

She gasped. The world around her stretched, every detail—every grain of dust, every flicker of light—stretched into something vast and unyielding. The train’s wheels spun faster. The tunnel itself seemed to breathe, expanding and contracting in slow, deliberate waves.

Then—

The moment shattered. The world snapped back into its usual rhythm. She gasped, catching her breath, the tunnel’s stale air hitting her lungs like a slap. The train’s engine roared to life, the rumble of its movement a relentless, unbroken rhythm.

She stood, trembling, as the last traces of the slow-motion haze faded. The subway was still. The station was still. And she was back in the world she thought she’d left behind.
