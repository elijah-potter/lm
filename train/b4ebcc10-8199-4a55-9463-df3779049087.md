Once upon a time, in a corporate boardroom, a new policy was unveiled: **"No late submissions."** The memo was passed out, the meeting adjourned, and the directive was filed under "Internal Procedures." For the next three months, every deadline was met to the letter—no exceptions. Then, the chaos hit: a team member’s server crashed, a hurricane disrupted travel plans, and a last-minute client emergency demanded all hands on deck. By the fourth quarter, the rule had been forgotten—until the audit.

Fast forward to the next year, the same boardroom. A new IT audit revealed the same old issue: missed deadlines, missed emails, missed *everything*. The "No late submissions" rule was dusted off, but this time, it wasn’t a one-time fix—it was a recurring lesson. Somewhere between the memo and the audit, the lesson had been learned, but the system was already broken.

---
**Rule:** *"Deadlines are not suggestions; they are contracts. Treat them as such—even if you don’t have to remember."*
