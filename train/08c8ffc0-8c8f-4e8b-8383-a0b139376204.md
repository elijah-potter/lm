The subway doors hissed shut behind him, a sharp metallic clap echoing through the dimly lit car. He exhaled sharply, gripping the armrest as the train lurched forward, sending a wave of condensation dripping onto his coat sleeve. His phone buzzed—an unknown number—his pulse quickening.

*"Late again?"* he muttered, rubbing his temples. The 3 AM clock on his desk read **7:05 AM**. His boss’s text was already waiting: *"Where are you? I need the report by 8."*

He had been in a meeting with his team at the old warehouse downtown, arguing over the quarterly projections. His assistant had called five minutes before the meeting ended, saying his car was stuck in traffic. The "emergency" had been a lie—he’d just wanted to avoid the rush-hour crush.

Now, the train rattled to a stop, and he stepped onto the platform, relief flooding him. The doors opened, and he nearly collided with a sleepy-looking guy in a worn leather jacket, who muttered *"Damn,"* then nodded toward the exit.

His phone buzzed again—this time his own number. *"I’m on my way. Be there in 10."* He smirked. Ten minutes. That was how long it took to get here.

Outside, the cold air hit him like a slap. He adjusted his jacket, already calculating the time he’d have to waste on the train to the office. Somewhere, a coffee shop’s blender spun, the sound of a stranger’s half-asleep voice drifting through the open window.

He took a deep breath, exhaling through his nose. *Just ten minutes.*

The train’s engine roared to life, and he boarded, already mentally drafting his apology email.
