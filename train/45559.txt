How to Edit Wikipedia Articles
Wikipedia is a collaborative platform with articles written by anonymous volunteers who write without pay. Editing a Wikipedia page is simple and does not require any special skills or knowledge. However, a few guidelines and rules should be followed to ensure that your edits are constructive and beneficial to the page.
Editing a Wikipedia page refers to making changes to existing content or adding new content to a page. By editing a Wikipedia page, you are helping to create and maintain accurate, up-to-date, and well-sourced information for the public. Anyone can edit a Wikipedia page, and users who see errors or omissions are encouraged to make corrections. If you're looking forward to making your first edit, you should know a few things. This guide will cover all the basics of editing a Wikipedia page, from how to find a page to edit to the different types of edits you can make.
Who Can Edit Articles on Wikipedia?
Anyone can edit Wiki articles - you don't need to have any special qualifications. All you need is a willingness to contribute and a desire to help make the world's largest encyclopedia as accurate and comprehensive as possible. Wikipedia is a collaborative project, which means that multiple people can work on the same article at the same time. This makes it possible for articles to be constantly updated with new information, which is why Wikipedia is such a valuable resource.
Of course, there are some guidelines that you should follow when editing articles. These are designed to keep the encyclopedia accurate and reliable and ensure that everyone can have a good experience using Wikipedia. If you're not sure about something, the best thing to do is ask a question from other Wikipedia editors on the talk page associated with the article you're interested in. Always remember to be respectful of other people's work when editing an article. If you make a mistake, don't worry - anyone can edit an article to fix it.
How To Find Wikipedia Articles That Need Editing
Editing Wiki articles to keep the site updated with accurate information is crucial. However, knowing where to start or what articles need editing can be difficult. This guide will explain how to find Wikipedia articles that need editing. One way to find articles that need editing is to look at the "Recent changes" page. This page lists all of the most recent edits made to Wikipedia articles. By looking at this page, you can see which articles have been edited recently and which have not.
You can also look at the "talk" pages for each article. The talk pages are where users discuss the changes that should be made to an article. By looking at the talk pages, you can see which articles are being discussed and which need editing. Finally, you can also ask other users for help finding articles that need editing. When you find an article that needs editing, it is essential to read the entire article before making any changes. This will help you understand what the article is about and what changes need to be made.
The Procedure to Follow When Editing Wikipedia Articles
In just a few steps, you can be on your way to editing Wikipedia entries! Below are the procedures to follow:
1. Register an account with Wikipedia
First, you need to sign up for a Wikipedia account. This is a quick process that requires an email address and a password. Once you have created your account, you can begin editing articles!
When you find an article, you would like to edit, simply click on the "edit" button at the top of the page. This will bring you to the editing interface, where you can make changes.
There are two ways to edit Wikipedia pages:
- Through the visual editor: The visual editor is an excellent way for beginners to start editing. It functions similarly to a word processor and allows you to see your changes as you make them.
- In source editor: Source mode is an advanced method to edit pages and allows you to edit the wiki markup code directly. This mode is excellent for experienced editors who are familiar with the code.
2. Visit the talk page of the article
This is where users discuss changes to make to the article. If you don't see a discussion about the specific change you want to make, start a new discussion. In the discussion, explain what change you want to make and why. If other users agree with your proposed change, go ahead and make it! If there is disagreement about your proposed change, try to reach a consensus with other users.
3. Check for broken links
Next, you will want to check the article for any broken links. This can be done by clicking on the "link" button at the top of the page. This will open up a list of all the links in the article. If any links are broken, they will be marked in red. Click the "edit" button next to the broken link and enter the correct URL.
4. Add citations
Reliable sources must back up all Wikipedia articles. These sources can be either external (such as a news article) or internal (such as another Wikipedia article).
To add a citation, click on the "cite" button at the top of the page. This will open up a citation template.
Fill in as much information as possible, and then click "Insert." The citation will be added to the article.
5. Add internal links
Internal links are links to other Wikipedia articles. They help readers explore related topics and make the Wikipedia experience more enjoyable.
To add an internal link: Highlight the text you want to turn into a link. Click the "Insert link" button above the edit window. A popup window will appear. In the "Link title" field, type in the name of the page you want to link to. Click "OK." That's it! Your link will now appear in the article.
6. Add references
References are external sources that support the information in an article. They can be a URL or a citation to a book or article.
To add a reference, click on the "References" button at the top of the page. This will open up a reference template.
Fill in the template with as much information as possible. The more information you can provide, the better.
7. Add relevant images (with captions) if required, and ensure that they are legally free to use
Images can make an article more enjoyable and help readers visualize the topic.
To add an image:
- Click on the "Add features and media" button in the editor
- Click on "Media"
- Enter the file name of the image in the search bar (make sure to check the "license" drop-down menu to ensure you are only searching for images that are legally free to use)
- Click on the image you wish to add, then click "Insert"
- Add a caption in the "Caption" field
8. Categorize your article
Categorizing articles helps readers find similar articles on Wikipedia and makes browsing easier. To add categories to your article, scroll down to the bottom of the main editing window. Under "Categories", type in the category you want to add, then click "Add category".
9. In the "edit summary" box which appears before you save your changes, briefly describe the changes you have made
The "edit summary" is a short description of your changes to an article. It appears under the article's title on the history page, allowing other editors to understand what changes you have made quickly.
10. Click "Publish changes"
Once satisfied with your changes, click "Publish changes" to make them live on the article. The changes will be published immediately, and anyone looking at the article will see the updated version.
