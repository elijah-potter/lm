The first mistake came in the form of a loose screw on the old clock in the attic. Elias had been fixing it for weeks, and finally, after applying a drop of oil and a few turns of the wrench, the hands moved again—though they were off by a day. He checked his pocket watch; it was still correct. He checked the clock again. The hands were wrong. Elias didn’t know why, but he trusted the pocket watch.

That night, he woke to the sound of something scraping outside his window. He opened it cautiously, expecting rats or mice. Instead, he saw a figure—tall, cloaked, and holding a lantern. Elias froze. The figure tilted the lantern toward him, revealing a face half-hidden by shadow. "You should have fixed the clock right," the stranger whispered.

Elias stumbled back. "What do you mean?"

The stranger stepped closer. "Time doesn’t move in straight lines. It bends. And you just let it slip through your fingers." The figure vanished before Elias could respond. The next morning, the clock was still wrong, but Elias noticed something else: the sky was brighter, the air sharper. He had missed the sunrise.

By noon, the pocket watch had stopped. Elias checked his watch again—it was still running, but the clock in the attic was wrong. He checked his watch. It was wrong. Elias dropped it. The world seemed to shift around him. He reached for the clock, but the hands spun wildly, then froze midway between twelve and one. The attic door creaked open.

A second figure stood there, this one smiling. "You see?" the first voice said. "You can’t trust anything."

The clock struck midnight. Elias looked at his hands. They were wrong. He had made a mistake, but the mistake had made him wrong. The figures vanished, and the clock chimed. Twelve strikes. Then twelve again. Elias looked down—his reflection was wrong. It was smiling. His hands moved on their own, twisting the screw back into the clock’s face.

The hands turned. The clock was right. Elias exhaled. He had fixed it. But the world was wrong. The pocket watch still ran, but the sky had darkened. The figures were still there, watching. The clock chimed twelve. Elias stood, watching. The pocket watch stopped. The clock struck again. Twelve times.

The attic door opened. The figures were gone. The clock was right. Elias looked at his hands—his reflection was wrong. It was smiling. He picked up the pocket watch, turned it over. The second hand was still moving. The clock was right. He looked at the clock in the attic—it was wrong.

The figures returned. They were everywhere. In the mirror, in the window, in the walls. The clock struck twelve. Elias stood, watching. The pocket watch stopped. The clock struck again. Twelve times.

The figures were silent. The clock was still wrong. Elias looked at his hands. They were wrong. He had fixed the clock, but the mistake had never ended. The figures were gone. The clock was still wrong. Elias looked at the clock in the attic—it was right.

The pocket watch had stopped. The clock struck twelve. Elias stood, watching. The figures were silent. The clock was still right. The mistake had become his own. The world was wrong, but the clock was right. Elias picked up the pocket watch, turned it over. The second hand was still moving. The clock was right.

The figures returned. They were gone. The clock was still right. Elias looked at his hands. They were wrong. He had fixed the clock, but the mistake had never ended. The clock struck twelve. Elias stood, watching. The world was wrong, but the clock was right. The pocket watch stopped. The clock struck again.

Elias woke. The clock in the attic was right. His pocket watch was still moving. The sky was dark. The figures were gone. He checked the clock. Twelve strikes. He checked the pocket watch. The second hand was still moving. The clock was right. The mistake had become him.

Outside, the wind howled. The clock struck. Elias opened his eyes. The figures were silent. The clock was right. He had fixed it. But the world was wrong. The clock struck twelve. Elias looked at his hands—wrong. The pocket watch stopped. The clock struck again. Twelve times.

The figures returned. They were gone. The clock was still right. Elias looked at the clock in the attic—it was wrong. He had fixed the clock, but the mistake had never ended. The pocket watch had stopped. The clock struck twelve. Elias stood, watching. The world was wrong, but the clock was right. He had made a mistake. The mistake had made him wrong. The clock struck again.

Twelve.
