A sprawling, semi-abandoned warehouse in a quiet suburban neighborhood, repurposed as a communal hub. Its high ceilings and fluorescent lighting cast eerie shadows, while cracked vinyl floors host makeshift seating made of cardboard boxes and discarded furniture. No clear schedule exists—attendance fluctuates between 20 and 50 individuals, most of whom wear casual, slightly rumpled clothing. Some bring food, others bring tools or art supplies. Conversations drift between mundane topics and cryptic references to "the next project," but no one enforces structure. A large, unmarked door leads to a back alley where discarded items—old electronics, scrap metal, and a few pieces of fabric—accumulate. Occasional fireworks crackle in the distance, and a faint, repetitive sound of a metronome ticks in one corner. No official records, no bylaws; just a shared, unspoken understanding that this place is a temporary sanctuary for those drawn to its odd, unplanned energy.
