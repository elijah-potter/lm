Two figures stand at opposite ends of a dimly lit tavern, their postures rigid. One clutches a glass of sour wine, the other a half-empty tankard of ale. The air hums with unspoken tension.

The bartender leans against the counter, arms crossed. "The stable boy came in, said there’s been an accident. A horse fell—killed. The guards are still searching the stable."

The first man—tall, with a scar running from his temple to his jaw—stares at the floor. His fingers tighten around his glass. "He was mine. Mine for the season."

The second man—leaning against the wall, his coat worn thin—nods slowly. "So? Horses die. It happens."

The first man’s jaw clenches. "Mine didn’t. It was mine. You mark my words, I’ll see it’s counted." He pulls out a crumpled receipt from his pocket, smooths it. "This is the last payment. The butcher’s ledger. The price I was promised."

The second man exhales through his nose. "And if it wasn’t yours?"

The first man’s voice drops to a growl. "Then you’ll know why the guards are searching *for me* now."

Silence. The second man’s eyes flick to the stable door, where the sound of muffled shouting drifts in. The first man’s grip on the glass betrays his trembling.

"Worth it," he mutters. "Worth every coin."
