The last light of dusk bleeds through the cracked windowpanes, painting the crumbling floor in gold. The air smells of damp earth, burnt coffee, and something older—old wood, old paper, old secrets left to rust. The walls, once white, are now a sickly yellow, peeling like a blister. A single, flickering bulb casts long shadows across the floor where a cracked vinyl rug once covered a pattern of faded roses. The bookshelves groan under the weight of dust and time; spines are split, pages curled like dying leaves. A half-empty bottle of whiskey sits on the counter, its neck blackened with age. The refrigerator hums weakly, its contents long gone—only a single moldy sandwich wrapper remains. The last thing you notice is the faint echo of laughter from a room that’s been abandoned for years, now just a hollow space where laughter used to ring. The door is slightly ajar, the hinges groaning as if whispering goodbye.
