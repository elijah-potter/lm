A quiet afternoon in a crowded café, where the hum of whispered conversations and clinking cups felt like a foreign language. She sat across from a group of strangers, each absorbed in their own world—phones, laughter, animated discussions. Her hands fidgeted with her sleeves, the fabric too tight, too familiar, yet nothing felt right. The familiar scent of coffee and cinnamon rolled over her, but her stomach twisted, unsure if it was hunger or the weight of being an outsider. The walls of the café seemed to pulse with energy she couldn’t grasp, and the warmth of the space felt like a shield, keeping her out. She wondered if anyone else noticed the way she stood apart, the way her thoughts lingered on the edges of the conversation, half-listening, half-observing. The moment felt like a slow unraveling of a carefully stitched together identity.
