**The First Light**

The morning had always been a cruel joke. The air smelled of damp earth and rusted metal, the kind of stale, unwashed scent that clung to the back of your throat like a forgotten promise. You woke to the same dull ache in your bones, the same slow, grinding routine of pulling on socks that were too small, of staring at your reflection in the cracked mirror of the bathroom stall, where your hair was still damp from last night’s rain. The city hummed outside, indifferent, as it always did—cars honking, sirens wailing, the distant clatter of a thousand hands moving in unison across the same pavement.

You had been waiting for this moment for years.

The train arrived at 7:43, just like clockwork. The compartment was already packed with people who didn’t glance at you—who didn’t care. Your seat was next to an old man with a newspaper folded like a broken wing, his fingers trembling as he read the same headline every morning. You left your bag on the floor, half-asleep, and sat down, watching the world blur past the window like a bad dream.

By the time the train slowed to a stop, the sky had turned the color of bruised flesh. The platform was crowded with people who moved like ghosts in a place that had forgotten how to be real. A girl with too much makeup and too little understanding approached you, her voice sharp as a blade.

*"Lost?"*

You didn’t answer. You didn’t have to.

She leaned in, close enough that you could smell the whiskey on her breath. *"You look like you’ve been running for a while."*

The words hit you like a punch to the gut. You didn’t fight it. You let her see it—the way the world had started to tilt, the way the weight of everything you’d carried for so long suddenly felt lighter, like you were floating just below the surface of something you didn’t understand.

She reached out, her fingers brushing yours. *"You know, most people who find themselves in places like this… they don’t make it out."*

You pulled your hand back. The train was already moving again, carrying you forward, deeper into the heart of the unknown.

---

The train doors hissed shut behind you, sealing you in the dark. The girl’s laughter echoed in the empty compartment, sharp and final. You stared at your hands, at the way the light in them had changed—cooler, sharper, like something had shifted inside you.

Outside, the city breathed. And you, for the first time, knew you were ready to breathe with it.
