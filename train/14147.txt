The destruction of the tooth structure is called Cavity or tooth decay. Tooth decay cannot only affect the enamel (the outer coating on the tooth), but also the dentin (the dense, bony tissue below the enamel). If the dentin may get affected, it is much difficult to treat. So, before your dental issues go severe, visit Dentist Bolton for the best treatment.
Here we elaborated the primary causes of the cavity, its symptoms, and a few prevention tips to avoid it:
Causes of Tooth Decay
The primary causes of tooth decay are mentioned below:
- Inadequate Brushing: plaque starts building up on teeth after 20 minutes of eating. Avoid brushing soon after eating results in plaque buildup.
- Eating and Drinking: When you eat and drink, Carbohydrates stay on your teeth until you brush.
- Dry Mouth: Plaque can build up more speedily if you have very little saliva in your mouth.
- Bacteria & Acid:
- Medical Issues: Some medical treatments like chemotherapy or other cancer treatments cause bacteria to build up in your mouth, as a result, plaque buildup.
- Eating Disorders
- Nighttime Infant Feedings: Babies food like Formula, milk, juice, or other liquids stays on baby’s and toddler’s teeth for hours during their sleep. As a consequence, they have tooth decay.
Signs and Symptoms of Tooth Decay
The common symptoms that indicated you have tooth decay are as below:
- Toothaches – sharp, spontaneous pain
- Visible holes or pits in your teeth
- Tooth sensitivity
- Pain when eating something sweet, hot, or cold
- Pain when chewing or biting into food
- Staining on the surface of your teeth
Follow the following prevention tips to avoid tooth decay:
- With fluoride-based toothpaste, brush your teeth at least twice a day.
- Avoid having snacks between meals.
- Drink tap water or public water as it contains fluoride
- Eat a healthy and well-balanced diet and, avoid consuming foods that are heavy in carbohydrates.
- Rinse at least once a day with a mouthwash that contains fluoride.
- Use Dental sealants: It is a protective plastic coating that helps to cover your molars, seals off grooves and cracks where the food may get stuck.
