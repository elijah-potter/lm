A small, dimly lit **bohemian jazz club** in a high-rise apartment building, where the neon "OPEN" sign flickers like a dying star. The crowd—mostly elderly librarians, a former mime artist, and a retired jazz musician in a stained 1970s blazer—dances to a distorted, vinyl-scratching set by a local DJ who plays a mix of 1960s doo-wop and a 1980s synthwave remix of a 1920s ragtime piece. The only glaring inconsistency: a **children’s birthday party** in full swing across the lobby, complete with inflatable slides, a clown in a top hat, and a piñata shaped like a tiny, confused elephant. The birthday girl—an 8-year-old with a glittery tiara—keeps asking, *"Why are we here, Mom?"* while the parents in the corner exchange glances. A single, mismatched **glittery crown** from the party floats onto a table, where it clatters onto a half-empty whiskey glass. The DJ pauses mid-set, squints at it, and sighs, *"Kids these days."* The birthday girl suddenly grabs a nearby **candle in a tiny lantern** (from the party) and tosses it into the air, setting off a cascade of sparklers that briefly illuminate the ceiling before the smoke and fireworks dissolve into static. The crowd murmurs, then erupts in a hushed, awkward applause. The DJ whispers, *"That’s the most original thing I’ve heard all night."* The librarian next to him mutters, *"I’ll be damned."* Outside, the apartment building’s elevator doors slide open, revealing a **sleek, modern luxury penthouse** where a family of four—all in designer attire—are having a formal dinner with a live jazz pianist. One of the kids, a 5-year-old in a tiny tuxedo, accidentally knocks over a vase, and the pianist sighs, *"Another night."* The elevator doors close. The birthday girl’s mom sighs. *"What now?"* The DJ turns to the crowd. *"Alright, let’s dance."* They start moving to the music—some in sync, some in terrible rhythm—while the party in the lobby continues unchecked, oblivious. The mismatched crown glints in the neon, the half-empty whiskey glass is now half-full with something darker, and somewhere, a child’s laughter echoes through the hall.
