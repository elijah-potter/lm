One morning, a person started replacing their nightly coffee with a daily espresso ritual, claiming it improved focus. Initially, it worked—study hours increased, but the habit expanded: afternoon energy crashes led to another shot, then an afternoon pick-me-up, and finally, a third. By month two, caffeine dependence was evident: withdrawal headaches followed missed doses, and the habit had become an addiction. The person’s sleep quality deteriorated, leading to fatigue and irritability during the day. The original intent—boosting productivity—had morphed into a cycle of dependency and stress, overshadowing the mental clarity it was meant to enhance. The routine now controlled them more than they controlled it.
