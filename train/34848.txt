A rise in the number of cases of swine flu that are resistant to Tamiflu has raised fresh doubts about the effectiveness of the controversial drug.
Research presented in Australia today also revealed that these strains of flu appeared in patients who had never been treated with the drug, which means the strains were passed from person to person.
Britain has spent more than £500 million stockpiling Tamiflu as a precautionary measure to combat an outbreak of swine flu. However, the drugs company Roche, which makes Tamiflu, has been accused of withholding evidence that suggests it is barely more effective than paracetamol in treating swine flu.
