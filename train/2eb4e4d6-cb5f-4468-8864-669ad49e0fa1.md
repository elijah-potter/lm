The meeting had just concluded when the sudden gust of wind through the open bay window shattered the fragile momentum of their strategy. The leader, arms crossed, stared at the scattered papers on the conference table, half-burned in the candlelight. "We were going to—" she trailed off, glancing at the clock. The others exchanged glances, some rubbing their temples, others staring at their phones like they’d been handed a bad news alert.

Outside, the rain had begun, pattering against the windows in a rhythm that mirrored the tension inside. The first speaker cleared their throat, then paused. "We’re pulling the trigger on the project," they said, more to themselves than anyone else.

The room fell silent. No one moved. The air smelled of damp wood and old coffee. The plan wasn’t dead—it was just… *waiting*.
