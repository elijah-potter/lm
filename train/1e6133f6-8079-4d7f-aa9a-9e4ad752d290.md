A small, worn wooden figurine with a cracked face sits between fingers. One person’s hand, calloused from manual labor, grips it tightly, thumb brushing the worn carving of a ragged figure holding a rusted knife. The object is passed to another’s palm—a younger, more delicate grip—who adjusts it, fingers tracing the grooves of the blade’s hilt. The recipient’s breath catches slightly, eyes flicking to the detail of the figurine’s patina. The exchange happens quickly, hands darting in an unspoken rhythm, the figurine’s weight a silent mediator between two lives. The next hand, a third, takes it with a practiced ease, fingers already forming a loop around the figurine’s base. The scene repeats in a loop, hands shifting like dancers in a forgotten ballet.
