A new urban park is emerging in the heart of the city’s underused lot, lined with young bamboo and wildflower seedlings. Workers use wheelbarrows of topsoil and rain barrels to prep the soil, while local kids sketch designs in the dirt. The first wooden play structures are being bolted together by volunteers, their hands calloused but eager. Nearby, a small café’s pop-up sign flickers: *"Breakfast at the New Routine"*—coffee cups are already half-full before 7 AM. The city’s forgotten edges are becoming a rhythm: early morning joggers, teenagers playing soccer, and old men trading gossip near the new community garden plots. The gridlines of the old city are bending, and something new is taking root.
