Smooth obsidian glistens under the sun, its dark surface etched with ancient runes that shift subtly with humidity. The air hums with static, carrying the scent of damp earth and ozone. Roughened granite forms jagged ridges beneath worn leather boots, each step revealing layers of time—pitted with moss and embedded with quartz crystals that catch the light like scattered stars. Beneath your palms, the stone feels like memory itself, warm in places, cold in others, as if the earth remembers the weight of your touch. The wind carries the whisper of whispering pines, their bark peeling in strips, revealing the gnarled roots twisting like serpents through the soil. Moss blankets the forest floor in thick, velvety patches, damp and yielding, while the forest floor itself feels like a tapestry of decaying leaves, brittle but yielding to the pressure of bare feet. The air smells of damp earth, crushed pine needles, and distant rain. The ground is uneven—some places soft with damp moss, others sharp with exposed roots or smooth with fallen logs. A patch of lichen clings to the bark of an ancient tree, its texture like fine sandpaper, dulling the edge of your fingernail as you brush against it.
