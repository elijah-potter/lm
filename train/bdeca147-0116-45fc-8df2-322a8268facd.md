The rain finally stopped at dusk, puddles reflecting the last golden light of the setting sun. As I sat on the rooftop of the abandoned bookstore, a book I’d carried for weeks—its spine cracked with age—dropped into my hands. Inside, a poem I’d been searching for: *"Light bends when it hits glass, but glass stays straight."* The words, scribbled by a stranger in the 1980s, fit the way my life had felt like a puzzle for too long. The answer wasn’t in the words I’d written, but in the ones I hadn’t yet known to read. For the first time, the fragments of my confusion began to click together—not perfectly, but with a quiet, stubborn clarity. The book closed. The moment was over. But for the first time, I understood why I’d been here at all.
