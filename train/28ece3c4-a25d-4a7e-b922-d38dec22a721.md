A crumbling stone archway, weathered by centuries, stands half-buried in a forgotten garden. Its bricks bear the faintest traces of moss, yet the carvings—once proud of an ancient coat of arms—now look like a child’s scribbles. The garden itself is a labyrinth of overgrown paths, where ivy climbs like a living thing, swallowing the stones whole. The air hums with the persistent drip of water from an unseen fountain, and the scent of damp earth lingers, thick and unchanging. A rusted sign, cracked and illegible, still clings to a post: *"This Place Belongs to No One"*—though it knows better. The soil remembers the roots of trees that fell long ago, their hollow trunks forming a natural amphitheater where fireflies gather in summer evenings. The earth here is old, but the memory of its former purpose is only a whisper. The sky above is vast and endless, as if the heavens themselves are holding their breath, waiting to see if this place will last another day.
