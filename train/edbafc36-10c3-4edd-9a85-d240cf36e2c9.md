A flickering candle burns in an empty room, its wax pooling unevenly against the cracked glass of a cracked mirror. The incantation is repeated in a monotone voice, the syllables stretching like a dying breath:

*"By blood and bone, by rusted chains,*
*by echoes left behind,*
*the weight of what was lost still clings."*

The bowl of salt swirls in the light, but no salt remains—only dust. The knife’s edge is dull, its edge worn smooth by decades of use. The sacrifice was once a binding: the first drop of blood from the wrist, the tear of the firstborn’s eye, the scream of a chosen one’s throat. Now, the blood drips into the bowl like rain, and the tears fall silently onto the floor.

A prayer is whispered into the void: *"Speak, if you still remember."* The walls whisper back, but only static. The altar is a pile of ashes and forgotten bones, arranged in the shape of a cross that no longer points to heaven. The ritual is complete, but nothing happens. The candle goes out. The ritual has outlived its purpose. It has become just another story carved into the bones of the past, waiting to be forgotten.
