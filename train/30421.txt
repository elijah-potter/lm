The first Canadian case of a deadly pig virus has been confirmed in Ontario, according to the Ontario Ministry of Agriculture and Food.
The porcine epidemic diarrhea (PED) virus was found on a hog farm in Middlesex County, a rural county in Southwestern Ontario.
The virus does not pose a risk to human health, but it is responsible for killing millions of piglets in the United States, Europe and Asia.
The source of the virus is not known and there could be more cases because it is very difficult to contain, the ministry said in a statement.
“All Ontario and Canadian swine farms are extremely vulnerable to this disease and we would urge all producers to immediately review and strengthen their existing biosecurity and disinfection protocols,” said Amy Cronin, chair of Ontario Pork, which represents 1,600 producers in the province.
PED was first discovered in the United States in May and has since spread to 23 states, according to Canadian Swine Health Board. It is a very aggressive virus causing diarrhea and vomiting and kills nearly 100 per cent of infected nursing piglets.
The ministry said it is working closely with the producer, the pork industry, and the Canadian Food Inspection Agency to control the spread of the virus. The producer is fully cooperating with the ongoing investigation and the farm followed strict biosecurity protocols.
“We will continue to monitor the situation and update our producers when more information becomes available,” Cronin said.
