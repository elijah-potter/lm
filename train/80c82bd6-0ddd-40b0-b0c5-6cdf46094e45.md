The classroom buzzed with the hum of whispered conversations, each student absorbed in their own world of textbooks and digital screens. Liam sat hunched over his desk, fingers tracing the worn edges of his notebook, the ink smudged from days of scribbling. He had arrived late, his backpack still damp from the rain that had turned the pavement into a slick mirror. His coat, the one he’d worn that morning, was still wet, the sleeves clinging to his arms like second skin.

He had expected the other students to notice his disheveled appearance—maybe even pity him—but no one did. Instead, they seemed to be in their own heads, lost in the rhythm of lectures and the quiet camaraderie of shared notes. His peers laughed at jokes he couldn’t hear, their laughter echoing off the empty walls. He wasn’t part of it. Not really.

The teacher’s voice cut through the silence, sharp and commanding. *"Liam, would you mind checking the attendance for the group project?"* His name was a foreign word, almost like a warning. He stood up slowly, his chair scraping against the floor, and followed the teacher’s gaze to where his group sat—all of them nodding, engaged, already discussing the task at hand. They didn’t even look up.

He hesitated at the door, the weight of unspoken questions pressing down on him. *Why does everyone else seem so at ease here?* He had done well in school before, but something had changed. Maybe it was the way he carried himself—always a little too quiet, a little too still. Maybe it was the way his hands shook when he wrote, or the way he avoided eye contact when he was nervous.

Outside the classroom, the world felt larger, more indifferent. The streets were alive with people rushing to their destinations, their voices a chorus of familiar sounds. He wondered if he belonged here. Maybe he didn’t. Maybe he was just another face in the crowd, invisible until the moment someone needed to count heads.

His phone buzzed in his pocket. A text from his mom: *"Where are you? We’re worried about you."* He glanced at it, then back at the classroom. The other students were already moving, their energy palpable. He thought about leaving, about disappearing into the crowd of people who didn’t need to be counted.

But then he saw it—the way the teacher’s eyes lingered on him for a second longer than necessary. A flicker of something unreadable crossed her face, something that made him pause. Maybe she thought he was hiding something. Maybe she thought he didn’t belong.

He exhaled slowly, his shoulders slumping. He had never felt so alone before. Not like this. Not like he was just another face in a room, forgotten when the moment of truth came.

The text from his mom lit up again. *"Are you okay?"*

He looked down at his phone, the screen glowing faintly in the dim light. He should answer. He should say something. But what? The words stuck in his throat.

Instead, he stepped back inside, the classroom door clicking shut behind him. The silence that followed was deafening. He sat down at his desk again, his fingers trembling as he reached for his notebook. The page was blank. Not even a single word to fill the space.

He had never felt so out of place.
