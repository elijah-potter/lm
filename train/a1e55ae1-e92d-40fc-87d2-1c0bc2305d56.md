The abandoned factory loomed under a sky choked with rust-colored dusk, its skeletal metal framework stretched like skeletal fingers across the cracked asphalt. A rusted sign, half-buried in weeds, read *"Carpentry Works – Est. 1923"*, its paint peeling to reveal faded letters. The air smelled of damp concrete and old oil, thick with the scent of forgotten machinery—saws that had never stopped, drills that had long since rusted into silence.

Inside, the space pulsed with an eerie, half-lit glow. Broken windows let in a pale, shifting light that seemed to dissolve the edges of the room, warping the shadows into something neither solid nor absent. Dust motes danced in the weak light, and the floorboards groaned underfoot, their creaks echoing like the distant, unfinished whispers of a place that had outgrown its purpose.

A half-finished table stood in the center, its surface covered in half-assembled wooden planks, nails still protruding from the wood, their points sharp enough to catch the eye. Nearby, a workbench was piled with half-worn tools—chisels with blunt edges, hammers that had never struck properly, and a saw blade that had stopped spinning mid-turn, its teeth frozen in an impossible arc.

The walls were lined with shelves where tools had once hung, now empty and waiting. A ladder leaned against the ceiling, its rungs frayed, the top half missing, as if someone had abandoned the climb halfway. The ceiling itself was a network of exposed pipes and rusted beams, their joints loose, as if the building itself had grown tired of holding itself up.

Outside, the wind carried the scent of rain, but the sky above was a heavy, overcast gray, as if the clouds themselves were holding back tears. The place felt like it had been paused mid-crisis—construction halted, machines stopped, the rhythm of labor suspended. The air hummed with the quiet energy of something that had been almost complete, now left to rot.

A single, half-written sketch on the table depicted a door frame, its edges jagged and uneven, as if the wood had been cut too quickly, too carelessly. The lines were messy, the angles sharp and unrefined, as though the artist had abandoned it mid-draw.

The unfinished. That was the key. Not just the lack of completion, but the way it had *stopped*. The way the place had refused to settle into something final, something whole. Instead, it remained a work in progress, a half-built dream left to fester in the dark.
