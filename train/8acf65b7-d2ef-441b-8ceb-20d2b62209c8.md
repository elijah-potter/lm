**The Hollow Light**

The city hummed under the weight of forgotten promises, its neon veins pulsing with the same quiet desperation as the hands of the clock that never quite reached midnight. I sat in a café where the air smelled of stale coffee and something older—something like the breath of a city that had learned to exhale too slowly. The waitress, her fingers tracing the rim of her mug, said, *"You’re early."* I had been early for everything lately.

Outside, the skyline was a ghost of itself, a skeletal frame of glass and steel that refused to remember its own shape. I watched the reflections of the windows on the pavement, how they flickered like dying stars. *"You’re not the first to sit here,"* she murmured, her voice a whisper in the stillness. I had heard that before too.

**Midpoint Shift:**

The bell above the door jingled, sharp and sudden, like a blade cutting through silence. A man in a raincoat stepped inside, his boots splashing against the wet pavement just outside. His coat was damp, his face shadowed by the brim of his hat, but his eyes—oh, his eyes were sharp, like flint. He didn’t ask for coffee. He didn’t need it. He just sat, unspoken, and the air between us thickened, heavy with the weight of what wasn’t said.

The waitress set down two steaming mugs—black, bitter, the kind that burned without mercy. *"This one’s for you,"* she said, her voice low enough that only he heard. I watched them both, the way his fingers brushed the edge of the mug as if testing its weight, the way she lingered before moving to the next table. The city’s quiet had swallowed the tension, but I could still feel it, the pulse of something raw beneath the surface.

Outside, the rain had stopped. The streetlights flickered, casting long, skeletal shadows that danced like ghosts of things that had never been. I exhaled slowly, the breath curling into the air. *"What’s the matter?"* he asked, his voice a blade against the silence.

I didn’t answer. Instead, I reached into my coat and pulled out the photograph—old, yellowed edges, the kind of thing that had been pressed between pages for decades. He took it without hesitation. *"You keep it?"* he murmured.

*"I lost everything else."* The words tasted like ash. The photograph was all I had left of her, of him. The city, it seemed, had been waiting for someone to say it out loud. And now, it was time.
