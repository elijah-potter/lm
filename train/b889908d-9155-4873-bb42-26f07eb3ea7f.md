The radio crackled to life with a static hiss, the only sound in the dimly lit room. A single candle flickered on the table beside **Alex**, whose fingers drummed restlessly against the armrest of the old sofa. The news anchor’s voice, crisp and urgent, cut through the silence:

*"Breaking news—**a major fire at the industrial complex near downtown. Rescue efforts ongoing. Please stay tuned for updates.**"*

Alex exhaled sharply, gripping the blanket tighter. The TV screen flickered—images of smoke curling into the sky, flames licking at the rooftops. Outside, the wind howled through the cracked windows, rattling the glass. A single tear traced a path down Alex’s cheek.

The phone buzzed. A message from **Mira**:

*"They’re alive. Safe in the hospital. Just a burn. Don’t worry. But they need you at the station when they get out."*

Alex’s hands trembled as they scrolled the message, the weight of the moment pressing down like a physical force. The door creaked open. A gust of cold air slashed through the room, and in it came the scent of ash and smoke. **Lena** stood there, her uniform singed, her eyes wild with fear.

*"We got word,"* she said, voice raw. *"They’re okay. But they need you."*
