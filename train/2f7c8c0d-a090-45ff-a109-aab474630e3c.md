A shadowy figure moves through the dimly lit alley at dusk, their coat swirling like smoke. Their face is half-lit by a flickering streetlamp, but their eyes—deep and unfocused—scan the crowd. The crowd itself is a blur of indistinct shapes: figures in hooded jackets, the occasional flash of neon from passing cars. One woman pauses, her gaze lingering on the stranger’s back as they slip past, their breath visible in the cold night air. The stranger turns briefly, their expression unreadable, before disappearing into the crowd. No one recognizes them. No one remembers them. The alley hums with the murmur of unseen voices, the distant wail of a siren. The stranger moves on, leaving behind only the faint scent of rain and the echo of their unheard footsteps.
