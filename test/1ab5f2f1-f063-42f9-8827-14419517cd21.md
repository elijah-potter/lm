The 19th-century French village of Montmartre reported a series of bizarre, inexplicable occurrences following the construction of the Sacré-Cœur basilica’s initial stone foundations. Locals claimed to have witnessed a group of unknown figures in tattered cloaks gathering near the excavation site at midnight, murmuring in a language no human had ever heard. By dawn, workers found the foundations partially covered in an unidentifiable, iridescent substance that dissolved when exposed to sunlight, leaving behind faint, glowing residue that hummed faintly. The substance’s remnants were later analyzed by the Société d’Études Scientifiques, who concluded it was not organic but possessed properties of both solid and liquid states simultaneously—defying known physics. The village’s priest, Father LeBlanc, recorded that the ground trembled slightly after each visit of these figures, and that the local wells, which had been dry for weeks, suddenly began to flow again overnight. No one could explain the phenomenon, but whispers spread that the site was becoming a focal point for unseen forces. The Basilica’s final stone was never laid, as construction halted indefinitely after the events.
