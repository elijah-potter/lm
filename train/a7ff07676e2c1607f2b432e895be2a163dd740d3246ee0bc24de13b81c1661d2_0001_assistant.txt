Pneumonia often begins with a sickness in your throat that moves down, like you're swallowing food that doesn't want to be swallowed. Scientists call it "pneumonitis," or lung inflammation, but you can think of it as an invader. As the invaders try and march into the throat and lungs, your body sends its soldiers, or immune system, to fight it. Scientists call those soldiers "immunoglobulins." If the soldiers fail, the invaders can start to attack your lung's walls, breaking them down and invading the rest of your body. Doctors will be able to tell how bad your pneumonia is by seeing how strong the invaders are and how strong the soldiers are.
