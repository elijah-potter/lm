A dimly lit alleyway, the neon glow of a distant sign casting jagged shadows on the cobblestones. The air hums with the distant chatter of a crowded street, but the quiet of this space makes it feel alive. Rusted metal pipes crisscross above, their reflections shimmering in puddles of water that have collected in the gutter. A single streetlamp flickers, its light barely piercing the smog-choked sky, casting long, wavering beams across the pavement. The walls are lined with graffiti—faded, but still readable—of a forgotten rebellion. A half-eaten sandwich bag lies on the ground, its wrapper twisted into a question mark. The streetlight’s glow dances on the peeling paint, transforming the otherwise mundane into something eerie.
