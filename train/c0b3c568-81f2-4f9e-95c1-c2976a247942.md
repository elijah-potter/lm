The weight of the fork pressed between my fingers felt like a stone. The menu before me—lifelines, thinly veiled with sugar—had been laid out for me like a chessboard. Each dish a potential trap, each course a calculated risk. The first dish, *Miso Soup with Tofu*, whispered of surrender, its umami promise of surrendering to the inevitable. But the second, *Steamed Fish with Brown Rice*, pulsed with defiance, its silken texture a promise of resistance.

The waiter’s voice, soft but insistent, cut through the tension: *"Would you like to start with the miso?"* The words were a question, but the answer was already written in the way the light caught the steam rising from the bowls. I had chosen before I spoke. The choice was not one I made; it was a surrender to the weight of the past, the debt of the future.

I reached for the miso.

The broth swallowed the tofu whole, and with it, any lingering doubt. The fish would wait. The rice, too, would be left for another day. The choice had been made not by my hands, but by the hands of the universe—each forkful a step toward what was inevitable.
