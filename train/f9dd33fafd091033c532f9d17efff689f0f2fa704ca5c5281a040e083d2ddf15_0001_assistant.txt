Ancient Persians iced sweetened water, ground it into little pieces, and decorated it with tasty toppings and fruits. The Greek and Roman empires continued this expensive technique. When the Roman Empire fell, mountain sourced ice trade melted away, making ice cream more expensive.
Italian traders, during the 13th and 14th centuries, brought new ice cream recipes from the Middle East. After an Italian noblewoman introduced the continental nobility to ice cream in 1533, wealthy people drove innovation that increased ice cream’s popularity and availability. Soon, American street vendors sold ice cream.
During the industrial revolution, work began on creating continuous refrigeration. In 1926, electrical freezers became a reality. Manufacturers started producing large quantities of ice cream, which made it more affordable. After WW2, the ice cream industry expanded to include countless flavors and artificial ingredients. Today, ice cream sales, especially in the US, earn billions of dollars each year.
