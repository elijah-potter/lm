**Notes and Areas for Customization:**

*   **Profession:** I selected architectural design for the details. If your profession is different, you *must* replace the specific examples with relevant details. Be precise; vague complaints are less impactful and less professional.
*   **Reason for Leaving:** The reason I provided is relatively pointed. You can soften it if you prefer, but be truthful. A bland resignation letter can feel insincere.  Consider if you want to focus on a lack of growth opportunities, a change in company culture, or something else entirely.
*   **Specific Examples:**  The more specific you can be (without being unnecessarily harsh), the better. "Lack of opportunities" is weak. "The recent decision to eliminate the mentorship program for junior designers" is strong.
*   **Company Culture:**  If the reason for leaving relates to interpersonal issues or a toxic work environment, carefully consider how to phrase it.  You might want to focus on the impact on your ability to perform your work effectively rather than directly criticizing individuals.
*   **Transition Assistance:** Offering assistance with the transition is a professional courtesy.  Be prepared to follow through on this offer.
*   **Legal Review:** If you have concerns about potential legal ramifications (e.g., non-compete agreements), it is always prudent to have this letter reviewed by an attorney.
*   **Delivery:** Consider how you will deliver the letter. A printed, signed copy is often preferred, but an email with a scanned signature is acceptable in many situations.
