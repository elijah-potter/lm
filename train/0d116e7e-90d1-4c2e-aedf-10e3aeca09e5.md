The neon glow of the empty café flickered against the rain-streaked window as Elias sat at a corner table, a half-empty coffee cup between his hands. His fingers traced the rim, the weight of the silence pressing down like a second shadow.

Outside, the city hummed—a distant siren, the clatter of umbrellas, the occasional laugh from a group of strangers passing by. But Elias didn’t hear them. Not really.

He had chosen this: the absence of sound, the absence of words. Not because he was broken, but because some things weren’t meant to be spoken aloud. Some wounds festered best in quiet.

The waiter dropped off a plate of stale croissants, but Elias barely glanced at them. His gaze was fixed on the space between his hands, where the silence seemed to thicken, to hold its own shape.

A clock ticked. The café’s air smelled of stale coffee and damp wool. Elias exhaled slowly, letting the sound of his breath dissolve into the void.

When he finally spoke—if he could call it that—it was a whisper, barely audible over the city’s distant murmur:

*"It’s enough."*

Then silence. Again.
