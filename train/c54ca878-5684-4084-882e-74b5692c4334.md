A quiet promise was made in the fading light of an autumn evening by the old oak tree where the children played as children once did. The words were whispered between the branches, soft as the rustling leaves: *"I’ll be back."*

No one saw her return—just the way the lantern’s glow flickered against the windowpane, the way the dog stopped mid-sip from the cracked tin bowl, the sudden hush in the garden when the crickets ceased their chorus. The house felt lighter after she left, as if an unseen weight had been lifted. She came back the next morning, barefoot in the damp grass, carrying a single wildflower in her hand. She placed it on the kitchen table, its petals trembling like a secret kept too long.

The children found it the next day, crushed between their boots, a single stem still standing. The oak’s branches no longer whispered. The dog still barked at strangers. But the quiet promise had been kept.
