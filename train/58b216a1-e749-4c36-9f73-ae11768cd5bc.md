A gentle rain softened the street lamps, casting a mist over the empty plaza where the last light from the old clock tower flickered out. The iron bell, once loud and persistent, now sat still, its chains unbroken. Inside, the café’s window was half-drawn, its curtains swaying softly in the breeze. A lone figure—wearing a worn coat with a broken zipper—stepped out, their shoes silent on the cobblestones. They lit a cigarette, exhaled slowly, and walked toward the train tracks, where the last passenger car had been left unlocked. The train’s final whistle, muffled by distance, faded into the dusk. No one called out. No one followed. The city, as if remembering, breathed in quiet.
