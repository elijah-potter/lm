**Scene: The Last Train**

*The station platform was dimly lit by flickering streetlamps. Rain pattered against the glass as two strangers—**Lena** (a historian) and **Milo** (a journalist)—stood on opposite sides of the platform, watching the same train arrive.*

Lena adjusted her coat, fingers tracing the worn edge of her notebook.
*"The signal was green, but the guard’s expression… the way his eyes lingered on the clock—he was late. That means the train left *after* midnight."*

Milo adjusted his recorder, his brow furrowed.
*"The conductor’s whistle was sharp, precise. No delay. Just a standard schedule. Unless… unless he was intentionally late to avoid the strike? The union’s been pushing for pay raises for months."*

Lena’s pen paused mid-scribble. *"Or he was avoiding a confrontation with the strikebreakers. The police haven’t moved in for days."*

Milo’s voice dropped, urgent.
*"Then the strikebreakers were already on board. And they didn’t take seats—they stood near the front, watching."*

Lena’s eyes narrowed.
*"So the train didn’t just carry passengers. It carried a warning."*

Silence. The train doors hissed open. No one stepped onto it.
