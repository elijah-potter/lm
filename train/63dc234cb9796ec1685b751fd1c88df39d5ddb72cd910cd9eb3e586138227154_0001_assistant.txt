In a house, dark and large, we stood,
Before bedtime came, a story he sought.
With a hint of fear, in his voice so small,
He asked for tales, not too scary, but enthralling.

I tousled his hair, understanding his plight,
In the warm kitchen's comforting light.
"What story would you like me to weave?"
I asked, eager to make his fears recede.

He pondered a moment, thoughtful and wise,
"Not too scary, but with a hint of surprise.
You write scary stories, or so they say,
But I'm curious about Click-clack the Rattlebag's way."

I admitted my ignorance, not knowing that lore,
But he insisted it was a tale to explore.
"It's the best kind," he confidently claimed,
With a face unimpressed, his point was framed.

As shadows lingered by the door's embrace,
He urged me to lead him to his sleeping place.
A story, not too scary, he wished to receive,
For his bedroom, too, had darkness to conceive.
