**The Last Transmission**

It wasn’t supposed to happen like this.

The stars had aligned differently when she was still a girl in a village where the river ran black with secrets. They hadn’t warned her about the way the air tasted like ozone before the blackout, how the power lines sagged like dying things between the trees. The engineers called it a "temporary" failure, but she knew better. The old clock tower’s pendulum had stopped at 3:17 AM, the same time her father had vanished five years ago.

She found the signal on the third attempt, buried in static like a grave marker. The transmission was raw, the voice a crackle of static that could’ve been a prayer or a warning. *They’re coming for the core.* The words were cut off before she could hear more.

By dawn, the city had a new name on the maps—*Silence*—and the last thing she saw was the glow of a ship’s lights cutting through the smog, its engines humming like a dying beast. She ran until her lungs burned, until the road ended at a fence with barbed wire that still hummed with residual electricity. The door was already open.

The voice in the dark didn’t sound human anymore.
