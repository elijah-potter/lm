A promise made with trembling hands, its words slick as oil on a wet road—each syllable a weight between the speaker’s fingers. The offer came at dawn, whispered through the crack of a door, a promise to turn a cracked promise into gold. They said, *"I’ll be there by the end of the week."* But the week stretched like a river, its banks shifting with each passing hour.

The time was tight, the stakes sharper than a blade. The tools were half-finished, the materials were stolen, and the hands that held them shook like autumn leaves. The clock was a serpent, its coils tightening with every half-hour. The promise, though never broken, was buried in the dirt of unkept vows—thick with dust and the scent of regret.

They never came. The door remained closed. The words, once polished smooth, now chipped and dull. The world kept turning, indifferent as always. The promise was never meant to be kept, only sold—like a lie wrapped in silk.
