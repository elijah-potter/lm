**Mayor O’Reilly announces emergency shelter expansion amid record-breaking heatwave in Rivermere**
Rivermere—Residents of Rivermere are being urged to take immediate action as the city braces for a record-breaking heatwave forecasted to peak at 110°F tomorrow. Mayor Daniel O’Reilly declared a state of emergency at a press conference today, activating the city’s first-ever "Heatwave Response Protocol." The newly expanded emergency shelters, now operating at the old civic center and downtown rec center, will provide cooling stations, free water, and medical assistance for at-risk populations, including elderly residents, children, and outdoor workers.

Local hospitals reported a 40% increase in ER visits for heat-related illnesses since the alert was issued, with over 500 cases logged in the last 12 hours. The city’s parks department has temporarily closed all public spaces, including beaches, to prevent mass overcrowding and ensure safety. Authorities are urging residents to stay indoors, limit outdoor activities to early morning or evening hours, and monitor pets, as heatstroke remains a leading cause of accidental death during extreme heat events.

Schools in Rivermere have canceled all outdoor activities and will operate on reduced schedules until further notice, with remote learning available for all students. The city’s transit system is adjusting routes to minimize exposure, while volunteers from the Red Cross are distributing hydration packs and first-aid kits to neighborhoods most vulnerable to the heat.

Mayor O’Reilly emphasized, *"This is not just a weather event—it’s a public health crisis. We’re asking everyone to treat this like a fire drill: follow the rules, stay informed, and help your neighbors. The city will provide resources, but it’s up to each of us to keep each other safe."* No official curfews have been issued yet, but police will be enforcing a mandatory "cooling down" policy for public spaces after 7 PM.

Meanwhile, the Rivermere Chamber of Commerce has launched a community fundraiser to support displaced businesses and local nonprofits assisting displaced families. Donations can be made online at RivermereHeatRelief.org. The city’s social media channels (@RivermereSOS) will update real-time conditions and evacuation routes. Stay safe—Rivermere will respond.
