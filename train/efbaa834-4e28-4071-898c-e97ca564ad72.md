The man forced a smile as he handed the elderly lady the door. His hands trembled slightly, but his voice remained steady despite the sharp intake of breath.

"Good afternoon," he said, though his throat felt like sandpaper.

She blinked at him, her wrinkled face holding a look that was both confusion and faint amusement. "You don’t even know my name," she replied, her voice soft but unyielding.

He swallowed hard. "I—I didn’t mean to—"

"Don’t apologize," she interrupted, her eyes narrowing. "You’re just too polite."

His face burned. "I—I’m trying."

She let out a small, dry laugh. "Everyone is." She gestured vaguely toward the sidewalk. "The world doesn’t need more polite people. It needs people who don’t care enough to pretend."
