**The Last Light**

She traced the cracks in the old floor with her fingers, where the sun once bled through stained glass in a window long since boarded up. The paint was peeling, revealing the years like layers of dust. Outside, the city hummed—a distant, mechanical heartbeat—while the train tracks below whispered secrets to the wind.

She knelt, her breath shallow. The lantern flickered, casting elongated shadows that danced across the walls. Inside, the air smelled of damp wool and something metallic, like old blood. The last passenger had left hours ago, but the door remained ajar, as if the world outside had forgotten to close it.

Then she saw it.

A reflection in the half-lit mirror by the window—a face that wasn’t hers. It smiled, teeth too sharp, eyes too bright, the way a dream might stretch into something unnatural. The lantern’s flame trembled. The lantern went out.

The door swung inward on its own.
