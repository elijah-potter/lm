A sunrise flickers past my alarm’s bleak hum, the clock ticking like a metronome in slow motion. By mid-morning, the coffee’s warmth has dissolved into steam, and the morning’s routine feels like a slow, relentless sprint through a maze of obligations. The day unfolds in disjointed segments—coffee breaks stretching into half-hours, emails piling up like unpaid bills, meetings where time feels like a liquid draining from the glass.

The afternoon’s descent is inevitable: the hum of the printer, the distant sound of the office fridge opening and closing, the way conversations feel like half-formed thoughts, unfinished. The sun, once a golden promise, now glares through the blinds like a warning sign—too much light, too little shade. By evening, the day has stretched into a thin, aching ribbon, and the promise of "tomorrow" feels like a distant horizon, a place where the day might finally end.

The clock on the wall ticks slower than the heart of a patient, counting down the minutes until the evening’s quiet, the quiet that feels like a breath held too long.
