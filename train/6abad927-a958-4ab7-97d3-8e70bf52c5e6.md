The lighthouse keeper, Silas Blackwood, adjusted the wick of the lamp, the familiar scent of paraffin a comforting balm against the relentless churn of the North Atlantic. For thirty-seven years, he had tended the beacon on Cormorant’s Point, a solitary sentinel against the unforgiving sea. The wind, a constant companion, howled a mournful song around the granite tower, rattling the thick glass panes.

Tonight, however, the wind carried a different sound – a faint, rhythmic tapping against the stone. Silas frowned, his weathered face etched with a flicker of unease. He’s heard storms lash against the lighthouse countless times, but this was distinct, deliberate. It wasn't the crash of waves or the shriek of wind; it was a measured percussion against the structure itself.

He descended the spiral staircase, the iron steps cold beneath his worn boots, and made his way to the base of the tower. The tapping grew louder, originating from the seaward side. He opened the heavy oak door, bracing himself against the wind’s force, and peered into the gloom.

Standing on the slippery rocks, clinging precariously to the tower’s foundation, was a young woman. Her clothes, though soaked and clinging, were of fine quality, and her face, pale and drawn, possessed a striking beauty. 

“Miss,” Silas said, his voice rough from years of salt air and solitude, “what are you doing out here? This is no place for anyone, especially in this weather.”

The woman, whose name was Elara Thorne, explained that her ship, *The Wanderer*, had been caught in the storm and broken apart. She was the only survivor, having managed to cling to a piece of wreckage until it washed ashore near the lighthouse. 

Silas ushered her inside, offering a thick woolen blanket and a cup of strong tea. He listened patiently as she recounted her ordeal, her voice trembling with exhaustion and lingering shock. He provided what little comfort he could, a seasoned presence in the face of her recent trauma. 

“The storm is predicted to abate by morning,” Silas said, “I’m sending a message to the mainland. They’ll dispatch a vessel to retrieve you.”

Elara nodded, her gaze fixed on the flickering lamplight. "Thank you, Silas," she said softly. "You've saved my life." 

The hours passed in quiet companionship. Silas shared stories of his long years at Cormorant’s Point, tales of shipwrecks and rescues, of the sea’s capricious nature. Elara listened intently, finding solace in his calm demeanor and the simple rhythm of his words. 

As dawn broke, painting the sky with hues of rose and gold, a rescue vessel appeared on the horizon. Elara prepared to leave, a shadow of her former self, but with a newfound resilience in her eyes.

“I won’t forget you, Silas,” she said, extending a hand. Her touch was brief, but firm. 

Silas watched as the ship carried her away, a small figure against the vast expanse of the ocean. He returned to his duties, tending the lamp, the wind still howling, but the silence of Cormorant’s Point felt different now, touched by the memory of a life saved and a connection forged in the heart of a storm. The rhythmic sweep of the light continued, a constant promise of safety against the unpredictable sea.
