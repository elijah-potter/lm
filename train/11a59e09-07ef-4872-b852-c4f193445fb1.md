A half-empty coffee mug sat on the kitchen counter, its saucer cracked in two. The remaining coffee had long since cooled to lukewarm, the steam long vanished. Nearby, an open laptop screen flickered with a partially completed email draft, the cursor blinking at the end of an unfinished sentence.

The owner—someone who had been working late—leaned over, fingers tracing the rim of the mug. Their sharp intake of breath was audible. They reached into the pocket of their worn jeans, pulling out a crumpled wallet. Inside, the only remaining cash was a single twenty-dollar bill, its edges singed from the pocket’s frayed fabric.

A sigh escaped. The fridge door stood ajar, its contents untouched since the morning. The fridge itself, though, had been opened too many times—last week’s forgotten sandwiches, the expired yogurt, the half-drunk bottle of milk that had turned slightly cloudy.

They stood there for a long moment, staring at the mug and the empty spaces around it. Then, slowly, they pulled out their phone, thumb hovering over the camera icon.
