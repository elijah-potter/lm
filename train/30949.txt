All had suffered nerve damage in climbing and motorcycle accidents.
They were in pain and had little control over their real hands.
So the Austrian trio opted to amputate and have them replaced by advanced robotic prosthetics they control just by thinking.
“Existing surgical techniques for such injuries are crude and ineffective and result in poor hand function.”Professor Oska Aszmann
Three months on and for the first time since their injuries all three men can now undertake everyday tasks like dressing themselves, pouring drinks and picking up objects.
Professor Oska Aszmann developed the bionic reconstruction technique at the Medical University of Vienna.
He said the nerve injuries suffered by the men in effect amounted to "an inner amputation".
"Existing surgical techniques for such injuries are crude and ineffective and result in poor hand function.
"The scientific advance here was that we were able to create and extract new neural signals via nerve transfers amplified by muscle transplantation.
"These signals were then decoded and translated into solid mechatronic hand function," he said.
It took nine months of mental training to learn how to control a computer-generated virtual hand before the trio could practise with a prosthesis.
