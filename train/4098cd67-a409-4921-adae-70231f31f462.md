A miscommunication between two engineers led to an unexpected discovery. During a hardware test, an incorrect assumption about voltage tolerances caused a faulty circuit board to fail under simulated conditions. Upon reviewing schematics, the team noticed the board's overcurrent protection wasn’t designed for the new high-frequency signal source. Debugging revealed the signal’s peak amplitude exceeded specs by 15%, but the board’s protective circuit had been sized for a different application’s lower peak draw. Revising the design to include a wider tolerance margin for transient spikes resolved the issue. The lesson: assumptions about component limits can mask underlying design flaws until runtime.
