In a dimly lit attic, a single candle flickers against the dust-coated walls. Old wooden furniture creaks under the weight of decades. A framed photograph hangs on the wall, its edges yellowed with age—two children, one boy and one girl, smiling at the camera. The boy, now a man, sits at a cluttered desk, fingers tracing the worn edges of a diary. His mother’s voice echoes softly: *"Remember this promise, my love. Never forget what we built together."* The diary’s pages are filled with names, dates, and scribbled promises: *"I’ll learn to fly like you," "We’ll always have this place," "You’re the bravest person I know."* The boy’s breath hitches as he flips through the pages, each one a thread to a past that still burns bright in his memory. The candle’s flame dances like a ghost, watching.
