The neon glow of the old diner pulsed like a dying heartbeat, casting long shadows across the cracked pavement where the last of the midnight shift had strolled home. The jukebox, its records worn smooth by decades of use, played a song from 1978 that no one in this town had heard since the 80s: *"The Last Broadcast"* by *Static Clash*.

Inside, the counter was a graveyard of half-eaten burgers and abandoned coffee cups. Behind the bar, a man in a stained apron—*Harlan*, the owner—adjusted the flickering fluorescent lights. His reflection in the mirror above the cash register showed lines that hadn’t been there when he was a kid, when this place smelled of fresh fry oil and laughter. Now, it smelled of rust and the faint metallic tang of something old.

A door creaked open. A girl stepped inside, her boots scuffing against the linoleum. She was older than Harlan remembered, her dark hair streaked with silver, her hands calloused but steady. She carried a satchel, its contents hidden beneath a loose strap.

*"You remember me, don’t you?"* she asked, voice rough but not unkind.

Harlan exhaled through his nose. *"You’re the one who brought the tapes to me in ’92."*

She nodded. *"Found ‘em in an old trunk at my uncle’s house. Said they were from the ’60s. Said they were important."*

Harlan poured her a cup of black coffee, no sugar, no milk. *"And you’re telling me you’re not gonna listen to ‘em?"*

She took the cup, sipped. The steam curled in the cold air. *"I am. But not here."*

A beat. Then, the jukebox cut out. The silence was thick, broken only by the distant hum of the city beyond the window—cars, sirens, the occasional scream.

*"What if it’s not what you think?"* Harlan muttered.

She looked at him. *"I already know."*

Outside, the first light of dawn crept over the rooftops, painting the street in gold. Somewhere, a siren wailed. Somewhere else, a radio tuned to an old AM station, broadcasting static and whispers.

And then—

The door swung open again. This time, it wasn’t just a girl. It was a crowd. Not the kind that filled the streets at a protest or a parade. This crowd moved like shadows, like things that had been *waiting*. They carried no signs, no banners, no weapons—but their eyes burned with the same hunger as the city’s.

Harlan’s coffee cup shattered on the floor.

The girl’s voice was a blade. *"You should’ve listened."*

The last thing Harlan saw was the jukebox spinning again—*Static Clash*, *"The Last Broadcast"*—as the crowd filed out, leaving behind only the echo of a song that had never ended.
