The rain tapped against the window like unanswered questions, each drop a whispered doubt echoing through the hollow room. I sat cross-legged on the floor, fingers tracing the edge of the coffee cup—warm, forgotten—while the city beyond blurred into something neither light nor dark, just the weight of what wasn’t said.

Outside, the neon sign of the café flickered like a dying candle, casting long shadows that stretched into the corners where shadows *moved*, not of men, but of the things that lingered. I had come here to finish what I started, but the first draft was a mess of half-formed words and half-remembered faces, and the second one had dissolved into a puddle on the page. The third draft was just a skeleton, bones of ideas left to rust.

The barista, her eyes too knowing, slid a second cup toward me. "Empty," she said, and I took it without looking up. She didn’t ask if I was tired. She knew.

I pressed a button on my phone—an old habit, a crutch—and the voice mail played, a recording of my own voice, muffled, breathless: *"I don’t know what I’m doing. I don’t know what to do."* The words had been true then, and now they were just echoes, layered over with the sound of the city’s endless pulse.

The door creaked. A figure stepped inside, tall and quiet, and I nearly jumped. She wore a coat that was too dark, too *right*, like it had been made for someone who didn’t belong here. I should have run. I should have said something. But the words stuck in my throat, and the silence stretched between us like a bridge that had been built too soon.

She leaned against the counter, watching the rain. "You’re not alone," she said.

I swallowed. "No," I whispered. "But I’m not sure how to find the others."
