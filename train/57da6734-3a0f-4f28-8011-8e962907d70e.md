A dimly lit café in downtown Chicago at 3:17 AM. The neon sign flickers—*"The Last Stop"*—while rain taps against the window. Across the room, an older man in a worn trench coat sits at a corner table, nursing a half-empty glass of whiskey. His fingers trace the rim of the glass, recalling a time before the war when this very café was a diner in a small town in Kentucky. He doesn’t recognize the barista, the waitress, or the other patrons—yet he *feels* them, as if they’ve been here before.

A hand claps his shoulder. The man turns, startled. It’s a woman in a vintage dress from the 1920s—one he once knew as a child. She smiles, but her eyes are sharp with something unsaid. *"You’re still here,"* she murmurs. His breath catches. The waitress rushes over, whispering to the bartender, who glances up with wide, unblinking eyes. The café’s air smells like stale coffee and dust from a century ago. The past has found him, again.
