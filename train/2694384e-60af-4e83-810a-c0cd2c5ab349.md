In the dim glow of a cracked fluorescent light, Elias sat cross-legged on the cold concrete floor of his apartment, fingers tracing the worn grooves of his leather-bound journal. The weight of his last failure pressed against his chest like a physical blow. His reflection in the half-moon window above him was a stranger—eyes hollow, jaw clenched tight enough to draw blood. The silence was suffocating, broken only by the distant hum of the city.

He had been trying to write for three nights straight, but the words wouldn’t come. Not the ones he needed. Not the ones he *should* have. The truth was buried deeper than the cracks in the pavement, deeper than the guilt that coiled in his gut like a living thing. He had failed his brother again. Not by choice, but by inertia. By the way he had watched him suffer, helpless, while Elias stayed in the shadows, drowning in his own excuses.

The phone buzzed. A text from his therapist: *"You’re not alone in this."* Elias exhaled sharply, the breath curling into the cold air. He knew that. But how did you tell someone else what you’d been carrying for months? The words felt like sandpaper against his throat.

Outside, a car horn blared. The city pulsed with life—people rushing, laughter, the distant wail of sirens. Elias looked up at the window, at the light that had never been his. He had spent so long chasing things that weren’t his to find, that he’d forgotten how to just *be*. Now, the weight of it all settled over him like a shroud. He had to decide: keep running, or stop and look down the abyss for once and see what fell out.

The journal lay open on the floor beside him, its blank pages staring back. He reached for a pen, the tip trembling. What would he write? Or would he leave it empty, this time?
