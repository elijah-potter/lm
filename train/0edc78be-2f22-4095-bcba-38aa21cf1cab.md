The enforcement of mandatory seatbelt use in vehicles was formally implemented in 1983 via federal legislation, but local enforcement disparities led to enforcement gaps. By the mid-1990s, state compliance rates hovered around 40%, with enforcement inconsistencies, particularly in rural areas. A 2000 study by the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) found that only 52% of drivers adhered to the law, often citing perceived leniency or lack of consequences. Between 2001 and 2005, enforcement shifted to "spot checks" with a focus on high-risk areas, reducing overall citations by 30%. By 2010, the NHTSA reported a 12% decline in seatbelt usage, attributing it to relaxed enforcement and public perception of reduced penalties. The 2016 reauthorization of the Motor Vehicle Safety Act reaffirmed the law but did not mandate stricter enforcement protocols, leading to a 2018 study noting that only 78% of drivers consistently buckled up, citing "safety culture" rather than legal consequences. Enforcement has since shifted to high-visibility campaigns with minimal enforcement, maintaining a 65% compliance rate as of 2023, illustrating a subtler, systemic shift away from formal rule enforcement toward voluntary compliance and public perception.
