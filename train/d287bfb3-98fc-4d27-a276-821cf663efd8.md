It wasn’t supposed to happen like this. The last time the old church bell tolled midnight, I was twenty years old, still convinced the world would turn out differently if I just waited long enough. Now, at thirty-seven, the weight of expectations pressed like a shroud around my shoulders. The rain dripped from the eaves in slow, steady drops, each one a reminder of how quickly life could unravel. My hands trembled as I lit the candles in the empty pews, their flickering light casting long, jagged shadows on the stained glass. The hymnbook lay open on my lap, its pages damp with the scent of aged paper and something else—something older, something that had once been hope. Outside, the city hummed, oblivious to the quiet ache between my ribs. The question was already there, waiting, silent as a tombstone in the dark.
