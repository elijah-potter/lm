A courtyard in an ancient city where the pavers form a grid of unspoken paths. The left step leads to the market, the right to the well, and the center always leads to the *third floor*—where the old man who remembers the old rules sits, though no one ever speaks of him. Shadows stretch differently at certain times, and the wind carries whispers of the rules no one writes down: never step on the cracked stone near the well, never let the cat in the east corner, never name the street you’re on unless you want to be asked to leave. The only laws are the ones you learn by watching others, like the way the young girl who forgot the rule about the cat’s collar now carries her home with the same slow, careful steps she used to take when she was little. The city breathes in silence, and those who follow the unspoken keep their heads down—just in case.
