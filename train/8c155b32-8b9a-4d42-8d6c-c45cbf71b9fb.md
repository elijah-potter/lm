---
**Setting:** *A dimly lit attic, half-filled with boxes and forgotten trinkets. Rain taps against the rusted iron roof. Elias kneels beside a crumbling wooden chest marked with faded Cyrillic script.*

---

Elias’ fingers trace the grooves of the chest’s lock—a rusted mechanism that doesn’t budge. The keys in his pocket are blunt, dull. He drops them into the rain, then retrieves a tarnished silver keychain from the pocket of his worn coat. The chain snaps under pressure, but the single key inside fits perfectly. With a grunt, the chest clicks open. Inside: a leather-bound journal, its pages yellowed with age, and a photograph—black-and-white, grainy—of a woman in a long coat, her face obscured by a veil, holding a similar key.

A knock at the door. Elias freezes. The journal clatters to the floor. He grabs it, shoving the photograph into his coat pocket as he slips out the back door. The wind howls. The keychain clinks against his belt.

Outside, the streetlights flicker. The photograph’s edges blur under the damp. Elias exhales sharply. The woman’s eyes—*they’re watching*. And now, so are the neighbors. The key doesn’t belong to him. Not anymore.
