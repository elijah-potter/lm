## TIL the "Smell of Rain" is Actually Multiple Compounds

Today I learned that the distinct, earthy scent many associate with rain – often referred to as “petrichor” – isn't a single aroma, but a complex blend of several organic compounds released into the air. While the term "petrichor" itself was only coined in 1964 by two Australian scientists, Richard P. Thomas and Isabel Joy Bear, the phenomenon it describes has been experienced and appreciated for millennia.

The primary contributors to this smell are geosmin and plant oils. Geosmin, produced by bacteria called *Streptomyces*, resides in the soil and is released when raindrops impact the ground, aerosolizing the compound. Humans are incredibly sensitive to geosmin, able to detect it in concentrations as low as 5 parts per trillion. The second component arises from oils secreted by plants that accumulate on surfaces during dry periods. Rain then washes these oils into the air, creating a fragrant, sometimes citrusy, scent. The specific aroma can vary significantly depending on the soil composition, plant life, and even the intensity of the rainfall.




It’s fascinating how something so universally recognized and evocative is actually the result of such a specific and scientifically explainable process.
