Axiom Space, a Houston-based powerhouse, is on a formidable quest: to revolutionize the off-Earth economy and take it soaring to unprecedented levels.

Established in 2016, the company harbors an audacious dream, to erect its private abode in low Earth orbit (LEO). With the first foundational stone expected to be laid in the forthcoming years, Axiom's blueprint of the future features an intriguing alliance with SpaceX. Multiple tourist missions to the International Space Station (ISS) have been agreed upon, with the inaugural journey planned for launch in the closing days of February 2022.

But these individual projects, impressive as they are, merely form pieces of a grander tapestry. Axiom envisions an expanded human influence beyond the confines of our home planet, in the vast expanse of space.

"Axiom sees the future as a bustling habitat in the cosmos, fostering breakthroughs and perspectives that advance every person on every corner of the Earth. We're the gatekeepers to low Earth orbit, offering its keys to pioneers, governments, and individuals alike," claims the company on their website.

A plethora of esteemed aerospace experts are the architects behind this vision. Among them is Axiom's co-founder, president, and CEO, Mike Suffredini, a seasoned veteran who directed NASA’s ISS program from 2005 to 2015. Another visionary at the helm is co-founder and executive chairman Kam Ghaffarian, who is also known for co-establishing Stinger Ghaffarian Technologies. This contractor is renowned for training NASA astronauts and other personnel involved in the ISS program.

Adding to the stellar lineup are names such as Charlie Bolden, Michael López-Alegría, and Peggy Whitson. Bolden, a former space shuttle commander who spearheaded NASA from 2009 to 2017, serves as a business development consultant. López-Alegría and Whitson, both ex-NASA astronauts, contribute to the venture as the vice president of business development and astronaut consultant, respectively.

Their blueprint for establishing the commercial space station is methodical. Axiom plans to launch numerous modules to the ISS, eventually having this private infrastructure break free and establish an independent outpost.

The maiden module is penciled in for a September 2024 lift-off. Three more modules are projected to follow by the close of 2027, conditional on everything going according to the master plan.

"With the fourth module joining its siblings, Axiom Station will gain the autonomy to part ways from the ISS, metamorphosing into an upgraded, next-gen space station with enhanced crew accommodations, augmented payload capacity, and a devoted manufacturing and research lab module," Axiom's chief technology officer Matt Ondler explained to Space.com.

He anticipates a smooth transition as the timeline coincides with the current planned termination of ISS operations. The goal is to ensure an uninterrupted human presence in LEO.

While the ISS is currently green-lit to function until 2024, many anticipate its lifespan to be extended to 2030. President Joe Biden has pledged his support for this extension, though it requires the agreement of other ISS partners such as the European Space Agency, the Canadian Space Agency, the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency, and Russia's Roscosmos.

Axiom Station and other commercial stations in LEO could unlock fresh prospects for microgravity research, product development, tourism, and manufacturing, among other activities, according to Ondler.

"A commercial space station could be a boon for organizations like NASA, allowing them to pursue fundamental space science research and train astronauts for extended lunar missions and Martian journeys without the financial onus of maintaining a LEO station," he remarked.
