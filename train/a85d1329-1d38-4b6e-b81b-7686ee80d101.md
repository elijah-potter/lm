The sudden shift from deep twilight to golden dawn bathed the eastern horizon in an eerie, molten glow. Shadows stretched unnaturally long across the cracked pavement, while the air hummed with an unnatural silence—until the first chime of a church bell shattered it. The sky, once a canvas of muted indigo, now pulsed with streaks of molten copper and violet, as if the heavens themselves were bleeding. A single streetlamp flickered to life, its bulb burning with an unnatural, phosphorescent blue. The wind carried the scent of damp earth and ozone, thick and electric. A woman’s breath caught as she stepped forward, her shadow—no longer hers—stretched out in jagged, distorted lines behind her. The streetlights flared in unison, casting everything in sudden, distorted relief: a parked car’s headlights, now warped like liquid glass, a child’s laughter echoing off a building that seemed to twist and fold in on itself. The world held its breath.
