**The Last Light**

Beneath the city’s skeletal glow, where neon ghosts flickered like dying stars, the old man sat on the curb, fingers tracing the cracks in the sidewalk. His breath came slow, the kind that didn’t need to be forced. The traffic hummed past—cars that knew better than to stop, to linger. He had been here since dawn.

A match snapped in his palm. The flame danced, casting shadows that stretched like hands across the pavement. He exhaled smoke into the cold air, watching it curl into the sky, a ghost of something he’d once been. The city’s pulse throbbed behind him, indifferent. Somewhere, a siren wailed. He didn’t hear it.

The match burned out. He crushed it under his heel, then stood, dusting his knees. The pavement was his only witness now. He walked toward the river, where the water whispered secrets only the drowned could hear. The current pulled him forward, smooth and relentless. He wasn’t afraid. Not anymore.
