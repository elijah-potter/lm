**Notes on Decisions & Crafting:**

*   **Formal Tone:** The language is professional and respectful, avoiding colloquialisms or overly emotional phrasing.
*   **Specificity:** Instead of generic complaints ("I'm unhappy"), I included specific examples: the shift in priorities, the introduction of a new technology, the changes to the workflow.  This demonstrates that the decision was based on concrete observations, not just a general feeling.  I *strongly* recommend tailoring the bracketed details to your actual situation.
*   **Positive Framing (Initially):** Acknowledging the positive aspects of the experience (opportunities, team, project) softens the impact of the negative criticisms and shows that the decision wasn't made out of spite.
*   **Reasoned Explanation:**  The "why" is the most crucial part.  I explained the *reasoning* behind the concerns. It's not enough to say "the new process is bad"; it's important to explain *why* it’s problematic (e.g., "it introduces significant risks to the stability and security of our applications").
*   **Documentation of Attempts to Resolve:** Mentioning that you raised concerns through established channels demonstrates that you made a good-faith effort to improve the situation before resigning. This protects you from potential accusations of not being a team player.
*   **Offer of Assistance:** Offering to help with the transition shows professionalism and a desire to leave on good terms.
*   **Concise and Direct:** The letter gets to the point quickly and avoids unnecessary rambling.
*   **Grammar and Style:** I meticulously reviewed the letter for grammatical errors and awkward phrasing. The language is clear, precise, and polished.
*   **No Ambiguity:** The letter leaves no doubt about the intention to resign and the effective date.
