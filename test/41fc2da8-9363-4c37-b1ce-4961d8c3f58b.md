The morning sun peeked through the window of the small, cluttered apartment, casting long shadows across the floor. Mia sat on the edge of her bed, her notebook open in her lap, the pen hovering over the page. She had been writing for hours, but this time, she felt something different—like the words were trying to tell her a story she hadn’t even started yet.

Her phone buzzed. Another reminder to leave for school. She glanced at the clock: 7:15 AM. It was already too late to catch the bus.

Outside, the city was alive with the hum of traffic and the distant sound of sirens. Mia had lived here for six months now, ever since her parents got divorced. She’d moved in with her grandfather, who lived in an old apartment above a bookstore. The place was drafty, and the walls creaked, but it was home.

She shoved her notebook closed and stood up. The last thing she wanted was to face her new classmates again. She’d already made one friend, a boy named Leo who sat beside her in math class. He was quiet, always lost in his own thoughts, but sometimes he’d crack a joke that made the whole class laugh.

As she walked down the stairs, her grandfather called after her, “You’re not forgetting your homework again, are you?”

She groaned. “I don’t have any.”

“Good. Because I don’t have any more to give you.”

Mia stopped, surprised. “What?”

“You’re old enough to handle your own life now. You’re not my kid anymore.”

She opened her mouth to protest, but then she thought about the way he’d looked at her—like he was seeing her for the first time. Like he finally believed she could do more than just survive.

“Okay,” she said, her voice smaller than she wanted it to be. “I’ll take care of myself.”

---

The bus ride to school was quiet except for the occasional chatter from the other students. Mia sat in the back, staring out the window. The city skyline blurred past, a mix of neon lights and towering buildings. She thought about her life—how it had changed so much in such a short time.

Her phone buzzed again. A text from Leo: *Hey, got time to grab lunch after school?*

She typed back: *Yeah, but I don’t know what to get.*

Leo’s reply was immediate: *How about we split a pizza? You know I’m bad at picking things.*

Mia smiled, even though she wasn’t sure she wanted pizza. But Leo was Leo.

---

Lunch was always a blur at school. Mia and Leo sat at the same table, their plates piled with greasy pizza slices, fries, and soda. The other students laughed, talked, and passed around a football. Mia tried to focus on eating, but her mind kept wandering to the questions she’d been writing in her notebook earlier.

*What if I could see the future?*
*What if I could change it?*
*What if I was the hero of my own story?*

She took a bite of pizza, but the taste was sour. She should eat more. She should try harder.

Leo leaned over the table, his voice low. “You know, most people think they’re the main character in their own story. But sometimes, the story is bigger than you think.”

Mia looked at him, surprised. “What do you mean?”

Leo shrugged. “I just mean… you don’t know everything yet. There’s more to you than you realize.”

She didn’t know what to say. But for the first time in a long time, she felt something—like hope.

---

After school, Mia walked home alone, her thoughts still tangled. She passed the bookstore her grandfather worked at, but she didn’t stop. Instead, she turned onto her street, where her apartment was just a block away.

Her grandfather was waiting for her when she got home. He looked tired, but there was something in his eyes that made her heart ache.

“You’re late,” he said, his voice rough.

“I was on my way,” she lied.

“You didn’t eat?”

She shook her head.

“You know I’m not going to feed you again,” he said, his tone firm.

Mia’s stomach twisted. She wanted to argue, but she couldn’t. Instead, she just nodded.

Her grandfather walked her to the door. “Don’t forget, tomorrow is the first day of the rest of your life.”

She didn’t know what that meant, but for the first time, she didn’t care.

---

That night, Mia sat on the edge of her bed, her notebook open. She had been writing for hours, but now, something felt different. The words were coming out fast, like she was being pulled toward them.

*I see the future. I see it all—the good, the bad, the things that will happen, the choices I’ll make.*

She closed her eyes, trying to imagine what she saw.

A car accident. A boy she loved dying. A world turned upside down.

She opened her eyes. Her notebook was filled with the words she had written, but now, she felt something else—like she was being watched.

She looked around the room. The walls were dark, the air thick with the scent of old books and dust.

Then she heard it—a whisper.

“You’re not ready.”

Mia froze. She had heard that voice before, but where?

The whisper came again, clearer this time. “The story isn’t over. You have to choose.”

She looked at her notebook. The words were still there, but now she saw something else—something that made her breath catch.

A door.

Not just any door. A door that led somewhere else. Somewhere she had never been.

She stood up, her heart pounding. She grabbed her notebook and ran to the door.

Outside, the world was just a blur of colors and sounds. But she knew, without a doubt, that she was being called.

Somewhere, a story was waiting to be written. And she was the only one who could change it.
